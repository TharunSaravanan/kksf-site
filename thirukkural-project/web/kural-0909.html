Kural 909

Being Led by Women · Penvazhich Seral · பெண்வழிச் சேறல்

அறவினையும் ஆன்ற பொருளும் பிறவினையும் பெண்ஏவல் செய்வார்கண் இல்.
Aravinaiyum aandra porulum piravinaiyum peneval seyvaarkan il.

Meaning

Men who simply do whatever their wives command will have no good deeds, no real riches, and none of life's other joys either.

Elementary

A good life has three parts: being kind, working hard, and having fun. If someone lets another person run every part of their life, they can end up missing all three.

University

This verse maps directly onto the three aims of the Kural itself: aram (அறம், virtue), porul (பொருள், wealth) and, as Parimelazhagar glosses pira-vinai (பிறவினை, 'other deeds'), inbam (இன்பம், pleasure). He explains that once virtue and wealth are gone, even pleasure is lost because its direction lies with the wife, and he notes that verses 907-909 together treat the fault of doing the wife's bidding (eval). Manakkudavar adds that even men without fear, if they simply obey, lose all three. Textual note: some editions print pen-eval as one word (பெண்ணேவல்), as in 907, while all four sources consulted print 'பெண்ஏவல்' here. Kalaignar narrows the target to men enslaved by arrogant women's orders, a reading that limits the claim. Contemporary scholars stress that Valluvar's ideal of the wife elsewhere (chapter 6) is a partner who safeguards the household, and that the chapter's real target is the abdication of one's own judgment.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Kautilya teaches that a king should pursue dharma, artha and kama in balance without neglecting any, and separately advises vigilance about dangers that can arise in the royal women's quarters.
— Kautilya, Arthashastra 1.7 and 1.20 [5]
The statecraft manual treats balance of life's aims and guarding against palace intrigue as essential to rule.
The Kural's triad of virtue, wealth and pleasure, all lost by the man who abdicates judgment, echoes Kautilya's balance.
Christianity
God tells Adam that because he listened to his wife's voice and ate of the forbidden tree, the ground is cursed and he will toil for his bread.
— Genesis 3:17 [6]
In Genesis, heeding one's wife against God's command brings loss of ease and abundance.
This is the classic biblical text behind the Kural's theme, though modern scholars and feminist theologians stress Adam's own responsibility and the shared nature of the fault.
Islam
The Qur'an describes spouses as garments for one another.
— Qur'an 2:187 [7]
Husband and wife are presented as mutual protection, comfort and covering.
A counterpoint: the Qur'an's image of mutuality offers an alternative to the Kural's binary of commanding and obeying.
Judaism
“And Rav says: Nevertheless, anyone who follows the counsel of his wife descends into Gehenna... Rav Pappa said to Abaye: But don’t people say a popular proverb: If your wife is short, stoop and whisper to her and consult with her?”
— Babylonian Talmud, Bava Metzia 59a (William Davidson/Koren-Steinsaltz translation) [8]
The Talmud records a harsh dictum against following a wife's counsel, immediately challenges it with a proverb urging consultation, and resolves that each applies to different spheres.
This parallels the Kural's warning while showing a tradition debating and limiting it, which models a critical reading of the chapter.
Buddhism
“Flowing in (from all objects) and watered by craving, feelings of pleasure arise in beings. Bent on pleasures and seeking enjoyment, these men fall prey to birth and decay.”
— Dhammapada 341 (Buddharakkhita) [9]
Pleasure-seeking, watered by craving, binds beings to suffering.
The Kural adds that the man ruled by another's will loses even the pleasures he sought.
Jainism
Hemachandra lists as a quality of the ideal Jain householder 'Anyonya-pratibandhena trivargam sadhayan', which Sangave renders as fulfilling the three-fold aim of life (dharma, artha and kama) without excluding any of its elements.
— Hemachandra, Yogashastra, list of 35 householder qualities, no. 18, as rendered in V.A. Sangave, 'Jaina ethics' (jainworld.com) [10]
Jain lay ethics asks a householder to pursue virtue, wealth and pleasure in balance so that none crowds out the others.
Kural 909 says the man who obeys his wife in everything loses all three, aram, porul and inbam, which is the same trivarga the Jain householder is told to keep in balance.
Sikhism
“The four cardinal blessings are obtained by serving the Lord.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 108, Raag Maajh, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [11]
Guru Arjan says the four traditional aims of life (dharma, wealth, desire and liberation) come through devotion to God.
The Kural says virtue, wealth and pleasure all slip away from a man who obeys his wife in everything; Gurbani names the same aims but says they are secured by right devotion.
Greek Mythology
In Prodicus's allegory, the young Heracles at a crossroads is approached by two women, Vice offering easy pleasure and Virtue offering toil and lasting good, and he chooses Virtue.
— Prodicus, The Choice of Heracles (in Xenophon, Memorabilia 2.1) [12]
The allegory frames life's direction as a choice of which voice to follow.
The Kural's man who follows another's commands rather than his own judgment fails the choice Heracles made well.
Philosophy
John Stuart Mill argues that the legal subordination of one sex to the other is wrong in itself and corrupts both, and that marriage should be a partnership of equals.
— John Stuart Mill, The Subjection of Women (1869) [13]
Mill's critique rejects the whole framework of command and obedience within marriage.
A modern counterpoint: Mill would see the Kural's problem as hierarchy itself, in either direction, rather than a husband's submission.

Real Life Example

Emperor Xuanzong loses his capital, his throne and Yang Guifei
755-756 · Chang'an and Mawei, Tang China
Late in his long reign the Tang emperor Xuanzong withdrew increasingly from government and came under the influence of his consort Yang Guifei, whose relatives dominated the court. When the An Lushan rebellion took Chang'an in 756, the emperor fled toward Sichuan, and at Mawei his mutinous escort killed members of the Yang faction and demanded Yang Guifei's death, which he was unable to refuse. He then abdicated in favour of his son.
— Sources: [14] [15] [16]
An emperor who had once presided over a golden age lost his government, his capital and in the end the woman he loved.
The kural says those who do whatever their wives command lose virtue, wealth and even pleasure, and Xuanzong lost all three in a single year.
