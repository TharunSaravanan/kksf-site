Kural 766

The Excellence of an Army · Padaimaatchi · படைமாட்சி

மறமானம் மாண்ட வழிச்செலவு தேற்றம் எனநான்கே ஏமம் படைக்கு.
Maramaanam maanda vazhichchelavu thetram enanaange emam padaikku.

Meaning

Bravery, honor, following the good example of earlier soldiers, and being trustworthy are the four things that keep an army safe.

Elementary

A good scout troop needs four things: brave members, members who care about doing the right thing, members who follow the good habits of older scouts, and a leader who can trust them. Valluvar says an army needs the same four things to stay safe and strong.

University

The couplet names four qualities: maṟam (மறம், valour), māṉam (மானம், honour, sense of shame at disgrace), māṇṭa vaḻiccelavu (மாண்ட வழிச்செலவு, walking the excellent path of predecessors), and tēṟṟam (தேற்றம்). Parimelazhagar and Mu. Varadarasanar take tēṟṟam as ‘being trusted (tested and found reliable) by the king’, while Sivayogi Sivakumar renders it as ‘clear resolve’, a genuine interpretive divergence. Ēmam (ஏமம்) means ‘protection, safeguard’, and the emphatic nāṉkē (‘these four alone’) makes the list exclusive. Both sources agree on the wording (சந்தி: தேற்ற / மெனநான்கே யேமம்).

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Krishna lists the natural qualities of the kshatriya, the warrior class, as heroism, power, determination, resourcefulness, courage in battle, generosity and leadership.
— Bhagavad Gita 18.43 [3]
The Gita defines the warrior by a set of inner virtues rather than by weapons.
Like the Kural’s four safeguards, it sees a fighting force’s strength in valour, honour and character.
Christianity
Paul describes the Christian’s armour as the belt of truth, the breastplate of righteousness, the readiness of the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit.
— Ephesians 6:14–17 [4]
Each piece of protective equipment is matched to an inner virtue.
Like the Kural, it says a soldier’s real safeguard lies in qualities of character.
Islam
“And obey Allah and His Messenger, and do not dispute and [thus] lose courage and [then] your strength would depart; and be patient. Indeed, Allah is with the patient.”
— Qur’an 8:46 (Saheeh International) [5]
Before battle, believers are told that obedience, unity and patience preserve their courage and strength.
It names safeguards of an army, as the Kural does with its four, including trustworthy obedience.
Judaism
“What man is there that is fearful and faint-hearted? let him go and return unto his house, lest his brethren’s heart melt as his heart.”
— Deuteronomy 20:8 (JPS 1917, Mechon-Mamre) [6]
Torah law sends the faint-hearted home before battle so that fear does not spread through the ranks.
Keeping only the valiant protects the army, echoing the Kural’s maṟam as its first safeguard.
Buddhism
The Buddha says a king’s elephant is fit for royal service when it has four qualities: it listens, it destroys, it endures, and it goes quickly; a monk with the corresponding four qualities is worthy of offerings.
— Anguttara Nikaya 4.114, Nāga Sutta [7]
The sutta uses a fourfold standard for a war-elephant as a model of spiritual fitness.
It parallels the Kural’s fourfold list of what makes an army trustworthy and secure.
Jainism
“Therefore a worthy and wise man should be careful, ceasing from sin and being entirely happy. The virtuous heroes of faith (have chosen) the great road, the right and certain path to perfection.”
— Sūtrakṛtāṅga 1.2.1.21 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
Rishabha's sermon to his sons tells the wise man to follow the 'great road' already taken by earlier heroes of faith.
One of the Kural's four safeguards of an army is walking the excellent path of those who went before, and the Jain verse urges walking the road the heroes of faith have already travelled.
Sikhism
“If a servant, performing service, obeys the Will of his Master, his honor increases, and he receives double his wages. But if he claims to be equal to his Master, he earns his Master’s displeasure.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 474, Raag Aasaa (Asa di Vaar, pauri 22), Guru Angad (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Angad's pauri in Asa di Vaar says that a servant earns honour by faithfully obeying his master's will, and loses it through arrogance.
The Kural lists honour and being trusted by the king among an army's safeguards; Guru Angad ties honour to faithful service under a master's will, though he speaks of the soul serving God.
Greek Mythology
Plutarch records the Spartan mother who handed her son his shield with the words ‘either this or upon this’, meaning return with it or be carried home on it.
— Plutarch, Moralia, Sayings of Spartan Women 241F [10]
The saying captures Spartan honour: to lose one’s shield in flight was the deepest disgrace.
It embodies the Kural’s māṉam, honour, as a safeguard of the army.
Philosophy
“The Commander stands for the virtues of wisdom, sincerity, benevolence, courage and strictness.”
— Sun Tzu, The Art of War 1.9 (trans. Lionel Giles) [11]
Sun Tzu defines military excellence in terms of a short list of moral virtues.
Like Valluvar, he names a compact set of character qualities as the army’s real protection.

Real Life Example

Twenty-one soldiers of the 36th Sikhs defend Saragarhi
12 September 1897 · Saragarhi, Samana Range, North-West Frontier (now Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan)
Saragarhi was a small signalling post linking Fort Lockhart and Fort Gulistan, held by twenty-one men of the 36th Sikhs under Havildar Ishar Singh. When thousands of Orakzai and Afridi tribesmen attacked, the garrison refused to surrender and fought until all were killed. All twenty-one were posthumously awarded the Indian Order of Merit, then the highest gallantry award open to Indian soldiers, and the Sikh Regiment marks 12 September as Saragarhi Day.
— Sources: [12] [13]
The defenders were trusted with a vital post and kept that trust with valour and honour that their regiment still commemorates every year.
The kural names valour, honour, following the noble path of predecessors and being trusted as an army's four safeguards, and the Saragarhi garrison displayed all four in a single day.
