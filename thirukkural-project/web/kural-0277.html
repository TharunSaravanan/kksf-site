Kural 277

Inconsistent Conduct · Koodaavozhukkam · கூடாவொழுக்கம்

புறங்குன்றி கண்டனைய ரேனும் அகங்குன்றி முக்கிற் கரியார் உடைத்து.
Purangundri kandanaiya renum agangundri Mukkir kariyaar udaiththu.

Meaning

Some people are bright red on the outside like the kundri seed (a small red seed), but inside they are as black as the seed's dark tip.

Elementary

There’s a tiny seed that is shiny red all over — except for one small black spot. Some people are like that: they look bright and good on the outside, but they hide a dark spot inside. Don’t judge only by the shiny part.

University

The kundri (குன்றி, Abrus precatorius) is a brilliant scarlet seed with a black spot at its ‘nose’ (மூக்கு); Valluvar makes it an emblem of the hypocrite, crimson-bright in outward piety, black at the core. The line plays on the repeated குன்றி in புறம் (outer) and அகம் (inner) — the fundamental Tamil poetic pair — so that the seed embodies both states at once. The seed was also used as a jeweller’s weight and is poisonous, which adds a latent irony: something small, attractive and valued conceals toxicity. The image anticipates Jesus’ ‘whited sepulchres’ and Hesiod’s Pandora — beauty outside, deceit within.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“That Brahmana, who with downcast look, of a cruel disposition, is solely intent on attaining his own ends, dishonest and falsely gentle, is one who acts like a heron.”
— Manusmriti 4.196 (Bühler translation) [3]
Manu describes the ‘heron’ pretender who keeps his eyes lowered like a meditating saint but is cruel and selfish inside.
Like the kundri seed, the heron-ascetic shows a gentle surface and a dark interior.
Christianity
“Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye are like unto whited sepulchres, which indeed appear beautiful outward, but are within full of dead men’s bones, and of all uncleanness.”
— Matthew 23:27 (KJV) [4]
Jesus likens hypocrites to whitewashed tombs — bright outside, full of corruption inside.
The Christian twin of the kundri seed: a beautiful exterior with darkness at the core.
Islam
“The signs of a hypocrite are three: 1. Whenever he speaks, he tells a lie. 2. Whenever he promises, he always breaks it… 3. If you trust him, he proves to be dishonest.”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 33 [5]
The Prophet lists the hidden traits by which someone who looks like a believer can be recognised as a hypocrite.
Tells us how to find the ‘black tip’ behind the bright exterior: look at speech, promises and trust.
Judaism
“Burning lips and a wicked heart are like a potsherd covered with silver dross.”
— Proverbs 26:23 (KJV) [4]
Proverbs compares warm, fervent speech covering a wicked heart to a cheap clay pot coated with a silvery glaze.
A shiny surface over something base — the same contrast as the kundri’s red body and black tip.
Buddhism
“Not by mere eloquence nor by beauty of form does a man become accomplished, if he is jealous, selfish and deceitful.”
— Dhammapada 262 (tr. Buddharakkhita) [6]
The Buddha says fine speech and good looks mean nothing if the person inside is envious, selfish and deceptive.
Same contrast as the Kural: a fine exterior cannot redeem a dark interior.
Jainism
“He is empty like a clenched fist, (of no value) like an uncoined false Kârshâpana or like a piece of glass resembling turquoise, he is held lightly by men of discernment.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 20.42 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [7]
The false monk is compared to things that look valuable but are hollow or counterfeit, which discerning people see through.
Valluvar's kundri seed, bright red outside but black at the tip, matches this Jain image of coloured glass passing for a gem and a false coin passing for real money.
Sikhism
“Bronze is bright and shiny, but when it is rubbed, its blackness appears.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 729, Raag Soohee, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Guru Nanak opens a hymn in Raag Soohee with the image of polished bronze, bright on the surface but leaving black marks once it is rubbed, as a picture of a person who only looks good.
Valluvar's kundri seed is bright red outside with a black tip; Guru Nanak's bronze is likewise bright outside and black underneath.
Greek Mythology
“Also the Guide, the Slayer of Argus, contrived within her lies and crafty words and a deceitful nature at the will of loud thundering Zeus…”
— Hesiod, Works and Days 69–82 (tr. H. G. Evelyn-White) [9]
The gods adorn Pandora with beauty and finery, but Hermes places lies and a deceitful nature inside her.
Pandora is the mythic kundri seed: dazzling outside, deceit within.
Philosophy
“But the affectation of simplicity is like a crooked stick. Nothing is more disgraceful than a wolfish friendship (false friendship). Avoid this most of all. The good and simple and benevolent show all these things in the eyes, and there is no mistaking.”
— Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 11.15 (tr. George Long) [10]
Marcus Aurelius says pretended goodness is twisted and false friendship is the worst disgrace; real goodness shows plainly.
Condemns the same split between an appealing outside and a hidden, predatory inside.

Real Life Example

The collapse of Ivar Kreuger, the Match King
March 1932 · Paris, France, and Stockholm, Sweden
The Swedish financier Ivar Kreuger built a business empire that controlled much of the world's match production and lent large sums to governments, and he was widely admired as a financial genius. After his death in Paris in March 1932, auditors from Price Waterhouse found that many of the assets and profits recorded by his companies were fictitious and that he had forged Italian government bonds. His group of companies collapsed and many of them went bankrupt.
— Sources: [11] [12]
Kreuger's dazzling reputation concealed a hollow and fraudulent core that was revealed only when he was gone.
The kural speaks of people who look as bright red outside as the kundri seed but are black within like its tip, and Kreuger's empire was exactly such a bright surface over a dark centre.
