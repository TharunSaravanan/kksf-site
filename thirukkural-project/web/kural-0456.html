Kural 456

Avoiding Mean Associations · Sitrinanjeraamai · சிற்றினஞ்சேராமை

மனந்தூயார்க் கெச்சம்நன் றாகும் இனந்தூயார்க் கில்லைநன் றாகா வினை.
Mananthooyaark kechcham nanraagum inanthooyaark Killai nanraakaa vinai.

Meaning

People with pure minds leave behind a good name for those who come after them. People who keep pure company never do anything that is not good.

Elementary

People with clean hearts leave good things behind them: good children, a good name. People whose friends are good never end up doing bad things, because their friends keep them on track.

University

எச்சம் (‘that which remains’) is read by Parimelazhagar as good progeny, and by others as the good name that outlives a person; both senses make purity of mind a matter of legacy. The second line is sharper: for those with pure company there is no deed that is not good (இல்லை நன்றாகா வினை). The double negative is emphatic. Good company does not merely encourage good action; it rules out bad action. The couplet divides the labour: the mind decides what one leaves behind, and company decides what one does.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Krishna tells Arjuna that a sincere seeker who falls short in this life is reborn into a family of the pure and prosperous, or even into a family of wise yogis.
— Bhagavad Gita 6.41–42 [4]
Purity of mind is rewarded with a good lineage and a good circle in which to continue.
This matches the Kural’s link between a pure mind and a good ‘eccham’ (continuation or posterity).
Christianity
“Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit. A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit.”
— Matthew 7:17–18 (KJV) [5]
Jesus teaches that what a person produces flows from what they are.
This fits the Kural’s claim that a pure source leaves good fruit, and its double negative (‘cannot bring forth evil fruit’).
Islam
The Prophet taught that when a person dies, their deeds end except for three: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, or a righteous child who prays for them.
— Sahih Muslim 1631 [6]
A good legacy (the righteous child, lasting good) continues after death.
This corresponds closely to Parimelazhagar’s reading of எச்சம் as good progeny.
Judaism
“Blessed is the man that feareth the LORD… His seed shall be mighty upon earth: the generation of the upright shall be blessed.”
— Psalm 112:1–2 (KJV) [5]
The upright leave a blessed posterity behind them.
This is close to the Kural’s claim that the pure-minded leave a good எச்சம்.
Buddhism
“If with a pure mind a person speaks or acts happiness follows him like his never-departing shadow.”
— Dhammapada 2 (trans. Buddharakkhita) [7]
Deeds done from a pure mind leave an unbroken trail of good results.
Both texts see a pure mind as producing lasting good consequences.
Jainism
“He who adopts the conduct which the wise ones have attained by their virtues and always practised, will not incur blame.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 1.42 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The lecture on discipline promises that one who takes on the conduct practised by the wise will do nothing that deserves blame.
The kural's second line says those whose company is pure do no deed that is not good. The Jain verse likewise says that following the wise keeps one free of blameworthy acts.
Sikhism
“In the Company of the Holy, one's feet do not wander.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 271, Raag Gauree (Sukhmani), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Arjan says that in holy company a person does not step onto crooked paths; commentators gloss the line as not taking a step towards wrongdoing.
The Kural says those whose company is pure do no deed that is not good; Guru Arjan likewise says that in pure company one's steps do not go astray.
Greek Mythology
The pious old couple Baucis and Philemon welcome disguised gods. Their cottage becomes a temple, and at death they are turned into an intertwined oak and linden that are honoured ever after.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 8 [10]
Pure hearts leave a lasting and venerated legacy.
This fits the Kural’s ‘eccham’, the good that outlives the pure-minded.
Philosophy
“The friendship of good men is good, being augmented by their companionship.”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IX.12 (trans. W. D. Ross) [11]
Aristotle says good men grow better together, taking on from each other the traits they admire, whereas bad men become worse by growing alike.
This matches the Kural’s second line: those in good company are held to good action.

Real Life Example

The White Rose student resistance in Munich
1942-1943 · Munich, Germany
In 1942 a small circle of friends at the University of Munich, including Hans and Sophie Scholl, Alexander Schmorell, Christoph Probst, Willi Graf and their professor Kurt Huber, wrote and secretly distributed leaflets urging Germans to oppose the Nazi regime. On 18 February 1943 Hans and Sophie Scholl were caught distributing the sixth leaflet at the university. They and other members were tried by the People's Court and put to death in 1943, and they are now honoured in Germany as examples of moral courage.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A group of friends held one another to a standard of conscience at a time when most people stayed silent, and they left a lasting example.
The kural says the pure-minded leave a good legacy and those whose company is pure do no deed that is not good, and the White Rose circle acted together and is remembered together.
