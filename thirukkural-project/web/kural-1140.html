Kural 1140

The Abandonment of Reserve · Naanuth Thuravuraithal · நாணுத் துறவுரைத்தல்

யாம்கண்ணின் காண நகுப அறிவில்லார் யாம்பட்ட தாம்படா ஆறு.
Yaamkannin kaana nagupa arivillaar yaampatta thaampadaa aaru.

Meaning

People with no understanding laugh at me right in front of my eyes, only because they have never suffered what I suffer.

Elementary

Have you ever seen someone laugh at a friend who was crying, just because they didn't understand how sad the friend felt? The speaker says people who laugh at lovers only do it because they have never felt that pain themselves.

University

The couplet is built on a balanced contrast: yām paṭṭa (யாம் பட்ட), 'what I have suffered', against tām paṭā (தாம் படா), 'what they have not suffered', with āṟu (ஆறு), 'the manner, the reason', at the end. Aṟivillār (அறிவில்லார்), 'those without understanding', laugh kaṇṇiṉ kāṇa (கண்ணின் காண), 'so that I see it with my eyes', that is, openly to the speaker's face. Commentators take it as the closing lament of the chapter, spoken in the tradition as the heroine's words once her love is known, and Kalaignar turns it into a general maxim: only those who have never known love's pain mock its sufferers. Pope: 'Before my eyes the foolish make a mock of me, / Because they ne'er endured the pangs I now must drie.' The text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In her songs, the princess-poet Mirabai says that people call her mad and that she has thrown away family honour and public shame (lok-laj) for love of Krishna, and she accepts their mockery.
— Mirabai, padas (paraphrase; cf. tr. A.J. Alston, The Devotional Poems of Mirabai, 1980) [3]
The bhakti tradition preserves Mira's defiant answer to the scorn of her in-laws and her town.
Like the Kural's speaker, Mira is laughed at by those who have never felt the love that has seized her.
Christianity
“Others mocking said, These men are full of new wine.”
— Acts 2:13 (KJV) [4]
At Pentecost, onlookers who did not understand what had seized the apostles mocked them as drunk.
As in the Kural, people who have not shared an overwhelming experience laugh at those who have.
Islam
“This is the one for whose love you criticized me!”
— Qur'an 12:32 (tr. Mustafa Khattab, The Clear Quran) [5]
After the women of the city have seen Joseph for themselves and been overwhelmed, the noblewoman answers their earlier gossip.
Her retort makes the Kural's point: those who mock a lover change their tune once they have felt the same thing.
Judaism
“When David dances with all his might before the Ark, Michal mocks him for shamelessly uncovering himself, and he answers: “And I will be yet more vile than thus, and will be base in mine own sight.”
— II Samuel 6:20-22 (JPS 1917, via Sefaria) [6]
David refuses to be shamed out of his joyous, undignified devotion by someone who does not share it.
Like the Kural's speaker, David is mocked for abandoning reserve by one who has not felt what moves him.
Buddhism
The Buddha tells Atula that people have always blamed others: they blame the one who sits silent, the one who speaks much, and the one who speaks little; there is no one in the world who is not blamed.
— Dhammapada 227 (paraphrase; cf. tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The verse teaches that ridicule from others says more about the world than about the one ridiculed.
The Kural's lover is laughed at too; the Dhammapada advises not to measure oneself by such mockery.
Jainism
The Acaranga recounts that during Mahavira's wanderings in the rough Ladha country people struck him, set dogs on him and abused him, and he bore it all without anger.
— Acaranga Sutra 1.8.3 (paraphrase; tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22) [8]
Mahavira's calm under the scorn and violence of those who did not understand him is a model of Jain forbearance.
The Kural's lover is mocked by those who have not shared his experience; Mahavira shows a Jain answer to such ridicule, patient endurance.
Sikhism
“People say that Kabeer has gone insane. Only the Lord realizes the secret of Kabeer.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1158, Raag Bhairao, Bhagat Kabir (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Kabir answers those who mock him as mad by saying that only the Lord, who shares his love, understands what he feels.
The Kural's lover says thoughtless people laugh at him only because they have never suffered as he has; Kabir likewise says the mockers cannot know what only the Beloved knows.
Greek Mythology
When Hephaestus traps Ares and Aphrodite in his net, he calls the gods to see, and they break into unquenchable laughter at the lovers.
— Homer, Odyssey 8 (Song of Demodocus) (paraphrase) [10]
The bard Demodocus' song in the Odyssey shows lovers exposed to the laughter of onlookers.
Like the Kural's speaker, the lovers are laughed at openly once their love is known, though in Homer the laughter has more justification.
Philosophy
“As he forgets earthly interests and is rapt in the divine, the vulgar deem him mad, and rebuke him; they do not see that he is inspired.”
— Plato, Phaedrus 249d (tr. Benjamin Jowett) [11]
Socrates says the person seized by the divine madness of love is mocked by ordinary people who do not understand it.
This is the Kural's complaint exactly: those without understanding laugh at a lover because they have not felt what he feels.

Real Life Example

Richard and Mildred Loving are convicted for their marriage
1958-1959 · Caroline County, Virginia, United States
Richard Loving, who was white, and Mildred Jeter, who was of African American and Native American descent, married in Washington, D.C., on 2 June 1958 and returned home to Caroline County, Virginia, where interracial marriage was illegal. Weeks later the sheriff arrested them at home in the early morning, and in January 1959 they pleaded guilty and were sentenced to a year in prison, suspended on condition that they leave Virginia. In 1967 the United States Supreme Court unanimously struck the law down in Loving v. Virginia.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Officials who had no understanding of the couple's love treated it as a crime, and only years later did the nation's highest court see it differently.
The kural's lover says thoughtless people mock him because they have never suffered as he has; the Lovings were judged and punished by people who did not understand what their love meant to them.
