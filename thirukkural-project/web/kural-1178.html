Kural 1178

Eyes Consumed with Grief · Kanvithuppazhithal · கண்விதுப்பழிதல்

பேணாது பெட்டார் உளர்மன்னோ மற்றவர்க் காணாது அமைவில கண்.
Paenaadhu pettaar ularmanno matravark kaanaadhu amaivila kan.

Meaning

There is someone who wanted me but did not truly care for me. And yet my eyes cannot rest without seeing him.

Elementary

Have you ever had a friend who said they liked you but didn’t really act like it, and still you kept looking for them on the playground? The lady feels like that. She knows he isn’t caring for her the way he should, but her eyes still keep searching for him.

University

Pēṇātu peṭṭār, ‘one who desired (me) without cherishing (me)’, sets peṭṭal (to desire, to love) against pēṇutal (to care for, to tend); uḷar maṉṉō, ‘there is indeed such a one’, carries a sigh of reproach. Avark kāṇātu amaivila kaṇ: ‘without seeing him, the eyes do not settle’. Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah gloss the first line as ‘loved in word only, not in heart’, and Pope’s prose follows this: ‘He is indeed here who loved me with his lips but not with his heart but mine eyes suffer from not seeing him’, while his verse reads ‘Who loved me once, unloving now doth here remain’. Commentators thus differ on whether the husband is physically near but emotionally absent or simply away; the eyes’ restlessness is the same either way. No variants were found between ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In Jayadeva’s Gita Govinda, Radha, hurt that Krishna has gone off to sport with other cowherd women, still cannot stop longing for him and sends her friend to find him.
— Jayadeva, Gita Govinda, early cantos (Radha’s jealousy and longing) (paraphrase) [3]
Radha is wounded by Krishna’s neglect, yet her longing to see him only grows.
Like the Kural’s heroine, Radha knows her beloved has not cared for her as he should, but she cannot rest without seeing him.
Christianity
“By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not.”
— Song of Solomon 3:1 (KJV) [4]
The bride of the Song of Songs searches through the night for her beloved and cannot find him.
Her restless seeking is exactly the Kural’s eyes that ‘cannot rest without seeing him’.
Islam
“So We restored him to his mother that she might be content and not grieve and that she would know that the promise of Allah is true.”
— Qur’an 28:13 (Sahih International) [5]
Moses is returned to his mother; the Arabic phrase rendered ‘be content’ is literally that ‘her eye might be cooled’, i.e. at rest, by seeing him.
The Kural’s eyes have no such rest: only seeing the beloved could ‘cool’ them.
Judaism
“I opened to my beloved; But my beloved had turned away, and was gone. My soul failed me when he spoke. I sought him, but I could not find him; I called him, but he gave me no answer.”
— Song of Songs 5:6 (JPS 1917, via Sefaria) [6]
The beloved withdraws without explanation, and the woman goes out searching and calling though he does not answer.
As in the Kural, the beloved seems not to care, yet she cannot stop seeking to see him.
Buddhism
“Seek no intimacy with the beloved and also not with the unloved, for not to see the beloved and to see the unloved, both are painful.”
— Dhammapada 210 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Dhammapada names ‘not seeing the beloved’ as one of the two great sources of pain.
The Kural’s heroine lives exactly this pain: her eyes cannot rest because they do not see the one they love.
Jainism
Arishtanemi (Neminatha) abandons his bride Rajimati on their wedding day to become a monk; she, though forsaken, sets her heart on following him, and later renounces the world herself.
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra, Lecture 22 (Rathanemi) (paraphrase) [8]
The Uttaradhyayana tells how Rajimati, left by the groom she loved, still turns her whole life toward him.
Like the Kural’s heroine, Rajimati is loved and yet left uncared for, and still cannot turn her eyes from him.
Sikhism
“My mind longs for the Blessed Vision of the Guru's Darshan. It cries out like the thirsty song-bird. My thirst is not quenched, and I can find no peace, without the Blessed Vision of the Beloved Saint.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 96, Raag Maajh, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Arjan compares himself to the rain-bird (chatrik) crying for water: without the sight of his Beloved Guru, his thirst is never quenched and he has no peace.
The Kural's heroine says her eyes cannot rest without seeing him even though he does not truly care for her; Guru Arjan likewise says he finds no peace at all without the sight of the beloved.
Greek Mythology
Clytie, abandoned by the Sun god Helios for another, sits on the bare ground for days without food, gazing only at him as he crosses the sky, until she turns into a flower that always faces the sun.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 4.256-270 (paraphrase) [10]
Ovid’s Clytie loves a god who no longer cares for her, yet her eyes never leave him.
She is the Kural’s heroine exactly: loved and then neglected, with eyes that cannot rest unless they see him.
Philosophy
Aristotle notes that friendships based on pleasure or usefulness are easily dissolved, since when one party no longer finds the other pleasant or useful, he ceases to love.
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 8.3 (paraphrase) [11]
For Aristotle, love that is not grounded in care for the other person for their own sake does not last.
The heroine’s husband ‘loved without caring’, the kind of love Aristotle says fades, while her own eyes go on loving.

Real Life Example

Izumi Shikibu asks for one last meeting
c. early 11th century · Heian-kyo (Kyoto), Japan
Izumi Shikibu, one of the leading women poets of the Heian court, wrote a poem addressed to her beloved when she believed her life was drawing to a close. In it she asks for just one more meeting with him, so that she may carry its memory with her beyond this world. The poem was later chosen as number 56 of the Hyakunin Isshu anthology.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Her poem became one of Japan's best-known expressions of the simple, overwhelming need to see the beloved once more.
The kural says the heroine's eyes cannot rest without seeing him, and Izumi Shikibu's plea for a single last meeting shows that same longing to see the beloved again.
