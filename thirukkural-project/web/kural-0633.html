Kural 633

Ministry of State · Amaichu · அமைச்சு

பிரித்தலும் பேணிக் கொளலும் பிரிந்தார்ப் பொருத்தலும் வல்ல தமைச்சு.
Piriththalum paenik kolalum pirindhaarp Poruththalum valla thamaichchu.

Meaning

A good minister knows how to pull allies away from the enemy, keep their own side loyal, and win back people who have left.

Elementary

Imagine two teams in a playground game. A clever captain can persuade kids who are thinking of joining the other team to stay neutral, keep his own teammates happy, and make friends again with anyone who left in a huff. This kural says a good adviser has those three skills.

University

The couplet names three diplomatic skills. Piriththal is detaching allies from the enemy. Paenik kolal is keeping one’s own supporters, which Solomon Pappaiah explains as keeping them through gifts and kind words. Pirindhaar poruththal is reconciling those who have left. Commentators link these to the classical upayas (means of statecraft): bheda (division), dana (gifts) and sama (conciliation). Kautilya treats the same means at length. The last words, valla thamaichchu, are an older way of writing valladhu amaichchu (‘the minister is one capable of’), and all three sources agree on the text. Varadarasanar’s gloss and Kalaignar’s specify that those to be divided are the enemy’s helpers, which keeps the verse within fair statecraft rather than general intrigue.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Before the Kurukshetra war, Krishna travels to Hastinapura as envoy to try to reconcile the Kauravas and the Pandavas. He first appeals for peace and offers a settlement. He also warns Duryodhana’s allies and elders about the cost of war, which works on the loyalties within the Kaurava camp.
— Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva (Bhagavat-yana section), trans. K. M. Ganguli [4]
Krishna’s embassy uses conciliation, offers and division, the classical upayas, in one mission.
It shows the Kural’s three skills of reconciling, retaining and dividing as the work of a counsellor-envoy.
Christianity
“And when he had so said, there arose a dissension between the Pharisees and the Sadducees: and the multitude was divided.”
— Acts 23:6-7 (KJV) [5]
On trial before the council, Paul declares himself a Pharisee on the question of resurrection, which splits his hostile judges into two factions.
This is piriththal, dividing an opposing party, done with a single well-judged word.
Islam
“And hold firmly to the rope of Allah all together and do not become divided. And remember the favor of Allah upon you - when you were enemies and He brought your hearts together and you became, by His favor, brothers.”
— Qur’an 3:103 (Sahih International) [6]
The Qur’an reminds the believers that once-hostile groups were reconciled into brotherhood and warns them against splitting apart.
It praises the Kural’s last two skills, holding one’s own people together and reuniting those who were divided.
Judaism
“And Absalom and all the men of Israel said: ‘The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the counsel of Ahithophel.’—For the LORD had ordained to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel, to the intent that the LORD might bring evil upon Absalom.”
— II Samuel 17:14 (JPS 1917) [7]
David sends his loyal friend Hushai into the rebel Absalom’s council, where Hushai’s advice overturns Ahithophel’s dangerous plan.
Hushai breaks up the enemy’s counsel from within while staying loyal to his king. This is piriththal carried out by a counsellor.
Buddhism
In the Maha-parinibbana Sutta, King Ajatasattu sends his minister Vassakara to ask the Buddha about attacking the Vajjians. The Buddha lists seven conditions, among them meeting often and in harmony, that keep the Vajjians strong. Vassakara concludes that they cannot be conquered by force, only through diplomacy or by breaking up their unity.
— Maha-parinibbana Sutta, Digha Nikaya 16 [8]
The sutta shows both sides: unity protects a people, and a shrewd minister sees that division is the way to defeat them.
It sets keeping one’s people together against dividing an opponent, the two poles of the Kural’s verse.
Jainism
“All your princes have been seduced by King Śreṇika. Money has been sent them to make them his; and they, after accepting it, will bind you and deliver you to my father. The money has been buried in their dwellings for their benefit. Dig and look.”
— Hemacandra, Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra, Book 10, ch. 11, part 2 ‘Contest between Pradyota and Abhaya’ (tr. Helen M. Johnson, GOS; wisdomlib.org) [9]
Abhayakumara buries money in the camp of Pradyota's allied princes and then warns Pradyota that they have been bribed; Pradyota digs, finds the money and flees, and his allies' army breaks up.
The Kural praises the minister who can split the enemy's allies away; Abhaya's stratagem does exactly that, turning Pradyota against his own princes without a battle.
Sikhism
During the long siege of Anandpur a band of Sikhs disowned Guru Gobind Singh and deserted him. Later, repentant, they returned and died fighting for him at Khidrana on 29 December 1705. The Guru tore up their written disclaimer and blessed them as the Chali Mukte, the Forty Liberated Ones, and the place became Muktsar.
— Encyclopaedia of Sikhism (Punjabi University, Patiala, ed. Harbans Singh), 'Chali Mukte', via The Sikh Encyclopedia (paraphrase) [10]
The Chali Mukte are remembered daily in the Sikh Ardas; their story is one of estranged followers taken back and honoured rather than rejected.
The Kural prizes the minister who can bring back those who have broken away; Guru Gobind Singh received the deserters back and erased the record of their desertion.
Greek Mythology
After Achilles withdraws from the fighting in anger, Agamemnon sends Odysseus, Ajax and old Phoenix to him with offers of gifts to win him back to the Greek army.
— Homer, Iliad 9 (trans. Samuel Butler) [11]
The embassy is a formal attempt to reconcile an estranged ally through gifts and persuasion.
This is pirindhaar poruththal, bringing back those who have split away, which the Kural counts as a minister’s skill.
Philosophy
Sun Tzu advises that when the enemy’s forces are united they should be separated, and he treats breaking up the enemy’s alliances as better than attacking his army in the field.
— Sun Tzu, The Art of War I and III (trans. Lionel Giles) [12]
Classical Chinese strategy puts diplomatic division before open battle.
This is the Kural’s first skill, piriththal, as a doctrine of statecraft.

Real Life Example

Talleyrand splits the victorious allies at the Congress of Vienna
3 January 1815 · Vienna, Austria
At the Congress of Vienna the four victorious powers, Britain, Austria, Prussia and Russia, negotiated the post-Napoleonic settlement, with defeated France represented by its foreign minister, Talleyrand. When Russia and Prussia pressed claims that alarmed Britain and Austria, Talleyrand proposed a secret treaty of guarantee between Britain, France and Austria. It was signed on 3 January 1815, setting three powers against Russia and Prussia and giving France a seat at the great powers' table.
— Sources: [13] [14]
Representing a defeated country with no allies, Talleyrand turned the disagreements of the victors to France's advantage by drawing two of them into an alliance with his own country.
The kural praises the minister who can detach the enemy's allies and bring others over to his side, which is exactly what Talleyrand did to the coalition that had defeated France.
