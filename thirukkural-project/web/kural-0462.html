Kural 462

Acting after Due Consideration · Therindhu Seyalvakai · தெரிந்து செயல்வகை

தெரிந்த இனத்தொடு தேர்ந்தெண்ணிச் செய்வார்க்கு அரும்பொருள் யாதொன்றும் இல்.
Therindha inaththotu therndhennich cheyvaarkku, Arumporul yaadhondrum il.

Meaning

Nothing is too hard for people who first talk things over with carefully chosen advisers and then study the matter themselves before they act.

Elementary

If you pick good, trustworthy friends, talk your plan over with them, and think it through yourself too, there is almost nothing you cannot get done.

University

The couplet joins two acts of discernment: தெரிந்த இனம் (a circle that has itself been tested and chosen — echoing the preceding chapters on seeking the help of the great and avoiding mean company, 44–46) and தேர்ந்து எண்ணி (having examined and reasoned). Counsel is only as good as the counsellors, so selection comes before consultation. The strong universal claim (‘no object is hard to attain’) is typical of Valluvar’s rhetoric, which states the ideal case to motivate the practice. The idea of the ruler’s council (mantra) was a central institution of classical Indian statecraft, and the Kural places it at the heart of deliberate action.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Kautilya opens his chapter on counsel by teaching that all undertakings of state should be preceded by deliberation in a properly constituted council, and that counsel should be kept secret and taken with tested ministers.
— Kautilya, Arthashastra, Book 1, Chapter 15 [4]
India’s classic manual of government says a king should never act on big matters alone: he should first talk them through with carefully chosen, trustworthy advisers.
Same pairing as the Kural: chosen counsellors plus deliberation before action.
Christianity
Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established.
— Proverbs 15:22 (KJV) [5]
Plans fail when people make them alone; they succeed when many wise advisers help shape them.
Matches the Kural’s promise that consultation makes goals achievable.
Islam
The Qur’an describes the believers as those “whose affair is [determined by] consultation among themselves” (shura).
— Qur’an 42:38 (Sahih International) [6]
The Qur’an praises communities that decide their shared matters by talking them over together rather than by one person’s whim.
Shura is the Islamic institution of deliberating with others before acting.
Judaism
Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety.
— Proverbs 11:14 (Hebrew Bible; KJV rendering) [5]
A nation without good advice collapses; with many wise advisers it stays safe.
Same principle as the Kural, applied to national affairs.
Buddhism
When King Ajatasattu plans to attack the Vajjians, the Buddha says they will prosper and not decline so long as they meet together often, meet in harmony, and conduct their business in harmony.
— Mahaparinibbana Sutta, Digha Nikaya 16 [7]
The Buddha says a people who regularly gather and decide things together, in agreement, will be very hard to defeat.
Collective, well-ordered deliberation makes a group strong, as in the Kural.
Jainism
“This is the road to it: to serve the Gurus and the old (teachers), to avoid throughout foolish people, to apply oneself earnestly to study, and to ponder zealously on the meaning of the Sūtras.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 32.3 (cf. 32.4–5) (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; wisdomlib.org) [8]
The Uttaradhyayana's path to deliverance begins with choosing one's company: serve the teachers, shun fools, study, and reflect. The next verses tell the monk to select a companion of right understanding who equals or surpasses him in virtue (32.4–5).
Like the Kural, it joins careful choice of counsellors with one's own examination and reflection as the way to reach what is otherwise hard to attain.
Sikhism
“The chosen ones, the self-elect, are accepted and approved. The chosen ones are honored in the Court of the Lord. … The chosen ones meditate single-mindedly on the Guru.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 3, Japji Sahib (pauri 16), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the sixteenth pauri of Japji Sahib, Guru Nanak praises the panch, the chosen ones whose minds are fixed on the Guru, as accepted and pre-eminent. Later Sikh tradition took the five chosen Sikhs as the model for weighty collective decisions.
The Kural says nothing is hard for those who deliberate with a well-chosen circle; Japji Sahib honours just such a tested, chosen company.
Greek Mythology
Volunteering for the night raid on the Trojan camp, Diomedes asks for a companion, saying that when two go together one sees the advantage before the other, while a man alone has thinner wit; he picks Odysseus.
— Homer, Iliad 10.222–247 [10]
Diomedes chooses the cleverest partner he can find, because two minds notice what one would miss.
Choosing the right companion to think with before a risky action is the Kural’s point.
Philosophy
“We deliberate not about ends but about means.” Aristotle adds that on important questions we call in others to help us deliberate, distrusting our own ability to decide alone.”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III.3 (tr. W. D. Ross) [11]
Aristotle says real deliberation is working out how to reach a goal, and for big decisions sensible people ask others for help.
Aristotle’s deliberation with others mirrors the Kural’s deliberation with a chosen circle.

Real Life Example

The Delta Committee plans the Netherlands' Delta Works
1953-1986 · Zeeland and South Holland, Netherlands
After the North Sea flood of 1 February 1953 killed more than 1,800 people in the Netherlands, the government set up a Delta Committee of experts led by the director-general of Rijkswaterstaat, A. G. Maris, within weeks. Its studies produced the Delta Plan in 1955, which became law in 1958, and over the following decades engineers built a chain of dams, sluices and storm-surge barriers, ending with the Eastern Scheldt barrier in 1986.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A task once thought impossible, closing off the sea arms of the southwestern delta while keeping key shipping routes open, was achieved through years of study by a chosen body of specialists.
The kural promises that nothing is too hard for those who deliberate with well-chosen counsellors and examine matters themselves, and the Delta Works grew from exactly such a committee.
