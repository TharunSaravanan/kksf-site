Kural 210

Dread of Evil Deeds · Theevinaiyaccham · தீவினையச்சம்

அருங்கேடன் என்பது அறிக மருங்கோடித் தீவினை செய்யான் எனின்.
Arungedan enbadhu arika marungodith, Theevinai seyyaan enin.

Meaning

Know this: a person who never steps off the right path to do wrong will be safe from ruin.

Elementary

When you walk on a narrow bridge, you’re safe as long as you don’t step off the sides. This kural says life is like that bridge: if you never wander off the good path to do bad things, nothing can really ruin you.

University

The chapter closes on a note of assurance rather than warning. அருங்கேடன் combines அரு (‘rare/absent’) with கேடு (‘ruin’): ‘one for whom ruin is rare or impossible’. The pivotal image is மருங்கோடி — ‘running off to the side’ — which pictures righteousness as a straight road and evil as a swerve off it; Parimelazhagar and later commentators read it as ‘deviating from the proper path’. After nine couplets on the consequences of evil, Valluvar states the positive corollary: since ruin comes from one’s own deeds, the one who does not swerve has nothing to fear. The swerve metaphor has close parallels in Proverbs (‘turn not to the right hand nor to the left’), in the Isha Upanishad’s prayer against ‘crooked’ sin, and in Aristotle’s virtue as a mean between deviations.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“O god Agni, knowing all things that are manifested, lead us by the good path to the felicity; remove from us the devious attraction of sin.”
— Isha Upanishad 18 (trans. Sri Aurobindo) [4]
The Upanishad’s closing prayer asks the divine to keep us on the good path (supathā) and away from ‘crooked’ (juhurāṇam) sin.
Same picture of a straight good path and evil as a crooked deviation from it.
Christianity
“Turn not to the right hand nor to the left: remove thy foot from evil.”
— Proverbs 4:27 (KJV) [5]
The father in Proverbs urges his son to keep a straight path, since swerving to either side leads into evil.
Nearly identical to மருங்கோடி — evil as a sideways deviation from the straight road.
Islam
“And, [moreover], this is My path, which is straight, so follow it; and do not follow [other] ways, for you will be separated from His way.”
— Qur’an 6:153 (Sahih International) [6]
The Qur’an presents righteousness as one straight path and wrongdoing as side-roads that lead away from it.
Same straight-path imagery; not straying is the key to safety.
Judaism
“Ye shall observe to do therefore as the LORD your God hath commanded you: ye shall not turn aside to the right hand or to the left.”
— Deuteronomy 5:32 (KJV) [5]
Moses commands Israel to keep exactly to the way of the commandments without deviation, followed by the promise that it may go well with them.
Non-deviation leading to well-being — the Kural’s formula.
Buddhism
“If on the hand there is no wound, one may carry even poison in it. Poison does not affect one who is free from wounds. For him who does no evil, there is no ill.”
— Dhammapada 124 (trans. Buddharakkhita) [7]
Just as poison cannot enter unbroken skin, harm finds no entry into the life of one who commits no evil.
Exactly the Kural’s assurance: the one who does no evil is safe from ruin.
Jainism
“A careful man who does not injure living beings, is called ‘circumspect’ (samita). The sinful Karman will quit him as water quits raised ground. In thoughts, words, and acts he should do nothing injurious to beings who people the world, whether they move or not.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 8.9–10 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895) [8]
Kapila's verses promise that sinful karma runs off the careful, harmless person as water runs off high ground, provided he does no harm in thought, word or deed.
Valluvar assures the one who never strays from the right path into evil that he is safe from ruin; the Jain text says sinful karma cannot settle on such a person.
Sikhism
“The path of the faithful shall never be blocked.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 3, Japji Sahib, Pauri 14, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Japji Sahib, Guru Nanak says that one whose mind accepts and trusts the Name meets no obstacle on life's path.
The Kural assures that one who never swerves from the straight road into evil is safe from ruin; Guru Nanak likewise promises an unblocked road to those who keep faith.
Greek Mythology
Daedalus warns his son Icarus to fly a middle course between sea and sun; Icarus, exhilarated, swerves upward toward the sun, the wax of his wings melts, and he falls into the sea.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 8.183–235 (paraphrase) [10]
The myth makes safety depend on keeping to the prescribed course, and ruin the result of veering off it.
A vivid picture of மருங்கோடி — ruin comes from swerving from the right path.
Philosophy
“Virtue, then, is a state of character concerned with choice, lying in a mean, i.e. the mean relative to us, this being determined by a rational principle.”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.6, 1106b–1107a (trans. W. D. Ross) [11]
For Aristotle, virtue is a middle course, and vices are deviations to one side (excess) or the other (deficiency).
Shares the geometry of the Kural: goodness as a line, evil as a veering off it.

Real Life Example

Lal Bahadur Shastri resigns as Railways Minister
1956; Prime Minister from 9 June 1964 · New Delhi, India
Lal Bahadur Shastri, India's Minister for Railways, resigned in 1956 after a major railway accident in Madras state (at Ariyalur) caused heavy loss of life, taking responsibility although he was not personally at fault. His biographer in the Oxford Dictionary of National Biography calls this a demonstration, rare in post-independence Indian politics, of strict responsibility and accountability. He was brought back into the government in 1957, became Home Minister in 1961, and was chosen as India's second Prime Minister on 9 June 1964.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Shastri's refusal to evade responsibility, and his reputation for simple, honest living, did not end his career but became the foundation of the trust that later made him Prime Minister.
The kural assures that one who never swerves from the right path is safe from ruin, and Shastri's resignation, far from ruining him, raised him in public esteem.
