Kural 717

The Knowledge of the Council Chamber · Avaiyarithal · அவையறிதல்

கற்றறிந்தார் கல்வி விளங்கும் கசடறச் சொல்தெரிதல் வல்லார் அகத்து.
Katrarindhaar kalvi vilangum kasadarach Soltheridhal vallaar akaththu.

Meaning

The learning of people who have studied deeply shines brightest among those who can judge words without any mistakes.

Elementary

A great chess move looks like nothing special to someone who doesn’t know chess. Show it to a chess champion and they gasp, “Wow!” Your best work shines most in front of people who can understand it.

University

Vilangum (will shine, become manifest) is the key verb. Learning does not show its worth in a vacuum. It becomes visible only in the akam (midst, company) of those who are kasadara soltheridhal vallaar, able to examine words kasadara, without flaw or dross. Kasadu (impurity, dross) links back to Valluvar’s famous command in ch. 40, Kalvi (kural 391): “கற்க கசடறக் கற்பவை.” There is a pairing here: flawless study (391) meets flawless hearing (717). Commentators note that this kural balances 716. The expert audience that punishes a slip is also the only audience that can properly recognise real learning. The ytamizh/thirukkural.net text “சொல்தெரிதல்” and the sandhi form “சொற்றெரிதல்” are the same word.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
King Janaka offers a thousand cows with gold on their horns to the most learned of the Brahmins at his sacrifice. Yajnavalkya claims them, and his learning is tested one after another by the finest scholars of the court, including Asvala, Artabhaga, Uddalaka and Gargi. His answers stand, and the assembly acknowledges his mastery.
— Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, Book 3 (trans. F. Max Müller) [4]
The Upanishad shows a sage’s learning fully displayed only when it is tested by a court of equally learned questioners.
Yajnavalkya’s learning shines because his hearers can judge flawlessly, which is exactly what the Kural says.
Christianity
“And all that heard him were astonished at his understanding and answers.”
— Luke 2:46-47 (KJV) [5]
The twelve-year-old Jesus sits among the teachers in the Temple, listening and asking questions, and those learned men are amazed by his understanding.
His wisdom shows most clearly among the doctors of the law, hearers able to recognise it, as the Kural says.
Islam
“Say, ‘Are those who know equal to those who do not know?’ Only they will remember [who are] people of understanding.”
— Qur’an 39:9 (Saheeh International) [6]
The Qur’an asserts the difference between the knowing and the unknowing, and says only people of understanding will take the lesson to heart.
Only “people of understanding” can take in what is said, just as the Kural says learning shines only before skilled judges.
Judaism
“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”
— Proverbs 27:17 (KJV), as applied in Babylonian Talmud Taanit 7a [5]
The Talmud (Taanit 7a) applies this verse to Torah scholars, who sharpen each other in study. Learning grows keen among peers able to test it.
The Kural’s learning that shines among skilled judges of words is the rabbinic picture of scholars sharpening scholars.
Buddhism
Before their famous dialogue, the monk Nagasena tells King Milinda that he will discuss only if the king talks as scholars do, where each side is corrected without anger, and not as kings do, who punish anyone who contradicts them. The king agrees, and the dialogue that follows shows Nagasena’s learning at its best.
— Milindapanha, Book 2, ch. 1 (trans. T. W. Rhys Davids, The Questions of King Milinda) [7]
The Milindapanha makes a discerning, fair-minded partner the condition for real learning to appear.
Nagasena’s insistence on a “scholar’s” hearing is the Kural’s point: learning shines only before those able to judge words well.
Jainism
“As water put into a shell shines with a doubled brilliancy, so do the piety, fame, and knowledge of a very learned monk.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 11.15 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The lecture on 'The Very Learned' praises learning that shines all the more because of what holds it, opening a long series of similes for the learned monk.
The Kural says learning shines brightest in the company of those who can judge words without flaw, and the Jain image likewise has learning gaining 'doubled brilliancy' from a fitting setting.
Sikhism
“They alone purchase the gem, O Nanak, who have virtue in their purse. Those who do not appreciate the value of the jewels, wander like blind men in the world.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 954, Raag Ramkali, Ramkali ki Vaar (salok), Guru Angad (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Angad says the jewel (the Name and the Guru's word) is valued only by those qualified to recognise it; others pass it by like the blind.
The Kural says learning shines only among those able to judge words flawlessly; Guru Angad says jewels are prized only by those who can appraise them.
Greek Mythology
At the court of the Phaeacians, Odysseus tells the story of his wanderings. King Alcinous praises him for telling his tale with the skill of a bard, and the whole hall sits spellbound.
— Homer, Odyssey 11.363-369 (trans. Samuel Butler) [10]
Among refined and discerning hosts, Odysseus’s wisdom and eloquence are fully recognised.
Odysseus’s gifts shine among hearers able to value them, as the Kural says learning does.
Philosophy
In the Seventh Letter, Plato writes that real understanding cannot be handed over like other subjects. It flashes out suddenly, like light kindled from a leaping spark, only after long shared inquiry and friendly questioning between capable minds.
— Plato, Seventh Letter 341c-d, 344b [11]
Plato holds that insight shows itself only in the company of fit fellow-inquirers.
The Kural’s vilangum (shines forth) matches Plato’s image of learning lit up among worthy interlocutors.

Real Life Example

Hilbert and Klein bring Emmy Noether to Gottingen
1915-1919 · University of Gottingen, Germany
In 1915 the leading Gottingen mathematicians David Hilbert and Felix Klein invited Emmy Noether, then working without pay at Erlangen, to help with mathematical problems connected with the theory of relativity, because they needed an expert in invariant theory. She soon proved the result now called Noether's theorem, linking symmetries in physics to conservation laws, which Einstein praised for its penetrating mathematical thinking. Because women could not then obtain the habilitation, her courses were advertised under Hilbert's name while he and Klein fought the university authorities, and in 1919 she was finally made a Privatdozent.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Noether's brilliance, long unrecognised in its official standing, was recognised and championed by the very people best able to judge it.
The kural says real learning shines among those who can judge words flawlessly, and Noether's genius found its proper setting among the greatest mathematicians of Gottingen.
