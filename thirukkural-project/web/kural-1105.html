Kural 1105

Delight in Union · Punarchi Makizhthal · புணர்ச்சி மகிழ்தல்

வேட்ட பொழுதின் அவையவை போலுமே தோட்டார் கதுப்பினாள் தோள்.
Vetta pozhudhin avaiyavai polume thottaar kadhuppinaal thol.

Meaning

The shoulders of the girl with flowers in her hair give me joy, just like each thing I long for gives joy at the moment I want it.

Elementary

Imagine a magic lunchbox that always holds exactly what you are hungry for: soup when you are cold, ice cream when you are hot. The lover says that being close to the one he loves is like that box: it always gives the right happiness at the right time.

University

Vēṭṭa poḻutiṉ avaiyavai pōlumē says that her shoulders (tōḷ, standing for her embrace) are ‘like each of those things at the time they are desired’: the comparison is with any object of desire enjoyed exactly when wished for. Tōṭṭār katuppiṉāḷ (தோட்டார் கதுப்பினாள்) is ‘she whose tresses (katuppu) are full of petals or flowers (tōṭu)’, an epithet of the adorned beloved. Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah both read it as ‘whenever one unites with her, she gives the delight of whatever one desires’, and Kalaignar compares it to a wished-for thing arriving whenever wished; the image recalls the wish-granting tree and cow of Indian lore, though the Kural does not name them. Pope: ‘In her embrace, whose locks with flowery wreaths are bound, / Each varied form of joy the soul can wish is found.’ Text identical on both source sites.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
The Chandogya Upanishad teaches that one who has come to know the Self moves freely in all worlds: if he desires the world of the fathers, or of mothers, or of song, or of fragrance and garlands, by his mere wish it rises up for him.
— Chandogya Upanishad 8.2.1-10 (tr. F. Max Müller, SBE 1) [3]
For the knower of the Self, every desired joy appears the moment it is wished for.
The Kural gives the beloved this same power to supply each wished-for joy at the moment of wishing.
Christianity
“How fair and how pleasant art thou, O love, for delights!”
— Song of Solomon 7:6 (KJV) [4]
The lover exclaims that the beloved is beautiful and pleasant, made for delights.
Like the Kural, the Song finds every kind of delight in the beloved.
Islam
“Circulated among them will be plates and vessels of gold. And therein is whatever the souls desire and [what] delights the eyes, and you will abide therein eternally.”
— Qur’an 43:71 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur’an describes Paradise as a place where every soul finds whatever it desires.
The Kural gives the beloved’s embrace this Paradise-like quality of answering every desire.
Judaism
The Talmud teaches that the manna in the wilderness was a single food in which the Israelites could taste whatever flavour they wished, as an infant finds every taste in its mother’s milk.
— Babylonian Talmud, Yoma 75a [6]
One gift from heaven held every desired taste for each person who ate it.
The Kural’s beloved is like this manna: one source that gives each longed-for sweetness.
Buddhism
“There is no satisfying sensual desires, even with the rain of gold coins. For sensual pleasures give little satisfaction and much pain.”
— Dhammapada 186-187 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Buddha teaches that no amount of pleasure can ever fill sensual desire.
The Kural claims the beloved satisfies each desire as it arises; Buddhism denies that any pleasure can truly satisfy desire.
Jainism
“As the fruit of the Kimpâka is beautiful in taste and colour, when eaten; but destroys the life when digested, (being) poison; similar in their effect are pleasures.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 32.20 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
Mahavira compares pleasures to a fruit that tastes delicious but turns to poison.
The Kural praises a delight that answers every wish; Jainism warns that every such delight is sweet only at first.
Sikhism
“The Guru is the Sacred Shrine of Pilgrimage, the Guru is the Wish-fulfilling Elysian Tree.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 52, Sri Raag, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Sri Raag, Guru Arjan calls the Guru the parjat, the wish-granting tree of Indian legend, which gives whatever one desires.
The Kural says the beloved's embrace gives each desired joy exactly when it is wished for, an image the commentators link to the wish-granting tree; Guru Arjan uses that same tree for the Guru.
Greek Mythology
The horn of the goat Amalthea, which nursed the infant Zeus, was said to supply meat or drink in abundance, whatever one might wish.
— Apollodorus, Library 2.7.5, citing Pherecydes (tr. J. G. Frazer) [10]
The horn of plenty gives each person exactly what they wish for.
The Kural’s beloved is like this horn, giving each desired joy at the moment it is wished.
Philosophy
Aristotle teaches that friendships based on pleasure last only while the pleasure lasts, because such friends love one another not for their character but because each finds the other pleasant.
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII.3, 1156a (tr. W. D. Ross) [11]
For Aristotle, love that rests only on pleasure is the least stable kind of friendship.
The Kural celebrates the pleasure of the beloved; Aristotle would add that lasting love needs more than pleasure.

Real Life Example

Marc Chagall paints Birthday after Bella's visit
7 July 1915 · Vitebsk, Russian Empire (now Belarus)
On Marc Chagall's birthday, 7 July 1915, Bella Rosenfeld, the young woman he was about to marry, came to his room in Vitebsk bringing flowers and food as birthday gifts. Chagall painted the scene as Birthday, in which he floats up from the floor and bends backwards through the air to kiss her while she holds the bouquet. The couple married later that same month, and the painting is now in the Museum of Modern Art, New York.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
The painting became one of the best-known images of joyful love in modern art, showing a happiness so complete that it lifts the lover off the ground.
The kural says the beloved's embrace gives delight just as each longed-for thing does at the very moment it is wanted, and Bella arrived on Chagall's birthday with exactly the gifts of the day, filling his room with a joy he could only paint as flight.
