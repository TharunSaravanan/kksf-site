Kural 865

The Might of Hatred · Pakai Maatchi · பகை மாட்சி

வழிநோக்கான் வாய்ப்பன செய்யான் பழிநோக்கான் பண்பிலன் பற்றார்க்கு இனிது.
Vazhinokkaan vaayppana seyyaan pazhinokkaan panbilan patraarkku inidhu.

Meaning

A person who does not look for the right way, does not do what is fitting, does not care about shame, and has no good character is a joy to their enemies.

Elementary

A kid who never reads the rules, doesn’t do his homework, doesn’t care if people think badly of him and isn’t kind is very easy for a rival to beat. His rival is actually happy to face him! Learning the right way and caring about your good name make you hard to beat.

University

Vazhi nokkaan (வழிநோக்கான்) is glossed by Parimelazhagar’s tradition, and by Puliyur Kesigan and Pappaiah, as one who does not study the treatises of right conduct (neethi nool); Varadarasanar and Kalaignar read it more broadly as not seeking the good path. Vaayppana seyyaan (வாய்ப்பன செய்யான்) is one who fails to do what those texts enjoin or what is fitting; pazhi nokkaan (பழிநோக்கான்) disregards blame or disgrace; panbilan (பண்பிலன்) lacks character. Patraar (பற்றார்) are ‘those who do not hold to him’, that is, enemies, and inidhu (இனிது, sweet) is deliberately ironic: such a man is a pleasure to fight. The sequence moves from knowledge to action to reputation to character. Text identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Krishna warns that one who casts aside the injunctions of scripture and acts on impulse attains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the supreme goal, and therefore scripture should be one’s guide in what to do and what to avoid.
— Bhagavad Gita 16.23-24 [3]
The Gita insists that right action must be guided by authoritative teaching, not whim.
Valluvar’s vazhi nokkaan and vaayppana seyyaan are exactly the man who ignores such guidance.
Christianity
Jesus compares the one who hears his sayings but does not do them to a foolish man who built his house on sand; when the storm came, it fell, and great was its fall.
— Matthew 7:26-27 (KJV) [4]
Knowing what is right without doing it leaves a person with no foundation in a crisis.
The Kural’s man who neither seeks nor does what is fitting will likewise collapse when an enemy strikes.
Islam
The Qur’an speaks of those who have hearts with which they do not understand, eyes with which they do not see and ears with which they do not hear, calling them heedless.
— Qur’an 7:179 [5]
The verse condemns people who possess every faculty for guidance but refuse to use it.
Valluvar’s man who will not look to the right path is heedless in the same way.
Judaism
Hosea proclaims that the people are destroyed for lack of knowledge, because they have rejected knowledge and forgotten the teaching of their God.
— Hosea 4:6 (Tanakh) [6]
The prophet links national ruin directly to the neglect of instruction.
As in the Kural, refusing to learn the right way leaves one open to destruction.
Buddhism
The Dhammapada likens fine words not put into practice to a beautiful flower that has colour but no fragrance.
— Dhammapada 51 [7]
Teaching is fruitless unless it is lived.
The Kural’s man who does not do what is fitting is all colour and no fragrance.
Jainism
“Benighted men of wrong faith, (though) they be renowned as heroes, exert themselves in a bad way, which will have, in all respects, evil consequences for them.”
— Sūtrakṛtāṅga I.8.22 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; wisdomlib.org) [8]
The Sūtrakṛtāṅga contrasts the effort of those who lack right faith and follow a wrong path, which brings them only ruin, with the effort of the wise who follow the right path.
The Kural says a man who does not look to the right path or do what is fitting delights his enemies, and the Jain verse likewise says that effort cut off from the right path brings about its own downfall.
Sikhism
“Greed and sin are the king and prime minister; falsehood is the treasurer.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 468, Raag Aasaa, Asa di Vaar (salok, Guru Nanak) (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the Asa di Vaar Guru Nanak pictures a corrupt age as a court in which greed rules, sin advises and falsehood keeps the treasury.
The Kural says a ruler who ignores the right path, does what is unfitting, has no shame and no good character is a delight to his enemies; Guru Nanak pictures the same rule by vice in place of right conduct.
Greek Mythology
Hesiod says the best man is he who thinks things out for himself, the good man is he who listens to good advice, but the one who neither thinks for himself nor heeds another is useless.
— Hesiod, Works and Days 293-297 [10]
Hesiod ranks people by how they relate to wisdom, and puts the one who ignores all counsel at the bottom.
Valluvar’s man who does not look to the path is Hesiod’s useless man.
Philosophy
“Your enemy, wide awake, is constantly lying in wait to take advantage of your actions, and seeking to gain some hold on you, and keeping a constant patrol about your life …”
— Plutarch, Moralia, How to Profit by One's Enemies (De capienda ex inimicis utilitate), ch. 3 (tr. F. C. Babbitt) [11]
Plutarch says an enemy watches every action for a fault to seize on, and he urges that this watchfulness be turned to profit by living so carefully that the enemy finds nothing to use.
Read from the other side, Plutarch's point matches the Kural's: a person who neglects right conduct, propriety and shame hands his watching enemies exactly what they want.

Real Life Example

Honorius refuses terms and Alaric sacks Rome
408-410 · Ravenna and Rome, Italy
The Western Roman emperor Honorius, based in Ravenna, had his leading general Stilicho arrested and executed in 408, after which many soldiers of barbarian origin defected to the Visigothic leader Alaric. Honorius repeatedly refused Alaric's demands during sieges of Rome in 408 and 409. In August 410 Alaric's forces entered Rome, which had not been taken by a foreign enemy for nearly 800 years, and occupied it for three days.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
An emperor who removed his ablest defender and would neither fight effectively nor negotiate gave his enemy an opportunity it could hardly have hoped for.
The kural says one who does not follow the right path, does not do what is fitting and has no care for disgrace is a delight to his enemies, and Honorius's conduct handed Alaric the city.
