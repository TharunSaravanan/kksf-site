Kural 684

The Envoy · Thoodhu · தூது

அறிவுரு வாராய்ந்த கல்விஇம் மூன்றன் செறிவுடையான் செல்க வினைக்கு.
Arivuru vaaraaindha kalvi-im moondran Serivudaiyaan selga vinaikku.

Meaning

Send a person on the mission only if they have three things together: good sense, a fine appearance, and learning that has been tested.

Elementary

To show the new kid around school, the teacher picks someone who is smart, looks neat and friendly, and has learned the school rules properly. Someone with only one of these would not be enough. You need all three together.

University

Parimelazhagar makes each of the three precise. Aṟivu is innate intelligence (iyaṟkai aṟivu). Uru is a presence that viewers find attractive (kaṇṭār virumpum tōṟṟap polivu). Ārāynta kalvi is learning that has been examined ‘with many, over a long time’. The key word is ceṟivu, ‘combination’ or ‘density’: he notes that the three rarely come together in one person, and that together they make the envoy respected, so the mission succeeds. ‘Viṉai’ is the business of embassy to foreign kings. Manakkudavar reads ceṟivu as ‘containment’ (aṭakkam). Pappaiah adds that the person should be able to frame his message himself, which anticipates the ‘self-composing’ envoy of 687. On the text: ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net print கல்விஇம் மூன்றன் (a vowel hiatus); the sandhi-joined text at thirukkural-thamizh.org reads கல்வியிம் மூன்றன், with the glide y written in.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“(Such) an ambassador is commended to a king (who is) loyal, honest, skilful, possessing a good memory, who knows the (proper) place and time (for action, who is) handsome, fearless, and eloquent.”
— Manusmriti 7.64 (trans. G. Bühler, Sacred Books of the East vol. 25) [5]
Manu lists appearance next to memory, skill and eloquence as qualifications for an envoy.
Like the Kural’s aṟivu, uru and kalvi, Manu counts a good appearance as a real qualification alongside intelligence and learning.
Christianity
“And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.”
— Luke 2:52 (KJV) [6]
Luke describes the young Jesus growing in wisdom and in bodily stature together, and winning people’s favour.
Wisdom, bearing and the goodwill they earn are the combination the Kural looks for in an envoy.
Islam
Dihya al-Kalbi, whom the Prophet sent with his letter to the Byzantine ruler, was known for his striking good looks. Umm Salama mistook the angel Gabriel for Dihya, calling him ‘a handsome person amongst the companions’.
— Sahih al-Bukhari 7 (Dihya carries the letter); Sahih al-Bukhari 3634 (his handsome appearance) [7]
The early community’s envoy to the emperor was remembered for his fine appearance.
The Kural lists uru, an impressive presence, as part of what makes an envoy respected abroad.
Judaism
“That is cunning in playing, and a mighty valiant man, and a man of war, and prudent in matters, and a comely person, and the LORD is with him.”
— 1 Samuel 16:18 (KJV) [6]
Saul’s servant recommends David for royal service by listing skill, courage, good judgement and good looks.
Prudence, comeliness and cultivated skill are a close match for the Kural’s aṟivu, uru and kalvi.
Buddhism
Sāriputta was won over to the Dhamma when he saw the monk Assaji on his alms round and was struck by his calm, graceful bearing. He asked him who his teacher was and received a short verse of teaching.
— Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga I.23 [8]
Assaji’s outward bearing opened the way, and his wisdom finished the work.
Presence first earns respect and learning then carries the message, as the Kural expects of an envoy.
Jainism
“As water put into a shell shines with a doubled brilliancy, so do the piety, fame, and knowledge of a very learned monk.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 11.15 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [9]
The sutra says the virtues and knowledge of a learned monk shine out doubly, as water does in a conch shell.
The Kural joins natural sense, an impressive presence and tested learning in its envoy; the Jain verse only partly matches, linking inner learning with an outward radiance others can see.
Sikhism
“Wisdom leads us to serve our Lord and Master; through wisdom, honor is obtained.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1245, Raag Saarang, Sarang ki Vaar (salok), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
In Sarang ki Vaar Guru Nanak praises good sense (akal) as what makes one's service to the Master effective and wins honour.
The Kural wants an envoy with natural good sense, presence and tested learning so that he is respected and succeeds; Guru Nanak likewise links sound judgement to good service and the honour it earns.
Greek Mythology
Before Odysseus appears before the Phaeacians, Athena makes him taller and sturdier-looking so that he will be welcomed, feared and respected, and will succeed in the contests ahead.
— Homer, Odyssey 8.18-23 [11]
Homer treats appearance as something that decides how a stranger pleading his cause will be received.
This matches Parimelazhagar’s point that uru helps the envoy be well regarded, and so helps the mission succeed.
Philosophy
Socrates says the nature fit for the highest training must be quick to learn, have a good memory, and be high-minded and gracious. Such natural gifts, perfected by education and maturity, deserve to be entrusted with the state.
— Plato, Republic 6.487a [12]
Plato holds that the best public servant joins natural talent with long, tested education.
This is close to the Kural’s pairing of innate aṟivu with ārāynta kalvi, learning examined over time.

Real Life Example

Dag Hammarskjold's mission to Peking for the detained airmen
30 December 1954 to 13 January 1955; airmen released in 1955 · Peking (Beijing), China
Asked by the UN General Assembly to seek the release of American airmen who had served under the UN Command in Korea, Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjold travelled to Peking and negotiated directly with Zhou Enlai, since the People's Republic of China was not represented at the United Nations. Hammarskjold, who held a doctorate in economics and had been the outstanding student of his day at Uppsala, kept the talks going by personal correspondence after he left. In 1955 China released all fifteen detained airmen.
— Sources: [13] [14]
The mission succeeded through a combination of sound judgement, personal standing and deep learning, qualities the Nobel Committee and the UN later credited for what was called his first major victory.
The kural asks for an envoy in whom good sense, an impressive presence and tested learning are combined, and Hammarskjold's Peking mission is an example of all three bringing a difficult task to success.
