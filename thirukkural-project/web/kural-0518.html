Kural 518

Selection and Employment · Therindhu Vinaiyaadal · தெரிந்து வினையாடல்

வினைக்குரிமை நாடிய பின்றை அவனை அதற்குரிய னாகச் செயல்.
Vinaikkurimai naadiya pindrai avanai Adharkuriya naagach cheyal.

Meaning

After finding out what work a person is good at, put them fully in charge of it and give them the power to do it.

Elementary

If you find out your friend is the best goalkeeper, you don’t just let them try out. You make them the goalkeeper for real, with the gloves and everything.

University

The key word is உரிமை, suitability and also rightful ownership. Once fitness for a task (வினைக்குரிமை) has been established, the ruler must make the person its rightful holder (அதற்குரியனாக). Pappaiah stresses the second half: having appointed a man, let him exercise the powers of the post himself. The couplet thus balances Kural 517: 517 is about handing over, 518 about conferring the standing and authority that make the handover real. It is Valluvar’s closest statement of the specialisation principle, which Plato made the definition of justice and the Gita discussed in terms of svadharma.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“It is better to engage in one’s own occupation, even though one may perform it imperfectly, than to accept another’s occupation and perform it perfectly. Prescribed duties, according to one’s nature, are never affected by sinful reactions.”
— Bhagavad Gita 18.47 (Prabhupada, As It Is) [3]
Krishna says each person does best following the duty that fits his own nature.
Work should match the person’s nature: the Kural’s வினைக்குரிமை (fitness for a task).
Christianity
“Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.”
— Acts 6:3 (KJV) [4]
The apostles pick seven qualified men and formally put them in charge of the daily food distribution.
Find the qualified people, then formally give them the task: the Kural’s two steps.
Islam
“When honesty is lost, then wait for the Hour (Doomsday).” ... “When the power or authority comes in the hands of unfit persons, then wait for the Hour (Doomsday.)”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 59 [5]
The Prophet counts giving authority to unfit people as a sign that honesty has been lost from the world.
Authority belongs to those truly suited to it; the hadith states negatively what the Kural states positively.
Judaism
“Forasmuch as God hath shown thee all this, there is none so discreet and wise as thou. Thou shalt be over my house, and according unto thy word shall all my people be ruled...”
— Genesis 41:39-40 (JPS 1917) [6]
Having seen Joseph’s wisdom for himself, Pharaoh gives him full authority over Egypt.
After seeing fitness, Pharaoh makes Joseph the real holder of the office, exactly the Kural’s அதற்குரியனாகச் செயல்.
Buddhism
The Buddha publicly named particular disciples as foremost (etadagga) in particular qualities: Sariputta in wisdom, Moggallana in psychic power, Upali in monastic discipline, and so on.
— Anguttara Nikaya 1.188-267 (Etadagga Vagga) [7]
He recognised each disciple’s special strength and gave each a matching role and standing.
Finding each person’s aptitude and giving them standing in it reflects the Kural.
Jainism
“At last he anointed his hundred sons as kings, and gave each a kingdom.”
— Kalpa Sutra, Life of Rishabha §211 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22, 1884; sacred-texts.com) [8]
Before renouncing the world, Rishabha handed over rule by consecrating his sons and giving each a realm of his own.
The kural asks that a man found fit for a task be made its rightful holder with full authority; Rishabha's act of anointing each son over his own kingdom is a formal transfer of authority of exactly that kind.
Sikhism
In 1708 at Nanded, Guru Gobind Singh initiated Banda Singh into the Khalsa and, before sending him north to the Punjab, gave him a drum, a banner and five arrows as emblems of authority, with five Sikhs to accompany him.
— Encyclopaedia of Sikhism (Punjabi University, Patiala, ed. Harbans Singh), 'Banda Singh Bahadur', via The Sikh Encyclopedia (paraphrase) [9]
The Encyclopaedia of Sikhism records that the Guru gave Banda Singh both a mission and visible signs of the authority needed to carry it out.
The Kural says that once a person's fitness for a task is known, he should be made fully responsible for it with the authority that goes with it; Guru Gobind Singh gave Banda Singh both.
Greek Mythology
In Protagoras’ myth, Epimetheus hands out abilities to the animals so that each kind has what suits its way of life, giving speed to some, strength to others, and wings or burrows to others.
— Plato, Protagoras 320d-321c [10]
The myth pictures a world in which capacities are matched to roles.
It dramatises the fit between being and function that the Kural asks rulers to honour.
Philosophy
“...one man should practise one thing only, the thing to which his nature was best adapted;—now justice is this principle or a part of it.”
— Plato, Republic IV, 433a (Jowett) [11]
Plato defines justice in the city as each person doing the one task his nature fits him for.
Plato raises the Kural’s principle, find each person’s fit and make that their work, to the definition of justice.

Real Life Example

Napoleon III makes Haussmann prefect of the Seine
22 June 1853 · Paris, France
Emperor Napoleon III appointed Georges-Eugene Haussmann prefect of the Seine in 1853, in effect the administrator of Paris, with the task of transforming the city. Over the next seventeen years Haussmann cut broad boulevards through old districts, greatly expanded the sewers and water supply, and created new parks. He raised large loans with little oversight and was dismissed in 1870 when a more liberal government came in.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Napoleon III gave one administrator both the task and the authority and money to carry it out, and Paris was remade as a result.
The kural says that once a person's fitness for a task is established he should be made fully responsible for it with the authority that goes with it, which is how Haussmann was empowered.
