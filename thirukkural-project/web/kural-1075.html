Kural 1075

Baseness · Kayamai · கயமை

அச்சமே கீழ்களது ஆசாரம் எச்சம் அவாவுண்டேல் உண்டாம் சிறிது.
Achchame keezhgaladhu aasaaram echcham avaavundel undaam siridhu.

Meaning

Fear is the only thing that makes mean people behave well. Beyond that, they behave a little better only when they want something.

Elementary

Some children only behave when the teacher is watching, or when they hope to get a sweet. When nobody is looking and there is no prize, they misbehave. The Kural says base people are like that: they are good only out of fear or for a reward.

University

Aasaaram (ஆசாரம், from Sanskrit ācāra, 'right conduct, propriety') in the base has two sources only: achcham (அச்சம், 'fear') and, as echcham (எச்சம், 'remainder'), avaa (அவா, 'desire, greed'). Parimelazhagar specifies the fear as fear of the king's punishment, and Puliyur Kesikan follows ('அரசரால் துன்பம் வரும் என்னும் அச்சம்'); Solomon Pappaiah likewise reads it as fear of the government. The second line says that if desire for some gain is present, a little more good conduct appears. The couplet thus defines the base by motive: they have no inner love of virtue. Pope: 'Fear is the base man's virtue; if that fail, / Intense desire some little may avail.' The text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“The whole world is kept in order by punishment, for a guiltless man is hard to find; through fear of punishment the whole world yields the enjoyments (which it owes).”
— Laws of Manu 7.22 (tr. G. Bühler, SBE 25) [3]
Manu teaches that fear of the king's rod (danda) is what keeps most people in line.
This is the Kural's claim about the base: fear of punishment, not virtue, produces their good behaviour.
Christianity
“For rulers are not a terror to good works, but to the evil. Wilt thou then not be afraid of the power? do that which is good, and thou shalt have praise of the same:”
— Romans 13:3 (KJV) [4]
Paul says the authorities are something to fear only for wrongdoers, and that doing good earns praise.
The verse names the two motives the Kural assigns to the base, fear of rulers and hope of reward.
Islam
“You [believers] are more fearful within their breasts than Allāh. That is because they are a people who do not understand.”
— Qur'an 59:13 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur'an says the hypocrites fear people more than God, so their conduct is governed by fear of others, not by faith.
Like the Kural's base, their behaviour comes from fear of an outside power rather than inner conviction.
Judaism
“pray for the welfare of the government, for were it not for the fear it inspires, every man would swallow his neighbor alive.”
— Pirkei Avot 3:2 (Mishnah Yomit, tr. Joshua Kulp, via Sefaria) [6]
Rabbi Hanina teaches that fear of the ruling power is what keeps people from devouring one another.
This is the Kural's first line: fear of authority is what keeps the base in order.
Buddhism
“In four ways, young householder, should one who appropriates be understood as a foe in the guise of a friend: (i) he appropriates his friend's wealth, (ii) he gives little and asks much, (iii) he does his duty out of fear, (iv) he associates for his own advantage.”
— Sigalovada Sutta, Digha Nikaya 31 (tr. Narada Thera) [7]
The Buddha describes a false friend who does what he should only out of fear and for his own gain.
These are exactly the two motives the Kural gives the base: fear first, and a little more good conduct when there is something to gain.
Jainism
“Better it is that I should subdue my Self by self-control and penance, than be subdued by others with fetters and corporal punishment.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 1.16 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
Mahavira's teaching sets self-restraint chosen from within against restraint forced from outside by chains and punishment.
The Kural's base man is the one who must be 'subdued by others' through fear, because he will not restrain himself.
Sikhism
“One who does not frighten anyone, and who is not afraid of anyone else”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1427, Saloks of Guru Tegh Bahadur (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In his saloks, Guru Tegh Bahadur describes the truly wise person as one who neither frightens others nor is ruled by fear of them.
The Kural says the base behave well mainly out of fear of punishment; Guru Tegh Bahadur's ideal person acts rightly without being driven by fear at all.
Greek Mythology
In Plato's Republic, Glaucon tells the story of Gyges, a shepherd who found a ring that made him invisible and then used it to seduce the queen, kill the king and seize the throne, arguing that anyone would act unjustly if he could do so without fear of being caught.
— Plato, Republic 2.359c-360d (tr. Benjamin Jowett) [10]
The story suggests that for many people, justice is only fear of consequences.
Glaucon's Gyges is the Kural's base man: remove the fear and his good conduct disappears.
Philosophy
“For these do not by nature obey the sense of shame, but only fear, and do not abstain from bad acts because of their baseness but through fear of punishment”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 10.9 (1179b) (tr. W. D. Ross) [11]
Aristotle says that most people are kept from wrongdoing not by shame or love of the noble but by fear of punishment, which is why laws are needed.
Aristotle states the Kural's point almost word for word, even naming 'baseness'.

Real Life Example

Shang Yang's laws of rewards and punishments in Qin
4th century BCE (died 338 BCE) · State of Qin, China
The statesman Shang Yang reorganised the state of Qin with strict, uniform laws, harsh penalties even for minor offences, and a system in which neighbours and relatives were held responsible for each other's crimes. He also created ranks of merit as rewards. He taught that people like rank and rewards and dislike punishments, so a ruler can steer them with both; Sima Qian credited him with making Qin's people law-abiding.
— Sources: [12] [13]
His Legalist approach helped Qin become the state that later unified China, and it remains the classic case of government built on fear of punishment and hope of gain.
The kural says fear is the main reason the base behave, with a little good conduct when they want something; Shang Yang built a whole state on exactly those two motives.
