Kural 1264

Mutual Desire · Avarvayin Vidhumpal · அவர்வயின் விதும்பல்

கூடிய காமம் பிரிந்தார் வரவுள்ளிக் கோடுகொ டேறுமென் நெஞ்சு.
Kootiya kaamam pirindhaar varavullik koaduko daerumen nenju.

Meaning

Thinking of the return of the one who left, with all the love we shared, my heart climbs from branch to branch to look out for him.

Elementary

Have you ever climbed a tree to see far away? The lady in this verse feels so excited thinking her husband might be coming home that it is as if her heart climbs up into the treetops, branch after branch, trying to spot him on the road.

University

The striking image is kōṭu koṭu ēṟum eṉ neñcu, ‘my heart climbs branch upon branch’. Mu. Varadarasanar and Kalaignar take it literally as the heart climbing to the treetop to watch the road; Sivayogi Sivakumar compares it to a creeper that outgrows the pole it climbs; Solomon Pappaiah reads it as the heart, freed of sorrow, spreading branches and growing upward in joy, which is close to Pope’s ‘my heart with rapture swells’. Kūṭiya kāmam pirintār, ‘he who parted from the love we had joined in’, recalls their earlier union. The commentary tradition associated with Parimelazhagar takes the couplet as the heroine’s eager anticipation at news or thought of his coming. Text agrees on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In the Gita Govinda, Radha’s companion describes her waiting for Krishna in the forest bower, starting at every rustle and decking the bower in expectation of his coming.
— Jayadeva, Gita Govinda, Radha’s waiting in the bower (paraphrase) [3]
Jayadeva’s Radha lives in a state of eager, restless anticipation of her beloved’s arrival.
Radha’s restless watching is the same leap of the heart the Kural pictures as climbing branch after branch.
Christianity
“Hope deferred maketh the heart sick: but when the desire cometh, it is a tree of life.”
— Proverbs 13:12 (KJV) [4]
The proverb sets the sickness of delayed hope against the life that floods back when the desire is fulfilled.
The heroine’s heart, at the very thought of his return, rises like a tree; the proverb uses the same image for fulfilled desire.
Islam
As the caravan carrying Joseph’s shirt leaves Egypt, Jacob tells those around him that he perceives the scent of Joseph, even though they think him weak in mind.
— Qur’an 12:94 (paraphrase of Saheeh International) [5]
Before any news arrives, the longing father’s heart already senses that his son is on the way back to him.
The heroine’s heart likewise runs ahead to meet the returning beloved before he has arrived.
Judaism
“Hark! my beloved! behold, he cometh, leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the hills. My beloved is like a gazelle or a young hart; behold, he standeth behind our wall, he looketh in through the windows, he peereth through the lattice.”
— Song of Songs 2:8-9 (JPS 1917, via Mechon-Mamre) [6]
The bride hears and imagines her beloved’s approach before he has even reached her.
The heroine’s heart climbing to look out for him is the same eager picture of a lover’s coming.
Buddhism
“The craving of one given to heedless living grows like a creeper. Like the monkey seeking fruits in the forest, he leaps from life to life (tasting the fruit of his kamma).”
— Dhammapada 334 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Buddha pictures craving as a creeper and a monkey, always climbing and leaping toward the next thing it wants.
The Kural’s heart climbing branch to branch is almost the same image, though the Kural celebrates the climbing while the Dhammapada warns against it.
Jainism
“Cast aside from you all attachments, as the (leaves of) a lotus let drop off the autumnal water, exempt from every attachment, Gautama, be careful all the while!”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 10.28 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
Mahavira tells Gautama to let attachments fall away as water runs off a lotus leaf.
The Kural’s heart reaches up and out toward the beloved; Jain teaching counsels the opposite movement, letting attachment drop away.
Sikhism
“O Nanak, the soul-bride is gazing upon Your Path; please listen, O Supreme Soul.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1107, Raag Tukhaari, Barah Maha, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the opening of his Barah Maha (song of the twelve months), Guru Nanak pictures the soul-bride watching the road for her Husband Lord and begging Him to hear her.
The Kural's heart climbs from branch to branch to look out for the returning husband; Guru Nanak's soul-bride likewise keeps watch on the road for the Beloved.
Greek Mythology
Night after night, Hero climbs her tower at Sestos and lights a lamp, watching across the Hellespont for Leander to swim to her.
— Musaeus, Hero and Leander (paraphrase) [10]
Hero’s tower and lamp make her whole being a watch for the beloved’s coming.
Hero climbs her tower to watch for him; the Kural’s heroine sends her heart up the branches to do the same.
Philosophy
Spinoza defines hope as an inconstant pleasure arising from the idea of something future or past whose outcome we still doubt.
— Baruch Spinoza, Ethics III, Definitions of the Affects 12 (paraphrase) [11]
Hope, for Spinoza, is a real joy, but an unsteady one, because it depends on something not yet certain.
The heroine’s heart leaps with exactly this inconstant joy at the mere thought of his return.

Real Life Example

Alexander Selkirk is rescued after four years marooned
September 1704 - February 1709 · Mas a Tierra, Juan Fernandez Islands (now Chile)
After a quarrel with his captain in September 1704, the Scottish sailor Alexander Selkirk asked to be put ashore on the uninhabited island of Mas a Tierra, and he regretted the decision as soon as the ship sailed. During more than four years alone, two ships landed, but they were Spanish and he had to hide from their crews. In February 1709 he was picked up by an English ship commanded by Woodes Rogers.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Selkirk's long wait for a friendly sail later helped inspire the story of Robinson Crusoe.
The kural's heroine sends her heart climbing from branch to branch to look out for her beloved's return; Selkirk's years of hope for a rescuing ship are a documented case of such watching.
