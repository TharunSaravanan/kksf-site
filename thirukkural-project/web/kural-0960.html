Kural 960

Lineage · Kudimai · குடிமை

நலம்வேண்டின் நாணுடைமை வேண்டும் குலம்வேண்டின் வேண்டுக யார்க்கும் பணிவு.
Nalamvendin naanudaimai vendum kulamvendin venduga yaarkkum panivu.

Meaning

If you want to be good, you must have a sense of shame about doing wrong. If you want your family to be respected, be modest and polite toward everyone.

Elementary

Kids who say 'I'm the best!' all the time do not get respect. Kids who are polite to everyone, even the youngest, do. Valluvar says that if you want your family to be respected, be humble with everyone.

University

The closing couplet turns the chapter from description to instruction with a double condition built on the verb vendu (வேண்டு, 'to want, to require'): nalam vendin (நலம் வேண்டின், 'if you want good') requires naanudaimai (நாணுடைமை, the possession of shame), and kulam vendin (குலம் வேண்டின், 'if you want family standing') requires panivu (பணிவு, humility) towards yaarkkum (யார்க்கும், everyone). Puliyur Kesikan specifies shame at blame (பழிக்கு நாணம்), and Kalaignar adds 'without arrogance' (ஆணவமின்றி). The effect is to make lineage something earned and maintained by humility rather than simply inherited, which ties the chapter's ending back to the 'by nature' of 951. Naanudaimai is also the title of chapter 102, and the two chapters are often read together. The Tamil text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Krishna begins his list of the marks of true knowledge with humility (amanitvam) and freedom from pride (adambhitvam), followed by non-violence, patience and simplicity.
— Bhagavad Gita 13.8 [3]
The Gita places humility first among the qualities of the wise.
Like the Kural, it makes humility the foundation of real standing.
Christianity
“For whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.”
— Luke 14:11 (KJV) [4]
Jesus teaches that honour comes to those who humble themselves, not to those who seek it.
This matches the Kural's paradox: humility is the way to a noble name.
Islam
“And the servants of the Most Merciful are those who walk upon the earth easily, and when the ignorant address them [harshly], they say [words of] peace.”
— Qur'an 25:63 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur'an describes God's true servants as walking humbly and answering rudeness with peace.
Like the Kural, it makes humility towards everyone, even the rude, the mark of the honoured.
Judaism
“Rabbi Meir said: Engage but little in business, and busy yourself with the Torah. Be of humble spirit before all men.”
— Pirkei Avot 4:10 (Mishnah Yomit, tr. Joshua Kulp) [6]
Rabbi Meir tells his students to be humble before every person.
'Before all men' is the Kural's yaarkkum panivu, humility towards everyone.
Buddhism
“To be respectful, humble, contented and grateful; and to listen to the Dhamma on due occasions — this is the greatest blessing.”
— Maha-mangala Sutta, Sn 2.4 (tr. Narada Thera) [7]
The Mangala Sutta counts humility among the highest blessings of life.
Like the Kural, it makes humility a source of good fortune and standing.
Jainism
“'Frequently (I have been born) in a high family, frequently in a low one; I am not mean, nor noble, nor do I desire (social preferment).' Thus reflecting, who would brag about his family or about his glory, or for what should he long?”
— Acaranga Sutra I.2.3.1 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22) [8]
Because the soul has been born countless times in high and low families, the Acaranga says no one has grounds to be proud of their family; Jain doctrine also teaches that pride and self-praise bind karma that leads to low birth, while humility leads to high birth.
Like the Kural, it makes humility, not boasting, the right attitude to family standing, though for Jainism high birth is itself the fruit of humility.
Sikhism
“Sweetness and humility, O Nanak, are the essence of virtue and goodness.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 470, Raag Aasaa, Asa di Vaar, salok of Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Asa di Vaar, Guru Nanak names sweetness of speech and humility (nimrata) as the sum of all good qualities.
The Kural closes by saying that one who wants a noble name must be humble towards everyone; Guru Nanak makes humility the essence of goodness itself.
Greek Mythology
Niobe, daughter of Tantalus, boasts to the women of Thebes about her divine ancestry and her fourteen children and says she deserves worship more than the goddess Leto; Apollo and Artemis kill her children and she is turned to weeping stone.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 6 [10]
Ovid tells the story as the destruction of a proud family through boasting.
Niobe shows the reverse of the Kural's advice: pride in lineage ruins the lineage.
Philosophy
In the Laws, Plato's Athenian says that justice always follows God, and whoever would be happy holds fast to her and follows in her company with humility and order, while one who is lifted up with pride of wealth, rank or beauty is soon left alone and brings ruin on himself and his house.
— Plato, Laws 4.716a (tr. Benjamin Jowett) [11]
Plato ties lasting good fortune to humility and warns that pride ruins both the person and his household.
Like the Kural, he makes humility the condition for a person's and a family's good standing.

Real Life Example

Emperor Akihito and Empress Michiko kneel to speak with evacuees
10 July 1991 · Shimabara, Nagasaki Prefecture, Japan
On 10 July 1991, in his first official visit to a disaster area as emperor, Akihito travelled with Empress Michiko to Shimabara after the eruption of Mount Unzen had killed 43 people. At an evacuation shelter the couple knelt on the floor to talk face to face with residents, something scarcely imaginable under earlier emperors, and some people complained that the emperor had been made to kneel. They continued the practice in later disasters, and it became known in Japan as the Heisei style.
— Sources: [12] [13]
The imperial couple's humility toward ordinary people helped bring the monarchy closer to the public and won it wide affection.
The kural says that if you want a noble family name you must be humble toward everyone, and the emperor of an ancient line kneeling to listen to evacuees showed exactly that.
