Kural 360

Knowledge of the True · Meyyunarthal · மெய்யுணர்தல்

காமம் வெகுளி மயக்கம் இவ்மூன்றன் நாமம் கெடக்கெடும் நோய்.
Kaamam vekuli mayakkam ivmoondran Naamam kedakkedum noi.

Meaning

When desire, anger and confusion are destroyed so completely that even their names are gone, the suffering they cause is destroyed too.

Elementary

Weeds keep coming back if you only snip off the tops. You have to pull them out by the roots so nothing is left. This kural says greed, anger and confusion are like three weeds: pull them out so completely that even their names are forgotten, and the trouble they cause disappears.

University

The chapter closes by naming a triad: kaamam, vekuli, mayakkam (desire, anger, delusion). Its correspondence with the Buddhist ‘three roots’ or ‘three poisons’ (raga, dosa, moha: greed, hatred, delusion) is nearly exact, and it overlaps substantially with the Gita’s ‘three gates of hell’ (lust, anger, greed). Valluvar’s hyperbole, that the very names must perish (naamam keda), insists on eradication rather than suppression: not even the latent tendency or memory may remain. Because the chapter began with delusion (marul) as the cause of birth, ending with mayakkam as the third root closes the argument in a ring. True knowledge removes the delusion that fuels desire and anger, and with all three gone, the ‘disease’ (noi) of suffering and rebirth ends.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“There are three gates leading to this hell — lust, anger, and greed. Every sane man should give these up, for they lead to the degradation of the soul.”
— Bhagavad Gita 16.21 [3]
Krishna names three inner forces as the doorways to self-destruction and tells every sensible person to abandon them.
A near-parallel triad to the Kural’s kaamam–vekuli–mayakkam, with the same call to be rid of them completely.
Christianity
“For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world.”
— 1 John 2:16 (KJV) [4]
John names a threefold root of worldly corruption, and says none of it comes from God.
Like the Kural, a triad of inner drives is identified as the source of spiritual sickness to be overcome.
Islam
“But as for he who feared the position of his Lord and prevented the soul from [unlawful] inclination, then indeed, Paradise will be [his] refuge.”
— Qur’an 79:40–41 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur’an promises Paradise to the one who holds back the lower self (nafs) from its passions.
Both texts make victory over passion the condition for escaping suffering.
Judaism
“Rabbi Elazar Ha-kappar said: envy, lust and [the desire for] honor put a man out of the world.”
— Pirkei Avot 4:21 (trans. Joshua Kulp) [6]
The Mishnah names three inner vices that destroy a person’s life in this world and the next.
A striking triad parallel: three passions that ruin a person, which the Kural says must be destroyed down to their names.
Buddhism
“Reverend, the ending of greed, hate, and delusion is called extinguishment.”
— Samyutta Nikaya 38.1 (trans. Bhikkhu Sujato) [7]
Asked what Nibbana (extinguishment) is, Sariputta defines it simply as the ending of the three roots: greed, hate and delusion.
An almost exact match: the Kural’s kaamam–vekuli–mayakkam are the Buddhist raga–dosa–moha, and their destruction is liberation.
Jainism
“I shall explain in due order the means which must be adopted by him who wants to thoroughly uproot love, hatred, and delusion.” … “He will get rid of all misery which always afflicts mankind; recovered from the long illness, as it were, and glorious, he becomes infinitely happy, and obtains the (final) aim.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 32.9 and 32.110 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895, pp. 185, 192) [8]
Mahāvīra's sermon sets out to uproot completely the triad of rāga, dveṣa and moha (love, hatred and delusion). It ends with the liberated sage cured of all misery 'as from a long illness'.
Valluvar's triad of desire, anger and delusion, destroyed so completely that even their names are gone, and the suffering (noi, 'disease') ending with it, corresponds almost term for term to the Jain uprooting of love, hatred and delusion and the cure of the 'long illness'.
Sikhism
“I have brought the ten sensory organs under my control, and erased every trace of the five thieves.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 693, Raag Dhanaasree, Bhagat Namdev (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Bhagat Namdev says he has mastered the senses and wiped out the five thieves (kaam, krodh, lobh, moh, ahankar); the Gurmukhi says even their name (naavau) is erased.
The Kural says that when desire, anger and delusion are destroyed so completely that even their names are gone, suffering ends; Namdev claims to have erased the very name of the passions.
Greek Mythology
When Heracles fought the Lernaean Hydra, each head he cut off grew back as two, until his companion Iolaus burned the stumps so that none could regrow; the immortal head was buried under a heavy rock.
— Apollodorus, Library 2.5.2 [10]
The monster could only be defeated by destroying it at the root so nothing could grow back.
Like the Kural’s ‘even the names must perish’, cutting off passions superficially fails; they must be destroyed at the source.
Philosophy
Spinoza argues that a passion ceases to be a passion as soon as we form a clear and distinct idea of it, and that the more we understand our emotions, the less we are ruled by them.
— Spinoza, Ethics, Part V, Proposition 3 [11]
For Spinoza, true understanding does not merely restrain destructive emotions; it dissolves them as passions.
Matches the chapter’s whole logic: knowledge of the truth is what destroys desire, anger and confusion at the root.

Real Life Example

The World Health Assembly declares smallpox eradicated
8 May 1980 · Geneva, Switzerland (World Health Organization)
After a global vaccination and case-finding campaign led by the World Health Organization from 1967, the world's last naturally occurring case of smallpox was found in Somalia in October 1977. Following two further years of searching for cases, a global commission certified eradication in December 1979. On 8 May 1980 the World Health Assembly, in resolution WHA33.3, formally declared that the world was free of smallpox.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Smallpox is the only human disease ever eradicated, which was possible only because the campaign aimed at complete removal of the disease rather than control.
The kural says the suffering caused by desire, anger and delusion ends only when they are destroyed so completely that even their names are gone, and smallpox ended only when it was rooted out everywhere rather than merely reduced.
