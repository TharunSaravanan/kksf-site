Kural 509

Selection and Confidence · Therindhu Thelithal · தெரிந்து தெளிதல்

தேறற்க யாரையும் தேராது தேர்ந்தபின் தேறுக தேறும் பொருள்.
Thaerarka yaaraiyum thaeraadhu thaerndhapin Thaeruga thaerum porul.

Meaning

Do not trust anyone without checking them first. After checking, trust them with the things they can be trusted with.

Elementary

Before you lend your favourite bike, you watch how your cousin treats their own toys. Once you’ve seen they’re careful, you lend it without nagging them every five minutes. Check first, then really trust.

University

The couplet plays on two nearly identical roots: தேர் (to examine, choose) and தேறு (to trust, be confident). Almost every word is built on them, a characteristic Valluvar wordplay that makes the rule easy to remember: தேறற்க … தேராது (do not trust without examining), தேர்ந்தபின் தேறுக (after examining, trust). The closing phrase தேறும் பொருள் is read in two ways: Varadarasan and Kalaignar take it as trusting in what deserves trust, while the older commentary followed by Pope takes it as entrusting to each person the duties suited to what the test showed. Pappaiah adds the practical corollary that one should not doubt someone after appointing them, which leads into 510. Both sources agree on the text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Of these tried ministers, those whose character has been tested under religious allurements shall be employed in civil and criminal courts…”
— Kautilya, Arthaśāstra 1.10 (trans. R. Shamasastry) [3]
After the four tests, Kautilya assigns each minister to the kind of work his proven integrity suits.
Examine first, then entrust the matters the person has proved fit for, as the Kural’s தேறும் பொருள் suggests.
Christianity
“Lay hands suddenly on no man, neither be partaker of other men’s sins: keep thyself pure.”
— 1 Timothy 5:22 (KJV) [4]
Paul warns Timothy not to ordain anyone hastily, since he shares responsibility for their faults.
This is the first half of the Kural: trust no one without examination.
Islam
“Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due and when you judge between people to judge with justice.”
— Qur’an 4:58 (Sahih International) [5]
Trusts, including offices, must be given to those who are properly qualified for them.
Giving each trust to its rightful holder is the Kural’s ‘trust him in what he can be trusted with’.
Judaism
Ben Sira advises that if you want a friend you should acquire him through testing and not be quick to trust him, since some are friends only when it suits them.
— Ben Sira (Ecclesiasticus) 6:7 [6]
The Jewish sage makes testing the doorway to trust.
The same sequence as the Kural: test, then trust.
Buddhism
The Buddha tells the Kalamas not to accept teachings on report, tradition or a teacher’s authority, but when they know for themselves, after examination, that something is skilful and leads to welfare, then to take it up and live by it.
— Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.65, Kālāma Sutta [7]
Acceptance is to follow personal examination, and once examined it is to be acted on fully.
The same two-step: examine, then commit.
Jainism
The Jain teacher Haribhadra is remembered for declaring that he had no partiality for Mahavira and no hatred for Kapila, and that whoever's words are reasonable should be accepted.
— Haribhadra, Lokatattvanirnaya (famous couplet; attribution as summarised in Wikipedia, 'Haribhadra') [8]
Haribhadra insisted that even religious trust must follow rational examination rather than prior loyalty.
The kural says trust no one without examination and, once examined, trust fittingly; Haribhadra applies the same order of examining first and then trusting to the teachers one follows.
Sikhism
“First, examine the merchandise, and then, make the deal.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1410, Salok Vaaran Thay Vadheek, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Nanak gives a trader's rule: test what you are dealing in before you commit to it.
The Kural says to trust no one without examining him and, once he has been examined, to trust him; Guru Nanak's rule puts the testing first and the commitment after.
Greek Mythology
In Aesop’s fable, a man buying an ass takes it home on trial and turns it loose among his herd; it goes straight to the laziest, greediest animal, so he returns it, saying he can already tell what it is from the company it chose.
— Aesop’s Fables, ‘The Ass and His Purchaser’ [10]
The buyer tests before committing, and the test reveals the animal’s character.
Examine before you trust, as the Kural’s first line commands.
Philosophy
Seneca tells Lucilius to consider for a long time whether to admit someone as a friend, but once he has decided, to welcome him wholeheartedly and speak as freely with him as with himself.
— Seneca, Moral Letters to Lucilius 3.2 [11]
Deliberation comes before friendship and full confidence after it.
This mirrors the Kural’s two commands almost exactly: do not trust without examination; after examination, trust.

Real Life Example

The Soviet Union selects Yuri Gagarin for Vostok 1
1960-1961 · Moscow and Star City, Soviet Union (now Russia)
In 1960 the Soviet Union chose its first group of 20 cosmonaut candidates from among military pilots after extensive medical and psychological testing. After further training and examinations, managers ranked Yuri Gagarin first, with Gherman Titov as his backup, and on 12 April 1961 Gagarin made the first human spaceflight. The candidates themselves had voted overwhelmingly for Gagarin as the one they wanted to fly first.
— Sources: [12] [13]
No one was trusted with the first human spaceflight until he had been examined repeatedly, and the trust was then given in full.
The kural says to trust no one without examining him and, once examined, to trust him in what he is fit for, as the Soviet selection did.
