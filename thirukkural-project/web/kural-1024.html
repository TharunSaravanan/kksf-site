Kural 1024

Family Maintenance · Kudiseyalvakai · குடிசெயல்வகை

சூழாமல் தானே முடிவெய்தும் தம்குடியைத் தாழாது உஞற்று பவர்க்கு.
Soozhaamal thaane mudiveydhum thamkudiyaith thaazhaadhu unjatru bavarkku.

Meaning

For people who work hard without delay to lift up their family, the work gets done by itself, even without careful planning.

Elementary

When you practise piano every day without putting it off, one day a hard song just seems to play itself. Valluvar says that when people keep working for their family without delay, success often arrives before they even sit down to plan it.

University

Soozhaamal (சூழாமல், ‘without deliberating, without planning’) and thaane (தானே, ‘of itself’) frame the promise that the task mudiveydhum (முடிவெய்தும், ‘reaches completion’) for those who thaazhaadhu unjatrupavar (தாழாது உஞற்றுபவர், ‘strive without delay’). Kesikan, following the older commentary, attributes this to divine aid (தெய்வ உதவியால்), linking it to 1023; Varadarasanar and Pappaiah read it naturalistically, saying the capacity to finish comes to the prompt worker unsought. Karunanidhi has victories ‘pile up of themselves.’ Pope translates ‘Without a thought spontaneously, his end will gain.’ The text is identical on both sources.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Therefore, without being attached to the fruits of activities, one should act as a matter of duty, for by working without attachment one attains the Supreme.”
— Bhagavad Gita 3.19 (Bhagavad-gītā As It Is, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda) [3]
Krishna teaches that steady action as duty, without fretting over results, itself brings the highest attainment.
Like the Kural, it says the outcome comes of itself to the one who simply keeps working.
Christianity
Proverbs teaches that the slack hand brings poverty but the hand of the diligent makes rich.
— Proverbs 10:4 (KJV) [4]
The sage links prosperity directly to diligent work.
Both texts say success comes almost naturally to the person who works without slackening.
Islam
“And that there is not for man except that [good] for which he strives”
— Qur'an 53:39 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur'an says a person receives the good that matches his own striving.
It agrees with the Kural that the result follows from the effort itself.
Judaism
“the day is short, and the work is plentiful, and the laborers are indolent, and the reward is great, and the master of the house is insistent.”
— Mishnah, Pirkei Avot 2:15 (tr. Joshua Kulp, Mishnah Yomit, via Sefaria) [6]
Rabbi Tarfon warns that the time is short and the work great, so delay is costly and the reward large.
Like the Kural, it urges striving without delay and promises that great reward follows.
Buddhism
“He who acquires his wealth in harmless ways like to a bee that honey gathers, riches mount up for him like ant hill's rapid growth.”
— Digha Nikaya 31, Sigalovada Sutta (tr. Narada Thera, Access to Insight) [7]
The Buddha tells the householder Sigala that steady, honest work makes wealth grow on its own, like an anthill.
Its image of riches mounting up by themselves matches the Kural's success that ‘comes of itself’ to the steady worker.
Jainism
“Clever talking will not work salvation; how should philosophical instruction do it?”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 6.10 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
The Uttaradhyayana warns that knowing and discussing the path achieves nothing without actually walking it.
It agrees with the Kural that doing, not deliberating, brings results, though for liberation rather than family welfare.
Sikhism
“Make the effort, and you shall live; practicing it, you shall enjoy peace.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 522, Raag Gujri, Gujri ki Vaar (salok), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Gujri ki Vaar, Guru Arjan urges udam (active effort): those who exert themselves find life, and those who keep practising find peace. The salok goes on to say that through meditation one meets God and anxiety ends.
The Kural promises that the work of those who strive without delay reaches its goal of itself; Guru Arjan likewise promises that effort, kept up, brings its own fulfilment.
Greek Mythology
“Through work men grow rich in flocks and substance, and working they are much better loved by the immortals”
— Hesiod, Works and Days, passage at ll. 293-319 (tr. H.G. Evelyn-White, sacred-texts.com) [10]
Hesiod promises his brother that steady farm work brings both wealth and the favour of the gods.
Like the Kural, it says prosperity flows naturally to the one who keeps working.
Philosophy
Aristotle argues that we become just by doing just acts and brave by doing brave acts, so that repeated practice makes good action second nature.
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 2.1 [11]
For Aristotle, steady practice turns hard actions into habits that then come almost without effort.
This explains the Kural's claim that for the constant striver the work completes itself without deliberation.

Real Life Example

Vinoba Bhave receives an unasked-for gift of land at Pochampalli
18 April 1951 · Pochampalli, Telangana, India
While walking through Telangana, Vinoba Bhave met landless Dalit families at Pochampalli who said they needed land to live. When he asked those present whether anything could be done, a landowner named Ramachandra Reddy rose and offered 100 acres, and from this unplanned beginning the Bhoodan (land-gift) movement grew, collecting millions of acres in later years.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A movement of national scale began not from a plan but from a single spontaneous response during Vinoba's tireless walking tour.
The kural says that for those who strive without delay to raise their people, the work reaches its goal of itself even before it is planned, as the first land gift came to Vinoba unasked.
