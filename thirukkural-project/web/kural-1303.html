Kural 1303

Pouting · Pulavi · புலவி

அலந்தாரை அல்லல்நோய் செய்தற்றால் தம்மைப் புலந்தாரைப் புல்லா விடல்.
Alandhaarai allalnoai seydhatraal thammaip pulandhaaraip pullaa vidal.

Meaning

Not hugging and making up with the one who is sulking is like adding fresh pain to someone who is already suffering.

Elementary

If your little sister is sad and pretending not to talk to you, and you just walk away, she feels even worse than before. A hug at the right moment is what she was secretly waiting for.

University

The address now turns to the husband: to leave unembraced (pullā viṭal) those who have sulked with him (tammaip pulantār) is like inflicting allal nōy (‘anguish, sickness’) on alantār, ‘those already distressed.’ Mu. Varadarasanar and Kalaignar gloss ‘not dispelling the sulk and embracing’ (ūṭal uṇarttit taḻuvāmal), Solomon Pappaiah names the wife explicitly, and Puliyur Kesikan calls such neglect a cruelty; the point is that the sulk is itself a symptom of the pain of separation, so ignoring it compounds that pain. Pope’s explanation reads ‘For men not to embrace those who have feigned dislike is like torturing those already in agony.’ The two web texts agree.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In the Gita Govinda, Krishna comes to Radha in her anger and pleads with her at length, begging her to give up her pique, offering himself to any punishment she chooses, and asking her to place her foot on his head as a sign of forgiveness.
— Jayadeva, Gita Govinda, Sarga 10 (paraphrase; cf. tr. Barbara Stoler Miller) [3]
The divine lover does not leave his beloved to her sulk; he humbles himself to end it.
This is exactly the response the Kural asks of the husband: go to the one who sulks and reconcile, rather than adding to her pain.
Christianity
“As he that taketh away a garment in cold weather, and as vinegar upon nitre, so is he that singeth songs to an heavy heart.”
— Proverbs 25:20 (KJV) [4]
The proverb lists actions that make a bad situation worse for someone already hurting.
The Kural also uses a comparison to show that ignoring a hurting partner adds pain to pain.
Islam
“And if a woman fears from her husband contempt or evasion, there is no sin upon them if they make terms of settlement between them - and settlement is best.”
— Qur’an 4:128 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur’an urges spouses who drift apart to seek reconciliation, declaring that settlement is best.
The Kural likewise tells the husband that the right response to his wife’s withdrawal is to make peace, not to leave her alone.
Judaism
“Rav says: A person must always be careful about mistreatment of his wife. Since her tear is easily elicited, punishment for her mistreatment is immediate.”
— Babylonian Talmud, Bava Metzia 59a (William Davidson Edition, Sefaria) [6]
The Talmud warns husbands that a wife’s hurt is close to the surface and wronging her carries quick consequences.
The Kural gives the husband the same warning: his sulking wife is already in pain, and neglect only deepens it.
Buddhism
Finding a monk lying sick and neglected in his own filth, the Buddha and Ananda wash and tend him, and the Buddha tells the monks that whoever would tend him should tend the sick.
— Vinaya Pitaka, Mahavagga 8.26 (paraphrase) [7]
The Buddha makes caring for the suffering a duty and rebukes those who leave a sufferer alone.
The Kural similarly condemns leaving someone already in distress without the comfort they need.
Jainism
The Tattvartha Sutra names four attitudes to cultivate: friendliness toward all beings, joy at the virtuous, compassion (karunya) toward the afflicted, and equanimity toward the ill-behaved.
— Tattvartha Sutra 7.11 (Digambara numbering; 7.6 in the Svetambara recension) (paraphrase) [8]
Compassion toward those already suffering is a core Jain disposition.
The Kural’s husband is asked for exactly this compassion toward a partner whose sulk is itself a form of suffering.
Sikhism
“Humility is the word, forgiveness is the virtue, and sweet speech is the magic mantra.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1384, Salok Farid, Bhagat Shaikh Farid (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Shaikh Farid teaches that humility, forgiveness and gentle words are what win the Beloved.
The Kural says it is cruel not to embrace and reconcile with one who is sulking; Farid's verse gives the forgiving, gentle way that ends such a rift.
Greek Mythology
Once Penelope has tested and recognized Odysseus, she runs to him in tears and embraces him, and he holds her weeping, the reunion as welcome as land to shipwrecked swimmers.
— Homer, Odyssey 23 (paraphrase) [10]
The long estrangement and testing end in an embrace that heals both.
The Kural insists that a sulk must end in such an embrace, since withholding it would only prolong pain.
Philosophy
Plutarch praises the Pythagoreans who, whenever anger led them to harsh words, would join right hands and embrace before the sun went down and be reconciled.
— Plutarch, On Brotherly Love (De fraterno amore), Moralia (paraphrase) [11]
Plutarch holds up quick, physical reconciliation as the mark of people bound by real affection.
The Kural asks the husband for the same prompt embrace that ends a quarrel before it hardens.

Real Life Example

Gandhi nearly turns Kasturba out of the house, then shuts the gate
Early 1898 · Durban, Natal (now South Africa)
In early 1898, at the Gandhis’ home in Durban, a Christian Indian guest of so-called untouchable parentage left his chamber pot to be emptied, and Gandhi expected Kasturba to help clean it. She carried it out but was in tears and angry, and Gandhi, furious that she did not do it cheerfully, took her by the hand and dragged her to the gate meaning to push her out. Weeping, she asked whether he had no sense of shame and where she could possibly go; ashamed, he shut the gate, and he later wrote that their many quarrels always ended in peace.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Gandhi later presented the incident as one of the most shameful of his life, a moment when he added to his wife’s distress instead of easing it.
The kural says that refusing to embrace and reconcile a hurt and sulking partner is like heaping fresh pain on someone already suffering, which is what Gandhi came to see he had done before he shut the gate.
