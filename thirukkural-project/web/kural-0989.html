Kural 989

Perfectness · Saandraanmai · சான்றாண்மை

ஊழி பெயரினும் தாம்பெயரார் சான்றாண்மைக்கு ஆழி எனப்படு வார்.
Oozhi peyarinum thaampeyaraar saandraanmaikku aazhi enappadu vaar.

Meaning

People who are called the ocean (the deep, steady home) of perfect character will not change, even if the whole world comes to an end.

Elementary

A big rock on the beach stays in the same place year after year while the waves, the weather and the people all change around it. Valluvar says truly good people are like that: whatever happens around them, they stay good.

University

Oozhi (ஊழி) is the cosmic age or aeon, and its 'shifting' (peyarinum, பெயரினும், 'even if it moves') evokes the dissolution at the end of an age, when, in Indian cosmology, the seas break their bounds. Against this, those called aazhi (ஆழி) of saandraanmai do not shift (thaam peyaraar, தாம்பெயரார்). Aazhi can mean either 'ocean' or 'shore, boundary', and the commentators divide: Varadarasanar and Karunanidhi take it as ocean ('the ocean of perfection'), while Pappaiah and Kesikan, with Pope ('of perfect virtue's sea the shore'), read it as the shore that holds the sea in, an image which sharpens the contrast, since the shore holds even when the seas of the age-end overflow. Pope's line 'Who, though the fates should fail' renders oozhi loosely as fate. Sivayogi Sivakumar reads oozhi as ordinary change over time. The Tamil text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“A person who is not disturbed by the incessant flow of desires – that enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still – can alone achieve peace, and not the man who strives to satisfy such desires.”
— Bhagavad Gita 2.70 (Bhagavad-gītā As It Is, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda) [3]
Krishna compares the steady sage to the ocean, which stays unmoved though rivers pour into it constantly.
The Kural uses the same ocean image for the noble person who stays unchanged through every change.
Christianity
“In whose eyes a vile person is contemned; but he honoureth them that fear the LORD. He that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not. He that putteth not out his money to usury, nor taketh reward against the innocent. He that doeth these things shall never be moved.”
— Psalm 15:4-5 (KJV) [4]
The psalm describes the righteous person who keeps his word even at a cost and promises that he will never be moved.
Like the Kural, it portrays the person of integrity as one who 'changeth not' and stands firm.
Islam
“Indeed, those who have said, "Our Lord is Allah" and then remained on a right course - the angels will descend upon them, [saying], "Do not fear and do not grieve but receive good tidings of Paradise, which you were promised.”
— Qur'an 41:30 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur'an praises those who, having professed faith, remain steadfast on the right path.
Like the Kural, it honours constancy of character that does not waver.
Judaism
“A song of ascents. Those who trust in GOD are like Mount Zion that cannot be moved, enduring forever.”
— Psalm 125:1 (The JPS Tanakh: Gender-Sensitive Edition, 2023, via Sefaria) [6]
The psalmist likens those who trust in God to a mountain that never moves.
Like the Kural, it pictures the faithful as permanently steady amid change.
Buddhism
“Just as a solid rock is not shaken by the storm, even so the wise are not affected by praise or blame.”
— Dhammapada 81 (trans. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Buddha compares the wise person to a rock that the storm cannot shake.
Like the Kural, it describes the perfected person as unmoved by whatever changes around them.
Jainism
“... difficult to attack like a lion, steady and firm like Mount Mandara, deep like the ocean, mild like the moon, refulgent like the sun, pure like excellent gold; like the earth he patiently bore everything; like a well-kindled fire he shone in his splendour.”
— Kalpa Sutra, Life of Mahavira §118 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22) [8]
The Kalpa Sutra describes the houseless Mahavira through a series of similes, including the steadiness of Mount Mandara and the depth of the ocean.
Like the Kural's 'ocean of perfection', it pictures the perfected one as deep as the ocean and immovable as the world-mountain.
Sikhism
“The soul becomes steady, and does not waver, by the natural inclination of the Guru's Loving Will.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 87, Sri Raag, Guru Amar Das (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Amar Das describes the Gurmukh whose soul, attuned to the Guru's love, becomes adol, unshakable. Gurbani often praises this steadiness against the swaying of Maya and the five passions.
The Kural says the ocean-like perfected do not change even when the ages shift, and Guru Amar Das likewise describes a soul made steady so that it does not waver.
Greek Mythology
In Odyssey XX, Odysseus, disguised as a beggar in his own house and enraged by the suitors' insolence, strikes his breast and tells his heart to endure, reminding it that it endured worse in the Cyclops' cave; he holds firm until the right moment.
— Homer, Odyssey XX.17-24 (trans. Samuel Butler) [10]
Odysseus stays steady in purpose through twenty years of upheaval.
His steadfastness under changing fortunes echoes the Kural's noble person who does not change when the age changes.
Philosophy
“Be like the promontory against which the waves continually break, but it stands firm and tames the fury of the water around it.”
— Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 4.49 (trans. George Long) [11]
Marcus Aurelius urges himself to stand firm like a headland that the waves cannot move.
This is close to the Kural's image, especially in the reading of aazhi as the shore that holds while the seas surge.

Real Life Example

Jan Hus refuses to recant at the Council of Constance
1414-1415 · Constance (Konstanz), Holy Roman Empire (now Germany)
The Bohemian reformer Jan Hus travelled to the Council of Constance in 1414 under a safe-conduct, but was arrested soon after arriving and held in confinement. Tried as a heretic, he was repeatedly urged to recant his teachings to save his life and refused to disavow what he believed. He was condemned and put to death on 6 July 1415.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Hus held to his convictions even when the whole weight of the church and empire pressed on him to change.
The kural says those who are an ocean of perfect character will not shift even if the ages themselves shift, and Hus would not move from his convictions even at the cost of his life.
