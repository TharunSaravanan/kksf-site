Kural 635

Ministry of State · Amaichu · அமைச்சு

அறனறிந்து ஆன்றமைந்த சொல்லான்எஞ் ஞான்றுந் திறனறிந்தான் தேர்ச்சித் துணை.
Aranarindhu aandramaindha sollaan enjnjaandrum Thiranarindhaan thaerchchith thunai.

Meaning

The best person to plan with is someone who knows what is right, speaks with deep knowledge and care, and always knows how to get things done.

Elementary

When you need advice about something hard, you go to the person who knows right from wrong, speaks calmly and carefully, and knows the practical steps. This kural says that kind of person is the best helper for a leader.

University

The couplet sets out three qualifications for a thaerchchith thunai, a ‘companion in deliberation’. The first is aranarindhu, knowing aram (dharma or righteousness). The second is aandramaindha sollaan, one whose speech is full (aandru, ‘abundant, filled with learning’) and measured (amaindha, ‘settled, restrained’). The third is enjnjaandrum thiranarindhaan, one who always knows the method. Commentators differ over whether aandru points to learning, as Varadarasanar and Pappaiah take it, or to maturity. Pappaiah reads amaindha as modest or restrained speech. Parimelazhagar’s reading places moral knowledge first, so the counsellor’s skill serves aram rather than mere expediency. All three editions agree on the text; the older one divides it as ‘aranarindh aandramaindha sollaanenjnjaandrun’.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“He whom neither anger nor joy, nor pride, nor false modesty, nor stupefaction, nor vanity, can draw away from the high ends of life, is considered as wise.”
— Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva 33 (Vidura Niti), trans. K. M. Ganguli [4]
Vidura, the Mahabharata’s model counsellor, describes the wise man as one whom no passion can pull away from life’s proper aims.
This is the aranarindhu counsellor, grounded in dharma, whom the Kural calls the best partner in deliberation.
Christianity
“Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.”
— Colossians 4:6 (KJV) [5]
Paul asks believers to speak graciously and thoughtfully, fitting each answer to the person addressed.
This mirrors the Kural’s aandramaindha sol, full and measured speech.
Islam
“And those who have responded to their lord and established prayer and whose affair is [determined by] consultation among themselves, and from what We have provided them, they spend.”
— Qur’an 42:38 (Sahih International) [6]
The surah named Ash-Shura (Consultation) lists mutual consultation among the defining traits of the faithful.
Shura makes the Kural’s thaerchchith thunai, the partner in deliberation, a religious ideal.
Judaism
“Where no wise direction is, a people falleth; but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety.”
— Proverbs 11:14 (JPS 1917) [7]
Proverbs teaches that a nation stands or falls by the quality of the guidance it receives.
The Kural’s point is the same: a ruler needs capable counsellors beside him.
Buddhism
“Speak only the speech that neither torments self nor does harm to others. That speech is truly well spoken.”
— Subhasita Sutta, Sutta Nipata 3.3 (trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu) [8]
The Buddha describes well-spoken speech as speech that harms no one.
This fits the Kural’s aandramaindha sol, restrained and weighty speech, as part of a good counsellor’s character.
Jainism
“… circumspect in his speaking … guarding his thoughts, guarding his words, guarding his acts, guarding his senses, guarding his chastity; without wrath, without pride, without deceit, without greed …”
— Kalpa Sutra, Life of Mahavira §118 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22, 1884; sacred-texts.com) [9]
The Kalpa Sutra describes Mahavira as careful in speech (bhasha-samiti) and guarded in word and deed, free from the four passions.
The Kural's best counsellor knows what is right and speaks with learning and restraint; the Jain ideal of careful, guarded speech free of passion covers the restraint, though not the giving of counsel.
Sikhism
“O Baba, speak only that which will bring you honor.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 15, Siree Raag, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
In a hymn of Siree Raag, Guru Nanak urges that speech be weighed, so that one says only what is worthy and brings honour.
The Kural wants as a counsellor one whose speech is measured and full of learning; Guru Nanak likewise asks for speech that is weighed and worthy.
Greek Mythology
When Agamemnon and Achilles quarrel, old Nestor of Pylos rises to calm them. Homer calls him the clear-voiced speaker from whose tongue words flowed sweeter than honey, and he draws on his long experience of great men.
— Homer, Iliad 1 (trans. Samuel Butler) [11]
Nestor is Homer’s model counsellor: experienced, eloquent and respected by both sides.
He combines the Kural’s learned, measured speech with long practical knowledge.
Philosophy
Aristotle says that a speaker earns trust through three qualities: practical wisdom (phronesis), virtue (arete) and goodwill (eunoia). A speaker who lacks any one of them gives bad or untrustworthy advice.
— Aristotle, Rhetoric II.1 [12]
For Aristotle, credible counsel needs good judgement, good character and genuine care for the listener.
Virtue, wisdom and trustworthy speech correspond closely to the Kural’s aram, learned speech and practical skill.

Real Life Example

Nizam al-Mulk founds the Nizamiyya colleges and writes the Book of Government
1067 (founding of the Baghdad Nizamiyya) · Baghdad and the Seljuq Empire (Iran and Iraq)
Nizam al-Mulk, a Persian from Tus, became vizier to the Seljuq sultan Alp Arslan in the 1060s and continued under his son Malik Shah, holding the office for about 30 years. He founded a network of colleges named Nizamiyyas after him, the most famous being the Nizamiyya of Baghdad, founded in 1067, where he appointed scholars such as al-Juwayni and al-Ghazali. He set down his views on government in the Siyasatnama, the Book of Government.
— Sources: [13] [14]
Nizam al-Mulk is remembered in Persian tradition as the model vizier, a statesman who was also a patron of learning and an author on the right conduct of rulers.
The kural describes the ideal counsellor as one who knows what is right, is full of learning and always knows how things are to be done, and Nizam al-Mulk's long vizierate, colleges and treatise on government embody that combination.
