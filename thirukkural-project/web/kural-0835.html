Kural 835

Folly · Pedhaimai · பேதைமை

ஒருமைச் செயலாற்றும் பேதை எழுமையும் தான்புக் கழுந்தும் அளறு.
Orumaich cheyalaatrum pedhai ezhumaiyum thaanpuk kazhundhum alaru.

Meaning

In just one life, a fool can do enough wrong to fall into a hell of suffering that lasts through seven future lives.

Elementary

A few minutes of scribbling with permanent marker on your bedroom wall can leave a mess that takes weeks to clean. The poet says the foolish person can make, in one life, a mess so big it sticks to him for a very long time.

University

The couplet sets orumai (ஒருமை, one birth) against ezhumai (எழுமை, the seven births), and alaru (அளறு, literally ‘mire’ or ‘mud’) is the traditional word for naragam, hell. Parimelazhagar, Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah all read it eschatologically: in one life the fool is able to make (ஆற்றும்) the hell he will enter and sink into across seven births. Kalaignar departs from this, reading orumaich cheyal as ‘self-willed action’ and alaru as a mire of suffering in this world, a deliberately non-theological gloss; Puliyur Kesigan’s note on thirukkural.net is truncated. Pope’s ‘The fool will merit hell in one brief life on earth’ follows the traditional reading. The text is the same in both base sources.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Those who are envious and mischievous, who are the lowest among men, I perpetually cast into the ocean of material existence, into various demoniac species of life.”
— Bhagavad Gita 16.19 (As It Is) [3]
Krishna describes how persistent wrong conduct leads to repeated low births within samsara.
Valluvar’s fool likewise builds, in one life, a hell that follows him through many births.
Christianity
“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting.”
— Galatians 6:7-8 (KJV) [4]
Paul teaches that what a person sows in this life decides what he reaps, even into eternity.
The Kural similarly says the fool’s one life sows a harvest of long suffering.
Islam
“And they will say, ‘If only we had been listening or reasoning, we would not be among the companions of the Blaze.’”
— Qur’an 67:10 (Sahih International) [5]
The people of the Fire confess that their failure to listen and to reason in this life is what put them there.
Both texts tie unreason in one life to lasting punishment after it.
Judaism
“There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.”
— Proverbs 14:12 (Tanakh, KJV translation) [4]
Proverbs warns that a path chosen without wisdom can lead to ruin even when it looks right.
Valluvar’s fool likewise walks confidently into a lasting downfall.
Buddhism
“Fools of little wit are enemies unto themselves as they move about doing evil deeds, the fruits of which are bitter.”
— Dhammapada 66 (tr. Buddharakkhita) [6]
The Dhammapada says the fool is his own worst enemy, planting deeds whose fruit is bitter.
Valluvar’s fool, too, digs his own hell by his own actions.
Jainism
“The capital is human life, the gain is heaven; through the loss of that capital man must be born as a denizen of hell or a brute animal. ... Having once forfeited them, he will have to endure these two states of misery; it will be difficult for him to attain an upward course for a long time to come.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 7.16, 7.18 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [7]
Mahāvīra's parable of the three merchants treats a human life as capital; the fool who squanders it in one life falls into hell or animal birth and struggles for a very long time to rise again.
Valluvar says the fool in one birth makes the hell he will sink into for seven; the Uttarādhyayana likewise has one wasted human life bring rebirth in hell from which an 'upward course' is hard to win 'for a long time to come'.
Sikhism
“Vibrate, and meditate on the Lord of the Universe, and never forget Him. This is the blessed opportunity of this human incarnation. … Otherwise, you shall regret and repent afterwards, and you shall not be carried across to the other side.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1159, Raag Bhairao, Bhagat Kabir (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Kabir teaches that this one human birth is the chance to be freed from the round of births; wasted, it leads to regret and to staying in that round.
The Kural says the fool can make in one life the suffering of seven births; Kabir likewise says the chance of this single life, once wasted, keeps one bound to rebirth.
Greek Mythology
In the underworld Odysseus sees Tityos, Tantalus and Sisyphus undergoing endless punishments for wrongs committed in their lifetimes.
— Homer, Odyssey 11.576-600 [9]
Homer pictures the afterlife as the place where one life’s misdeeds become unending suffering.
The Kural similarly makes one lifetime’s folly the cause of long-lasting torment.
Philosophy
In the Myth of Er, the soul that drew the first lot hastily chose the life of a great tyrant without examining it, and only afterwards discovered it was fated to devour its own children; Plato notes he had lived his previous life in a well-ordered city by habit, without philosophy.
— Plato, Republic X, 619b-d [10]
Plato shows how an unexamined choice can doom a soul across its next life.
Valluvar’s fool likewise sets the course of many births by one life’s foolish deeds.

Real Life Example

Thomas Austin releases rabbits at Barwon Park
1859 · Barwon Park, near Geelong, Victoria, Australia
Shortly after Christmas 1859, twenty-four European wild rabbits imported from England by the grazier Thomas Austin arrived in Melbourne, and Austin released some of them on his Barwon Park property so that he and his friends could hunt them. The rabbits multiplied rapidly, crossing into New South Wales by 1880, reaching Queensland in 1886 and Western Australia in 1894. Genetic research has traced most of Australia's rabbits to this release, and they became one of the country's most destructive pests, damaging crops, pasture and native plants for generations.
— Sources: [11] [12] [13]
A single act done for one man's sport created a problem that has cost Australia's land and farmers dearly for more than a century and a half.
The kural says the fool can in one lifetime create a hell that lasts through seven births, and Austin's release of a few rabbits caused harm that has lasted through many generations.
