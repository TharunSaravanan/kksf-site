Kural 454

Avoiding Mean Associations · Sitrinanjeraamai · சிற்றினஞ்சேராமை

மனத்து ளதுபோலக் காட்டி ஒருவற்கு இனத்துள தாகும் அறிவு.
Manaththu ladhupolak kaatti oruvarku Inaththu ladhaagum arivu.

Meaning

A person’s good sense seems to live in their own mind. But it really comes from the group of people they spend time with.

Elementary

We think our good sense is all our own. But look closely and much of it comes from the people around us: what they talk about, what they value, how they act.

University

காட்டி (‘making it appear’) is the key word. Valluvar grants that wisdom seems to live in one’s own mind, then corrects the appearance: it is actually இனத்துளது, lodged in one’s group. This is a strikingly early statement of what is now called distributed or socially embedded cognition: judgement is sustained by the conversations, norms and examples of a circle. It does not deny individual minds, which 453 affirmed, but it undercuts the pride of thinking oneself self-sufficient. The practical corollary, drawn out in 455–460, is that choosing one’s company is choosing one’s wisdom.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Duryodhana relies on his uncle Shakuni’s counsel. The plan of the rigged dice game, which leads to the Pandavas’ exile and in the end to the war, is Shakuni’s idea.
— Mahabharata, Sabha Parva [4]
Duryodhana’s ‘wisdom’ in the crisis is really his adviser’s cunning working through him.
This fits the Kural’s claim that what looks like a person’s own mind is supplied by their circle.
Christianity
“The disciple is not above his master: but every one that is perfect shall be as his master.”
— Luke 6:40 (KJV) [5]
Jesus says a student, once fully trained, becomes like the one he learns from.
In both, a person’s wisdom takes on the shape of the company they learn from.
Islam
The Prophet taught that the believer is a mirror to his fellow believer.
— Sunan Abi Dawud 4918 [6]
A good companion shows you your faults and virtues, so self-knowledge comes through others.
This fits the Kural’s claim that clear understanding is received from one’s company rather than produced in isolation.
Judaism
Rabbi Hanina says he learned much from his teachers, more from his colleagues, and most of all from his students.
— Babylonian Talmud, Ta’anit 7a [7]
The Talmud locates the growth of wisdom in the web of people one studies with.
This confirms the Kural’s point that wisdom lives within one’s company.
Buddhism
“Should one find a man who points out faults and who reproves, let him follow such a wise and sagacious person as one would a guide to hidden treasure.”
— Dhammapada 76 (trans. Buddharakkhita) [8]
Wisdom is found by keeping company with the wise, who show one what one cannot see alone.
This echoes the Kural’s point that true understanding comes from the company one keeps.
Jainism
“When the worthy teachers, who are thoroughly enlightened and from early times well versed in conduct, are satisfied (with a pupil), they will make over to him their extensive and weighty knowledge of the sacred texts.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 1.46 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [9]
In Jain monastic life, knowledge of the scriptures passes from teacher to pupil; a disciple's learning is literally handed over by the enlightened company he serves.
The kural holds that wisdom seems to live in one's own mind but really lives in one's company. The Jain verse likewise has the pupil's wisdom come from the worthy teachers whose company he has won.
Sikhism
“In the Company of the Holy, spiritual wisdom is revealed.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 271, Raag Gauree (Sukhmani), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
In the Sukhmani Guru Arjan teaches that true wisdom (sugiaan) comes to light within the Saadh Sangat.
The Kural says wisdom only seems to live in one's own mind but really resides in one's company; Guru Arjan places the revelation of wisdom in holy company.
Greek Mythology
Athena, disguised as Mentor, travels with young Telemachus and guides him to Nestor and Menelaus. In their company the hesitant boy grows into a prudent young man.
— Homer, Odyssey, Books 2–4 [11]
Telemachus’s new judgement is formed through his companions.
His wisdom seems to be his own but comes from his company, just as in the Kural.
Philosophy
Epictetus warns that a person who often talks with others must either become like them or bring them round to his own ways.
— Epictetus, Discourses 3.16 [12]
Conversation is formative, so an untrained mind is reshaped by the company it keeps.
This supports the Kural’s claim that what passes for one’s own mind is largely the imprint of one’s circle.

Real Life Example

Young physicists gather at Niels Bohr's institute in Copenhagen
1921-1930 · Copenhagen, Denmark
Niels Bohr's Institute for Theoretical Physics opened at the University of Copenhagen in 1921, and Bohr set out to make it a place where a constantly renewed group of young physicists could work together. Through the 1920s visitors from many countries, including Werner Heisenberg, Wolfgang Pauli and Paul Dirac, spent time there arguing over the problems of the atom. Much of the work that produced the new quantum mechanics in the mid-1920s was done by people who studied or debated in this circle, which they nicknamed Bohr's kindergarten.
— Sources: [13] [14]
One of the greatest advances in modern science grew out of a community of debate rather than from a single solitary mind.
The kural says wisdom only seems to live in one's own mind but really resides in one's company, and quantum mechanics was in large part the product of the Copenhagen circle.
