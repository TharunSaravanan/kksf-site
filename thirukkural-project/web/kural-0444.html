Kural 444

Seeking the Aid of Great Men · Periyaaraith Thunaikkodal · பெரியாரைத் துணைக்கோடல்

தம்மிற் பெரியார் தமரா ஒழுகுதல் வன்மையு ளெல்லாந் தலை.
Thammir periyaar thamaraa ozhukudhal Vanmaiyu lellaan thalai.

Meaning

Getting people who are greater than you to treat you as one of their own is the greatest of all strengths.

Elementary

Being strong isn’t just about muscles or money. This kural says the best kind of strength is behaving in a way that makes people who are wiser and better than you want to be on your side.

University

‘Thammil periyaar’ — those greater than oneself — is the pointed phrase. Rulers and the powerful typically surround themselves with inferiors who flatter; Valluvar calls instead for the humility to keep superiors as intimates. ‘Ozhukudhal’ (conducting oneself) makes this a matter of sustained behavior, not a single act. Calling it the chief of all ‘vanmai’ (strengths) reframes power itself: the strength that multiplies other strengths is the ability to attract and retain better minds.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Before the Kurukshetra war, Arjuna and Duryodhana both come to Krishna; offered a choice between Krishna’s vast Narayani army and Krishna himself, unarmed and non-combatant, Arjuna chooses Krishna, and Duryodhana gladly takes the army.
— Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva (tr. K. M. Ganguli) [4]
Arjuna values having the greater being at his side above any quantity of soldiers — and that choice decides the war.
A near-perfect enactment: making the one greater than oneself one’s own is the supreme strength.
Christianity
“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”
— Proverbs 27:17 (KJV) [5]
Friends shape and strengthen one another the way one iron blade hones another.
Shares the Kural’s view that one’s strength is multiplied through close companions.
Islam
“O you who have believed, fear Allah and be with those who are true.”
— Quran 9:119 (Sahih International) [6]
Believers are commanded not only to be truthful but to place themselves in the company of the truthful.
Being ‘with’ the righteous is presented as a source of spiritual strength, as the Kural presents alliance with the great.
Judaism
“After Honi HaMe’agel awoke from seventy years’ sleep and found no one in the study house recognized him, he prayed for death; the Talmud concludes, “O chavruta o mituta” — “either companionship or death.”
— Babylonian Talmud, Taanit 23a [7]
Without learned companions who know and receive him, even a great sage finds life unbearable.
Companionship with the learned is treated as the essential strength without which life itself fails.
Buddhism
After his awakening, the Buddha reflected that one who dwells without reverence and deference dwells in suffering; finding no one superior to himself, he resolved to honor and respect the Dhamma itself.
— Garava Sutta, SN 6.2 [8]
Even the Buddha felt the need to dwell under something greater than himself.
Affirms the Kural’s insight that honoring and staying close to what surpasses oneself is a source of strength.
Jainism
“Disobedient, rough speaking, ill-behaved pupils will exasperate even a gentle teacher; but those will soon win even a hot-tempered teacher who humour him and are polite.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 1.13 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [9]
The Uttaradhyayana's lecture on discipline teaches that it is the junior's own conduct, humble and courteous, that wins over a superior and keeps that superior's goodwill.
The kural calls it the greatest strength to conduct oneself so that those greater than oneself become one's own. The Jain verse likewise makes winning the great a matter of one's own humble conduct.
Sikhism
Lehna, while on a pilgrimage to a Hindu goddess's shrine, met Guru Nanak and resolved to follow him; known for his loyalty to the first Guru, he became Nanak's successor as Guru Angad.
— Encyclopaedia Britannica, 'Guru Angad' (paraphrase) [10]
Lehna placed himself under one greater than himself and served him with such devotion that Guru Nanak chose him as the second Guru.
The Kural calls it the foremost strength to conduct oneself so that those greater than oneself become one's own; Lehna's loyal discipleship made the greatest teacher of his age wholly his own.
Greek Mythology
In Iliad 2, after Nestor’s advice, Agamemnon exclaims that if he had ten such counselors among the Achaeans, Priam’s city would soon fall.
— Homer, Iliad 2.370–374 [11]
The supreme commander rates the counsel of the aged Nestor above multitudes of warriors.
Just as the Kural calls such alliance the chief of all strengths.
Philosophy
“Friendship “stimulates to noble actions — ‘two going together’ — for with friends men are more able both to think and to act.”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII.1 (tr. W. D. Ross) [12]
Aristotle argues that friends increase our capacity for both thought and action.
Friendship as a multiplier of strength, the Kural’s central claim.

Real Life Example

Lincoln appoints his rivals to his cabinet
1860-1861 · Springfield, Illinois, and Washington, D.C., United States
After winning the presidency in 1860, Abraham Lincoln filled the most important posts in his cabinet with the men who had competed against him for the Republican nomination. William H. Seward, the front-runner he had defeated, became Secretary of State; Salmon P. Chase became Secretary of the Treasury; Edward Bates became Attorney General; and Simon Cameron became Secretary of War.
— Sources: [13] [14]
Lincoln chose to surround himself with powerful and experienced figures rather than loyal followers, gaining their abilities for the Union during the Civil War.
The kural calls it the foremost strength to conduct oneself so that those greater than oneself become one's own, and Lincoln made his strongest rivals his closest colleagues.
