Kural 271

Inconsistent Conduct · Koodaavozhukkam · கூடாவொழுக்கம்

வஞ்ச மனத்தான் படிற்றொழுக்கம் பூதங்கள் ஐந்தும் அகத்தே நகும்.
Vanja manaththaan padittrozhukkam boodhangal Aindhum agaththe nagum.

Meaning

When someone with a deceitful mind pretends to live a holy life, the five elements (earth, water, fire, air and sky) that make up their own body laugh at them from inside.

Elementary

Imagine someone pretending to be very good while secretly cheating. People outside might be fooled, but the earth, water, fire, air and sky that his body is made of can see everything — and they giggle at the silly act. You can’t hide a lie from the stuff you are made of.

University

The chapter opens with a striking image: the five great elements (pancha bhutas — earth, water, fire, air, ether) that compose the hypocrite’s body are themselves the witnesses who see through his sham piety and laugh inwardly. The word படிற்றொழுக்கம் (padittrozhukkam) denotes counterfeit or disguised conduct, and அகத்தே (agaththe) plays on “inside” — both inside the body and inside themselves. The point is that concealment is metaphysically impossible: the deceiver carries his witnesses with him. Parimelazhagar’s tradition reads the elements’ laughter as mockery of a man who thinks he can fool the world while his own substance knows the truth — a theme paralleled by Manu’s list of cosmic witnesses and the Qur’anic image of skins testifying.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“The wicked, indeed, say in their hearts, ‘Nobody sees us;’ but the gods distinctly see them and the male within their own breasts. The sky, the earth, the waters, (the male in) the heart, the moon, the sun, the fire, Yama and the wind, the night, the two twilights, and justice know the conduct of all corporeal beings.”
— Manusmriti 8.85–86 (Bühler translation) [3]
Manu tells witnesses that no one lies unseen: the sky, earth, waters, fire, wind and the inner self are all watching everything a person does.
Almost the same list as the Kural’s five elements: the physical world itself is the witness that knows the hypocrite’s real conduct.
Christianity
“Beware ye of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy. For there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed; neither hid, that shall not be known.”
— Luke 12:1–2 (KJV) [4]
Jesus warns his disciples against hypocrisy and says that whatever is hidden will eventually come out into the open.
Like the Kural, it treats religious pretence as ultimately transparent: what the hypocrite hides is already known.
Islam
“Until, when they reach it, their hearing and their eyes and their skins will testify against them of what they used to do.”
— Qur’an 41:20 (Sahih International) [5]
On the Day of Judgement, the Qur’an says a person’s own body parts — ears, eyes, skin — will speak up and report what that person did.
The same idea as the Kural: the hypocrite’s own body is the witness against him.
Judaism
“He that hateth dissembleth with his lips, and layeth up deceit within him… Whose hatred is covered by deceit, his wickedness shall be shewed before the whole congregation.”
— Proverbs 26:24–26 (KJV) [4]
Proverbs describes a person who hides malice behind pleasant words and says his wickedness will eventually be exposed publicly.
Shares the Kural’s conviction that a deceitful inner life cannot stay covered; exposure and shame follow.
Buddhism
“Whoever being depraved, devoid of self-control and truthfulness, should don the monk’s yellow robe, he surely is not worthy of the robe.”
— Dhammapada 9 (tr. Buddharakkhita) [6]
The Buddha says a person who is corrupt and untruthful gains nothing by wearing a monk’s robe — he simply does not deserve it.
Names the exact sin of the chapter — holy dress over a deceitful mind — though without the image of the elements mocking him.
Jainism
“My own Self is the doer and undoer of misery and happiness; my own Self, friend and foe, according as I act well or badly.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 20.37 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [7]
The monk tells King Srenika that no outside agent protects or condemns a person; the self itself is the author and witness of its own good and evil.
Valluvar pictures the hypocrite's own bodily elements laughing at him from within; the Jain verse likewise places the true judge of conduct inside the self rather than in outward appearances.
Sikhism
“Outwardly, they wear religious robes, but within is the filth of Maya. They cannot conceal this, no matter how hard they try.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 267, Raag Gauree, Sukhmani Sahib, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
In the Sukhmani Guru Arjan describes people who wear holy dress over a mind full of worldly filth, and says the truth cannot be hidden however much they try.
Valluvar pictures the hypocrite's own five elements laughing at his sham holiness; Guru Arjan likewise says the inner corruption under the robe cannot be concealed.
Greek Mythology
In the song of Demodocus, Aphrodite and Ares carry on a secret affair in Hephaestus’ house; Helios the Sun, who sees everything, reports it, Hephaestus traps them in an invisible net, and the assembled gods burst into laughter at the exposed pair.
— Homer, Odyssey 8.266–366 [9]
A hidden wrongdoing is seen by a cosmic power (the Sun), exposed, and met with the gods’ mocking laughter.
Echoes the Kural’s picture of a natural, all-seeing element exposing concealed misconduct and laughing at it.
Philosophy
“Hypocrisy is the homage vice pays to virtue.”
— La Rochefoucauld, Maxims 218 [10]
La Rochefoucauld observes that hypocrites pretend to be good precisely because goodness is admired; their disguise is a backhanded compliment to virtue.
Diagnoses the same phenomenon the Kural mocks — vice dressed up as virtue — with a similarly ironic, laughing tone.

Real Life Example

Margaret Fox confesses that the spirit rappings were a hoax
21 October 1888 · Academy of Music, New York City, United States
In 1848 the young sisters Margaret and Kate Fox of Hydesville, New York, began producing rapping sounds that they presented as messages from spirits, and their seances helped launch the Spiritualist movement. In October 1888 Margaret told the New York World, and then a public audience at the New York Academy of Music, that the raps had been a fraud produced chiefly by movements of the joints of their toes. She retracted the confession the following year.
— Sources: [11] [12]
Four decades of claimed contact with the spirit world turned out to have come from the sisters' own bodies.
The kural says the five elements of the hypocrite's own body laugh at his false show of holiness, and in the Fox case the deceivers' own bodies were the secret source of the sham.
