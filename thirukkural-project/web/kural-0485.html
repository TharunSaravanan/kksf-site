Kural 485

Knowledge of Apt Timing · Kaalamarithal · காலமறிதல்

காலம் கருதி இருப்பர் கலங்காது ஞாலம் கருது பவர்.
Kaalam Karudhi Iruppar Kalangaadhu Gnaalam Karudhu Pavar.

Meaning

People who want to win the world do not get upset or hasty. They wait calmly and watch for the right time.

Elementary

A cat watching a mouse-hole sits very still and doesn’t get upset or jump too early. People with big goals are like that. They stay calm and wait for their chance.

University

The couplet picks up gnaalam karudhu (‘aiming at the world’) from the previous kural and shows the inner attitude that goes with it. Such people iruppar, ‘stay’ or ‘sit still’, and do so kalangaadhu, ‘without being agitated’. Parimelazhagar explains that they are not disturbed by the enemy’s present prosperity or by their own present setbacks, because they know that the favourable time will come. The virtue described is patience that is also watchfulness, an active waiting rather than inertia. The printed division ‘karudhu pavar’ is a metrical split of karudhupavar, and the two sources agree.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“When (the king) knows (that) at some future time his superiority (is) certain, and (that) at the time present (he will suffer) little injury, then let him have recourse to peaceful measures.”
— Manusmṛti 7.169 (tr. G. Bühler) [3]
Manu advises a king who is sure to gain the upper hand later to stay at peace for now, since waiting costs him little.
This is the Kural’s calm waiting for the right time, written as a rule of royal policy.
Christianity
“Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain.”
— James 5:7 (KJV) [4]
James uses the farmer who waits calmly for the rains as the model of patience before a great outcome.
The waiting farmer matches the Kural’s unperturbed person who waits for the moment.
Islam
At Hudaybiyyah (628 CE), the Prophet accepted a truce whose terms many companions found humiliating. They could not enter Mecca that year and had to return fugitives. The Qur’an nevertheless called it ‘a clear victory’ (48:1), and within two years Mecca opened to him.
— Qur’an 48:1; sira accounts of the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah [5]
The Prophet held back from forcing the issue and waited calmly while circumstances turned in his favour.
This is the Kural’s waiting ‘without agitation’ by one who aims at the greater goal.
Judaism
David was already anointed king by Samuel, yet he refused to seize the throne from Saul. For years he lived as a fugitive in the wilderness and twice spared Saul’s life when he had the chance to kill him, waiting for the kingship to come in its own time.
— 1 Samuel 24 and 26 [4]
David’s restraint while marked for kingship is the Bible’s classic picture of patient waiting for one’s hour.
He was one who ‘aimed at the realm’ and waited without panic for the right time.
Buddhism
The Dhammapada says that as a solid rock is not shaken by the wind, the wise are not moved by praise or blame.
— Dhammapada 81 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [6]
The wise person keeps an inner steadiness whatever happens outside.
Kalangaadhu, ‘unshaken’, names the same calm that the Kural requires while waiting.
Jainism
“A restrained, very learned, and wise (monk) should accept such alms as are freely given him, being free from passions and waiting for his end. This is the doctrine of the Kêvalin.”
— Sutrakritanga I.11.38 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [7]
The Path lecture closes by describing the sage who lives free of passion and calmly waits for his appointed end.
The kural praises those who wait undisturbed for their time; the Jain sage also waits calmly and without agitation, though what he awaits is the end of life and liberation, not the chance to win the world.
Sikhism
“Within yourself, make patience the bow, and make patience the bowstring. Make patience the arrow, the Creator will not let you miss the target.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1384, Salok Sheikh Farid (salok 115) (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Sheikh Farid, whose saloks are included in the Guru Granth Sahib, makes patience (sabar) the whole weapon: bow, string and arrow, which God will not let miss.
The Kural shows those who aim at the world waiting calmly and unshaken for their time; Farid teaches that patience itself is the weapon that finds its mark.
Greek Mythology
For years Penelope held off the suitors. She promised to choose one of them after finishing a shroud for Laertes, and each night she secretly unwove what she had woven by day. She kept the household intact until Odysseus returned.
— Homer, Odyssey, Books 2 and 19 [9]
She could not overpower the suitors, so she bought time with calm cunning, waiting for the moment when things would change.
Penelope shows the Kural’s patient waiting, steady amid pressure and alert to the coming time.
Philosophy
“Sun Tzu said: The good fighters of old first put themselves beyond the possibility of defeat, and then waited for an opportunity of defeating the enemy.”
— Sun Tzu, The Art of War IV.1 (tr. Lionel Giles) [10]
Sun Tzu teaches that security lies in your own hands, while the chance of victory must be waited for, since the enemy provides it.
This is exactly the Kural’s picture: secure and unshaken, the aspirant waits for the time.

Real Life Example

Fabius Maximus, the Delayer, wears down Hannibal
217-216 BCE and after · Italy
After Hannibal's victories in Italy, Quintus Fabius Maximus was made dictator in 217 BCE and refused to meet Hannibal in pitched battle, instead shadowing his army through the hills, harassing his foraging parties and cutting off his supplies. His caution was unpopular in Rome, earning him the nickname Cunctator, the Delayer, and his command was divided with his critic Minucius. After the Romans abandoned the strategy and suffered a disastrous defeat at Cannae in 216 BCE, they returned to his approach.
— Sources: [11] [12]
Fabius endured public ridicule rather than fight on the enemy's terms, and his patience was later recognised as having saved Rome.
The kural says those who aim high wait unshaken for their time, undisturbed by the enemy's present success, and Fabius held to his course while Hannibal seemed unstoppable and Rome mocked him.
