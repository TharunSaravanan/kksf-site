Kural 681

The Envoy · Thoodhu · தூது

அன்புடைமை ஆன்ற குடிப்பிறத்தல் வேந்தவாம் பண்புடைமை தூதுரைப்பான் பண்பு.
Anbudaimai aandra kudippiraththal vendhavaam Panbudaimai thoodhuraippaan panbu.

Meaning

An envoy (a messenger sent to speak for a king) should be loving, come from a respected family, and have the good manners that kings value.

Elementary

When your class sends someone to ask the principal for a longer break, you pick a kind person who comes from a family everyone trusts and who knows how to be polite to grown-ups. The person carrying a message has to be someone the listener already respects.

University

The chapter on the envoy (tūtu) belongs to the Amaichiyal, the section on ministers (chs. 64-73), and opens with the general qualifications. Parimelazhagar glosses aṉpuṭaimai as affection toward one’s own kin (cuṟṟattār), ‘āṉṟa kuṭi’ as a family fit to bear ministerial office, and ‘vēntu avām paṇpu’ as the qualities royalty desires. He adds that kin-affection means the envoy keeps his relatives from harm, and good lineage means he has heard of envoy-craft from his forebears. Manakkudavar reads the first quality differently, as love for one’s own king, and explains ‘qualities kings desire’ as not coveting what the king covets. Mu. Varadarasanar, Solomon Pappaiah and Kalaignar keep the three-part list, Pappaiah widening the love to people, state and kin. On the text: ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net print அன்புடைமை ஆன்ற; the sandhi-joined text at thirukkural-thamizh.org reads அன்புடைமை யான்ற, the same words with the glide written in.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Let him also appoint an ambassador who is versed in all sciences, who understands hints, expressions of the face and gestures, who is honest, skilful, and of (noble) family.”
— Manusmriti 7.63 (trans. G. Bühler, Sacred Books of the East vol. 25) [5]
The Laws of Manu tell the king how to choose his envoy, and good family is on the list along with learning, honesty and skill.
Like the Kural, Manu makes noble birth one of the envoy’s basic qualifications.
Christianity
“Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.”
— Acts 6:3 (KJV) [6]
When the apostles need people to act on the community’s behalf, they ask for men already known for good reputation and wisdom.
As in the Kural, a person is sent to represent others because of character that people already recognize.
Islam
“I asked you about his family and your reply was that he belonged to a very noble family. In fact all the Apostles come from noble families amongst their respective peoples.”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 7 [7]
Questioning Abu Sufyan about the Prophet, the Byzantine emperor Heraclius treats noble lineage as a mark of a genuine messenger.
Heraclius and the Kural both assume that a trustworthy messenger comes from a respected family.
Judaism
“He that loveth pureness of heart, for the grace of his lips the king shall be his friend.”
— Proverbs 22:11 (KJV) [6]
The proverb says a person with a pure heart and gracious speech wins the friendship of kings.
This is the Kural’s ‘quality that kings love’ (vēntu avām paṇpu): good character and pleasant manners.
Buddhism
Among the highest blessings the Buddha lists in the Mangala Sutta are supporting one’s parents, cherishing spouse and children, and helping one’s relatives.
— Mangala Sutta, Sutta Nipata 2.4 [8]
The discourse treats care for one’s own kin as a mark of a well-ordered and admirable life.
It matches Parimelazhagar’s reading of aṉpuṭaimai as affection for one’s own kin.
Jainism
“He who always acknowledges his allegiance to his teacher, who has religious zeal and ardour for study, who is kind in words and actions, deserves to be instructed.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 11.14 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [9]
The sutra names loyalty to one's teacher's house, eagerness to learn and kindness in speech and action as what makes a disciple fit to be trusted with instruction.
As the Kural asks for a loving nature, good standing and refined conduct in an envoy, the Jain text asks for loyalty, zeal and kind conduct in one who is to be trusted.
Sikhism
“Recognize the Lord's Light within all, and do not consider social class or status; there are no classes or castes in the world hereafter.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 349, Raag Aasaa, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
Guru Nanak rejects birth and social rank as a measure of worth, telling the listener to see the one divine Light in every person.
The Kural counts birth in a family of good standing among an envoy's qualifications; Sikh teaching agrees that a loving nature and refined conduct matter, but explicitly refuses to rank anyone by birth.
Greek Mythology
When the Greeks send an embassy to the angry Achilles, Nestor has Phoenix lead it. Phoenix is the old man who raised Achilles, and he later appeals to that bond of love.
— Homer, Iliad 9.168-170 and 9.434-605 [11]
Phoenix is chosen for his loving relationship with Achilles, which the Greeks hope will make the hearer receptive.
In the Kural too, affection and good standing are what qualify someone to carry a message.
Philosophy
In his essay ‘Of Negotiating’, Francis Bacon advises using agents who have a personal attachment to the business they are sent on, and matching the agent’s temperament to the task.
— Francis Bacon, Essays (1625), ‘Of Negotiating’ [12]
Bacon argues that a negotiator who cares about the cause works harder at it, and that personal qualities should be chosen to fit the mission.
Like the Kural, Bacon judges an envoy first by character and attachment, and only then by skill.

Real Life Example

Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit becomes the first woman to preside over the UN General Assembly
1953 · United Nations, New York, United States
Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit, daughter of the nationalist leader Motilal Nehru and sister of Jawaharlal Nehru, led India's delegation to the United Nations and served as ambassador in Moscow and Washington after independence. In 1953 the member states elected her president of the UN General Assembly, the first woman ever to hold the post. She later served as India's high commissioner in London.
— Sources: [13] [14]
Her career shows how a diplomat from a family long schooled in public life, with a manner that won the respect of other states, could be entrusted with her country's voice and then chosen to preside over the assembly of nations.
The kural names good family standing and the conduct that rulers value as marks of an envoy, and Pandit's background and the trust placed in her by the world's governments reflect both.
