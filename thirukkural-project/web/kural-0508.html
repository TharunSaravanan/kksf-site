Kural 508

Selection and Confidence · Therindhu Thelithal · தெரிந்து தெளிதல்

தேரான் பிறனைத் தெளிந்தான் வழிமுறை தீரா இடும்பை தரும்.
Thaeraan piranaith thelindhaan vazhimurai Theeraa idumbai tharum.

Meaning

Someone who trusts a stranger without checking them first brings endless trouble, even to their own children and grandchildren.

Elementary

If a stranger at the park says, “Give me your house key and I’ll water your plants,” and you just hand it over, trouble could follow your whole family for a long time. Trust has to be earned before you give it.

University

தேரான் (‘without examining’) and பிறன் (‘another, an outsider’) define the fault, and தெளிந்தான் (‘he who trusted’) the one who commits it. The key phrase வழிமுறை is read by Varadarasan and Kalaignar as ‘those who come after him’, his descendants, so the harm outlives the one who made the mistake; Pope follows this (‘on all his race’). Pappaiah shifts பிறன் to someone indifferent to the nation’s interests, a modern political reading. The second line தீரா இடும்பை தரும் is repeated word for word in 510, framing the chapter’s last three couplets with the same warning. ytamizh prints the couplet without the final full stop; the words are otherwise identical in both sources.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Queen Kaikeyi trusts the counsel of her maid Manthara without examining her motives, demands Rama’s exile and Bharata’s coronation, and so brings grief on Dasharatha, who dies, and on the whole royal house.
— Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda 7-12 [3]
One unexamined confidence sets off suffering for a whole dynasty.
The Kural warns that unexamined trust brings sorrow that passes down to one’s line.
Christianity
“Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves.”
— Matthew 7:15 (KJV) [4]
Jesus warns that people who look harmless may be dangerous inside, so appearances must be tested.
The Kural too warns against trusting an unexamined outsider on appearances.
Islam
“O you who have believed, if there comes to you a disobedient one with information, investigate, lest you harm a people out of ignorance and become, over what you have done, regretful.”
— Qur’an 49:6 (Sahih International) [5]
Believers are told to check reports from untrustworthy people before acting on them.
The Kural’s rule is the same: investigate first or face lasting regret.
Judaism
“And the men took of their provision, and asked not counsel at the mouth of the LORD.”
— Joshua 9:14 (JPS 1917) [6]
The Gibeonites pretend to be travellers from far away; Joshua’s leaders make a sworn treaty without proper inquiry, and that oath binds Israel for generations, returning as a famine in David’s time (2 Samuel 21).
An unexamined agreement with strangers brings trouble on later generations, which is the Kural’s வழிமுறை.
Buddhism
“Not by appearance is a man rightly known, nor should trust be based on a quick glance, — for, disguised as well-restrained, the unrestrained go through this world.”
— Saṃyutta Nikāya 3.11, verse (trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, as given in notes to Udāna 6.2) [7]
The Buddha warns King Pasenadi that people who look disciplined may not be, so trust must not be given at a glance.
This is the Kural’s தேரான் தெளிவு: trust without examination.
Jainism
“Just as a person is free while climbing a tree but once he starts falling then he has no power to check it.”
— Saman Suttam v. 60 (Precepts on Karma; English tr. T.K. Tukol and K.K. Dixit; jainworld.com) [8]
The Jain teaching on karma says a person is free when binding karma but loses control once its consequences begin to unfold.
The kural warns that a careless act of trust lets loose sorrow that cannot be recalled; the Jain image makes the same point that the choice is free but its fall cannot be stopped.
Sikhism
“Do not associate with the faithless cynics; they would bring you to ruin.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1369, Salok Bhagat Kabir (salok 93) (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Kabir warns that close association with those turned away from God ends in ruin for the one who keeps their company.
The Kural says that putting confidence in someone unexamined brings endless sorrow; Kabir likewise warns that the wrong companion brings ruin.
Greek Mythology
The Trojans take in Sinon, a Greek stranger with a convincing story, believe him without proper testing, and drag the wooden horse into the city, leading to the fall of Troy and the end of Priam’s line.
— Virgil, Aeneid 2.57-249 [10]
Laocoön’s warning is ignored and one act of unexamined trust destroys a whole city and dynasty.
Trusting an untested stranger brings sorrow that does not end, just as the Kural says.
Philosophy
Cicero, following Theophrastus, says one should love after judging, not judge after loving, and notes that people who make friends hastily often come to regret it.
— Cicero, On Friendship (De Amicitia) 85 [11]
Judgment must come before attachment, or regret follows.
The Kural states the same order and the same cost of reversing it.

Real Life Example

Lobengula signs the Rudd Concession
30 October 1888 · Bulawayo, Matabeleland (now Zimbabwe)
King Lobengula of the Ndebele signed a concession with agents of Cecil Rhodes led by Charles Rudd, believing he was granting limited mining rights. The written document gave the grantees exclusive rights to all metals and minerals in his kingdoms, and Lobengula later repudiated it and sent envoys to London to protest. The British government accepted the concession in 1889 and used it to charter the British South Africa Company, which went on to take over his kingdom.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Trust placed in outsiders without fully examining what was being signed cost the king his realm and his people their land for generations.
The kural warns that trusting a stranger without examination brings endless sorrow even on one's descendants, which is what followed this signature.
