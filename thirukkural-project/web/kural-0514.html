Kural 514

Selection and Employment · Therindhu Vinaiyaadal · தெரிந்து வினையாடல்

எனைவகையான் தேறியக் கண்ணும் வினைவகையான் வேறாகும் மாந்தர் பலர்.
Enaivagaiyaan theriyak kannum vinaivagaiyaan Veraagum maandhar palar.

Meaning

Even after being tested and approved in every way, many people turn out to be different once they actually start doing the work.

Elementary

Someone can do great in a practice game and then play very differently in the real match. Tests help, but you only really know a person once they’re doing the actual job.

University

Having spent chapter 51 on testing (தெரிந்து தெளிதல்), Valluvar now admits its limits: however thoroughly a man is examined (எனைவகையான் தேறிய), the nature of the work itself (வினைவகையான்) can change him. Parimelazhagar sees here the corrupting pressure of office, wealth and power, and Pappaiah reads it as simple underperformance against expectations. The practical lesson, developed in Kural 520, is that selection must be followed by continuing supervision. The saying of Bias preserved by Aristotle, “rule will show the man,” states the same insight: power reveals what screening could not.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Just as it is impossible not to taste the honey or the poison that finds itself at the tip of the tongue, so it is impossible for a government servant not to eat up, at least, a bit of the king’s revenue.”
— Kautilya, Arthashastra 2.9 (Shamasastry) [3]
Kautilya warns that officials in charge of revenue are constantly tempted, and even tested men may start to skim once the money is in their hands.
The work itself changes the worker, which is exactly the Kural’s warning.
Christianity
“This he said, not that he cared for the poor; but because he was a thief, and had the bag, and bare what was put therein.”
— John 12:6 (KJV) [4]
Judas, one of the Twelve chosen by Jesus himself, was trusted with the common purse and stole from it.
Even someone chosen after close personal contact changed once he held the money, as the Kural warns.
Islam
“And recite to them, [O Muhammad], the news of him to whom we gave [knowledge of] Our signs, but he detached himself from them; so Satan pursued him, and he became of the deviators.”
— Qur’an 7:175 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur’an tells of a man given great knowledge who later turned away and went astray.
Being gifted and approved at the start does not guarantee faithfulness later.
Judaism
“It repenteth Me that I have set up Saul to be king; for he is turned back from following Me, and hath not performed My commandments.”
— 1 Samuel 15:11 (JPS 1917) [6]
Saul, chosen and anointed as king, failed once in power to carry out what he was charged with.
Even a man chosen by the highest authority can turn out differently once in the role.
Buddhism
Devadatta, a monk who had gained psychic powers, was ruined by the gain, honour and fame that came to him; the Buddha likens this to a plantain that dies after it bears fruit.
— Samyutta Nikaya 17.35-36 [7]
Success and position changed Devadatta until he turned against the Buddha.
The pressures of a role can transform someone who once seemed sound, as Kural 514 observes.
Jainism
“After they have been instructed, admitted into the order, and nourished with food and drink, they disperse in all directions like geese whose wings have grown.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 27.14 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The teacher Garga laments pupils who were instructed, accepted and cared for, yet once in service would not carry out their tasks and scattered.
The kural observes that many people tested and approved still turn out differently once they are doing the work; Garga's pupils, received and trained with care, prove exactly that.
Sikhism
The masands, appointed by Guru Arjan to preach and to collect the community's offerings, were at first chosen for their piety and devotion. As time passed many grew neglectful of their office and took to enriching themselves, until Guru Gobind Singh charged them with corruption and oppression, punished the guilty and abolished the institution.
— Encyclopaedia of Sikhism (Punjabi University, Patiala, ed. Harbans Singh), 'Masands' (paraphrase) [9]
The Encyclopaedia of Sikhism traces how agents who had been carefully selected were changed by the power and wealth their office brought.
The Kural warns that even people tested in every way often turn out differently once they are doing the work; the masands, chosen for piety, were corrupted by their office.
Greek Mythology
Pindar tells how Tantalus, honoured by the gods and admitted to their table, could not bear his great good fortune and stole nectar and ambrosia to give to mortal companions, and was punished for ever.
— Pindar, Olympian 1 [10]
A man who seemed worthy of the gods’ trust betrayed it once he had it.
Trust granted after approval can still be abused, which is the Kural’s point.
Philosophy
“This is why the saying of Bias is thought to be true, that ‘rule will show the man’; for a ruler is necessarily in relation to other men and a member of a society.”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics V.1, 1130a (Ross) [11]
Aristotle quotes the sage Bias: you only discover what a person is really like when he holds power over others.
The Kural says the same: many who passed every test turn out differently once they are actually in the job.

Real Life Example

Thomas Becket turns from royal chancellor to opponent of Henry II
1162-1164 · Canterbury and London, England
Thomas Becket had served Henry II as chancellor and was the king's close friend, and in 1162 Henry had him made Archbishop of Canterbury. Soon afterwards Becket gave up the chancellorship against the king's wishes and began defending the rights of the Church against the crown. The dispute came to a head over the Constitutions of Clarendon of 1164, and Becket went into exile in France until 1170.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A man the king knew intimately and had tested for years became a very different person once he held the new office.
The kural says that even people tested and approved in every way often turn out differently once they are actually doing the work, as Becket did after becoming archbishop.
