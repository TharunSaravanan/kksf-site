Kural 948

Medicine · Marundhu · மருந்து

நோய்நாடி நோய்முதல் நாடி அதுதணிக்கும் வாய்நாடி வாய்ப்பச் செயல்.
Noinaadi noimudhal naadi adhuthanikkum vaainaadi vaaippach cheyal.

Meaning

Find out what the sickness is, find its cause, find the way to ease it, and then treat it in the right way.

Elementary

When your bike makes a funny noise, a good mechanic first finds which part is wrong, then why it broke, then how to fix it, and only then gets out the tools. Valluvar says a good doctor does the same with a sick person.

University

The most quoted couplet of the chapter is built on a threefold naadi (நாடி, 'having examined'): the disease (noi), its root or first cause (noi mudhal, நோய் முதல்), and the means (vaai, வாய்) that will abate it (thanikkum, தணிக்கும்). Only then comes vaaippach cheyal (வாய்ப்பச் செயல்), 'act fittingly': Kesikan reads it as treating without error in procedure, Varadarasanar as in a way suited to the body. Commentators note the sequence of diagnosis, aetiology and therapy as a complete clinical method; Kalaignar adds that it applies to the ills of society as well as of the body. The repetition of naadi and of vaai/vaaippa gives the line a deliberate, step-by-step rhythm. The couplet appears in the sung version of the chapter by P. A. Muthukrishnan quoted on thirukkural.net. Text identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Charaka instructs the physician to examine the disease first and only afterwards the medicine, and then to begin treatment with knowledge.
— Charaka Samhita, Sutrasthana 20.20, paraphrase [3]
Classical Ayurveda puts diagnosis before any prescription.
This is the Kural's order: know the disease and its cause before choosing the remedy.
Christianity
“Then give place to the physician, for the Lord hath created him: let him not go from thee, for thou hast need of him. There is a time when in their hands there is good success. For they shall also pray unto the Lord, that he would prosper that, which they give for ease and remedy to prolong life.”
— Sirach (Ecclesiasticus) 38:12-14 (KJV Apocrypha) [4]
Ben Sira honours the physician and describes him seeking the right remedy and the right moment to bring ease.
It supports the Kural's skilled, careful physician, though without its diagnostic method.
Islam
“There is a remedy for every malady, and when the remedy is applied to the disease it is cured with the permission of Allah, the Exalted and Glorious.”
— Sahih Muslim 2204 [5]
The Prophet teaches that every disease has its proper remedy, which cures when correctly matched to it.
Matching the right remedy to the disease is the Kural's 'find the means that abates it'.
Judaism
The Talmud derives from the words 'and he shall surely heal' that the Torah grants the physician permission to heal, so that medical treatment is a sanctioned and even obligatory act.
— Babylonian Talmud, Bava Kamma 85a (on Exodus 21:19), paraphrase [6]
Rabbinic law affirms the physician's work as legitimate rather than an intrusion on God's will.
It provides the religious warrant for the physician the Kural describes, though not his method.
Buddhism
Buddhaghosa explains the Four Noble Truths through medicine: suffering is like the disease, its origin like the cause of the disease, its cessation like the cure, and the path like the medicine.
— Visuddhimagga XVI.87 (tr. Bhikkhu Nanamoli), paraphrase [7]
The commentarial tradition reads the Buddha's core teaching as a physician's diagnosis, aetiology, prognosis and prescription.
This is the Kural's sequence of disease, its cause and its remedy, raised to a spiritual method.
Jainism
“Love and hatred are caused by Karman, and they say that Karman has its origin in delusion; Karman is the root of birth and death, and birth and death they call misery. Misery ceases on the absence of delusion, delusion ceases on the absence of desire, desire ceases on the absence of greed, greed ceases on the absence of property.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 32.7-8, cf. 32.110 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
The Uttaradhyayana traces suffering back through karma and delusion to its roots and shows how removing each root ends it, calling final liberation recovery 'from the long illness'.
This is the Kural's method of finding the disease, then its root cause, then what removes it, applied to the soul's misery.
Sikhism
“O physician, you are a competent physician, if you first diagnose the disease. Prescribe such a remedy, by which all sorts of illnesses may be cured. Administer that medicine, which will cure the disease, and allow peace to come and dwell in the body.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1279, Raag Malaar, Malaar ki Vaar, salok of Guru Angad (Mahala 2) (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In a salok in Guru Nanak's Malaar ki Vaar, Guru Angad addresses the physician: first find the disease, then the remedy, then give the medicine that truly cures.
This is almost the Kural's own three steps: find the disease, find the remedy, then act in the way that fits.
Greek Mythology
When Menelaus is wounded by an arrow, the healer Machaon, son of Asclepius, comes, draws the arrow, looks at the wound, sucks out the blood and skilfully spreads on it the soothing remedies that Chiron had given his father.
— Homer, Iliad 4.210-219 (tr. Samuel Butler), paraphrase [10]
Homer's physician examines the wound first and then applies the proper remedy.
Machaon follows the Kural's order: look at the injury, then bring the fitting treatment.
Philosophy
Plato contrasts the slave doctor who prescribes hastily with the free doctor who examines the disease from its origin and according to nature, questions the patient and his friends, and prescribes only after understanding the case.
— Plato, Laws IV, 720a-e (tr. Benjamin Jowett), paraphrase [11]
Plato's model physician investigates the nature and origin of the illness before he treats.
This is the Kural's physician who examines the disease and its cause before acting.

Real Life Example

Walter Reed's commission confirms that mosquitoes spread yellow fever
1900-1901 · Havana, Cuba
In 1900 the United States Army sent a commission led by Walter Reed to Cuba to study yellow fever, which was killing soldiers and civilians. Testing the hypothesis of the Cuban physician Carlos Finlay, the commission showed that the disease was carried by a mosquito and not spread by contact with patients' clothing or bedding. William Gorgas then directed a campaign in Havana to screen patients and destroy mosquito breeding places, and within months of its start in 1901 the city was freed of yellow fever.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Identifying the true cause of the disease made an effective remedy possible, and the same methods later protected workers building the Panama Canal.
The kural's method is to find the disease, find its cause, find what will relieve it and then act, and the Reed commission and Gorgas followed that sequence step by step.
