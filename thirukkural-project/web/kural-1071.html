Kural 1071

Baseness · Kayamai · கயமை

மக்களே போல்வர் கயவர் அவரன்ன ஒப்பாரி யாங்கண்ட தில்.
Makkale polvar kayavar avaranna oppaari yaangkanda thil.

Meaning

Low, mean people look exactly like human beings. I have never seen any two other things look so much alike.

Elementary

Imagine a shiny plastic apple in a fruit bowl. It looks just like a real apple, but you cannot eat it. Some people are like that: they look like everyone else on the outside, but inside they lack the kindness and goodness that make a real person.

University

Kayamai (கயமை, 'baseness') closes the Porutpal and its Kudiyiyal section, as a dark mirror to the preceding chapters on nobility of birth, honour, greatness, perfect goodness and courtesy (chs. 96-100). The first couplet is a famous piece of irony: kayavar (கயவர், 'the base') are makkale polvar (மக்களே போல்வர், 'just like humans'), and oppaari (ஒப்பாரி, 'resemblance') so exact is found nowhere else. Parimelazhagar and the commentators who follow him (Mu. Varadarasanar, Puliyur Kesikan) read it as saying the base share only the outward form of humanity, so that the likeness is complete in shape and absent in substance; Solomon Pappaiah adds that no animal or bird species shows such a perfect double. Pope renders it 'The base resemble men in outward form, I ween; / But counterpart exact to them I've never seen.' The text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Arrogance, pride, anger, conceit, harshness and ignorance—these qualities belong to those of demonic nature, O son of Pṛthā.”
— Bhagavad Gita 16.4 (tr. A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada) [3]
Krishna names the traits that mark the asuric (demonic) nature in people who are outwardly human.
Like the Kural, the Gita locates what is base not in a person's form but in qualities that hollow out their humanity.
Christianity
“Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye are like unto whited sepulchres, which indeed appear beautiful outward, but are within full of dead men's bones, and of all uncleanness.”
— Matthew 23:27 (KJV) [4]
Jesus compares hypocrites to painted tombs that look fine outside and are full of decay inside.
The Kural's base person is likewise perfect on the outside and empty within.
Islam
“They have hearts with which they do not understand, they have eyes with which they do not see, and they have ears with which they do not hear. Those are like livestock; rather, they are more astray.”
— Qur'an 7:179 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur'an describes people who have every human faculty but do not use them, and so fall below the animals.
Like the Kural, it says such people have the human frame but not what makes it human.
Judaism
“In a place where there are no men, strive to be a man.”
— Pirkei Avot 2:5 (Mishnah Yomit, tr. Joshua Kulp, via Sefaria) [6]
Hillel teaches that being truly human is a moral achievement, especially where others fail at it.
Hillel's saying assumes the Kural's point, that many who look like men are not men in the full sense.
Buddhism
“Not by mere eloquence nor by beauty of form does a man become accomplished, if he is jealous, selfish and deceitful.”
— Dhammapada 262 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Buddha says outward gifts like fine speech or good looks count for nothing in a person who is envious, selfish and deceitful.
As in the Kural, outward appearance cannot make up for a base character.
Jainism
“The base in prosperity will not do these things which those who are of high birth will do even in adversity. Though one place rings (of gold) upon the tusks of a hog, O thou who hast eyes like a lance! it will never become a warlike elephant.”
— Naladiyar 36.8, chapter 'Baseness or Envy' (English translation in The Indian Antiquary, via wisdomlib.org) [8]
The Jain Naladiyar says that dressing up a hog's tusks in gold rings does not turn it into a war elephant, and outward finery does not change the nature of the base.
Like the Kural, the stanza says the base may look like the real thing but stay what they are underneath.
Sikhism
“They belong to the human species, but they act like animals.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 267, Raag Gauri, Sukhmani, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Sukhmani Sahib, Guru Arjan describes people who are human by birth but whose conduct is that of beasts.
The Kural ironically says the base look exactly like human beings; Guru Arjan makes the same point, that a human form can hide conduct that is not human at all.
Greek Mythology
Diogenes the Cynic is said to have lit a lamp in broad daylight and walked about saying he was looking for a man.
— Diogenes Laertius, Lives of Eminent Philosophers 6.41 (tr. R. D. Hicks) [10]
The joke was that the streets were full of people but he could not find a real human being among them.
Diogenes makes the Kural's ironic point: many look like men, but few are.
Philosophy
Aristotle writes that man, when perfected, is the best of animals, but when separated from law and justice he is the worst of all.
— Aristotle, Politics 1.2 (tr. Benjamin Jowett) [11]
For Aristotle, being human is fulfilled only through virtue and justice; without them a human being sinks below the beasts.
Aristotle, like the Kural, separates the human form from the human excellence that should fill it.

Real Life Example

Anna Anderson claims to be Grand Duchess Anastasia
1922–1994 · Berlin, Germany, and Charlottesville, Virginia, United States
In 1922 a young woman who later became known as Anna Anderson appeared in Berlin claiming to be Anastasia, the youngest daughter of the murdered Russian tsar Nicholas II. For decades she persuaded many people, and she later lived in Charlottesville, Virginia, where she died in 1984. In 1994 separate British and American scientific teams tested her DNA and concluded that she was not a Romanov.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Her case shows how closely a person can match an outward likeness, even convincing people who had known the royal family, while the inner truth is quite different.
The kural's irony is that the base look exactly like true human beings; Anderson's near-perfect outward resemblance to a princess, exposed only by DNA, shows how convincing a mere likeness can be.
