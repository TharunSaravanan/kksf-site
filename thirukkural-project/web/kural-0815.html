Kural 815

Evil Friendship · Thee Natpu · தீ நட்பு

செய்தேமஞ் சாராச் சிறியவர் புன்கேண்மை எய்தலின் எய்தாமை நன்று.
Seydhemanj saaraach chiriyavar punkenmai eydhalin eydhaamai nandru.

Meaning

Small-minded people will not protect you, even after you have done a lot for them. It is better never to have their poor friendship than to have it.

Elementary

Think of an umbrella with holes in it. You can carry it, take care of it and open it every time, but it still won’t keep you dry. Some friends are like that, and it’s better not to count on them at all.

University

The key compound is seydhemam saaraa (செய்தேமம் சாரா): ēmam (ஏமம்) is ‘protection, safety’, and the phrase means ‘not coming to be a protection even when it has been made one’. Mu. Varadarasanar reads it as ‘though set to guard, they do not guard’, Solomon Pappaiah as ‘though we help them in many ways, they are no protection to us’, and Puliyur Kesigan as ‘they do not come to rescue us when trouble arrives’. Chiriyavar (சிறியவர்) are ‘the small’, people mean in mind, and their bond is pun-kenmai (புன்கேண்மை), ‘paltry friendship’. The conclusion, eydhalin eydhaamai nandru, ‘not attaining is better than attaining’, extends 814: such friendship is not neutral but a net loss. The text is uniform across the three sources, the sandhi-joined edition writing ‘யெய்தலி னெய்தாமை’.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“He is a true friend who does not forsake us in time of need, misfortune, famine, or war, in a king’s court, or at the crematorium (smasana).”
— Chanakya Niti 1.12 (Sri Chanakya Niti-sastra, English translation) [4]
Chanakya defines a true friend by presence in hardship, listing the moments when friends are most needed.
The Kural’s petty friends are the negative of this definition: they fail to be a protection when it counts.
Christianity
“At my first answer no man stood with me, but all men forsook me: I pray God that it may not be laid to their charge.”
— 2 Timothy 4:16 (KJV) [5]
Paul recalls that at his first defence before the court, none of his companions stood by him.
It is the experience the Kural warns of: companions who are no protection in the hour of need.
Islam
“Oh, woe to me! I wish I had not taken that one as a friend.”
— Qur’an 25:28 (Sahih International) [6]
On the Day of Judgement the wrongdoer regrets the companion who led him astray.
This is the Kural’s eydhalin eydhaamai nandru voiced as regret: better never to have had that friend.
Judaism
“To him that is afflicted pity should be shewed from his friend … My brethren have dealt deceitfully as a brook, and as the stream of brooks they pass away”
— Job 6:14-15 (Hebrew Bible, KJV rendering) [5]
Job complains that his friends, like seasonal streams, ran dry just when he needed them.
Job’s friends are the Kural’s pun-kenmai: no protection when it matters.
Buddhism
“(i) he guards the heedless, (ii) he protects the wealth of the heedless, (iii) he becomes a refuge when you are in danger, (iv) when there are commitments he provides you with double the supply needed.”
— Sigalovada Sutta (DN 31), tr. Narada Thera [7]
The Buddha describes the true ‘helpmate’ friend as one who protects and becomes a refuge in danger.
The Kural’s petty friends are the exact opposite: they never become the refuge (ēmam) a friend should be.
Jainism
“The friendship of illustrious men is eminently valuable, and is productive of benefit as timely rain. But the f'riendship of the mean, even in the time of their prosperity, resembles, O lord of the land of clear water! the failure of rain in its due season.”
— Naladiyar, ch. 24 'Improper Friendship', stanza 2 (English tr. in The Indian Antiquary; wisdomlib.org) [8]
The poet contrasts the friendship of noble people, which helps like timely rain, with the friendship of the mean, which fails you like rain that does not come when it is due.
The Kural calls the friendship of small-minded people who will not protect you, even when you have done everything for them, better never gained, and the Naladiyar likewise says the friendship of the mean fails when it is most needed.
Sikhism
“In this world, I have seen love to be false. Whether spouses or friends, all are concerned only with their own happiness. All say, “Mine! mine!”, and attach their consciousness to you with love. But at the very last moment, none shall go along with you. How strange are the ways of the world!”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 536, Raag Devgandhari, Guru Tegh Bahadur (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Tegh Bahadur observes that those who profess love and call us their own are moved by their own comfort, and none of them stands by us when it finally counts.
The Kural says the paltry friendship of small-minded people, who give no protection even when you have done everything for them, is better never gained; Guru Tegh Bahadur describes those who claim us lovingly yet are not there at the decisive moment.
Greek Mythology
When Philoctetes was bitten by a snake on the way to Troy and his wound festered, his Greek comrades marooned him alone on the island of Lemnos, though he carried the bow of Heracles they would later need.
— Sophocles, Philoctetes [10]
The companions he had joined in a common cause abandoned him at the moment he most needed protection.
Philoctetes’ comrades are the Kural’s small-minded friends, who give no protection when it is due.
Philosophy
Cicero has Laelius quote Ennius’s line that a sure friend is discerned in an unsure situation, and observes that many fail this test, deserting friends in adversity.
— Cicero, De Amicitia (On Friendship) 64 [11]
For Cicero, adversity is the touchstone of friendship, and those who desert in hardship were never true friends.
The Kural applies the same touchstone and concludes that friends who fail it are better not had.

Real Life Example

Pompey is killed on arriving in Egypt
28 September 48 BCE · Off the coast of Egypt near Pelusium
After his defeat by Julius Caesar at Pharsalus, Pompey the Great sailed to Egypt, expecting help from the court of the young king Ptolemy XIII, whose father Ptolemy XII had been his ally and client. The king's advisers, including Pothinus and the general Achillas, feared offending the victorious Caesar. Pompey was killed in a small boat as he was brought ashore, before he could set foot in Egypt.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
Pompey had supported the Ptolemaic house in its time of need, but when he came seeking protection, those he had helped gave him none.
The kural says the friendship of small-minded people who will not protect you even after you have done everything for them is better never gained, and Pompey met his end at the hands of the court he had long supported.
