Kural 1027

Family Maintenance · Kudiseyalvakai · குடிசெயல்வகை

அமரகத்து வன்கண்ணர் போலத் தமரகத்தும் ஆற்றுவார் மேற்றே பொறை.
Amaragaththu vankannar polath thamaragaththum aatruvaar metre porai.

Meaning

In battle, the heavy load falls on the brave. In the same way, in a family, the load falls on those who are able to carry it.

Elementary

In a soccer game, the toughest players stand in front when the other team attacks. Valluvar says families are the same: when trouble comes, the strongest, most capable person has to step forward and carry the load.

University

The couplet balances amaragaththu (அமரகத்து, ‘in the battlefield’) against thamaragaththum (தமரகத்தும், ‘among one's own people too’); the vankannar (வன்கண்ணர், ‘the hard-eyed, the fearless’) of war correspond to the aatruvaar (ஆற்றுவார், ‘those who are able’) in the family, and on them falls porai (பொறை, ‘burden, load’). Varadarasanar stresses that though many are born into a family, the burden lies on the capable. Text note: ytamizh.com reads தமரகத்தும் (with the inclusive -um) while thirukkural.net prints தமரகத்து ஆற்றுவார்; the meaning is the same and this edition follows the -um reading, which Pope's ‘Amid his kindred so’ also reflects.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Considering your specific duty as a kṣatriya, you should know that there is no better engagement for you than fighting on religious principles; and so there is no need for hesitation.”
— Bhagavad Gita 2.31 (Bhagavad-gītā As It Is, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda) [3]
Krishna tells Arjuna that the warrior's duty is to stand and fight, not to shrink back.
The Kural uses exactly this warrior who does not shrink as its model for the family member who carries the load.
Christianity
“We then that are strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves.”
— Romans 15:1 (KJV) [4]
Paul says those with strength must carry the burdens of the weaker members of the community.
It is almost the Kural's rule: the burden belongs to those able to bear it.
Islam
“A strong believer is better and is more lovable to Allah than a weak believer, and there is good in everyone, (but) cherish that which gives you benefit (in the Hereafter) and seek help from Allah and do not lose heart”
— Sahih Muslim 2664 (sunnah.com) [5]
The Prophet prizes strength in the believer and tells him not to lose heart.
Like the Kural, it honours the strong who stand firm, though it does not speak of carrying the family.
Judaism
“In a place where there are no men, strive to be a man.”
— Mishnah, Pirkei Avot 2:5 (tr. Joshua Kulp, Mishnah Yomit, via Sefaria) [6]
Hillel teaches that when no one else steps up, you must be the one who takes responsibility.
The Kural likewise says the load falls on whoever has the strength to take it.
Buddhism
“As an elephant in the battlefield withstands arrows shot from bows all around, even so shall I endure abuse.”
— Dhammapada 320 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita, Access to Insight) [7]
The Buddha compares his patient endurance to a war elephant that stands firm under a hail of arrows.
Its battlefield image of steady endurance matches the Kural's fearless warrior who bears the brunt.
Jainism
“Do not get into an uneven road like a weak burden-bearer; for you will repent of it afterwards; Gautama, &c.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 10.33 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
Mahavira warns his disciple not to stumble like a weak porter who leaves the smooth road under his load.
The image of the strong and weak burden-bearer echoes the Kural's point that the load needs someone able to carry it, though the Jain load is the monastic path.
Sikhism
“He alone is known as a spiritual hero, who fights in defense of religion. He may be cut apart, piece by piece, but he never leaves the field of battle.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1105, Raag Maru, Bhagat Kabir (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Bhagat Kabir describes the true soorma (hero) who stands his ground for the right even if cut to pieces. The verse is central to the Sikh ideal of the saint-soldier.
The Kural says that as the burden of battle falls on the fearless, so the burden of a family falls on those able to bear it; Kabir's hero is the one who carries that burden and never leaves the field.
Greek Mythology
When the Trojans break through to the Greek ships, Ajax son of Telamon stands almost alone on the decks, fending off fire and spears to protect the whole army.
— Homer, Iliad, Book 15 [10]
With the other leaders wounded, the burden of defence falls on the one hero strong enough to bear it.
It is the Kural's battlefield image in action: the load rests on the one who can carry it.
Philosophy
“Because in the army the most hazardous services are assigned to the bravest soldiers: a general sends his choicest troops to attack the enemy in a midnight ambuscade, to reconnoitre his line of march, or to drive the hostile garrisons from their strong places.”
— Seneca, Of Providence (De Providentia) 4.8 (tr. Aubrey Stewart) [11]
Seneca explains why God sends hardship to the best of men: as a general gives the hardest tasks to his finest troops, they say not 'he has dealt hardly with me' but 'he has judged well of me'.
Seneca uses the Kural's own battlefield image: the heaviest load falls on those strong enough to bear it.

Real Life Example

The Widow Clicquot takes over her husband's wine firm
1805 · Reims, France
When Francois Clicquot died in 1805, his 27-year-old widow Barbe-Nicole Clicquot Ponsardin persuaded her father-in-law to back her and took control of the family's wine business. She ran it through difficult wartime years, and the house dates her invention of the riddling table, a method of clearing sediment from champagne still in use, to 1816.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A young widow with no business training carried the family firm and became one of the founders of the modern champagne industry.
The kural says that, as on the battlefield the load falls on the fearless, in a family it falls on those able to bear it, and the burden of the Clicquot house fell to Barbe-Nicole.
