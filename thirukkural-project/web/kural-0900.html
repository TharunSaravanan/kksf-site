Kural 900

Not Offending the Great · Periyaaraip Pizhaiyaamai · பெரியாரைப் பிழையாமை

இறந்தமைந்த சார்புடையர் ஆயினும் உய்யார் சிறந்தமைந்த சீரார் செறின்.
Irandhamaindha saarputaiyar aayinum uyyaar sirandhamaindha seeraar serin.

Meaning

Even people with endless support and powerful friends cannot escape if truly great people become angry with them.

Elementary

Having lots of friends to stick up for you is great, but if you do something really wrong to a truly good and respected person, even all those friends can’t save you. Being respectful is better protection than having a big gang.

University

The final couplet sets iRandhu amaindha saarbu (இறந்தமைந்த சார்பு, supports exceeding all measure, from iRa, to go beyond) against siRandhu amaindha seeraar (சிறந்தமைந்த சீரார், those whose glory is excellently formed). The verb uyyaar (உய்யார், they will not survive) echoes kural 896, and seRin (செறின், if angered) echoes 895 and 897, rounding off the chapter. Pope’s explanation, following the traditional commentary, glosses the great here as the noble whose penance is boundless, so the chapter ends where it began, with the two kinds of greatness, worldly and spiritual. Sivayogi Sivakumar reads iRandhamaindha as like the dead, that is renunciants, a markedly different interpretation of the first phrase. Both sources agree on the text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
King Vishvamitra, with his whole army, tries to seize the wish-granting cow of the sage Vasishtha; Vasishtha’s spiritual power destroys his forces and sons, and the king concludes that a warrior’s might is nothing beside a sage’s power.
— Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda (sargas 52-56, Vishvamitra and Vasishtha) [3]
All the king’s armies and resources could not prevail against the anger of a great sage.
This is the Kural’s conclusion: boundless support does not save one who angers the truly great.
Christianity
“There is no king saved by the multitude of an host: a mighty man is not delivered by much strength.”
— Psalm 33:16 (KJV) [4]
The psalmist declares that neither great armies nor personal strength can secure deliverance.
This is nearly the Kural’s own claim that vast support cannot save the offender.
Islam
“The Day when there will not benefit [anyone] wealth or children”
— Qur’an 26:88 (Saheeh International) [5]
Abraham’s prayer describes the Day when the usual supports of wealth and family avail nothing.
As in the Kural, one’s supports, however great, cannot save one in the decisive hour.
Judaism
“Thus saith the LORD, Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom, neither let the mighty man glory in his might, let not the rich man glory in his riches.”
— Jeremiah 9:23 (KJV) [4]
Jeremiah warns against trusting wisdom, might or riches as one’s security.
The Kural similarly teaches that worldly supports offer no safety against the anger of the great.
Buddhism
According to the Dhammapada Commentary, Devadatta, despite the backing of the powerful prince Ajatasattu, plotted repeatedly against the Buddha and was finally swallowed by the earth.
— Dhammapada Commentary, story for verse 17 (trans. E. W. Burlingame, Buddhist Legends) [6]
Royal patronage could not save one who set himself against the Buddha.
As in the Kural, mighty allies do not protect one who offends the truly great.
Jainism
“What is this delusion of yours? You were told earlier, indeed, that there was no escape for any one here, except you two.”
— Hemacandra, Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra, Book 8 (Neminātha-caritra), ch. 11, part 2 (tr. Helen M. Johnson, GOS; wisdomlib.org) [7]
As Dvārakā burns, Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa, the mightiest heroes of their age, haul their parents' chariot to the gate by their own strength, but the fire-god Dvaipāyana tells them no one else can be saved.
The Kural says that even those with boundless support and powerful allies cannot escape once the truly great are angered, and the Yādavas, protected by Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma themselves, still perish.
Sikhism
“The slanderer of the Saint cannot be saved by anyone else.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 280, Raag Gauree (Sukhmani), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Guru Arjan says no outside protector can rescue one who sets himself against the holy, though the same verse adds that the Saint himself may forgive.
The Kural says even those with boundless support and powerful allies will not survive if the truly great are angered, and Gurbani likewise says no other protector can save them.
Greek Mythology
Capaneus, one of the Seven against Thebes, boasts that not even Zeus can stop him scaling the walls, and is struck down by Zeus’s thunderbolt despite the great army around him.
— Aeschylus, Seven Against Thebes (Capaneus) [9]
His boast against a supreme power destroyed him in the midst of a mighty host.
This mirrors the Kural’s point that great allies do not save one who provokes the great.
Philosophy
In a note on Sun Tzu, the commentator Li Ch’uan recalls Fu Chien, who was warned not to despise his enemy but boasted of a million troops, and was soon disastrously routed at the Fei River.
— Sun Tzu, The Art of War 3.18, with Li Ch’uan’s commentary (trans. Lionel Giles) [10]
A ruler confident in vast forces fell after scorning a capable opponent.
This joins the Kural’s last couplet to its first: despising the able cannot be redeemed by numbers.

Real Life Example

Syngman Rhee resigns after the April Revolution
April 1960 · Seoul, South Korea
After government claims that South Korea's March 1960 election had given President Syngman Rhee more than 90 percent of the vote, student protests against electoral fraud spread across the country, culminating in mass demonstrations on 19 April. On 25 April university professors marched in Seoul in support of the students. Under pressure that included a unanimous vote of the National Assembly and the views of the United States government, Rhee resigned on 27 April 1960 and went into exile in Hawaii.
— Sources: [11] [12]
A president who controlled the police, the ruling party and a powerful foreign alliance could not survive once students and their teachers stood together against him.
The kural says that even those with boundless support cannot escape when people of outstanding worth are roused against them, and Rhee's support melted away in weeks.
