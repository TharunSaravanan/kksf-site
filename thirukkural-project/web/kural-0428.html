Kural 428

Knowledge Possession · Arivudaimai · அறிவுடைமை

அஞ்சுவ தஞ்சாமை பேதைமை அஞ்சுவது அஞ்சல் அறிவார் தொழில்.
Anjuva thanjaamai pethaimai anjuvathu Anjal arivaar thozhil.

Meaning

Not being afraid of what you should be afraid of is foolish. Being afraid of what you should be afraid of is the way of the wise.

Elementary

Being brave does not mean being scared of nothing. A person who isn't afraid of touching a hot stove or running across a busy road is not brave, just foolish. Wise people are careful about the things that really are dangerous.

University

The couplet rests on the repeated verb அஞ்சு (to fear) in a tight chiastic pattern. அஞ்சுவது அஞ்சாமை ('not fearing the fearworthy') is பேதைமை (folly), and அஞ்சுவது அஞ்சல் ('fearing the fearworthy') is the practice (தொழில்) of the wise. Fear is treated not as a weakness to overcome but as an emotion to calibrate. Commentators apply it to wrongdoing, the anger of the powerful, and real dangers. This is almost exactly Aristotle's doctrine of courage as a mean: the brave person fears the right things in the right way, and fearlessness toward everything is rashness. The Dhammapada (317) and Proverbs (14:16) make the same point. In the Porutpal context, a king who fears nothing, not even enemies, public anger, or his own misdeeds, is a danger to the state.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“O son of Pṛthā, that understanding by which one knows what ought to be done and what ought not to be done, what is to be feared and what is not to be feared, what is binding and what is liberating, is in the mode of goodness.”
— Bhagavad Gita 18.30 [4]
Krishna defines sattvic buddhi (clear understanding) partly as knowing what is and is not to be feared.
This is a direct parallel: both texts identify correctly sorting what is fearworthy as a function of true understanding.
Christianity
“And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.”
— Matthew 10:28 (KJV) [5]
Jesus redirects his disciples' fear away from human persecutors and toward what is truly to be feared.
Both texts teach that the issue is not whether to fear but what to fear.
Islam
“Only those fear Allah, from among His servants, who have knowledge.”
— Qur'an 35:28 (Sahih International) [6]
The Qur'an connects knowledge ('ilm) directly with rightly placed fear (khashya) of God.
Both texts link knowledge with fearing what should be feared.
Judaism
“A wise man feareth, and departeth from evil; but the fool behaveth overbearingly, and is confident.”
— Proverbs 14:16 (JPS 1917) [7]
Proverbs contrasts the wise man, who is afraid of evil and avoids it, with the fool, who charges ahead full of confidence.
This is almost a paraphrase of the Kural: the wise fear the fearworthy, and the fool fears nothing.
Buddhism
“Those who see something to fear where there is nothing to fear, and see nothing to fear where there is something to fear — upholding false views, they go to the state of woe.”
— Dhammapada 317 [8]
The Buddha condemns misplaced fear in both directions, fearing the harmless and ignoring the truly dangerous, as wrong view.
This is almost the Kural's exact claim, extended to the opposite error as well.
Jainism
“A monk should step carefully in his walk (i.e. in his life), supposing everything to be a snare for him.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 4.7 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [9]
The Jain ideal of vigilance (apramāda) asks the aspirant to move through life as if surrounded by snares: an alert, well-placed caution toward what can bind the soul with karma, not panic.
Valluvar calls it folly not to fear what should be feared; the Jain monk's habit of treating every step as a possible snare is exactly that wise fear of real dangers.
Sikhism
“Without the Fear of God, no one crosses over the world-ocean. This Fear of God adorns the Love of the Lord.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 151, Raag Gauree Gwaarayree, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
In Raag Gauree Guru Nanak teaches that reverent fear of God (bhau) is necessary and that it adorns rather than opposes love.
The Kural calls it folly not to fear what should be feared and wisdom to fear it; Guru Nanak names the one fear that must never be lost, the fear of God.
Greek Mythology
Icarus ignores his father Daedalus's warning not to fly too near the sun. Exulting in flight and without fear, he soars upward until the wax of his wings melts and he falls into the sea.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 8.183-235 [11]
Icarus's lack of fear of a real danger, which his wiser father rightly feared, destroys him.
This illustrates the Kural's first line: not fearing what should be feared is folly.
Philosophy
“The man, then, who faces and who fears the right things and from the right motive, in the right way and at the right time, and who feels confidence under the corresponding conditions, is brave.”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 3.7, 1115b (tr. W. D. Ross) [12]
Aristotle defines courage not as fearlessness but as fearing the right things appropriately. Excess of confidence is rashness.
This is the Kural's thesis stated as moral theory: fearing the fearworthy is wisdom, and not fearing it is folly.

Real Life Example

John Snow has the Broad Street pump handle removed
7-8 September 1854 · Soho, London, England
During a severe cholera outbreak in Soho in 1854, the physician John Snow, who held that cholera spread through contaminated water rather than through bad air as most authorities then believed, analysed the addresses of the dead and interviewed residents, and found that the cases centred on a public water pump in Broad Street. On the evening of 7 September he presented his evidence to the parish Board of Guardians of St James's, and the pump handle was removed the next day. The outbreak was already declining by then, but Snow's investigation became a founding example of modern epidemiology.
— Sources: [13] [14] [15]
Snow directed fear at the real danger, the water people trusted, at a time when most experts feared the wrong cause.
The kural says it is folly not to fear what ought to be feared and wisdom to fear it, and Snow rightly feared the contaminated water that his neighbours and many authorities did not.
