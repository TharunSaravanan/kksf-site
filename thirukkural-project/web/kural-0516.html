Kural 516

Selection and Employment · Therindhu Vinaiyaadal · தெரிந்து வினையாடல்

செய்வானை நாடி வினைநாடிக் காலத்தோடு எய்த உணர்ந்து செயல்.
Seyvaanai naadi vinainaadik kaalaththodu Eydha unarndhu seyal.

Meaning

First judge the person who will do the work, then judge the work itself, and then act when the time is right for both.

Elementary

To bake a cake you need the right baker, the right recipe, and the right moment, so it’s ready when the party starts. Get all three right and it works.

University

Kural 516 sets out a three-part planning method: examine the doer (செய்வானை நாடி), examine the deed (வினைநாடி), and fit both to the proper time (காலத்தோடு எய்த உணர்ந்து). Pappaiah reads it as bringing agent and task together and then matching the pair to the moment. The couplet also draws on the earlier chapters on strength (48), time (49) and place (50). Plato’s specialisation principle, that things go best when each person does the one thing natural to him and does it at the right time, runs remarkably parallel.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“That gift which is given out of duty, at the proper time and place, to a worthy person, and without expectation of return, is considered to be charity in the mode of goodness.”
— Bhagavad Gita 17.20 (Prabhupada, As It Is) [3]
Krishna says a good action needs the right recipient, the right time and the right place.
The Gita’s check of person, time and place mirrors the Kural’s check of doer, deed and time.
Christianity
“For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it?”
— Luke 14:28 (KJV) [4]
Jesus says a sensible builder works out beforehand whether he has what it takes to finish the job.
Assess the task and your means before you start, as the Kural advises.
Islam
“...and consult them in the matter. And when you have decided, then rely upon Allah.”
— Qur’an 3:159 (Sahih International) [5]
The Prophet is told to consult his people, reach a considered decision, and then act with trust in God.
Deliberate first, then act decisively, is the procedure the Kural lays out.
Judaism
“To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven:”
— Ecclesiastes 3:1 (JPS 1917) [6]
Ecclesiastes teaches that every action has its proper time.
This matches the Kural’s third element, fitting the work to the right moment.
Buddhism
“...a sense of himself, a sense of moderation, a sense of time, a sense of social gatherings, & a sense of distinctions among individuals.”
— Anguttara Nikaya 7.64 (Thanissaro) [7]
The Buddha lists the qualities of a worthy person, among them knowing oneself, knowing the right time, and knowing how individuals differ.
A sense of time plus a sense of differences between people covers the Kural’s timing and choice of person.
Jainism
“At the right time a monk should sally forth, and he should return at the right time; avoiding to do anything out of time, he should do what is appropriate for each period of the day.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 1.31 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
Jain monastic discipline insists that each action be fitted to its proper time.
The kural's third step is to act when doer and deed fit the right time; the Jain rule makes timeliness a matter of discipline, though it does not address sizing up doer and deed.
Sikhism
“One who reflects upon his allotted span of life, becomes the slave of God.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 84, Siree Raag, Siree Raag Ki Vaar (salok), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Nanak praises the person who thinks about the time he has been given and uses it for its true purpose.
The Kural asks that the doer and the deed be weighed and then matched to the right time; Guru Nanak likewise asks that one's time be considered before it is spent.
Greek Mythology
Kairos, the Greeks’ personification of the opportune moment, was shown in Lysippus’ statue with a lock of hair in front and bald behind: the moment can be seized as it comes but not once it has passed.
— Posidippus, epigram on Lysippus’ Kairos (Greek Anthology 16.275) [10]
The right moment has to be recognised and grasped before it slips away.
This reflects the Kural’s demand to fit the work to the proper time.
Philosophy
“...we must infer that all things are produced more plentifully and easily and of a better quality when one man does one thing which is natural to him and does it at the right time, and leaves other things.”
— Plato, Republic II, 370c (Jowett) [11]
Socrates argues that work goes best when each person does the task suited to his nature, at the right moment.
The right person, the right task and the right time is almost exactly the Kural’s formula.

Real Life Example

Ferdinand and Isabella back Columbus after the fall of Granada
January-April 1492 · Granada and Santa Fe, Spain
Christopher Columbus sought support from Ferdinand and Isabella from 1486, and they had a commission examine his proposal while they were fighting the war against Granada. After at least two rejections, he obtained royal support in January 1492, soon after Granada surrendered on 2 January. In April 1492 he was granted the title Admiral of the Ocean Sea, and he sailed in August.
— Sources: [12] [13]
The monarchs examined both the man and the plan, and committed to it only when their war was over and resources were free.
The kural says to size up the doer, then the deed, and then act when both fit the right time, and the monarchs' long-delayed decision followed that order.
