Kural 703

The Knowledge of Signals · Kurippu Arithal · குறிப்பறிதல்

குறிப்பிற் குறிப்புணர் வாரை உறுப்பினுள் யாது கொடுத்தும் கொளல்.
Kurippir kurippunar vaarai uruppinul Yaadhu koduththum kolal.

Meaning

People who can read what others mean from the signs on their face are worth getting as helpers, whatever you have to give to win them.

Elementary

If a football team finds a player who always knows where a teammate will pass before the ball is kicked, the coach will do almost anything to keep that player. Valluvar says a leader should treat people who read signals the same way.

University

The phrase kurippir kurippu unarvaar is the chapter’s technical formula, repeated in 705: ‘those who perceive the kurippu by means of kurippu’. Parimelazhagar takes the first kurippu as the outward signs (of face and eye) and the second as the inner intention they betray; Pope and Drew follow a further gloss in which the reader uses the indications of his own mind as a key to another’s. Uruppu here means the constituent ‘limbs’ of a kingdom (arasu uruppu: army, people, treasury, ministers, allies, forts, as set out in ch. 39), so yaadhu koduththum kolal is ‘secure them by giving whichever of these is asked’. Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah both read it as ‘giving anything among the country’s possessions’. The advice sits in a long Indian tradition of the king paying any price for an able minister. Uruppu will recur in 704 and 705 in the bodily sense (limbs, sense-organs), a deliberate word-play.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
On first meeting Hanuman, Rama tells Lakshmana that Hanuman’s speech shows deep learning and discernment, and that a king with such a messenger will have every undertaking succeed.
— Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda, Sarga 3 [3]
Rama recognises at once that Hanuman reads situations and people with rare skill and values him accordingly.
Like the Kural, the Ramayana treats such a perceptive helper as worth securing at any cost.
Christianity
“Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water; but a man of understanding will draw it out.”
— Proverbs 20:5 (KJV) [4]
A person’s real purpose lies deep and hidden, and only a discerning person can bring it to the surface.
The ‘man of understanding’ who draws out hidden purposes is the helper the Kural says to acquire at any price.
Islam
“And the king said, ‘Bring him to me; I will appoint him exclusively for myself.’ And when he spoke to him, he said, ‘Indeed, you are today established [in position] and trusted.’”
— Qur’an 12:54 (Saheeh International) [5]
After Joseph reads the meaning hidden in the king’s dream, the king takes him out of prison and appoints him to his personal service.
The king does what the Kural advises: when he finds a man who reads hidden meanings, he secures him at once and at great cost.
Judaism
The queen tells Belshazzar about Daniel, a man of excellent spirit who explains riddles and dissolves doubts; the king summons him, offers purple, a gold chain and third rank in the kingdom, and gives them to him.
— Daniel 5:10-16, 5:29 (JPS 1917) [6]
A king faced with an unreadable sign pays the highest price to bring in the one man who can read it.
Belshazzar’s rewards illustrate ‘give whatever it takes’ for a reader of signs.
Buddhism
In the Maha-Ummagga Jataka, King Vedeha hears of the boy Mahosadha’s uncanny discernment, brings him to court despite his rivals, and makes him his chief adviser.
— Maha-Ummagga Jataka (No. 546) [7]
The Bodhisatta as Mahosadha repeatedly reads the hidden intentions of rivals and enemy kings and saves the kingdom.
The story is the Kural’s advice in narrative form: a king who wins such a discerner at any cost is saved by him.
Jainism
“An intelligent man who has learned (the sacred texts) takes his duties upon himself, and he becomes renowned in the world; as the earth is the dwelling of all beings, so he will be a dwelling of all duties.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 1.45 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The verse follows the praise of the pupil who guesses his teacher's thoughts and needs no express directions (1.43–44), and calls such a person a support on which everything rests.
The Kural says such perceptive people should be won at any price, and the Jain text says the same kind of person becomes indispensable, a 'dwelling of all duties'.
Sikhism
“Surrender body, mind, wealth, and everything to the Guru; obey the Order of His Will, and you will find Him.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 918, Raag Ramkali, Anand Sahib, Guru Amar Das (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Anand Sahib, Guru Amar Das tells the seeker to hand over body, mind and wealth to the Guru, who knows the inner state, and to live by his hukam.
The Kural says those who read inner intentions should be won whatever part of one's wealth it costs; Anand Sahib says to give everything to the Guru who sees within.
Greek Mythology
Agamemnon praises Nestor’s counsel and wishes he had ten such counsellors among the Achaeans, saying Troy would then soon fall.
— Homer, Iliad 2 (trans. Samuel Butler) [10]
The commander values one wise adviser above many warriors.
Agamemnon’s wish is the Greek version of ‘acquire such men whatever it costs’.
Philosophy
Machiavelli says the first judgement of a ruler’s intelligence is the quality of those around him, and distinguishes minds that understand for themselves, minds that grasp what others understand, and useless minds.
— Machiavelli, The Prince, ch. 22 (trans. W. K. Marriott) [11]
For Machiavelli, choosing perceptive ministers is itself the proof of a prince’s wisdom.
Both Machiavelli and Valluvar make securing perceptive servants a top royal priority.

Real Life Example

Churchill orders that the Bletchley codebreakers get all they want
21-22 October 1941 · Bletchley Park and London, England, United Kingdom
On 21 October 1941 four codebreakers working on the German Enigma ciphers at Bletchley Park, Alan Turing, Gordon Welchman, Hugh Alexander and Stuart Milner-Barry, bypassed their managers and wrote directly to Prime Minister Winston Churchill, saying their work was being held up for lack of a few dozen clerks, typists and naval staff. Churchill at once sent a handwritten minute to General Ismay, bearing an Action This Day label, that read: Make sure they have all they want on extreme priority and report to me that this has been done. On 18 November Ismay reported that the staff were being supplied very rapidly.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Churchill understood that the people who could read the enemy's hidden intentions were worth any price, and he backed them at once.
The kural says those who can read inner intentions from outward signs must be secured whatever must be given to win them, which is exactly the priority Churchill gave the codebreakers.
