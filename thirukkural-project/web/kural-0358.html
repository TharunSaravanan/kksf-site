Kural 358

Knowledge of the True · Meyyunarthal · மெய்யுணர்தல்

பிறப்பென்னும் பேதைமை நீங்கச் சிறப்பென்னும் செம்பொருள் காண்பது அறிவு.
Pirappennum pedhaimai neengach sirappennum Semporul kaanpadhu arivu.

Meaning

True wisdom is seeing the perfect truth, the highest good, so that the foolishness that causes us to be born again and again is taken away.

Elementary

Imagine being stuck on a merry-go-round that never stops because you forgot there’s an exit. The moment you notice the exit sign, you can step off. This kural says being born again and again is like forgetting the exit, and seeing what is truly real is the exit sign.

University

The couplet makes a bold equation: pirappu ennum pedhaimai, ‘the folly called birth’. Birth is not only caused by ignorance (as in 351); it is itself named a form of ignorance. Against it stands sirappu ennum semporul, ‘the perfect Truth called excellence’, which commentators read as the Supreme Being or the state of release (Mu. Varadarasanar glosses sirappu as mukti). Semporul (‘the fine, perfect substance’) is the same term used in 359 for the support of all things. Buddhism offers the closest structural parallel: in dependent origination, ignorance (avijja) is the first condition and rebirth (jati) the eleventh, so the fading of ignorance ends birth. Hindu parallels such as Gita 4.9 make knowledge of the divine the reason one ‘does not take birth again’, while John’s ‘the truth shall make you free’ expresses liberation through knowing within a linear rather than cyclical cosmology.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“One who knows the transcendental nature of My appearance and activities does not, upon leaving the body, take his birth again in this material world, but attains My eternal abode, O Arjuna.”
— Bhagavad Gita 4.9 [3]
Krishna says that truly knowing the divine reality ends rebirth: at death such a knower goes to the eternal abode instead.
Just as the Kural says, seeing the supreme Truth (semporul) removes the ‘folly called birth’.
Christianity
“And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.”
— John 8:32 (KJV) [4]
Jesus teaches that knowing the truth frees a person from bondage (in context, the bondage of sin).
Same core claim as the Kural: seeing the Truth is what liberates.
Islam
“That is because Allāh is the True Reality, and that which they call upon other than Him is falsehood, and because Allāh is the Most High, the Grand.”
— Qur’an 22:62 (Sahih International) [5]
One of God’s names is al-Haqq, ‘the Truth’ or ‘the True Reality’. Everything else people cling to as ultimate is, by comparison, falsehood.
The Kural’s ‘semporul’ (the perfect Truth) corresponds closely to al-Haqq as the one Reality to be recognized.
Judaism
“But let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and knoweth me, that I am the LORD which exercise lovingkindness, judgment, and righteousness, in the earth.”
— Jeremiah 9:24 (KJV) [4]
Through Jeremiah, God says that the only thing worth boasting of is not wisdom, strength or riches but understanding and knowing Him (da’at).
Both texts set knowledge of the ultimate Truth above every other kind of knowing.
Buddhism
“Ignorance is a requirement for choices. … Continued existence is a requirement for rebirth. … When ignorance fades away and ceases no residue left behind, choices cease. … When rebirth ceases, old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress cease.”
— Samyutta Nikaya 12.1 (trans. Bhikkhu Sujato) [6]
Dependent origination traces rebirth back, link by link, to ignorance; when ignorance ends completely, rebirth and all its suffering end too.
This is the Kural’s claim spelled out step by step: birth is rooted in ignorance, and removing ignorance removes birth.
Jainism
“Love and hatred are caused by Karman, and they say that Karman has its origin in delusion; Karman is the root of birth and death, and birth and death they call misery.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 32.7 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895, p. 185) [7]
The Uttarādhyayana traces birth and death to karma and karma to delusion, and then calls birth and death themselves misery.
Valluvar names 'the folly called birth'. The Jain verse also roots birth in delusion and treats birth as the very affliction that true insight removes.
Sikhism
“One whom the Creator Lord unites with Himself, is not consigned to reincarnation.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 433, Raag Aasaa (Patti), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
In the Patti (acrostic) of Raag Aasaa, Guru Nanak says that whoever the Creator unites with Himself is not born again.
The Kural says that seeing the perfect Truth removes the folly called birth; Guru Nanak says that union with the Creator likewise ends rebirth.
Greek Mythology
In a Greek legend, King Midas captured the satyr Silenus and asked what is best for mankind; Silenus answered that it is best never to be born, and next best to die soon. Sophocles’ chorus in Oedipus at Colonus echoes this: not to be born is best of all.
— Sophocles, Oedipus at Colonus 1224–1227; Silenus legend [9]
Greek tragic wisdom saw human birth itself as a misfortune.
Shares the Kural’s startling view of birth as something to be escaped, but offers no path of knowledge out of it.
Philosophy
Plotinus urges the soul to withdraw from the world of images and ‘flee to the beloved Fatherland’, turning inward to behold the One, the source of all being, from which the soul came and to which it can return.
— Plotinus, Enneads I.6.8–9 and VI.9 [10]
For Plotinus, the soul’s descent into bodies is a kind of forgetting, and contemplative vision of the One reverses it.
Like the Kural, embodiment is tied to ignorance, and seeing the supreme Reality is the way home.

Real Life Example

Pasteur's swan-neck flasks refute spontaneous generation
1859-1862 · Paris, France
From 1859 Louis Pasteur set out to settle whether living microbes could arise on their own from nonliving matter, a belief defended by the naturalist Felix Pouchet. Pasteur boiled broth in flasks whose long, curved necks let in air but trapped dust; the broth stayed clear, but when the neck was broken off it soon swarmed with microbes. In an 1862 paper he argued that the microbes came from germs in the air, not from spontaneous generation.
— Sources: [11] [12]
A careful look at what was really happening removed a long-standing false belief about how life is born and opened the way to germ theory.
The kural calls birth a folly that true seeing removes; Pasteur's experiments removed a folly about birth itself, the belief that life springs spontaneously from decay, by seeing the true cause.
