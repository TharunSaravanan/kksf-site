Kural 608

Unsluggishness · Madiyinmai · மடியின்மை

மடிமை குடிமைக்கண் தங்கின்தன் ஒன்னார்க்கு அடிமை புகுத்தி விடும்.
Madimai kudimaikkan thangindhan onnaarkku Adimai pugutthi vidum.

Meaning

If laziness settles into someone from a respected family, it will end up making them a slave to their own enemies.

Elementary

A castle with strong walls is safe only while its guards stay awake. If the guards get lazy and fall asleep, the enemy walks right in. Being from a great family won’t protect you if laziness moves in.

University

The couplet depends on a rhyme chain: மடிமை (sloth), குடிமை (nobility of birth), அடிமை (slavery). The sound itself follows the slide from high birth through sloth into bondage. தங்கின், ‘if it takes up residence’, presents sloth as a lodger that gradually takes over the house. ஒன்னார், ‘those who are not one with you’, i.e. enemies, and புகுத்திவிடும், ‘will thrust one into’, give the outcome. Pope’s rendering (‘Bondsmen to them that hate them shall they be’) and the Drew-Lazarus gloss both take the subject as a king of high birth, which fits the placement of the chapter in the அரசியல் (polity) section. Parimelazhagar notes that the sloth of a ruler becomes his enemies’ opportunity. The older text shows heavy sandhi (குடிமைக்கட் டங்கிற்றன் னொன்னார்க் / கடிமை) without any change in wording.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“If a king is energetic, his subjects will be equally energetic. If he is reckless, they will not only be reckless likewise, but also eat into his works. Besides, a reckless king will easily fall into the hands of his enemies. Hence the king shall ever be wakeful.”
— Kauṭilya, Arthaśāstra 1.19 (trans. R. Shamasastry) [3]
Kautilya warns that a heedless king corrupts his subjects and ‘easily falls into the hands of his enemies’, so he must always stay alert.
‘Fall into the hands of his enemies’ is almost word for word the Kural’s ஒன்னார்க்கு அடிமை புகுத்திவிடும்.
Christianity
“But know this, that if the goodman of the house had known in what watch the thief would come, he would have watched, and would not have suffered his house to be broken up.”
— Matthew 24:43 (KJV) [4]
Jesus warns that a householder who fails to keep watch lets the thief break in, using it as a picture of spiritual vigilance.
An unwatchful master whose house is broken into is a close image of the noble whose sloth hands him to his enemies.
Islam
“If you do not go forth, He will punish you with a painful punishment and will replace you with another people, and you will not harm Him at all.”
— Quran 9:38-39 (Sahih International) [5]
Following the rebuke of those who ‘adhere heavily to the earth’ (9:38), God warns that a people who will not stir will be replaced by others.
A people displaced because they clung to ease parallels the noble house handed over to its enemies because of sloth.
Judaism
“The hand of the diligent shall bear rule; But the slothful shall be under tribute.”
— Proverbs 12:24 (JPS 1917, via Sefaria) [6]
The proverb states a political law: the diligent end up ruling, while the lazy end up paying tribute or doing forced labour for others.
‘Under tribute’ (lamas, forced labour) is exactly the Kural’s அடிமை: sloth turns the free into subjects.
Buddhism
The Dhammapada opens its chapter on heedfulness by teaching that heedfulness is the path to the Deathless and heedlessness the path to death: the heedful do not die, while the heedless are as if already dead.
— Dhammapada 21 [7]
The Buddha treats slack heedlessness as a condition that hands a person over to death, the ultimate enemy.
Heedlessness surrendering a person to death is the spiritual form of sloth surrendering the noble to his foes.
Jainism
“Self is the one invincible foe, (together with the four) cardinal passions, (viz. anger, pride, deceit, and greed, they are five) and the (five) senses (make ten). These (foes), O great sage, I have regularly vanquished.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 23.38 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
Gautama tells Kesi that the real enemies are the unconquered self, the passions and the senses, and only someone who conquers them is free; the rest are held in their bondage.
Valluvar warns that sloth turns the noble-born into slaves of their enemies; in Jainism, the person who does not exert himself stays bound to his inner enemies, the passions.
Sikhism
“Within this body dwell the five thieves: sexual desire, anger, greed, emotional attachment and egotism. They plunder the Nectar, but the self-willed manmukh does not realize it; no one hears his complaint.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 600, Raag Sorath, Guru Amar Das (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Amar Das warns that the five thieves living inside a person rob him while he is unaware, and that no one answers his cry.
The Kural says laziness in one of noble birth makes him a slave to his enemies; Guru Amar Das likewise shows the unwatchful person plundered by enemies, though they are inner vices, not outside foes.
Greek Mythology
At the very end of Herodotus’ Histories, Cyrus rejects a proposal that the Persians move to richer, softer lands, warning that soft lands breed soft men. The Persians agree and choose to rule a harsh land rather than sow fertile plains as slaves to others.
— Herodotus, Histories 9.122 (trans. A. D. Godley) [10]
The founding king of Persia teaches that ease and comfort turn rulers into subjects.
‘Slaves to others’ as the fruit of softness is the Kural’s அடிமை reached through மடிமை.
Philosophy
In chapter 14 of The Prince, Machiavelli observes that princes who have thought more of ease than of arms have lost their states, and that a prince who neglects the art of war is despised and easily undone.
— Niccolò Machiavelli, The Prince, ch. 14 (trans. W. K. Marriott) [11]
Machiavelli argues that a ruler’s taste for comfort is the most common way states are lost to rivals.
Machiavelli’s ease-loving prince who loses his state is Valluvar’s noble who, through sloth, becomes his enemies’ subject.

Real Life Example

King Charles IV of Spain gives up his crown to Napoleon at Bayonne
1792-1808 · Madrid and Aranjuez, Spain, and Bayonne, France
Charles IV, king of Spain from 1788, lacked leadership qualities and from 1792 left the government largely to the royal favourite Manuel de Godoy, while he preferred hunting to governing. In March 1808 a riot at Aranjuez forced him to abdicate in favour of his son Ferdinand. Napoleon then summoned the royal family to Bayonne, where both father and son gave up the crown, which Napoleon gave to his brother Joseph, and Charles spent his remaining years in exile under French supervision.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
The heir to one of Europe's oldest crowns ended as a pensioner of the foreign ruler whose armies had occupied his country.
The kural warns that if sloth settles in a person of noble birth it will make him a subject of his enemies, and Charles IV's neglect of rule ended with his kingdom in Napoleon's hands and himself in Napoleon's custody.
