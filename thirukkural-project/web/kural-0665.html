Kural 665

Power in Execution · Vinaithitpam · வினைத்திட்பம்

வீறெய்தி மாண்டார் வினைத்திட்பம் வேந்தன்கண் ஊறெய்தி உள்ளப் படும்.
VeeReydhi maaNdaar vinaiththitpam vaendhankaN OoReydhi uLLap padum.

Meaning

The firm action of people who have become great reaches even the king, and so everyone comes to respect it.

Elementary

When a student works hard and finishes a great project, the teacher notices, then the principal hears about it, and soon the whole school knows. Steady, finished work travels up to the people in charge, and everyone respects it.

University

Vīṟu is distinction or excellence and māṇṭār those who are great or eminent; the verb māḷ can also mean ‘to die’, and Sivayogi Sivakumar’s gloss on ytamizh.com reads the phrase as ‘those who died valiantly’, but the mainstream commentators (Parimelazhagar, Mu. Varadarasanar, Solomon Pappaiah, Kalaignar, Puliyur Kesikan) take māṇṭār as ‘the eminent’. Parimelazhagar specifies that their eminence comes from excellence of counsel (eṇṇam), and that their firmness in execution, by ‘reaching’ the king (vēntaṉkaṇ ūṟu eyti, where ūṟu means contact or effect, not harm as in 662), is then esteemed by everyone (uḷḷappaṭum). The couplet thus explains how a minister’s firmness is rewarded: it becomes visible through the ruler’s regard. Pope’s ‘With king acceptance finds and fame through all the state’ follows the same reading. The three sources agree on the text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues.”
— Bhagavad Gita 3.21 (Prabhupada, Bhagavad-gita As It Is) [4]
Krishna notes that the deeds of eminent people become the standard everyone else follows.
As in the Kural, the firm action of the great becomes widely known and honoured.
Christianity
“His lord said unto him, Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things...”
— Matthew 25:21 (KJV) [5]
In the parable of the talents, the servant who carried his charge through faithfully is praised and promoted by his master.
Diligent execution reaches the lord’s notice and wins honour, as the Kural says.
Islam
“And the king said, "Bring him to me; I will appoint him exclusively for myself." And when he spoke to him, he said, "Indeed, you are today established [in position] and trusted.”
— Quran 12:54 (Sahih International) [6]
Having seen Joseph’s wisdom and integrity, the king of Egypt takes him into his own service and gives him high standing.
Joseph’s proven worth ‘reaches the king’ and brings him esteem, as the Kural describes.
Judaism
“See a man skilled at his work— He shall attend upon kings; He shall not attend upon the lowly.”
— Proverbs 22:29 (JPS Tanakh, Gender-Sensitive Edition, via Sefaria) [7]
Proverbs promises that someone who excels at his work will end up serving before kings.
Almost the Kural’s exact claim: excellence in action reaches the king.
Buddhism
“Ever grows the glory of him who is energetic, mindful and pure in conduct, discerning and self-controlled, righteous and heedful.”
— Dhammapada 24 (trans. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [8]
The Dhammapada says the reputation of an energetic, disciplined person keeps growing.
Both texts say sustained, disciplined action brings growing renown.
Jainism
“An intelligent man who has learned (the sacred texts) takes his duties upon himself, and he becomes renowned in the world; as the earth is the dwelling of all beings, so he will be a dwelling of all duties.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 1.45 (cf. 1.48) (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [9]
The Uttaradhyayana's lecture on discipline says the pupil who reliably shoulders his duties becomes renowned, and goes on to say he will be honoured by gods, Gandharvas and men.
Just as the kural says the firmness of eminent men reaches the king and wins general esteem, the Jain text makes steadfast discharge of duty the source of a person's renown.
Sikhism
“Nanak proclaimed Lehna's succession - he earned it.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 966, Raag Raamkalee (Ramkali ki Vaar), Satta and Balwand (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
The bards Satta and Balwand record that Guru Nanak raised his devoted disciple Lehna (Guru Angad) to the Guruship because of his proven, steadfast service.
The Kural says the firmness in action of the distinguished reaches the king and wins everyone's esteem; Lehna's steadfast service reached Guru Nanak himself and won him the highest seat.
Greek Mythology
“Then the king knew that he must be the valiant offspring of a god, so he kept him in Lycia, gave him his daughter in marriage, and made him of equal honour in the kingdom with himself...”
— Homer, Iliad 6 (trans. Samuel Butler) [11]
Sent by the Lycian king on deadly tasks, Bellerophon completes each one (the Chimaera, the Solymi, the Amazons, an ambush), and the king finally honours him instead.
Bellerophon’s firmness in carrying out every task reaches the king and wins him the highest honour.
Philosophy
“...honour is the prize of virtue, and it is to the good that it is rendered.”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV.3 (trans. W. D. Ross) [12]
Aristotle argues that honour is the proper reward that follows real excellence.
The Kural likewise has esteem follow proven excellence in action.

Real Life Example

Engineer M. Visvesvaraya becomes Diwan of Mysore and later receives the Bharat Ratna
1909-1955 · Mysore State (now Karnataka), India
Mokshagundam Visvesvaraya joined the Mysore State service as Chief Engineer in 1909 and led work on the Krishnaraja Sagara dam, using automatic sluice gates of his own design; the dam created what was then one of the largest reservoirs in Asia. In 1912 the Maharaja's government made him Diwan of Mysore, a post he held until 1918, during which the dam project was given top priority. In 1955 he received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour, and his birthday, 15 September, is observed as Engineers' Day in India.
— Sources: [13] [14] [15]
Visvesvaraya's record of carrying great works through to completion brought him first the ruler's trust and then the esteem of the whole nation.
The kural says the firmness in action of the eminent reaches the king and so comes to be held in regard by all, and Visvesvaraya's proven firmness as an engineer won him the Maharaja's highest office and, later, national honour.
