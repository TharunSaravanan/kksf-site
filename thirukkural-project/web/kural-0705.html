Kural 705

The Knowledge of Signals · Kurippu Arithal · குறிப்பறிதல்

குறிப்பிற் குறிப்புணரா வாயின் உறுப்பினுள் என்ன பயத்தவோ கண்.
Kurippir kurippunaraa vaayin uruppinul Enna payaththavo kan.

Meaning

If your eyes cannot read what someone means from the signs they give, what use are your eyes among all the parts of your body?

Elementary

What good is a flashlight if you never switch it on? Your eyes are for more than seeing colours and shapes. They are meant to notice when someone is sad, worried or happy. If they never notice that, it is as if the flashlight stays off.

University

The couplet repeats 703’s formula kurippir kurippu unar- in the negative (unaraa vaayin, ‘if they do not perceive’) and turns it on the eye itself. Uruppinul (‘among the organs’) now plainly means the body’s members, and enna payaththavo is a rhetorical ‘of what profit are they?’ Parimelazhagar explains that the eyes are the foremost of the sense-organs, and their special excellence is to catch another’s mind through the signs of face and eye; if they cannot do that they are no better than the other limbs. Drew and Pope render ‘if they cannot by their own indications dive those of another’, reflecting the reading that the eye both shows and reads kurippu. The rhetorical question anticipates 710, where the eye becomes the only measuring rod. The sandhi text reads குறிப்புணரா வாயி னுறுப்பினு / ளென்ன பயத்தவோ கண்.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Krishna tells Arjuna that he cannot see Krishna’s true form with his own eyes and grants him a divine eye to see it.
— Bhagavad Gita 11.8 [3]
Ordinary sight is shown to be inadequate for seeing what really matters until it is given a deeper power.
Both texts say that eyes which only see surfaces fall short of their true purpose.
Christianity
“Why reason ye, because ye have no bread? perceive ye not yet, neither understand? have ye your heart yet hardened? Having eyes, see ye not? and having ears, hear ye not?”
— Mark 8:17-18 (KJV) [4]
Jesus warns his disciples about the ‘leaven’ of the Pharisees, and they take it literally; he rebukes them for missing his meaning.
Jesus asks the Kural’s own question: what are eyes for if they miss the meaning behind the sign?
Islam
“For indeed, it is not eyes that are blinded, but blinded are the hearts which are within the breasts.”
— Qur’an 22:46 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur’an says the real failure of sight lies in the heart’s inability to understand what the eyes see.
As in the Kural, eyes that take in signs without understanding them are no better than blind.
Judaism
“Hear now this, O foolish people, and without understanding, that have eyes, and see not, that have ears, and hear not”
— Jeremiah 5:21 (JPS 1917) [6]
The prophet rebukes a people who have every faculty of perception yet grasp nothing of what is happening.
Jeremiah and Valluvar both mock eyes that see without understanding.
Buddhism
Shortly before his passing, the Buddha gives Ananda a broad hint that he could remain for an aeon if asked, but Ananda, his mind distracted, fails to grasp the sign and does not ask.
— Digha Nikaya 16, Mahaparinibbana Sutta [7]
Even the Buddha’s closest attendant misses a plain signal, and the chance is lost.
The episode shows the Kural’s point: failing to read an intended signal makes one’s eyes and ears useless at the moment they matter most.
Jainism
“He should not, in every case, wait for the express command (of the teacher) like an unbroken horse for the whip (of the rider), but like a broken horse which sees the whip (of the rider) he should commit no evil act.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 1.12 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The trained horse responds to the sight of the whip and does not need the blow; the good disciple likewise acts on what he sees rather than waiting to be told outright.
The verse turns on the eye's job of reading a sign, which is the faculty the Kural says is worthless if it cannot read intentions.
Sikhism
“Do not call them blind, who have no eyes in their face. They alone are blind, O Nanak, who wander away from their Lord and Master.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 954, Raag Ramkali, Ramkali ki Vaar (salok), Guru Angad (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Angad redefines blindness: having eyes in the face counts for nothing if they do not see what truly matters.
The Kural asks what use the eyes are among the body's organs if they cannot read intention; Guru Angad likewise says eyes that do not perceive leave a person blind.
Greek Mythology
Oedipus taunts the blind prophet Tiresias as blind in ears, mind and eyes, while Tiresias answers that Oedipus has sight yet cannot see the evil he is in.
— Sophocles, Oedipus Tyrannus (c. lines 366-413) [10]
The man with working eyes misses every sign, while the blind seer understands everything.
Sophocles dramatises the Kural’s question: eyes that cannot read the signs are worthless.
Philosophy
Heraclitus says that eyes and ears are poor witnesses for people whose souls do not understand their language.
— Heraclitus, fragment DK B107 (Burnet fr. 4) [11]
The senses deliver signs, but only an understanding mind can read them.
Heraclitus states the principle behind the Kural: sight without understanding gives no knowledge.

Real Life Example

The Opana radar sighting before Pearl Harbor is dismissed
7 December 1941 · Opana Point and Fort Shafter, Oahu, Hawaii, United States
At 7:02 a.m. on 7 December 1941, Privates Joseph Lockard and George Elliott, practising with the radar set at Opana on Oahu's northern tip, detected an unusually large formation of aircraft approaching from the north and telephoned the information center at Fort Shafter. Lieutenant Kermit Tyler, who was there for training, reasoned that it was an expected flight of American B-17 bombers and told them not to worry about it. The operators tracked the contact until about 7:40, and the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor began at 7:55 a.m.
— Sources: [12] [13]
The radar saw the attack coming, but no one interpreted what it showed, and the warning was wasted.
The kural asks what use eyes are if they cannot read the meaning behind what they see, and at Opana the new electronic eye saw everything yet the sign went unread.
