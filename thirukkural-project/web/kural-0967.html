Kural 967

Honour · Maanam · மானம்

ஒட்டார்பின் சென்றொருவன் வாழ்தலின் அந்நிலையே கெட்டான் எனப்படுதல் நன்று.
Ottaarpin sendroruvan vaazhdhalin annilaiye Kettaan enappadudhal nandru.

Meaning

It is better to be called a person who has lost everything than to live by running after people who do not respect you.

Elementary

Imagine choosing between staying poor but proud, or getting treats every day by doing whatever an unkind person tells you. This kural says it is better to lose the treats and keep your self-respect.

University

Ottaar (ஒட்டார்) are those who do not 'stick' to one: people who do not regard or accept one, or enemies. Living by following them (pin sendru vaazhdhal) is set against being called kettaan (கெட்டான், 'he is ruined', or 'he perished'). The phrase annilaiye (அந்நிலையே), 'in that very state', is read by Mu. Varadarasanar as remaining in one's own condition without stooping, while Puliyur Kesikan, Solomon Pappaiah and Kalaignar take kettaan more starkly as 'died'. Pope's English, quoted on ytamizh.com, follows the stronger reading: 'Better 'twere said, He's perished! than to gain / The means to live, following in foeman's train.' The couplet expresses a priority of honour over comfort, not an instruction to seek death. Text identical on both sources.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“People will always speak of your infamy, and for a respectable person, dishonor is worse than death.”
— Bhagavad Gita 2.34 (tr. A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada; vedabase.io) [3]
Krishna tells Arjuna that for a person of standing, disgrace is worse than dying.
The Kural says the same in its own way: better to be called ruined than to live in disgrace.
Christianity
“But I have used none of these things: neither have I written these things, that it should be so done unto me: for it were better for me to die, than that any man should make my glorying void.”
— 1 Corinthians 9:15 (KJV) [4]
Paul refuses to live off the financial support of his churches, saying he would rather die than lose the honour of preaching freely.
Very close to the Kural: better to perish than to live dependent in a way that empties one's honour.
Islam
“[Charity is] for the poor who have been restricted for the cause of Allah, unable to move about in the land. An ignorant [person] would think them self-sufficient because of their restraint, but you will know them by their [characteristic] sign. They do not ask people persistently [or at all].”
— Quran 2:273 (Sahih International) [5]
The Quran praises the poor whose dignity is so strong that people mistake them for well-off, because they will not beg.
Like the Kural, it honours those who would rather endure want than live by depending on others.
Judaism
“If our God whom we serve is able to deliver us, He will deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and out of thy hand, O king. But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.”
— Daniel 3:17-18 (JPS 1917, via Sefaria) [6]
Three young Jews tell the king they will not bow even if it costs them their lives.
They prefer possible death to living by submitting to a power that demands they betray themselves.
Buddhism
“Do I carry muñja grass? I spit on my life. Death in battle would be better for me than that I, defeated, survive.”
— Sutta Nipata 3.2, Padhana Sutta (tr. Thanissaro Bhikkhu; accesstoinsight.org; the site's text has the typo 'woud') [7]
Facing Mara's army, the Bodhisatta says he would rather die fighting than live on in defeat.
This strikingly echoes the Kural's 'better to be called dead' than to live in surrender.
Jainism
“The honourable, though they become through destitution mere bones and skin, will they follow those who are destitute of proper dispositions, to make known their afflictions to them?”
— Naladiyar, ch. 30 (மானம், 'Honour'), stanza 2 (English tr. in The Indian Antiquary; wisdomlib.org) [8]
The Naladiyar says the honourable, even when wasted by poverty, will not go after unworthy people to plead their troubles.
This is almost the Kural's own image of refusing to live by following those who do not value one.
Sikhism
“He alone is known as a spiritual hero, who fights in defense of religion. He may be cut apart, piece by piece, but he never leaves the field of battle.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1105, Raag Maru, Bhagat Kabir (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Bhagat Kabir describes the true hero as one who would be cut to pieces rather than leave the field, which Sikh tradition reads as standing firm for righteousness and the weak.
The Kural says it is better to be called ruined than to live by trailing after one's foes; Kabir's hero chooses to be cut down rather than give ground.
Greek Mythology
Antigone, forbidden by King Creon to bury her brother, does so anyway, telling Creon she knew she must die some day and that if she dies before her time she counts it gain.
— Sophocles, Antigone 450-470 [10]
Antigone chooses death over obeying a command that would dishonour her and her family.
She chooses, like the Kural, to be counted lost rather than live by submitting to one who scorns what she holds sacred.
Philosophy
“I thought that I ought not to do anything common or mean in the hour of danger: nor do I now repent of the manner of my defence, and I would rather die having spoken after my manner, than speak in your manner and live.”
— Plato, Apology 38e (tr. Benjamin Jowett; classics.mit.edu) [11]
Condemned to death, Socrates says he would rather die true to himself than live by pleading in a way unworthy of him.
A near one-to-one match: better to be called dead than to live by stooping to those who scorn one.

Real Life Example

Arturo Toscanini refuses to serve the Fascist and Nazi regimes
1931-1938 · Bologna, Italy; Bayreuth, Germany; Salzburg, Austria
On 14 May 1931 the conductor Arturo Toscanini refused to open a memorial concert in Bologna with the Fascist anthem Giovinezza, and was struck by a group of Fascist youths outside the theatre; his passport was later taken away for a time. In 1933 he withdrew from the Bayreuth Festival in protest at Nazi treatment of Jewish musicians, and in 1938 he broke with the Salzburg Festival as Austria came under Hitler's control. He refused to conduct in Italy while the Fascist regime lasted and went to the United States, returning to conduct at the rebuilt La Scala in 1946.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
Toscanini gave up the leading stages of Europe and his own country rather than lend his art to regimes he opposed.
The kural says it is better to be called ruined than to live by trailing after those who do not value you, and Toscanini chose exile over serving rulers who demanded his submission.
