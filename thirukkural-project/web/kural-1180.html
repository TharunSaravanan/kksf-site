Kural 1180

Eyes Consumed with Grief · Kanvithuppazhithal · கண்விதுப்பழிதல்

மறைபெறல் ஊரார்க்கு அரிதன்றால் எம்போல் அறைபறை கண்ணார் அகத்து.
Maraiperal ooraarkku aridhandraal empoal araiparai kannaar agaththu.

Meaning

Townspeople easily learn the secret in the hearts of women like me, because our eyes announce it like a beaten drum.

Elementary

Have you ever tried to keep a secret, but your face gave it away? Maybe you were trying not to smile about a surprise, and everyone could tell! The lady says her red, teary eyes are like a loud drum telling the whole town, ‘She misses him!’

University

Maṟai (மறை) is ‘secret’ and aṟai paṟai (அறைபறை) ‘a drum that is beaten’, the town crier’s drum used for public proclamations; kaṇṇār akattu, ‘in the hearts of those whose eyes (are such drums)’. The couplet closes the chapter by turning from the eyes’ private suffering to their public betrayal: whatever the heroine hides, her tear-stained eyes broadcast it. It also links back to chapter 115 (alar, village gossip). Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah read the drum as the weeping that reveals the inner state; Pope: ‘It is not hard for all the town the knowledge to obtain, / When eyes, as mine, like beaten tambours, make the mystery plain.’ The text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In Kalidasa’s play, Shakuntala tries to hide her love for King Dushyanta, but her friends Anasuya and Priyamvada read her pallor, sighs and fevered looks and gently draw the secret out of her.
— Kalidasa, Abhijnanashakuntalam, Act 3 (paraphrase) [3]
Shakuntala’s body and eyes betray the love she is too modest to speak.
Like the Kural’s heroine, she cannot keep her love secret, because her eyes and face announce it to those around her.
Christianity
“For nothing is secret, that shall not be made manifest; neither any thing hid, that shall not be known and come abroad.”
— Luke 8:17 (KJV) [4]
Jesus says that what is hidden will in time come into the open.
The heroine’s secret is ‘made manifest’ by her own eyes, as surely as if it were proclaimed.
Islam
“And when they hear what has been revealed to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflowing with tears because of what they have recognized of the truth.”
— Qur’an 5:83 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur’an notes that the tears in people’s eyes openly show what has moved their hearts.
In both texts the eyes make the inner state visible to everyone who looks.
Judaism
“A merry heart maketh a cheerful countenance; But by sorrow of heart the spirit is broken.”
— Proverbs 15:13 (JPS 1917, via Sefaria) [6]
Proverbs observes that the state of the heart shows plainly in the face.
The Kural applies the same principle to the eyes: the heart’s sorrow drums itself out through them.
Buddhism
In the Ummadanti Jataka, a king catches one glimpse of the beautiful Ummadanti and is so overcome with longing that he can no longer hide it; his minister sees his condition and learns its cause.
— Ummadanti Jataka (Jataka 527) (paraphrase) [7]
The king’s love-sickness shows itself so plainly that those around him discover what he would keep hidden.
As in the Kural, the lover’s secret is broadcast by the state of his body and eyes.
Jainism
“Anger, pride, deceit, greed; disgust, aversion to self-control and delight in sensual things; mirth, fear, sorrow, carnal desire for women, men, or both; all these manifold passions arise in him who is attached to pleasures…”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 32.102-103 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
The Uttaradhyayana lists the many emotions, sorrow among them, that well up in one who is attached through the senses.
The heroine’s sorrow is such an emotion, and it wells up so strongly that it shows in her eyes for all to see.
Sikhism
“Just as the beat of the drum is heard on all four sides (its sound cannot be hidden) ... Similarly, the Guru-oriented Sikh whose heart is enlightened by the love of the Lord and His meditation, cannot remain hidden. His silence gives him away.”
— Bhai Gurdas, Kabitt Savaiye 411, English translation as given in the BaniDB database [9]
Bhai Gurdas says that love for the Lord cannot be hidden any more than a beaten drum can, and that even the lover's silence reveals it. His Kabitt Savaiye are early exegesis, not scripture.
The Kural says the town easily learns the secret of women whose eyes proclaim it like a beaten drum; Bhai Gurdas uses the same image of a drum heard on every side for love that cannot be concealed.
Greek Mythology
In Euripides’ Hippolytus, Phaedra wastes away, refusing food and hiding her love for her stepson, but the chorus and her nurse can see that she is sick at heart, and the nurse finally draws the secret out of her.
— Euripides, Hippolytus, lines c. 170-361 (paraphrase) [10]
Phaedra’s concealed love is betrayed by her visible decline.
As the Kural says, a secret love cannot stay hidden when the body and eyes announce it.
Philosophy
La Rochefoucauld observes that no disguise can long hide love where it exists, nor feign it where it does not.
— François de La Rochefoucauld, Maxims, no. 70 (paraphrase) [11]
The moralist holds that real love inevitably shows itself, whatever one does to conceal it.
The heroine’s drum-like eyes prove his maxim: her love cannot be hidden from the town.

Real Life Example

Princess Margaret's gesture at the coronation reveals her romance
2 June 1953 · Westminster Abbey, London, United Kingdom
At the coronation of her sister, Queen Elizabeth II, on 2 June 1953, Princess Margaret was seen brushing a piece of lint from the uniform jacket of Group Captain Peter Townsend, her late father's former equerry. A journalist noticed the moment, and it set off public speculation about their relationship. Because Townsend was divorced, the match was opposed, and in 1955 Margaret issued a statement that she would not marry him.
— Sources: [12] [13]
One small, unguarded gesture in front of the world's cameras revealed a private love that the palace had kept quiet.
The kural says the secret in a lover's heart is no secret to the town when her own eyes beat it out like a drum, and Margaret's single gesture at the coronation announced her hidden love to everyone watching.
