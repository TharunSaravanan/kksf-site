Kural 513

Selection and Employment · Therindhu Vinaiyaadal · தெரிந்து வினையாடல்

அன்பறிவு தேற்றம் அவாவின்மை இந்நான்கும் நன்குடையான் கட்டே தெளிவு.
Anbarivu thetram avaavinmai innaankum Nangudaiyaan katte thelivu.

Meaning

You can safely trust a person who fully has these four things: loyal love, knowledge, clear and firm thinking, and freedom from greed.

Elementary

If you’re choosing someone to hold the class piggy bank, you want four things: they care about the class, they’re smart, they make clear decisions, and they don’t want the money for themselves. A person with all four is safe to trust.

University

Kural 513 is a compact job specification: அன்பு (attachment or loyalty to the ruler and the enterprise), அறிவு (knowledge), தேற்றம் (clarity and decisiveness; Varadarasan glosses it as the power to decide without doubt, Kalaignar as capacity to act), and அவாவின்மை (freedom from covetousness). நன்குடையான் stresses that the four must be held fully and steadily, not occasionally. Freedom from greed comes last, as the safeguard that keeps loyalty, knowledge and decisiveness from being turned to private gain. Jethro’s advice to Moses in Exodus 18:21, choosing capable men of truth who hate unjust gain, is an almost exact counterpart.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“One who is not envious but who is a kind friend to all living entities, who does not think himself a proprietor, who is free from false ego and equal both in happiness and distress, who is always satisfied and engaged in devotional service with determination and whose mind and intelligence are in agreement with Me—he is very dear to Me.”
— Bhagavad Gita 12.13-14 (Prabhupada, As It Is) [3]
Krishna describes the ideal devotee as friendly and kind, not possessive, content, and firm in determination.
Kindness (அன்பு), steady intelligence (அறிவு), firm determination (தேற்றம்) and contentment without possessiveness (அவாவின்மை) map closely onto the Kural’s four qualities.
Christianity
“Not given to wine, no striker, not greedy of filthy lucre; but patient, not a brawler, not covetous;”
— 1 Timothy 3:3 (KJV) [4]
Paul lists what a church overseer must be, stressing again and again that he must not love money.
Like the Kural, the New Testament makes freedom from greed a condition for being trusted with responsibility.
Islam
“One of the women said, “O my father, hire him. Indeed, the best one you can hire is the strong and the trustworthy.”
— Qur’an 28:26 (Sahih International) [5]
One of the daughters of the man of Midian recommends hiring Moses because he has shown both strength and trustworthiness.
Capability plus trustworthiness is the Qur’an’s short list; the Kural makes the same point with four qualities.
Judaism
“Moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating unjust gain; and place such over them, to be rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens.”
— Exodus 18:21 (JPS 1917) [6]
Jethro tells Moses to pick capable, God-fearing, honest men who hate unjust gain, and put them in charge.
Capability, integrity and hatred of gain closely match the Kural’s knowledge, clarity and freedom from greed.
Buddhism
The Buddha names three roots of wholesome action: non-greed (alobha), non-hatred (adosa, shown as goodwill) and non-delusion (amoha, i.e. wisdom).
— Anguttara Nikaya 3.69 (Mula Sutta) [7]
Good action grows from being free of greed, full of goodwill, and clear-seeing.
Goodwill, wisdom and non-greed correspond to three of the Kural’s four qualities (அன்பு, அறிவு, அவாவின்மை).
Jainism
“Anger destroys love, pride destroys modesty, deceit destroys friendship; greed is destructive of everything.”
— Saman Suttam v. 135 (Precepts on Passions; English tr. T.K. Tukol and K.K. Dixit; jainworld.com) [8]
Of the four passions (kashayas) that bind karma, greed is singled out as the one that ruins every virtue.
Freedom from greed is one of the four qualities the kural requires before trust; the Jain verse explains why it matters so much, since greed undoes everything else, love included.
Sikhism
As a young man Guru Nanak moved to Sultanpur and worked as a clerk in the Lodi administration. The janamsakhis, the traditional accounts of his life, remember him in these years for his honesty and devotion to truth.
— The Pluralism Project, Harvard University, 'Guru Nanak' (paraphrase; janamsakhi tradition) [9]
The Harvard Pluralism Project notes Guru Nanak's government post at Sultanpur and the janamsakhi tradition of his honesty in it.
The Kural trusts the one who combines loyalty, knowledge, clear judgement and freedom from greed; the janamsakhis present the young Nanak as just such an honest official.
Greek Mythology
Before sailing to Troy, Odysseus entrusts his whole household to his old friend Mentor, whose loyalty and good sense make him fit to keep it safe.
— Homer, Odyssey 2.225-227 [10]
Odysseus picks a friend known for loyalty and judgment to look after everything he owns.
Trust is given to someone with loyal love and wisdom, as the Kural recommends.
Philosophy
“And to this end they ought to be wise and efficient, and to have a special care of the State?”
— Plato, Republic III, 412c (Jowett) [11]
Socrates says the city’s guardians must be wise and capable and must truly care for the city; later he denies them private property so greed cannot corrupt them.
Wisdom, capability, love of the city and freedom from acquisitiveness are almost exactly the Kural’s four qualities.

Real Life Example

Garibaldi hands southern Italy to Victor Emmanuel and goes home to Caprera
October-November 1860 · Naples, Italy, and the island of Caprera, Sardinia
After conquering Sicily and Naples with his volunteers in 1860, Giuseppe Garibaldi held plebiscites and handed the whole of southern Italy to King Victor Emmanuel II, meeting him on 26 October 1860 and giving up his dictatorship of the South. He was the first to hail Victor Emmanuel as king of a united Italy. Immediately afterwards he returned to his farm on Caprera, refusing the rewards offered to him.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Garibaldi combined loyalty to the cause, military skill and decisive action with a striking lack of personal greed.
The kural names loyalty, knowledge, decisiveness and freedom from greed as the marks of a trustworthy agent, and Garibaldi's handover and refusal of rewards display all four.
