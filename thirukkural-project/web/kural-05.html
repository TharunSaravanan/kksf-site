Kural 5

The Praise of God · Kadavul Vaazhthu · கடவுள் வாழ்த்து

இருள்சேர் இருவினையும் சேரா இறைவன் பொருள்சேர் புகழ்புரிந்தார் மாட்டு.
Irul-ser iru-vinaiyum seraa iraivan, porul-ser pukazh-purindhaar maattu.

Meaning

The two dark deeds — the good and bad karma born of ignorance — will not attach themselves to those who truly delight in praising God with real understanding.

Elementary

Imagine collecting points for good things and bad things you do, without really understanding why they matter. This kural says truly understanding and loving God wipes the whole scoreboard clean — good points and bad.

University

“Irul” (darkness) signifies avidya (ignorance), and “iru vinai” (the twin deeds) refers to punya and papa — meritorious and demeritorious karma — both of which bind the soul to rebirth regardless of whether the deeds were “good” or “bad,” because both are performed from a standpoint of ignorance. Valluvar’s claim is that liberation is not more good karma outweighing bad, but a shift in understanding that removes a person from the karmic ledger altogether.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“buddhi-yukto jahātīha ubhe sukṛta-duṣkṛte” — “One who is united with wisdom casts off, even in this life, both good and evil deeds.”
— Bhagavad Gita 2.50 [3]
Krishna teaches that someone acting from real wisdom stops accumulating both 'good' and 'bad' karma — they step outside the whole scorekeeping system.
This is close to a word-for-word match for the Kural’s claim about the 'two dark deeds.'
Christianity
“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.”
— Romans 8:1 (KJV) [4]
Paul tells believers their standing is no longer measured by a record of right and wrong at all, because they are 'in Christ.'
A different route to a similar destination as the Kural — being freed from the deed-by-deed ledger through relationship rather than accumulated merit.
Islam
“Those who repent, believe and do righteous work. For them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good.”
— Qur'an 25:70, Al-Furqan [5]
This verse says sincere repentance actually converts the record of past evil into a record of good, not just erasing it.
An even closer image than Ezekiel's to the Kural’s claim, since the negative record is positively transformed, not just wiped.
Judaism
“If a wicked person turns away from all his sins... none of the transgressions that he has committed shall be remembered against him.”
— Ezekiel 18:21-22 (Tanakh / KJV) [4]
Ezekiel insists that God doesn’t keep a running total against someone who genuinely turns their life around — the old record simply isn’t held against them.
A real, if narrower, Jewish parallel to the Kural’s 'deeds don't stick' claim — here achieved through repentance rather than a general transcendence of karma.
Buddhism
Describes an awakened mind as one that has “gone beyond both merit and demerit,” for whom “there is no fear.
— Dhammapada, verse 39 [6]
The verse describes an awakened mind as one that has gone beyond the entire category of merit and demerit, not just avoided bad and pursued good.
Nearly identical conceptual move to the Kural: liberation isn’t better karma, it’s exiting the karma-ledger altogether.
Greek Mythology
Initiates carried inscribed gold tablets into the grave declaring: “I have flown out of the sorrowful, weary Wheel.
— Orphic gold tablets (Thurii tablets) [7]
These tablets were a kind of password, declaring the soul had escaped the exhausting cycle of rebirth through ritual purification.
A rare Greek parallel to the Kural’s 'stepping outside the whole system' idea, though framed as ritual escape from rebirth rather than transcending good and bad deeds specifically.
Philosophy
Spinoza’s ‘free man’ acts entirely from reason, understanding good and evil not as fixed cosmic categories but as relative to what helps or hinders a rational life.
— Spinoza, Ethics, Parts IV–V [8]
For Spinoza, becoming wise means outgrowing the whole ordinary framework of moralized 'good' and 'bad' for a clearer, more rational vantage point.
Shares the Kural’s basic move of transcending the entire good/bad framework through understanding, rather than just picking the 'good' side of it.
