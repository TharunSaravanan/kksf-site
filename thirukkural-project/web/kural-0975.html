Kural 975

Greatness · Perumai · பெருமை

பெருமை யுடையவர் ஆற்றுவார் ஆற்றின் அருமை உடைய செயல்.
Perumai yudaiyavar aatruvaar aatrin arumai udaiya seyal.

Meaning

People who have greatness will do, in the right way, things that are very hard for others to do.

Elementary

Some jobs are so hard that most people give up, like building a tall tower of blocks without it falling. A great person keeps going and does it carefully, the right way, until it stands.

University

The verse plays on aatru (ஆற்று), used twice: aatruvaar ('they will accomplish') and aatrin, which commentators take either as 'by the (proper) way' (ஆறு, 'path, method') or as 'if they set about it'. Mu. Varadarasanar and Kalaignar read the first: 'in the way proper to it' (உரிய நெறியில்). Puliyur Kesikan adds 'even when they have fallen into poverty' (வறுமையான போதும்), the traditional gloss also reflected in Pope's explanation '(Though reduced) the great will be able to perform…', which holds that greatness shows itself in rare deeds even without resources. The couplet makes concrete the 'deeds hard for others' that Kural 971 called the light of a person. The text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“A man engaged in devotional service rids himself of both good and bad reactions even in this life. Therefore strive for yoga, which is the art of all work.”
— Bhagavad Gita 2.50 (Bhagavad-gītā As It Is, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda) [3]
The Gita's famous phrase yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam calls disciplined action a skill or art in doing work.
The Kural's great person does hard things the right way, which is skill in action.
Christianity
“I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.”
— Philippians 4:13 (KJV) [4]
Paul says that, strengthened by Christ, he can face anything, whether plenty or want.
It echoes the Kural's confidence that the great can carry out what others cannot, even in hardship.
Islam
In Surat al-Kahf, Dhul-Qarnayn is asked by a threatened people to build a barrier against Gog and Magog; he organises the work, has iron blocks filled in and molten copper poured over them, completes the barrier, and attributes it to his Lord's mercy.
— Qur'an 18:94-98 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur'an presents a great ruler who accomplishes an enormous task by careful, proper method.
Like the Kural's great person, he does what others could not, and does it the right way.
Judaism
“In a place where there are no men, strive to be a man.”
— Pirkei Avot 2:5 (Mishnah Yomit, trans. Joshua Kulp, via Sefaria) [6]
Hillel teaches that when no one else steps up, one must take on the task oneself.
This is the Kural's great person, who undertakes what others leave undone.
Buddhism
“Easy to do are things that are bad and harmful to oneself. But exceedingly difficult to do are things that are good and beneficial.”
— Dhammapada 163 (trans. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Dhammapada notes that the good path is the hard one.
The Kural's great people are those who take up exactly these hard, worthwhile deeds.
Jainism
The Acaranga Sutra and Kalpa Sutra describe Mahavira's more than twelve years of austerities after renouncing the world, enduring hardship and hostility with equanimity, until he attained omniscience (kevala jnana).
— Acaranga Sutra I.8 and Kalpa Sutra, Life of Mahavira (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22) [8]
Jain scripture presents Mahavira's long, disciplined ascetic effort as the supreme example of a hard task carried through by the proper path.
Like the Kural, it shows greatness as completing what others cannot, in the right manner.
Sikhism
“If you desire to play this game of love with Me, then step onto My Path with your head in hand. ... give Me your head, and do not pay any attention to public opinion.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1412, Salok Vaaran te Vadheek, salok 20, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Nanak describes the Sikh path as something only the wholehearted can take up: one must be ready to give one's very head. Sikh tradition later saw this demand fulfilled in the Gurus' and early Sikhs' willingness to face martyrdom.
The Kural says the truly great accomplish what others find too hard, and Guru Nanak sets exactly such a hard path, open only to those who will give everything.
Greek Mythology
Set the labour of cleaning King Augeas' vast and long-neglected cattle yard in a single day, Heracles breached the yard's foundations and diverted the rivers Alpheus and Peneus to wash it out.
— Apollodorus, Library 2.5.5 (trans. J. G. Frazer) [10]
Heracles achieves an apparently impossible task by finding the right method rather than by brute effort alone.
This is the Kural's rare deed done in the proper way.
Philosophy
At the close of the Ethics Spinoza admits that the way to blessedness he has described is very hard and is rarely found, remarking that all excellent things are as difficult as they are rare.
— Spinoza, Ethics V, Prop. 42, Scholium (trans. R. H. M. Elwes) [11]
Spinoza links excellence with difficulty and rarity.
The Kural makes the same link: the great are known by deeds that are rare because they are hard.

Real Life Example

Galileo, under house arrest and going blind, completes Two New Sciences
1633-1638 (published July 1638) · Siena and Arcetri, near Florence, Italy, and Leiden, Netherlands
After his condemnation by the Inquisition in 1633, Galileo began assembling his Discourses on Two New Sciences while confined in Siena, and continued it under house arrest at his villa in Arcetri, where in 1634 he also lost his beloved daughter Maria Celeste. Because publishing in Italy was closed to him, the Dutch printer Louis Elzevir visited him in 1636 and agreed to print the book in Leiden; by January 1638 Galileo had lost his sight entirely. The book came off the press in July 1638, founding the science of the strength of materials and setting out his laws of motion, the pendulum and the parabolic path of projectiles.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Stripped of freedom, family and finally sight, the old Galileo still produced the work often regarded as the foundation of modern physics.
The kural says that the great accomplish, in the proper way, deeds that are hard for others, and Galileo completed his hardest scientific work when his circumstances were at their worst.
