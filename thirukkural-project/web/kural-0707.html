Kural 707

The Knowledge of Signals · Kurippu Arithal · குறிப்பறிதல்

முகத்தின் முதுக்குறைந்தது உண்டோ உவப்பினும் காயினும் தான்முந் துறும்.
Mukaththin mudhukkuraindhadhu undo uvappinum Kaayinum thaanmun thurum.

Meaning

Is anything wiser than the face? Whether a person is happy or angry, the face shows it first.

Elementary

When you get a present you love, your smile arrives before you can say ‘thank you’. When you are cross, your frown shows up before any words. Your face is like a messenger who always runs ahead of you.

University

Mudhukkuraindhadhu comes from mudhukkurai(mai), a Tamil idiom for mature wisdom or sagacity (literally ‘old-shortened’, an idiom whose sense is ‘wise beyond years’); the question ‘is there anything wiser than the face?’ credits the face with a kind of intelligence. Uvappinum kaayinum (‘whether it rejoices or burns with anger’) names the two poles of emotion, and thaan mun urum means ‘it comes forward first’, before speech or deliberate action. Parimelazhagar comments that the face runs ahead of the mind in disclosing liking and dislike, which is why it is called wise: it seems to know first. The irony is deliberate: the face is ‘wise’ precisely because it cannot keep a secret. Sandhi text: முதுக்குறைந்த துண்டோ வுவப்பினுங் / காயினுந் தான்முந் துறும்.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
On seeing his kinsmen drawn up for battle, Arjuna says his limbs give way, his mouth is parched, his body trembles and his hair stands on end.
— Bhagavad Gita 1.28-29 [3]
Arjuna’s inner turmoil shows in his body before he has made any decision.
As the Kural says, the body and face announce the mind’s state before words or decisions.
Christianity
“And Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell. And the LORD said unto Cain, Why art thou wroth? and why is thy countenance fallen?”
— Genesis 4:5-6 (KJV) [4]
Cain’s anger and resentment show on his face before he acts.
Cain’s fallen face runs ahead of his deed, exactly as the Kural says the face runs ahead of the mind.
Islam
“I know when you are pleased with me or angry with me.”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 5228 [5]
The Prophet tells Aisha he can tell her mood from a small change in how she swears: ‘by the Lord of Muhammad’ when pleased, ‘by the Lord of Abraham’ when angry.
Pleasure and anger leak out ahead of any declaration, and a loving observer reads them.
Judaism
Rabbi Ilai teaches that a person’s character is recognised by three things: his cup, his pocket and his anger (koso, kiso, ka‘aso).
— Babylonian Talmud, Eruvin 65b [6]
How someone behaves when drinking, when paying and when angry reveals what they really are.
Like the Kural, the Talmud treats moments of strong feeling such as anger as the times when the inner person shows through.
Buddhism
The Buddha, seeing Sariputta approach, remarks that his faculties are clear and his complexion pure and bright, and asks what meditation he has been practising.
— Majjhima Nikaya 151, Pindapataparisuddhi Sutta [7]
The Buddha reads Sariputta’s inner state from his face before Sariputta says a word.
Sariputta’s face announces his inner calm first, and the Buddha reads it, as the Kural describes.
Jainism
“Perceiving the teacher's anger one should pacify him by kindness, appease him with folded hands, and promise not to do wrong again.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 1.41 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The disciple is expected to notice his teacher's anger as soon as it shows and respond to it at once with humility.
It assumes what the Kural states: anger shows itself before anything is said, so the attentive person can read it.
Sikhism
“One who meditates on the Naam is at peace; his face is radiant and bright.” … “The faithless cynic barks out his lies, and his face is blackened.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 44, Sri Raag, Guru Arjan; and Ang 239, Raag Gauri, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In two hymns Guru Arjan describes the inner state showing on the face: the peace of the Name brightens it, while falsehood darkens it.
The Kural says the face is the first to announce whether a person is pleased or angry; Guru Arjan likewise treats the face as the place where the inner condition becomes visible.
Greek Mythology
“His heart was black with rage, and his eyes flashed fire as he scowled on Calchas”
— Homer, Iliad 1 (trans. Samuel Butler) [10]
When the seer blames him for the plague, Agamemnon’s fury is visible in his eyes and scowl before he speaks.
Agamemnon’s face runs ahead of his words, as the Kural says it always does in anger.
Philosophy
Wittgenstein remarks that the human body is the best picture of the human soul, and elsewhere that we see emotion in a face directly rather than inferring it from facial contortions.
— Wittgenstein, Philosophical Investigations II.iv; Zettel §225 [11]
For Wittgenstein, expression is not a clue from which we reason; the feeling is visible in the face itself.
This is the Kural’s ‘wise face’: the face presents the feeling immediately, ahead of any report.

Real Life Example

Paul Ekman tests facial expressions among the Fore of Papua New Guinea
1967-1968 · South East Highlands, Papua New Guinea
To test whether facial expressions of emotion are learned or universal, the American psychologist Paul Ekman travelled in 1967 and 1968 to the Fore people, an isolated community with almost no contact with the outside world. He told them simple stories and asked them to choose the photographed face that matched, and also asked them to show the expressions themselves. Ekman found that the Fore expressed emotions such as sadness, fear, surprise, anger and disgust with the same eye and mouth movements seen in Western populations.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
The study suggested that the face announces basic feelings in a common language that crosses cultures.
The kural says the face is wiser than anything, announcing pleasure or anger before words can, and Ekman found that faces speak the same emotional language even where no spoken language is shared.
