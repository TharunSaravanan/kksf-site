Kural 569

Absence of Terrorism · Veruvandha Seyyaamai · வெருவந்த செய்யாமை

செருவந்த போழ்திற் சிறைசெய்யா வேந்தன் வெருவந்து வெய்து கெடும்.
Seruvandha poazhdhil siraiseyyaa vaendhan Veruvandhu veydhu kedum.

Meaning

A king who does not build a strong fort ahead of time will be filled with fear when war comes, and will quickly fall.

Elementary

If you never build a shelter and a big storm comes, you get frightened and soaked. The kural says a ruler who doesn’t prepare protection in peaceful times panics and falls when trouble arrives.

University

சிறை here means a protective barrier or fortification (Mu. Varadarasanar: அரண்), not ‘prison’ in the modern sense; the couplet is about defence made ready before செரு (battle) arrives. The striking reversal is in வெருவந்து: the very dread (veru) the tyrant inflicted on others now falls on him, so the chapter’s key word turns back on its source. Commentators connect this to the preceding couplets: a king who has alienated his people through fear and cruelty lacks the human ‘fortress’ of their loyalty when danger comes, which is why this apparently military verse belongs in a chapter on not ruling by terror. வெய்து கெடும் means ‘perishes swiftly/painfully’. Both sources agree on the text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“That king, however, who is of righteous behaviour and who is ever engaged in attracting the hearts of his people, never sinks when attacked by foes. If overcome, he soon regains his position.”
— Mahabharata, Shanti Parva, Section LVII (trans. K. M. Ganguli) [3]
Bhishma teaches that the loyalty of a king’s people is his real protection when enemies attack.
The Kural’s unprotected king falls in war; Bhishma names the protection he lacked: the hearts of his people.
Christianity
“Or what king, going to make war against another king, sitteth not down first, and consulteth whether he be able with ten thousand to meet him that cometh against him with twenty thousand?”
— Luke 14:31 (KJV) [4]
Jesus uses the image of a king who plans before war to teach his followers to count the cost.
Preparation before battle is exactly what the Kural’s doomed king neglected.
Islam
“And prepare against them whatever you are able of power and of steeds of war by which you may terrify the enemy of Allāh and your enemy.”
— Quran 8:60 (Sahih International) [5]
The Quran commands the community to be prepared for defence so that aggressors are deterred.
Readiness before conflict is what the Kural’s king failed to make; notably, both texts direct deterrence at external enemies, never at one’s own people.
Judaism
“They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with those that laded, every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon.”
— Nehemiah 4:17 (KJV) [4]
Nehemiah’s people rebuild Jerusalem’s wall while armed, making their defences ready before the enemy strikes.
A people who build their own wall willingly is the opposite of the Kural’s ruler without a fortress.
Buddhism
When King Ajatasattu plans to attack the Vajjians, the Buddha says they cannot be defeated so long as they meet often in harmony, honour their elders, protect the vulnerable and keep their customs; Ajatasattu’s minister concludes they cannot be conquered by force.
— Mahaparinibbana Sutta (DN 16), 1.1–5 [6]
The Buddha presents a people’s internal harmony and justice as their strongest defence.
The Kural’s king lacks a protective ‘fortress’; the Vajjians’ fortress is the goodwill and order of their society.
Jainism
“Making Faith his fortress, Penance and Self-control the bolt (of its gate), Patience its strong wall, so that guarded in three ways it is impregnable”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 9.20-22 (cf. 9.18) (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [7]
When Indra urges the renouncing King Nami to build walls, gates, battlements and a moat, Nami answers that the true fortress is built from faith, self-control and patience.
The Kural's king perishes because he has prepared no place of defence; Nami agrees that defence must be prepared in advance but says the fortress that really protects is inner discipline, not stone.
Sikhism
“Millions of religious leaders failed to halt the invader, when they heard of the Emperor's invasion.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 417, Raag Aasaa (Babarvani), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Guru Nanak records that when Babar's invasion was announced, the Lodi rulers relied on the prayers and charms of holy men instead of real defence, and none of it stopped the invader.
The Kural says a king who has not prepared his defences beforehand will be seized with fear when war comes and quickly perish; Guru Nanak describes rulers caught unready and falling in just this way.
Greek Mythology
In the Odyssey, the bard Demodocus sings of the wooden horse that the Trojans dragged inside their own walls; with their guard down, Troy fell in a single night.
— Homer, Odyssey 8.499–520 [9]
A city that neglected its defence at the decisive moment was destroyed swiftly and completely.
Like the Kural’s king without protection, Troy was caught unready and perished quickly.
Philosophy
“Furthermore, those who wish to be feared must inevitably be afraid of those whom they intimidate.”
— Cicero, De Officiis 2.24–25 (trans. W. Miller, Loeb) [10]
Cicero illustrates with Dionysius of Syracuse, who dared not let a barber near his throat, and Alexander of Pherae, who needed armed guards even with his wife.
The Kural’s word வெருவந்து, the tyrant himself seized by fear, is exactly Cicero’s point.

Real Life Example

France enters the Franco-German War unprepared and the Second Empire falls
July-September 1870 · Sedan and Paris, France
France declared war on Prussia on 19 July 1870, but its armies were slow to mobilise and not ready to fight when German forces crossed the border, with shortages of equipment reported among the units. One French army was soon bottled up in Metz, and on 1-2 September at Sedan, Emperor Napoleon III was captured and a French army of nearly 100,000 men surrendered. On 4 September, after news of the defeat reached Paris, the Second Empire was overthrown and a republic was proclaimed.
— Sources: [11] [12]
An empire that went to war without having readied its defences collapsed within about six weeks of the first battles.
The kural warns that a ruler who has not prepared his defences beforehand will be seized with fear when war comes and quickly perish, and Napoleon III's unready empire fell almost as soon as the fighting began.
