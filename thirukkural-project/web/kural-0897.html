Kural 897

Not Offending the Great · Periyaaraip Pizhaiyaamai · பெரியாரைப் பிழையாமை

வகைமாண்ட வாழ்க்கையும் வான்பொருளும் என்னாம் தகைமாண்ட தக்கார் செறின்.
Vakaimaanta vaazhkkaiyum vaanporulum ennaam thakaimaanta thakkaar serin.

Meaning

If truly great people with fine character become angry with someone, what good are that person’s grand life and great riches?

Elementary

You could have the biggest toy collection in the world, but if you are mean to the kindest, wisest grown-up in town, everyone will stop trusting you. All your toys can’t fix that. Good people’s goodwill is worth more than riches.

University

Two pairs balance each other: vagai maaNDa vaazhkkai (வகைமாண்ட வாழ்க்கை, a life excellent in every kind) and vaan poruL (வான்பொருள், sky-high wealth) are worth ennaam (என்னாம், what will they become?) if thagai maaNDa thakkaar (தகைமாண்ட தக்கார், the worthy, distinguished in noble quality) seRin (செறின், are angered). Pope’s explanation, following the traditional commentary, applies this to a king whose government with all its auxiliaries and untold wealth is useless before the wrath of the righteous great; Solomon Pappaiah glosses the great as those excellent in character. Sivayogi Sivakumar reads the couplet differently, as saying that wealth and splendour cannot equal the richness of the worthy, showing an alternative interpretation of seRin. Both sources print the same text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
When Indra carelessly lets his elephant trample a garland given by the sage Durvasa, the sage curses him, and Indra and the gods lose their prosperity and fortune until the churning of the ocean restores it.
— Vishnu Purana, Book 1, Chapter 9 (trans. H. H. Wilson) [3]
All the wealth and splendour of the king of the gods vanished when he slighted a great sage.
This is the Kural’s claim exactly: splendid life and vast wealth are worthless once the worthy great are angered.
Christianity
“For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?”
— Mark 8:36 (KJV) [4]
Jesus teaches that worldly gain is worthless if the deepest good is lost.
The Kural’s question, what use are life and wealth, has the same rhetorical shape.
Islam
“His wealth will not avail him or that which he gained.”
— Qur’an 111:2 (Saheeh International) [5]
Said of Abu Lahab, who opposed and insulted the Prophet: his riches could not protect him.
Like the Kural, it declares wealth useless to one who sets himself against the great.
Judaism
“Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.”
— Proverbs 11:4 (KJV) [4]
The proverb teaches that wealth gives no protection when wrath arrives.
The Kural similarly says riches count for nothing once the worthy are angered.
Buddhism
“He who inflicts violence on those who are unarmed, and offends those who are inoffensive, will soon come upon one of these ten states: Sharp pain, or disaster, bodily injury, serious illness, or derangement of mind, trouble from the government, or grave charges, loss of relatives, or loss of wealth, or houses destroyed by ravaging fire.”
— Dhammapada 137-140 (trans. Acharya Buddharakkhita), excerpt [6]
The Buddha lists the calamities, including loss of wealth and property, that befall one who offends the harmless.
It echoes the Kural’s point that wealth and a fine life collapse when one wrongs the virtuous.
Jainism
“Prostrate yourself before him for protection, you together with all of them, if you want to save your life and your property; for in his wrath he might reduce the world to ashes.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 12.28 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [7]
Bhadrā urges the priest and his household to seek the offended monk's forgiveness at once, since life and property alike are forfeit if such a sage is roused.
The Kural asks what good a splendid life and great wealth are once the truly worthy turn their anger on someone, and the Jain verse puts life and property at stake before an offended sage in the same way.
Sikhism
“The slanderer of the Saint becomes miserable and poor.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 280, Raag Gauree (Sukhmani), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Guru Arjan says that wronging the holy strips away both happiness and wealth.
The Kural asks what use a splendid life and great wealth are once the truly worthy are angered, and Gurbani says such a person is left miserable and poor.
Greek Mythology
Erysichthon, a wealthy man, cuts down Demeter’s sacred oak despite warnings, and is cursed with insatiable hunger that devours all his riches.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 8.738-878 (Erysichthon) [9]
His great wealth was consumed entirely after he offended a goddess.
As in the Kural, prosperity proves worthless once the great are angered.
Philosophy
In the Apology, Socrates urges the Athenians to care for virtue above wealth, saying that wealth does not bring excellence, but excellence makes wealth and everything else good.
— Plato, Apology 30a-b [10]
For Socrates, riches have no value apart from goodness.
The Kural similarly subordinates wealth to the regard of the virtuous.

Real Life Example

Ida Tarbell's exposé and the break-up of Standard Oil
1902-1911 · New York and Washington, D.C., United States
From November 1902 to October 1904 the journalist Ida Tarbell published a 19-part series in McClure's Magazine documenting the unfair tactics and railroad rebates through which John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil had come to control nearly all oil refining and marketing in the United States. The federal government brought an antitrust suit against the company, and in May 1911 the Supreme Court ordered the Standard Oil trust dissolved, splitting it into 34 independent companies. The Library of Congress and Britannica both credit Tarbell's reporting as a major influence on the outcome.
— Sources: [11] [12]
The richest business in the world could not protect itself once a determined, principled investigator had laid out the truth before the public and the courts.
The kural asks what use splendid wealth is once the truly worthy turn against its holder, and Standard Oil's vast fortune did not save the trust.
