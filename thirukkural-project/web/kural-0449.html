Kural 449

Seeking the Aid of Great Men · Periyaaraith Thunaikkodal · பெரியாரைத் துணைக்கோடல்

முதலிலார்க் கூதிய மில்லை மதலையாஞ் சார்பிலார்க் கில்லை நிலை.
Mudhalilaark kooodhiya millai madhalaiyaanj Chaarbilaark killai nilai.

Meaning

Just as there is no profit without money to start with, there is no steadiness for those without good people to support them.

Elementary

You can’t earn money from a business if you have no money to start it. In the same way, you can’t stay steady and safe in life if you have no wise people to lean on.

University

An economic analogy fitting the Porutpaal: ‘mudhal’ (capital) is to ‘oodhiyam’ (profit) as ‘saarbu’ (support) is to ‘nilai’ (stability). ‘Madhalai’ is a prop or supporting beam — the support of the great is the load-bearing pillar of a leader’s position. The analogy implies that alliance with the worthy is not an optional extra but the principal on which all further returns are earned.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Sovereignty (rájatva) is possible only with assistance. A single wheel can never move.”
— Kautilya, Arthashastra 1.7 (tr. R. Shamasastry) [4]
Kautilya likens rule to a vehicle that cannot move on one wheel; the ruler’s helpers are the other wheel.
Like the Kural’s prop (madhalai), assistance is the precondition of stable rule.
Christianity
Jesus compares the one who hears and does his words to a wise man who built his house on rock, which withstood the storm, and the one who does not to a foolish man who built on sand, whose house fell.
— Matthew 7:24–27 [5]
Stability depends on the foundation beneath.
Like the Kural, a sound support is what keeps one standing.
Islam
“And hold firmly to the rope of Allah all together and do not become divided.”
— Quran 3:103 (Sahih International) [6]
The community’s stability depends on holding together to a common support.
Stability through a shared support, echoing the Kural’s ‘saarbu’.
Judaism
“For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”
— Ecclesiastes 4:10 (KJV) [5]
Qoheleth warns that the person without companions has no one to raise him when he falls.
Without support, there is no standing — the Kural’s ‘no stability’.
Buddhism
The Buddha lists admirable friendship as the first prerequisite for the development of the wings to awakening, preceding virtue, right talk, persistence and discernment.
— Sambodhi Sutta, AN 9.1 [7]
Good friendship is the foundational capital on which all further spiritual progress is built.
Parallels the Kural’s ‘capital’ image: without the support of the worthy, nothing further can be gained.
Jainism
“Just as they carry off a young bird whose wings are not yet grown, so many unprincipled men will seduce a novice who has not yet mastered the Law, thinking that they can get him in their power, when they have made him leave (the Gakkha).”
— Sutrakritanga 1.14.3 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The Sutrakritanga compares a novice who leaves the community of his teachers to a fledgling that leaves the nest too early and is snatched by birds of prey.
The kural says there is no stability without the support of the worthy, just as there is no profit without capital. The Jain image of the unfledged bird shows how quickly a person without that support falls.
Sikhism
“O Nanak, without capital, no one has ever departed with profit.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1244, Raag Saarang (Saarang ki Vaar, salok), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Nanak uses the language of trade: without the capital of spiritual wisdom and virtue, no one leaves this world with any gain.
The Kural says there is no profit without capital and no stability without the support of the worthy; Guru Nanak uses the same capital-and-profit image for the spiritual life.
Greek Mythology
The giant Antaeus was invincible while touching his mother Earth, who renewed his strength; Heracles defeated him by lifting him off the ground and crushing him in the air.
— Apollodorus, Library 2.5.11 [10]
Cut off from his source of support, the mightiest wrestler lost all strength.
An image of the Kural’s point: without one’s prop, there is no standing.
Philosophy
“For without friends no one would choose to live, though he had all other goods.”
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII.1 (tr. W. D. Ross) [11]
Aristotle says even the rich and powerful need friends most, to preserve and use their prosperity.
Friends as the foundation that makes other goods stable and useful.

Real Life Example

Emperor Maximilian of Mexico loses his French support
1864-1867 · Mexico City and Queretaro, Mexico
Archduke Maximilian of Austria became emperor of Mexico in 1864, his rule upheld by French troops sent by Napoleon III. Under pressure from the United States, Napoleon agreed in 1866 to withdraw, and the last French forces left in early 1867. Left with only a small army, Maximilian was besieged and captured at Queretaro by the republican forces of Benito Juarez and was executed in June 1867.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Maximilian's empire had no foundation of its own, and once the outside support that propped it up was removed, it fell within months.
The kural says there is no stability for those without strong support to prop them up, just as there is no profit without capital, and Maximilian's throne collapsed when its prop was withdrawn.
