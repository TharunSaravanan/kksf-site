Kural 469

Acting after Due Consideration · Therindhu Seyalvakai · தெரிந்து செயல்வகை

நன்றாற்ற லுள்ளுந் தவறுண்டு அவரவர் பண்பறிந் தாற்றாக் கடை.
Nandraatra lullun thavaruntu avaravar, Panbarin dhaatraak katai.

Meaning

Even when you try to do good, you can fail if you do not understand what each person is like and what suits them.

Elementary

Even being kind can go wrong if you don’t understand the person you’re helping. What helps one person might upset another.

University

The couplet carries deliberation into benevolence itself. நன்று ஆற்றல் (doing good, conferring benefits) is normally praised without qualification, but Valluvar notes a தவறு (error, failure) that arises when one does not know அவரவர் பண்பு (the respective natures of each person). Parimelazhagar explains that benefits given to those whose nature does not suit them may be misused or may turn them hostile. For a ruler, rewards and favours are tools of policy and must fit their recipients. The reduplicated அவரவர் (‘each respectively’) insists on individual discernment rather than uniform treatment. Some editions spell the verb தவுறுண்டு; the standard reading is தவறுண்டு.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In the Panchatantra, a bird in her snug nest advises shivering monkeys in the rain to build shelters for themselves; the offended monkeys tear her nest apart.
— Panchatantra, Book 1 (‘The Monkeys and the Bird’) [4]
The bird meant well, but she gave advice to creatures who couldn’t take it, and she lost her home.
Doing good without knowing the recipient’s nature backfires, exactly as the Kural warns.
Christianity
Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest they trample them under their feet, and turn again and rend you.
— Matthew 7:6 (KJV) [5]
Don’t offer something precious to those who can’t value it, or they may trample it and turn on you.
Good things given without regard to the recipient’s nature can do harm, as the Kural says.
Islam
Ali ibn Abi Talib said: speak to people according to what they can understand; do you want Allah and His Messenger to be denied?
— Sahih al-Bukhari 127 (Book of Knowledge) [6]
Even true teaching can backfire if it doesn’t fit what the listener can grasp.
Doing good (teaching truth) must fit each person’s capacity, as the Kural teaches.
Judaism
Reprove not a scorner, lest he hate thee: rebuke a wise man, and he will love thee.
— Proverbs 9:8 (Hebrew Bible; KJV rendering) [5]
The same correction that a wise person welcomes will make a scornful person hate you.
Whether a well-meant act helps or harms depends on the recipient’s character.
Buddhism
The Buddha explains to Prince Abhaya that he speaks only what is true and beneficial, and even then he knows the proper time for saying it, taking account of the listener.
— Abhaya Sutta (Abhayarajakumara Sutta), Majjhima Nikaya 58 [7]
The Buddha didn’t just say true, helpful things. He chose when and how to say them depending on who was listening.
Good speech must be adapted to the person and occasion, as the Kural requires of good deeds.
Jainism
“The effect of giving of the gift–dāna–has distinctions based on the specific manner in which it is given–vidhiviśeṣa, the specific thing given–dravyaviśeṣa, the specific nature of the giver–dātṛviśeṣa, and the specific nature of the recipient–pātraviśeṣa.”
— Tattvārtha Sūtra 7.39, with Pūjyapāda's Sarvārthasiddhi (tr. Vijay K. Jain, 2018; wisdomlib.org) [8]
Umasvati teaches that the fruit of a gift depends on how it is given, what is given, who gives and who receives. Pujyapada's commentary compares this to seed whose harvest depends on the soil.
The Kural warns that even doing good can fail if one ignores each person's nature, and the Jain sutra makes the recipient's nature one of the factors that decide a gift's result.
Sikhism
“Wisdom leads us to serve our Lord and Master; through wisdom, honor is obtained. Wisdom does not come by reading textbooks; wisdom inspires us to give in charity.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1245, Raag Saarang, Vaar (salok), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Raag Saarang Guru Nanak says that true giving is guided by wisdom, the same discernment that leads to serving God and earning honour.
The Kural says even doing good can fail without understanding those one deals with; Guru Nanak makes wisdom the guide of charity.
Greek Mythology
Helios, wishing to prove his love, swears to grant his son Phaethon any gift; Phaethon asks to drive the sun chariot, cannot control the horses, scorches the earth, and is struck down by Zeus.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 2.1–328 [10]
A father’s generous gift, given without thinking about whether his son could handle it, led to disaster.
A kindness given without regard to the recipient’s nature and capacity brings ruin, the Kural’s warning.
Philosophy
Aristotle observes that giving or spending money is easy and anyone can do it, but doing it to the right person, to the right extent, at the right time, with the right motive and in the right way is neither easy nor for everyone.
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.9 (tr. W. D. Ross) [11]
Being generous is easy; being generous well — to the right person in the right way — takes wisdom.
The Kural’s point that good deeds need discernment about the person.

Real Life Example

The Macartney Embassy's gifts fail to impress the Qianlong Emperor
September 1793 · Chengde and Beijing, Qing China
Britain sent Lord George Macartney to the Qing court with a large retinue and a great quantity of presents, including clocks, carriages and scientific instruments, hoping to open more ports and station an ambassador in China. The Qianlong Emperor treated the presents as tribute and, in edicts addressed to King George III, rejected every British request, stating that China had no need of Britain's manufactures.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Lavish gifts chosen without understanding how the recipient's court saw the world produced the opposite of the intended effect.
The kural warns that even well-meant giving can fail when one does not understand the nature of the person receiving it, and Britain's gifts misjudged the emperor and his court.
