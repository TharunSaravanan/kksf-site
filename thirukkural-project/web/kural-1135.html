Kural 1135

The Abandonment of Reserve · Naanuth Thuravuraithal · நாணுத் துறவுரைத்தல்

தொடலைக் குறுந்தொடி தந்தாள் மடலொடு மாலை உழக்கும் துயர்.
Thodalaik kurundhodi thandhaal madalodu maalai uzhakkum thuyar.

Meaning

The girl who wears small bangles strung like a garland has given me both the palm-stem horse and the sadness that hurts me every evening.

Elementary

Sometimes a person we like very much can make us feel happy and sad at the same time. The speaker says the girl with the pretty little bangles has given him two things: a heavy heart every evening, and the idea of riding the palm horse.

University

Toṭalaik kuṟun-toṭi (தொடலைக் குறுந்தொடி) is an epithet standing for the heroine: 'she of the small bangles (kuṟun-toṭi) that are like a garland or string (toṭalai)'. Commentators differ on toṭalai: Mu. Varadarasanar takes it as 'garland-like, continuous', Kesikan as 'a linked set', while Kalaignar reads in a girdle (mēkalai) as well; Pappaiah's text appears to read 'like a mountain' (malai pōla), probably a misprint for mālai. Mālai uḻakkum tuyar (மாலை உழக்கும் துயர்), 'the sorrow that harrows in the evening', invokes the akam convention that evening is the hardest hour for separated lovers. Pope: 'The maid that slender armlets wears, like flowers entwined, / Has brought me horse of palm, and pangs of eventide!' The text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In Kalidasa's play, King Dushyanta, after first meeting Shakuntala in the hermitage, grows pale and thin with longing and cannot rest, all because of the girl he has seen.
— Kalidasa, Shakuntala, Act III (paraphrase; cf. tr. Arthur W. Ryder) [3]
The love-sickness of the hero is portrayed as caused by a single glimpse of the heroine.
Like the Kural's speaker, Dushyanta can point to one girl as the source of all his torment.
Christianity
“Thou hast ravished my heart, my sister, my spouse; thou hast ravished my heart with one of thine eyes, with one chain of thy neck.”
— Song of Solomon 4:9 (KJV) [4]
The lover says that a single glance and a single necklace of the beloved have overpowered his heart.
As the Kural names the beloved by her bangles, the Song names an ornament, a neck-chain, as part of what has undone him.
Islam
“When they saw him, they were so stunned ˹by his beauty˺ that they cut their hands.”
— Qur'an 12:31 (tr. Mustafa Khattab, The Clear Quran) [5]
The women of the city, invited to see Joseph, are so overwhelmed by his beauty that they cut their own hands with their knives.
Like the Kural's speaker, they suffer pain caused by nothing more than the sight of the beloved's beauty.
Judaism
“Turn away thine eyes from me, For they have overcome me.”
— Song of Songs 6:5 (JPS 1917, via Sefaria) [6]
The lover begs the beloved to look away because her eyes overwhelm him.
Like the Kural's speaker, he admits that the beloved herself is the source of the power that undoes him.
Buddhism
In the Ummadanti Jataka, King Sivi sees the beautiful Ummadanti only once, from a distance, and falls so deeply in love that he grows ill and cannot attend to his duties, though in the end he masters himself.
— Ummadanti Jataka (Jataka 527) (paraphrase; cf. tr. E.B. Cowell et al.) [7]
A single sight of the woman brings the king a sickness of longing that he must overcome.
The Kural's speaker also traces his torment to one girl, though in the Jataka the virtue lies in conquering it.
Jainism
On the way to his own wedding, Arishtanemi sees animals penned for the feast, gives his ornaments to his charioteer, and renounces the world, leaving his bride Rajimati in sorrow.
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 22.14-28 (paraphrase; tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
In the Jain account, one person's decision brings both release for himself and grief for the one who loved him.
The Kural's speaker says the beloved 'gave' him sorrow; Rajimati received hers from Arishtanemi in the same way.
Sikhism
“O Enticing Lord, I cannot sleep; I sigh. I am adorned with necklaces, gowns, ornaments and make-up.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 830, Raag Bilaaval, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Arjan's bride-soul, dressed in all her jewellery, still lies sighing and sleepless because her Beloved is away.
The Kural blames the girl of the garlanded bangles for the lover's evening torment; here the adorned bride herself bears the same sorrow of separation as night falls.
Greek Mythology
Struck by Cupid's golden arrow, Apollo burns with love for the nymph Daphne, who flees him; the god suffers while she remains untouched until she is changed into a laurel.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 1 (Apollo and Daphne) (paraphrase) [10]
Ovid shows love as a wound inflicted on a lover by forces beyond his control, with the beloved as its focus.
Like the Kural's speaker, Apollo receives his torment through one maiden who does not share it.
Philosophy
Stendhal describes 'crystallization': the lover's mind keeps covering the beloved with new perfections, so that every small detail about her deepens his attachment and his suffering.
— Stendhal, On Love (De l'amour, 1822), ch. 2 (paraphrase) [11]
In On Love, the lover's imagination turns ordinary traits into sources of both delight and pain.
The Kural's lover identifies his beloved by a small detail, her little bangles, and blames her for all his sorrow.

Real Life Example

Ivan Turgenev follows the singer Pauline Viardot abroad
1843-1883 · St Petersburg, Russia; Paris, France; Baden-Baden, Germany
The Russian writer Ivan Turgenev fell in love with the singer Pauline Viardot when she first performed in Russia in 1843. In 1847 he left Russia for Paris with the Viardot circle and stayed there until 1850, and he later moved to Baden-Baden in Germany to be near her again. He wrote the libretto for her operetta Le dernier sorcier (1869) and spent his last years at Bougival near Paris.
— Sources: [12] [13]
One meeting with a singer reshaped the whole course of a great writer's life and kept him far from home for decades.
In the kural the lover says that the girl with the small bangles has given him both the palm-stem horse and the sorrow of every evening; Turgenev's love for one woman likewise gave him a lifetime of devotion and longing.
