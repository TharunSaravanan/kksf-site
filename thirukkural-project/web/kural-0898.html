Kural 898

Not Offending the Great · Periyaaraip Pizhaiyaamai · பெரியாரைப் பிழையாமை

குன்றன்னார் குன்ற மதிப்பின் குடியொடு நின்றன்னார் மாய்வர் நிலத்து.
Kundrannaar kundra madhippin kutiyotu nindrannaar maaivar nilaththu.

Meaning

If great people, firm as mountains, are looked down on, even those who seemed firmly settled on earth will be wiped out along with their families.

Elementary

Think of a mountain: it is big, steady and old. If you try to knock a mountain down, you will only hurt yourself. People who are great like mountains deserve respect, and those who treat them badly can lose everything, even their family’s good name.

University

The couplet plays on kunRu (குன்று, hill, mountain) and kunRa (குன்ற, to diminish): if kunRannaar (குன்றன்னார், those like mountains) are kunRa madhikkap paDin (thought of as diminished, slighted), or, in another reading, if they madhi (மதி, resolve) on diminishing someone, then even ninRannaar (நின்றன்னார், those who seem to stand forever) maaivar (மாய்வர், will perish) kuDiyoDu (குடியொடு, along with their lineage) nilaththu (நிலத்து, on earth). The two readings are visible in the commentaries: Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah take it as the mountain-like great resolving on destruction, while Sivayogi Sivakumar and Kalaignar take it as others belittling the great. Pope’s explanation, following the traditional commentary, identifies the mountain-like ones as devotees. The text is identical in both sources.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Young Yadavas mock the visiting sages Vishvamitra, Kanva and Narada by dressing Samba as a pregnant woman; the sages curse that an iron bolt will destroy the race, and the Yadava clan later perishes in fratricidal slaughter.
— Mahabharata, Mausala Parva, Section 1, trans. K. M. Ganguli [3]
A jest against great sages led to the destruction of an entire, seemingly invincible dynasty.
This is the Kural’s image of seemingly permanent people perishing with their whole kin for slighting the mountain-like great.
Christianity
“And whosoever shall fall on this stone shall be broken: but on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder.”
— Matthew 21:44 (KJV) [4]
Jesus speaks of the rejected cornerstone that breaks those who stumble on it and crushes those it falls upon.
The Kural’s mountain-like great similarly crush those who set themselves against them.
Islam
“And We caused the earth to swallow him and his home. And there was for him no company to aid him other than Allah, nor was he of those who [could] defend themselves.”
— Qur’an 28:81 (Saheeh International) [5]
Qarun, arrogant in his wealth and defiant toward Moses, was swallowed by the earth along with his house.
As in the Kural, one who seemed firmly established perished from the earth together with his household.
Judaism
“And the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed them up, and their houses, and all the men that appertained unto Korah, and all their goods.”
— Numbers 16:32 (KJV) [4]
Korah and his followers, who rebelled against Moses and Aaron, were swallowed by the earth with their households and possessions.
This mirrors the Kural’s warning that those who belittle the great perish from the earth with their whole family.
Buddhism
In the Upali Sutta, the Buddha asks whether Upali has heard how the Dandaka, Kalinga, Mejjha and Matanga forests came to be wilderness, and Upali answers that it was through the mental anger of seers.
— Majjhima Nikaya 56, Upali Sutta [6]
The sutta recalls a tradition that whole inhabited realms were laid waste because seers were offended.
This matches the Kural’s picture of established peoples wiped from the earth by the wrath of the great.
Jainism
“Kṛṣṇa, learning that his drunken sons and nephews had beaten the meditating ascetic Dvaipāyana almost to death, thought: “Oh! this lack of restraint on the part of these princes will be the death of the family.” Dvaipāyana, reborn as a fire-deity, later burned Dvārakā with its people.”
— Hemacandra, Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra, Book 8 (Neminātha-caritra), ch. 11, part 2 'Beating of Dvaipāyana' (tr. Helen M. Johnson, GOS; wisdomlib.org) [7]
In Hemacandra's Neminātha-caritra the Yādava clan, which seemed securely established, perishes with its city because its princes assaulted an ascetic.
The Kural warns that when those great as mountains are slighted, even people who seemed permanently established perish with their families, and the destruction of the Yādavas and Dvārakā is that fate told in full.
Sikhism
“The slanderer of the Saint has no heritage at all.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 280, Raag Gauree (Sukhmani), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Guru Arjan says the slanderer is left with no roots and no lasting line.
The Kural says that slighting the mountain-like great uproots even those who seemed permanent, together with their families, and Gurbani likewise speaks of a life left with no roots.
Greek Mythology
Queen Niobe boasts that her many children make her greater than the goddess Leto; Apollo and Artemis slay her children, and she is turned to weeping stone.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 6.146-312 (Niobe) [9]
Her pride against a divine mother destroyed her entire family.
As in the Kural, belittling the great brings ruin upon oneself together with one’s kin.
Philosophy
In the Melian Dialogue, the Melians refuse to yield to the far stronger Athenians, and the city is captured, its men killed and its women and children enslaved.
— Thucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War 5.84-116 [10]
Thucydides shows a small city destroyed as a community after defying a superior power.
It illustrates the Kural’s warning that whole households perish when the mighty are slighted, though Thucydides invites sympathy for Melos.

Real Life Example

The last Qing emperor abdicates
12 February 1912 · Beijing, China
In October 1911 a revolt broke out in Wuchang (Wuhan) and spread across China, and the revolutionary leader Sun Yat-sen was chosen as provisional president of the new republic. On 12 February 1912 the six-year-old emperor Puyi was forced to abdicate, ending 267 years of Qing rule and China's imperial system of some 2,000 years. Under the settlement Sun handed the presidency to Yuan Shikai, and Puyi was allowed to live on in the palace.
— Sources: [11] [12]
A dynasty that had ruled China for centuries and seemed permanent fell within months once the revolution gathered strength.
The kural says that even those who seem permanently established will perish together with their lineage when the mountain-like great resolve against them, and the Qing dynasty fell along with the imperial system itself.
