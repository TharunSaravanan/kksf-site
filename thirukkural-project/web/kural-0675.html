Kural 675

The Method of Execution · Vinai Seyalvakai · வினை செயல்வகை

பொருள்கருவி காலம் வினையிடனொடு ஐந்தும் இருள்தீர எண்ணிச் செயல்.
Porul karuvi kaalam vinai idanodu aindhum Irul theera ennich cheyal.

Meaning

Before you act, think clearly about five things until nothing is unclear: money, tools, timing, the task itself, and the place.

Elementary

Before baking a cake you check: Do I have the ingredients? Do I have a bowl and an oven? Do I have enough time before dinner? Do I know the recipe? Is the kitchen free? Once all five are clear, you start.

University

The kural lists five factors: porul (wealth, resources), karuvi (instruments, which Parimelazhagar extends to the army and other means), kaalam (time), vinai (the nature and method of the act itself) and idam (place). Irul theera, 'until the darkness is dispelled', means until every doubt has been removed. Parimelazhagar and Solomon Pappaiah add that each factor must be weighed for both oneself and the enemy, so this is a comparative strategic appraisal and not just a checklist. The structure closely matches the Arthashastra's five limbs of counsel (1.15), which include means, men and materials, and the apportionment of place and time, and it summarises earlier Kural chapters on strength (48), time (49) and place (50). Older editions print the line with full sandhi (வினையிடனொ டைந்து / மிருடீர வெண்ணிச் செயல்).

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Kautilya lists five limbs of counsel: the means of starting an undertaking, the excellence of men and materials, the allotment of place and time, remedies against failure, and the successful completion of the work.
— Kautilya, Arthashastra 1.15 (trans. R. Shamasastry) [3]
The Arthashastra makes these five points the required agenda of any royal council before an undertaking.
Both texts insist on a fixed five-part review, with resources, instruments, place and time at its core, before any action is begun.
Christianity
“If the iron be blunt, and he do not whet the edge, then must he put to more strength: but wisdom is profitable to direct.”
— Ecclesiastes 10:10 (KJV) [4]
The Preacher observes that working with a blunt tool wastes effort, and wisdom lies in preparing the instrument first.
This is the Kural's karuvi point: examining your instruments beforehand is part of acting wisely.
Islam
“And prepare against them whatever you are able of power and of steeds of war by which you may terrify the enemy of Allah and your enemy.”
— Quran 8:60 (Sahih International) [5]
The Quran instructs the community to prepare material strength and means before any confrontation.
It asks for the same preparation of resources and instruments that the Kural's first two factors describe.
Judaism
Rebuilding Jerusalem's wall under threat, Nehemiah has the builders work with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other, posts guards at the exposed places, and sets working hours from dawn until the stars appear.
— Nehemiah 4:13-23 [4]
Nehemiah plans resources, tools, place and time together so the work can be finished despite enemies.
His careful planning of means, instruments, time and place is a working example of the Kural's five-fold review.
Buddhism
The Buddha lists five wrong times for striving (old age, illness, famine, danger and a split in the community) and five right times when conditions support effort.
— Anguttara Nikaya 5.54, Samaya Sutta [6]
In the Samaya Sutta he teaches that the success of practice depends on choosing favourable times and conditions.
This reflects the Kural's kaalam factor: an act must be weighed against the time and conditions in which it is attempted.
Jainism
“A person should decide upon fasting after taking into consideration his physical strength, stamina, faith, state of health, place and time.”
— Saman Suttam 445 (Tapasutra), English tr. on jainworld.com [7]
Jain teaching on austerity requires that a practice be undertaken only after weighing one's own capacities together with the place and the time.
Like the kural's fivefold review of resources, instruments, time, act and place before acting, this verse makes a deliberate survey of capacity, place and time the precondition of an undertaking.
Sikhism
“First, examine the merchandise, and then, make the deal.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1410, Salok Vaaraan Thay Vadheek, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Guru Nanak uses the trader's rule of inspecting goods before buying as a model for judging carefully before committing oneself.
The Kural tells one to think through resources, means, time, the act and place until no doubt remains before acting; Guru Nanak's saying states the same principle of full appraisal before commitment.
Greek Mythology
Calypso gives Odysseus a bronze axe, an adze and augers, shows him where the tall dry trees stand, and brings sailcloth; he builds his raft in four days and sets out on the fifth with a fair wind she sends.
— Homer, Odyssey 5.228-263 [9]
Odysseus' escape succeeds because materials, tools, place, craft and timing all come together before he sails.
The episode is almost a story version of the Kural's five factors: resources, instruments, time, the work itself and place.
Philosophy
Aristotle lists the circumstances of an action: who acts, what is done, about what or in what it is done, sometimes with what instrument, for what end, and how.
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III.1 (1111a) [10]
He sets out these particulars to show what a person must know for an act to be truly voluntary and deliberate.
Like the Kural, Aristotle breaks action into a fixed set of circumstances, including instrument, manner and setting, all of which must be clear before acting well.

Real Life Example

Nansen's Fram is designed to drift with the Arctic ice
1893-1896 · Arctic Ocean
To test the theory that sea ice drifts from Siberia across the Arctic towards Greenland, Fridtjof Nansen had the ship Fram built with a rounded, wide, shallow hull and almost no keel, so that pressure from the ice would lift it rather than crush it. The ship was insulated and stocked so that its crew could live aboard for up to five years. In September 1893 the Fram was deliberately frozen into the ice off the New Siberian Islands, and it drifted across the Arctic Ocean until it broke free north of Spitsbergen in 1896, bringing back significant scientific data.
— Sources: [11] [12] [13]
Nansen matched the vessel, the supplies, the time and the route to the nature of the task, and the expedition returned intact from a region where many ships had been crushed.
The kural asks that resources, instruments, time, the nature of the task and the place all be thought through until no confusion remains, and the Fram expedition was planned around exactly those five factors.
