Kural 874

Recognizing the Quality of Enmity · Pakaithirandherithal · பகைத்திறந்தெரிதல்

பகைநட்பாக் கொண்டொழுகும் பண்புடை யாளன் தகைமைக்கண் தங்கிற்று உலகு.
Pakainatpaak kontozhukum panputai yaalan thakaimaikkan thangitru ulaku.

Meaning

The world holds together because of the greatness of a person of good character who makes enemies into friends and lives that way.

Elementary

Imagine a kid who gets pushed by someone, and instead of pushing back, invites that person to play and they become best friends. The poet says people like that are so great that the whole world is held together by them.

University

After three couplets warning against enmity, Valluvar names the ideal: the paṇpuṭaiyāḷaṉ (பண்புடையாளன், ‘man of noble character’) who conducts himself (oḻukum) by making pakai into naṭpu (நட்பு, friendship). Takaimai (தகைமை) is greatness or excellence, and taṅkiṟṟu ulaku (‘the world abides’) is a strong claim: the stability of the world itself depends on such people. Mu. Varadarasanar speaks of the world resting in his magnanimity; Solomon Pappaiah reads it of a ruler who converts enemies and lives with them, so that the world falls within his greatness; Kalaignar has the whole world praising such a person; Sivayogi Sivakumar says the world endures through such honoured conduct. Pope renders it as ‘worthy rule can change his foes to friends’, keeping the political frame. The couplet links back to Chapter 87 and forward to the strategic advice in 875.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
When Vibhishana, brother of the enemy king Ravana, came seeking refuge, Rama overruled his advisers’ suspicions, accepted him as an ally, and declared that he would give protection to anyone who sought it.
— Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda (canto 18) [3]
Rama turned a member of the enemy household into a trusted friend and partner.
This is the Kural’s gracious person who makes friends of foes, and on whom the right order of the world rests.
Christianity
“But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;”
— Matthew 5:44 (KJV) [4]
Jesus commands active goodwill toward enemies rather than retaliation.
Both texts hold up treating an enemy with friendship as the mark of the highest character.
Islam
“And not equal are the good deed and the bad. Repel [evil] by that [deed] which is better; and thereupon, the one whom between you and him is enmity [will become] as though he was a devoted friend.”
— Qur’an 41:34 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur’an promises that answering hostility with goodness can turn an enemy into a close friend.
This is almost exactly the Kural’s ideal of turning enmity into friendship.
Judaism
“Others say: [Who is mighty?] Who turns an enemy into his friend.”
— Avot de-Rabbi Natan 23:1 (tr. A. Cohen, Soncino) [6]
Avot de-Rabbi Natan records the view that true might is the ability to make an enemy into a friend.
The rabbis and Valluvar both place the highest strength and greatness in converting enemies to friends.
Buddhism
The Buddha walked toward the feared bandit Angulimala, who was trying to kill him; through his words Angulimala laid down his weapons, became a monk, and later attained liberation.
— Angulimala Sutta, Majjhima Nikaya 86 [7]
The most dangerous enemy of the region was transformed into a disciple and a friend of the Sangha.
It shows the Kural’s ideal at its most dramatic: a deadly foe made into a friend.
Jainism
“There is not the least hatred in me, neither now, nor before, nor in future. …” ‘You know the truth and the Law; you are not angry, compassionate sage; we take refuge at your feet, we together with all of them.’”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 12.32–33 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
After the priests and their followers have beaten him, the sage Harikeśa declares that he has no hatred at all, and his former attackers become his devotees and take refuge with him.
The Kural praises the noble person who turns enemies into friends, and Harikeśa, holding no enmity, turns his attackers into people who seek refuge at his feet.
Sikhism
According to the Janamsakhi tradition retold by Macauliffe, robbers (thags) on the road surrounded Guru Nanak meaning to rob him; he gave them spiritual instruction, and they gave up their trade, began to repeat the Name, and reformed their lives.
— Max Arthur Macauliffe, The Sikh Religion, vol. 1 (1909), Life of Guru Nanak, ch. V, pp. 71-72 (paraphrase) [9]
Instead of fleeing or fighting, Guru Nanak turned men who came as his attackers into followers of the Name.
The Kural praises the noble person who turns enemies into friends; in this story Guru Nanak does exactly that with men who came to rob him.
Greek Mythology
At the end of Aeschylus’ Eumenides, Athena persuades the angry Furies, who threaten to blight Athens, to accept honour in the city; they become the Kindly Ones and bless the land instead.
— Aeschylus, Eumenides (closing scenes) [10]
Athena turns implacable avengers into protectors through persuasion rather than force.
The city’s safety rests on this conversion, just as the Kural says the world rests on those who make foes into friends.
Philosophy
Diogenes Laertius records the sage Cleobulus as advising that one should do good to friends to keep them friends, and to enemies to make them friends.
— Diogenes Laertius, Lives of Eminent Philosophers 1.91 (Cleobulus) [11]
One of the Seven Sages makes winning over enemies a deliberate aim of kindness.
The Kural praises exactly this practice of turning enemies into friends.

Real Life Example

De Gaulle and Adenauer sign the Elysee Treaty
22 January 1963 · Elysee Palace, Paris, France
On 22 January 1963 French President Charles de Gaulle and West German Chancellor Konrad Adenauer signed a treaty of friendship between their two countries, long-standing enemies. The treaty set up regular consultations at every level of government on foreign, defence and youth policy and led to the Franco-German Youth Office. The two leaders had begun their rapprochement at Colombey-les-Deux-Eglises in 1958 and marked it publicly at a reconciliation mass in Reims Cathedral.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Two long-standing enemies became the closest of partners, and their friendship became a foundation of European integration and peace.
The kural says the world rests on the greatness of those who turn enemies into friends, and the Elysee Treaty turned Europe's oldest rivalry into a lasting friendship.
