Kural 936

Gaming (Gambling) · Soodhu · சூது

அகடாரார் அல்லல் உழப்பர்சூ தென்னும் முகடியான் மூடப்பட் டார்.
Agadaaraar allal uzhapparsoo thennum mugadiyaan moodappat taar.

Meaning

People who fall under gambling, which is like the goddess of bad luck, will never even fill their stomachs, and they will suffer many troubles.

Elementary

Picture a big dark cloud named 'Bad Luck' that swallows people whole. Once it has you, you never get enough to eat and troubles keep coming. Valluvar says gambling is that cloud.

University

Mugadi (முகடி) is Misfortune personified, the goddess known in Tamil as Moodevi or Jyeshtha (மூதேவி), the inauspicious elder sister of Lakshmi. Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah both gloss it as மூதேவி, and Pope's verse renders it 'Misfortune's other name'. Moodappattaar (மூடப்பட்டார்) means 'those covered or engulfed', which Mu. Varadarasanar renders as 'swallowed' (விழுங்கப்பட்டவர்). Agadaaraar (அகடாரார்) is 'those whose belly (அகடு) is never filled', and allal uzhappar means 'will toil in affliction'. Following Parimelazhagar's two-world reading, Kesikan and the Pope prose gloss take the second clause to mean suffering in the next world as well. Kesikan's text on thirukkural.net seems to drop the negation ('வயிறார உணவைப் பெறுவதுடன்'), which is likely a transcription slip. The couplet text matches on both sites.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Sad is the gambler when he sees a matron, another's wife, and his well-ordered dwelling. He yokes the brown steeds in the early morning, and when the fire is cold sinks down an outcast.”
— Rig Veda 10.34.11, trans. Ralph T. H. Griffith [3]
The Vedic gambler, gripped by the dice, ends each day cold, homeless and outcast while others prosper.
This is the endless affliction the Kural says comes to those engulfed by gambling.
Christianity
“And he would fain have filled his belly with the husks that the swine did eat: and no man gave unto him.”
— Luke 15:16 (KJV) [4]
The ruined prodigal son is so hungry he envies the pigs' food.
His unfilled belly is almost word for word the Kural's agadaaraar.
Islam
“...intoxicants, gambling, [sacrificing on] stone alters [to other than Allah], and divining arrows are but defilement from the work of Satan...”
— Qur'an 5:90 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur'an presents gambling as the work of a malign spiritual power.
Like the Kural's Mugadi, gambling is pictured as an evil force that takes hold of its victims.
Judaism
“A lover of money is never satisfied with money, nor a lover of wealth, with income. That too is futile.”
— Ecclesiastes 5:9 (Hebrew numbering; JPS Tanakh, Gender-Sensitive Edition, 2023, on Sefaria) [6]
Kohelet observes that chasing money never brings satisfaction.
The Kural's gamblers whose bellies are never full show the same insatiable want.
Buddhism
“Who plays with dice and drinks intoxicants, goes to women who are dear unto others as their own lives, associates with the mean and not with elders — he declines just as the moon during the waning half.”
— Sigalovada Sutta (DN 31), trans. Narada Thera [7]
The Buddha's verse describes the dice-player steadily waning like the moon.
The waning moon slowly covered by darkness parallels the Kural's gambler covered by Misfortune.
Jainism
“Then when death comes at last, the fool trembles in fear; he dies the 'death against one's will,' (having lost his chance) like a gambler vanquished by Kali.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 5.16 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
Mahāvīra compares the fool who dies unprepared to a gambler beaten by Kali, the losing throw of the dice, who has thrown away his chance.
Valluvar calls gambling a goddess of misfortune that swallows its victims and leaves them in want and sorrow; the Uttarādhyayana likewise uses the gambler overcome by Kali, the throw of ill luck, as its image of utter defeat.
Sikhism
“Thieves, adulterers and gamblers are pressed like seeds in the mill.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1288, Raag Malaar, Malaar ki Vaar, Salok, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Nanak names the gambler with the thief and the adulterer as people whose own deeds grind them, as an oil press crushes seeds.
The Kural says those swallowed by the misfortune called gambling will suffer many sorrows, and Guru Nanak’s gamblers, crushed in the mill, are suffering exactly that.
Greek Mythology
Herodotus reports a Lydian tradition that during a long famine the Lydians invented dice and other games, playing on one day so as not to think of food and eating on the next.
— Herodotus, Histories 1.94 (trans. George Rawlinson) [10]
The legend links the very origin of dice-games with hunger that could not be satisfied.
Dice and an empty belly go together in both texts, though Herodotus treats the games as a way to cope rather than a cause.
Philosophy
“We do not seek that easy and peaceful lot which permits us to think of our unhappy condition, nor the dangers of war, nor the labour of office, but the bustle which averts these thoughts of ours, and amuses us.”
— Blaise Pascal, Pensées §139 (trans. W. F. Trotter) [11]
Pascal argues that people chase play and bustle to escape facing their own wretchedness.
The Kural's gambler, covered by Misfortune, is trapped in this kind of distraction and never filled.

Real Life Example

Girolamo Cardano's gambling sends his family to the poorhouse
1533 · Gallarate and Milan, Duchy of Milan (now Italy)
The physician and mathematician Girolamo Cardano, later famous for the Ars Magna, had squandered his father's small bequest and turned to cards, dice and chess to make a living. In 1533, unable to practise medicine after the Milan College of Physicians refused him, he went back to gambling, and things went so badly that he had to pawn his wife's jewellery and some of their furniture. When the family moved to Milan they fared even worse and had to enter the poorhouse; his biographers note that gambling remained an addiction for many years that cost him time, money and reputation.
— Sources: [12] [13]
One of the finest minds of the Renaissance, who even wrote the first mathematical study of games of chance, could not keep his family fed while gambling held him.
The kural says those swallowed by the misfortune called gambling will never have their bellies filled and will suffer many sorrows, and Cardano's gambling brought his household to the poorhouse.
