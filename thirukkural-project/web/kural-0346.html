Kural 346

Renunciation · Thuravu · துறவு

யானென தென்னுஞ் செருக்கறுப்பான் வானோர்க் குயர்ந்த உலகம் புகும்.
Yaanenadhu ennum serukkaruppaan vaanork kuyarndha ulagam pugum.

Meaning

A person who cuts away the pride of thinking “I” and “mine” will reach a world higher even than the world of the gods.

Elementary

Imagine a kid who stops shouting 'Mine! Mine!' about every toy and stops acting like everything is about them. This kural says a person who drops 'me' and 'mine' reaches a place even better than heaven.

University

Yaan (“I”) and enadhu (“mine”) are the Tamil counterparts of ahankara and mamakara; Parimelazhagar glosses them as mistaking the body for the self and taking unconnected things as one's own, and calls this pairing serukku — pride or delusion. The reward, vaanorkku uyarndha ulagam (“a world higher than the gods'”), is read by traditional commentators as liberation (veedu), which lies beyond even the heavens, themselves impermanent. Karunanidhi, consistent with his rationalist reading, renders it instead as a renown surpassing heaven's. The couplet thus makes ego itself, not property, the root to be severed — property is simply 'mine' extended outward. The parallel with Gita 2.71 (nirmamo nirahankarah, 'without mine, without ego') is almost verbatim.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“A person who has given up all desires for sense gratification, who lives free from desires, who has given up all sense of proprietorship and is devoid of false ego—he alone can attain real peace.”
— Bhagavad Gita 2.71 (Prabhupada, As It Is) [3]
Krishna names the pair nirmama (without 'mine') and nirahankara (without 'I') as the conditions for supreme peace.
The Kural's yaan/enadhu is the exact Tamil counterpart of the Gita's ahankara/mamata; both promise the highest state to the one who cuts them.
Christianity
“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me”
— Galatians 2:20 (KJV) [4]
Paul describes the death of his old, self-centred ego and its replacement by Christ living within him.
Both texts see the dissolution of the ego-'I' as the doorway to a higher life.
Islam
“He said, "I was only given it because of knowledge I have.”
— Quran 28:78 (Sahih International) [5]
Qarun, fabulously rich, credits his wealth to himself; the Quran recounts his arrogance and his destruction.
A negative mirror of the Kural: Qarun's 'I' and 'mine' is exactly the pride the Kural says must be cut away.
Judaism
“be exceeding humble spirit, for the end of man is the worm.”
— Pirkei Avot 4:4 (tr. Joshua Kulp, Sefaria) [6]
Rabbi Levitas of Yavneh urges extreme humility, grounded in the reminder of mortality.
Humility here is the Jewish counterpart to cutting off the pride of 'I' and 'mine'.
Buddhism
“The fool worries, thinking, "I have sons, I have wealth." Indeed, when he himself is not his own, whence are sons, whence is wealth?”
— Dhammapada 62 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Buddha mocks the anxious claim to own sons and wealth, pointing out that the self does not even own itself.
Both expose the 'I' and the 'mine' as the root delusion; the Buddha adds that the 'I' is not even one's own.
Jainism
“He who ceasing from acts relinquishes the idea of property, relinquishes property itself. That sage has seen the path (to final liberation) for whom there exists no property.”
— Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.2.6.2 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22, 1884, pp. 25-26) [8]
The Ācārāṅga puts the root of possession in the mind. The real renunciation is giving up the idea of 'mine', and the sage for whom nothing is 'his' has found the road to liberation.
Valluvar says that cutting away the pride of 'I' and 'mine' leads to a world above the gods. The Ācārāṅga likewise makes dropping the sense of 'mine' the doorway to final liberation, which in Jain cosmology lies above every heaven.
Sikhism
“As long as I claimed, ‘Mine, mine!’, I was surrounded by wickedness and corruption. But when I dedicated my mind, body and intellect to my Lord and Master, then I began to sleep in peace.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 214, Raag Gauree (Gauree Maalaa), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Arjan names possessiveness, the cry of mine, as the cause of entanglement, and says peace came only when mind, body and intellect were offered to God.
The Kural promises a world beyond the gods to one who cuts off the pride of I and mine; Guru Arjan describes the same cutting off of mine as the passage from corruption into peace.
Greek Mythology
In the Myth of Er, the soul of Odysseus, cured of ambition by the memory of his former toils, chooses last and searches out the neglected life of a private man with no cares.
— Plato, Republic 10.620c-d [10]
At the close of the Republic, souls choose their next lives; Odysseus, remembering his past, gladly takes a humble, obscure one.
Odysseus's rejection of glory and self-importance is a Greek image of cutting away pride — though in Plato he still returns to earth.
Philosophy
“But you, after all, are neither property nor style.”
— Epictetus, Enchiridion 44 (tr. Elizabeth Carter) [11]
Epictetus corrects the reasoning 'I am richer, so I am better', insisting that a person is not identical with what he owns or displays.
Separating the self from 'mine' is exactly the cut the Kural demands.

Real Life Example

Pierre and Marie Curie decline to patent the radium process
c. 1898-1904 · Paris, France
After discovering radium, Pierre and Marie Curie published in detail the methods they had used to isolate it, without patenting any of them. When industrial production of radium began and the question arose whether they should patent the production process, both opposed it, holding that research should be done for its own sake and not mixed with commercial profit. A large radium industry grew up, from which they did not profit by patents.
— Sources: [12] [13]
The Curies chose not to claim as their own property a discovery that could have made them rich. Their decision kept the method open to all researchers and industries.
The kural praises cutting away the pride of 'I' and 'mine', and the Curies refused to treat their discovery as 'mine' to be owned and sold.
