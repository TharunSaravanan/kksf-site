Kural 457

Avoiding Mean Associations · Sitrinanjeraamai · சிற்றினஞ்சேராமை

மனநலம் மன்னுயிர்க் காக்கம் இனநலம் எல்லாப் புகழுந் தரும்.
Mananalam mannuyirk kaakkam inanalam Ellaap pugazhum tharum.

Meaning

A good mind brings a person success and plenty. Good friends bring them every kind of praise and fame.

Elementary

A good heart helps you do well in life. But good friends give you something extra: they help people everywhere know and respect you.

University

Here the parallelism moves from purity (தூய்மை) to goodness (நலம்), and the rewards from legacy to worldly outcomes: ஆக்கம் (prosperity, growth) from the mind, எல்லாப் புகழும் (every kind of fame) from company. The word எல்லா (‘all’) quietly ranks the second above the first. Prosperity may be private, but fame is by nature social and so depends on one’s circle. The couplet explains why reputation, discussed in 453, is not just vanity: in the Porul context of kingship and public life, renown is a resource, and it flows through association.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Vibhishana, righteous in mind, leaves his brother Ravana’s court and takes refuge with Rama. Through that company he wins the throne of Lanka and lasting honour as a model devotee.
— Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda [4]
His goodness of mind was real, but his renown came through the company he joined.
This fits the Kural’s two rewards exactly: mind gives worth, and company gives fame.
Christianity
“Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.”
— Acts 4:13 (KJV) [5]
Ordinary fishermen are recognised and honoured because of the company they had kept.
This is close to the Kural’s claim that goodness of company confers renown.
Islam
“And the first forerunners [in the faith] among the Muhajireen and the Ansar and those who followed them with good conduct — Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him.”
— Qur’an 9:100 (Sahih International) [6]
The early community, and those who followed them in goodness, are honoured as a group.
This fits the Kural’s idea that honour comes through belonging to a good company.
Judaism
Ruth the Moabite binds herself to Naomi (‘whither thou goest, I will go’), joins Naomi’s people, and becomes the great-grandmother of King David.
— Ruth 1:16; 4:13–17 (KJV) [5]
Her loyal goodness, joined to a good household, led to a renowned lineage.
This fits the Kural’s claim that goodness of company brings renown.
Buddhism
“Admirable friendship, admirable companionship, admirable camaraderie is actually the whole of the holy life.”
— Upaddha Sutta, Samyutta Nikaya 45.2 (trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu) [7]
When Ananda suggests that good friendship is half of the spiritual life, the Buddha corrects him: it is the whole.
Like the Kural, it treats good company as the source of every good, not just a helpful extra.
Jainism
“Even the hare in the wide, beauteous, and sublime heavens, since it is seen in the moon with refulgent beams, will be adored (by men). And in like manner even those who are without any dignity (of their own), if they obtain the love of the good, who are as mountains of virtue, will have dignity.”
— Naladiyar, ch. 18 'Good Society', stanza 6 (English tr. in The Indian Antiquary; wisdomlib.org) [8]
The Naladiyar says that just as the hare seen on the moon is honoured because of the moon, people who have no standing of their own gain dignity through the affection of the good.
Valluvar says goodness of company brings every kind of renown. The Naladiyar's hare in the moon shows good company conferring honour on someone who would otherwise have none.
Sikhism
“The poor castor oil plant, growing near the sandalwood tree, becomes fragrant; in the same way, the sinner, associating with the Saints, becomes acceptable and approved.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 861, Raag Gond, Guru Ram Das (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Ram Das says even the lowly castor plant takes on sandalwood's fragrance, and even a sinner gains honour and acceptance through the company of the saints.
The Kural says good company brings every kind of renown; Guru Ram Das shows company raising even the humblest to honour and approval.
Greek Mythology
Patroclus, the companion of Achilles, wins lasting fame in the Iliad; his name is bound up with that of the greatest of the Achaeans.
— Homer, Iliad, Books 16–23 [10]
His glory is closely tied to the company he kept.
This illustrates the Kural’s point that renown flows through good association.
Philosophy
“Associate with those who will make a better man of you. Welcome those whom you yourself can improve.”
— Seneca, Moral Letters to Lucilius 7 (trans. Gummere) [11]
Seneca tells Lucilius to choose companions who raise him up.
This shares the Kural’s view that goodness of company is a source of advancement.

Real Life Example

The first Impressionist exhibition
15 April 1874 · Paris, France
Frustrated by the official Salon, a group of about thirty artists formed a cooperative society and opened their own exhibition in the former studio of the photographer Nadar on the boulevard des Capucines. Among them were Claude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Edgar Degas, Camille Pissarro, Paul Cezanne and Berthe Morisot. The show drew few visitors and hostile reviews, and a critic mocked the group as impressionists after Monet's painting Impression, Sunrise, but the name stuck and the group went on to hold eight exhibitions by 1886.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Artists who would have struggled alone gained a shared identity, a platform and eventually world fame by showing together.
The kural says goodness of company brings every kind of renown, and the Impressionists' fame came to them as a group, under a single name.
