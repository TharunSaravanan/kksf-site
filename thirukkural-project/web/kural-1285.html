Kural 1285

Desire for Reunion · Punarchi Vidhumpal · புணர்ச்சி விதும்பல்

எழுதுங்கால் கோல்காணாக் கண்ணேபோல் கொண்கன் பழிகாணேன் கண்ட இடத்து.
Ezhudhungaal koalkaanaak kannaepoal konkan pazhikaanaen kanda idaththu.

Meaning

Just as the eye cannot see the brush that puts dark eye makeup on it, I cannot see my husband's faults at the moment I see him.

Elementary

Your eye can see everything around it, but it cannot see the little brush that touches it when someone puts on eyeliner, because it is too close. The wife says her husband’s faults are like that brush: when he is right in front of her, she cannot see them.

University

The simile draws on the practice of applying mai (collyrium, kohl) with a slender rod (kōl): eḻutuṅkāl, ‘while painting/applying’, the eye does not see the kōl, though it saw it a moment earlier. So too, kaṇṭa iṭattu (‘at the place/time of seeing him’), she does not see the paḻi (fault, blame) of her koṇkaṉ (husband). Solomon Pappaiah makes the comparison exact: the eye saw the rod before but not while it paints; she brooded on his fault in his absence but cannot see it in his presence. Kalaignar names the fault as his having left her. Mu. Varadarasanar and Puliyur Kesikan follow the same line. The domestic, almost tactile image is characteristic of the Kural’s Inbam section, and the couplet pairs closely with the next (1286). The text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
When Nala, who had abandoned Damayanti in the forest, is at last recognised and restored to her, she receives him without reproach, and the couple are reunited in joy.
— Mahabharata, Vana Parva, Nalopakhyana (paraphrase; cf. tr. K.M. Ganguli) [3]
The Mahabharata’s story of Nala and Damayanti ends with a wife whose grievance over desertion gives way entirely to reunion.
Like the Kural’s wife, Damayanti cannot hold her husband’s fault against him once he stands before her.
Christianity
“And above all things have fervent charity among yourselves: for charity shall cover the multitude of sins.”
— 1 Peter 4:8 (KJV) [4]
Peter teaches that fervent love covers over many faults.
The Kural’s wife experiences this directly: in her husband’s presence, love hides his faults from her sight.
Islam
“He said, ‘No blame will there be upon you today. Allah will forgive you; and He is the most merciful of the merciful.’”
— Qur’an 12:92 (Saheeh International) [5]
Joseph, meeting the brothers who wronged him, declares at once that he holds no blame against them.
As in the Kural, the moment of meeting erases the fault that had been borne in absence.
Judaism
“He that covereth a transgression seeketh love; but he that harpeth on a matter estrangeth a familiar friend.”
— Proverbs 17:9 (JPS 1917, via Mechon-Mamre) [6]
Proverbs teaches that love chooses to cover an offence, while dwelling on it drives people apart.
The Kural’s wife does not ‘harp on the matter’ once she sees her husband; love covers his fault.
Buddhism
The Dhammapada observes that the faults of others are easily seen, while one’s own are hard to see, and that people winnow others’ faults like chaff.
— Dhammapada 252 (paraphrase; cf. tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
Buddhist teaching notes how perception of faults is distorted by one’s position and attachments.
The Kural shows the reverse distortion: love so close that it cannot see the beloved’s fault, like the eye and the brush.
Jainism
“As the crane is produced from an egg, and the egg is produced from a crane, so they call desire the origin of delusion, and delusion the origin of desire.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 32.6 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
The Uttaradhyayana teaches that desire and delusion feed each other, clouding clear perception.
The Kural’s wife cannot see her husband’s faults in his presence; in Jain terms her desire produces exactly this blindness (moha).
Sikhism
“He covers the faults of His servant.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 285, Raag Gauree, Sukhmani, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the Sukhmani, Guru Arjan says the Lord hides and overlooks the faults of His devotee.
The Kural's wife cannot see her husband's faults when she sees him, as the eye cannot see the kohl brush; Guru Arjan's line, in reverse, has the Beloved overlooking the devotee's faults.
Greek Mythology
When Troy fell, Menelaus went to kill his faithless wife Helen, but at the sight of her he threw down his sword and took her back.
— Euripides, Andromache 627-631 (paraphrase; cf. Aristophanes, Lysistrata 155-156) [10]
In the tradition recorded by Euripides and others, Helen’s presence alone undoes Menelaus’s resolve to punish her.
The Kural’s wife likewise finds the fault she meant to punish invisible once she sees her husband.
Philosophy
Socrates remarks that a lover finds every feature of his beloved charming: a snub nose he calls winsome, a hooked nose royal, a dark skin manly, a pale one divine, so that no blemish is ever seen.
— Plato, Republic V, 474d-475a (paraphrase; cf. tr. Benjamin Jowett) [11]
Plato observes that love transforms perception so that the beloved’s defects disappear or become virtues.
This is the Kural’s insight exactly: in the beloved’s presence the lover cannot see faults.

Real Life Example

Ulysses S. Grant tells Julia to keep the eyes he fell in love with
Late 1863 · St. Louis, Missouri, United States
Julia Dent Grant had strabismus (crossed eyes) all her life and was self-conscious about it, especially once her husband became famous during the Civil War. In late 1863 she asked the St. Louis surgeon Charles Pope about an operation to straighten her eyes and was told it was too late. In her memoirs she recorded that when she told her husband, Ulysses S. Grant told her he had fallen in love with her with those same eyes, liked them as they were, and did not want her to change them.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
Grant simply did not see as a flaw what Julia feared made her a plain wife for a great man.
The kural says that just as the eye cannot see the kohl brush touching it, the lover cannot see the beloved’s faults when close to them, and Grant could not see his wife’s squint as a fault at all.
