Kural 1032

Agriculture · Uzhavu · உழவு

உழுவார் உலகத்தார்க்கு ஆணிஅஃ தாற்றாது எழுவாரை எல்லாம் பொறுத்து.
Uzhuvaar ulagaththaarkku aaNi-aq thaatraadhu ezhuvaarai ellaam poRuththu.

Meaning

Farmers are like the pin that holds a wheel in place for the world, because they feed all the people who do not farm and do other work instead.

Elementary

A wagon wheel has a small pin that holds it on the axle. Without that pin the wheel falls off and the whole cart stops. Valluvar says farmers are that pin: they feed everyone else, so the whole world keeps rolling because of them.

University

The image is the aaNi (ஆணி), the linchpin or axle-pin that keeps a cart's wheel in place; the ploughmen are the aaNi of ulagaththaar (உலகத்தார், 'the people of the world'). The second line gives the reason: aqthu aatraadhu ezhuvaarai ellaam poRuththu (அஃது ஆற்றாது எழுவாரை எல்லாம் பொறுத்து), 'bearing all who, unable to do that (ploughing), go off to other work'. Puliyur Kesikan, in the line of Parimelazhagar, glosses ezhuvaar as those who leave farming for other trades and are still fed by the farmers' grain; Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah read it the same way, and Pope gives 'linch-pin of the world'. The Tamil text matches on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net; ytamizh hyphenates the transliteration aaNi-aq.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“The ploughshare ploughing makes the food that feeds us, and with its feet cuts through the path it follows.”
— Rig Veda 10.117.7 (tr. Ralph T.H. Griffith, 1896, sacred-texts.com) [3]
In a hymn on liberality, the Vedic poet names the plough as the maker of the food everyone eats.
The Rig Veda and the Kural both say that the plough feeds us all.
Christianity
“Moreover the profit of the earth is for all: the king himself is served by the field.”
— Ecclesiastes 5:9 (KJV) [4]
The Preacher observes that the yield of the land supports everyone, the king included.
Like the Kural's linchpin, the field carries everyone, from commoner to king.
Islam
“There is none amongst the Muslims who plants a tree or sows seeds, and then a bird, or a person or an animal eats from it, but is regarded as a charitable gift for him.”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 2320 (sunnah.com) [5]
The Prophet counts every mouthful taken from a crop, even by birds and animals, as the sower's charity.
The farmer feeds all who do not sow, which is the Kural's picture of the ploughman bearing the world.
Judaism
“You make the grass grow for the cattle, and herbage for humans’ labor that they may get food out of the earth— … and bread that sustains human life.”
— Psalms 104:14-15 (JPS Tanakh, Gender-Sensitive Edition 2023, via Sefaria) [6]
The psalmist praises the order in which human labour on the earth yields the bread that keeps people alive.
Bread from tilled earth sustains human life, which is the support the Kural credits to farmers.
Buddhism
At sowing time the brahman Kasi Bharadvaja had about 500 ploughs yoked and was running a food distribution when the Buddha arrived for alms; he told the Buddha that he ploughed and sowed before he ate, and that a contemplative should do the same.
— Kasi Bharadvaja Sutta, Sn 1.4 (tr. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, accesstoinsight.org) [7]
The scene shows a farming household whose harvest feeds many people, including wandering monks who come for alms.
The farmer's food distribution feeding the non-ploughing alms-seeker is the Kural's ploughman carrying 'all who cannot till.'
Jainism
The Dasavaikalika Sutra opens by comparing the true monk to a bee that drinks a little from many flowers without harming them, gathering alms from householders' kitchens without burdening any one of them.
— Daśavaikālika Sūtra, Lecture 1 (the bee simile) [8]
Jain mendicants do not cook or farm. They live on small portions of food that householders have prepared for themselves.
This is a Jain picture of the non-producing world being carried by householders' food, the dependence the Kural describes.
Sikhism
“The farmers love to work their farms; they plow and work the fields, so that their sons and daughters may eat.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 166, Raag Gauri, Guru Ram Das (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Ram Das describes the farmer who ploughs with full effort so that his children may be fed, and uses this ordinary devotion as a simile for the devotee's love of the Lord's Name.
The Kural calls ploughmen the linchpin that carries everyone who does not till; Guru Ram Das pictures the farmer's toil as what keeps a whole household fed.
Greek Mythology
“Both gods and men are angry with a man who lives idle, for in nature he is like the stingless drones who waste the labour of the bees, eating without working”
— Hesiod, Works and Days 303-306 (tr. H.G. Evelyn-White, 1914, sacred-texts.com) [10]
Hesiod compares people who eat without working to drones living on the bees' labour.
This is the Kural's linchpin seen from the other side: workers carry those who do not till.
Philosophy
“Cultivators of the earth are the most valuable citizens. They are the most vigorous, the most independant, the most virtuous, and they are tied to their country and wedded to it’s liberty and interests by the most lasting bands.”
— Thomas Jefferson to John Jay, 23 August 1785 (Founders Online, National Archives) [11]
Jefferson ranks farmers above every other class as the foundation of the republic.
Jefferson's 'most valuable citizens' are the Kural's linchpin of the world, put in civic terms.

Real Life Example

Quesnay's Tableau economique names farmers the only productive class
1758 · Versailles, France
In 1758 Francois Quesnay, physician at the court of Louis XV, drew up the Tableau economique, a diagram of how wealth circulates through a nation. It divided society into landowners, a productive class of farmers and farm labourers, and a sterile class of artisans and merchants, holding that agriculture alone produced the net surplus on which all the others lived.
— Sources: [12] [13]
The Tableau founded the Physiocratic school and is regarded as one of the first models of a whole economy.
The kural calls ploughmen the linchpin of the world because they bear everyone who does other work, and Quesnay's model made exactly that claim in economic form.
