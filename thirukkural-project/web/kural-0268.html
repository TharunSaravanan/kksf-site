Kural 268

Penance · Thavam · தவம்

தன்னுயிர் தான்அறப் பெற்றானை ஏனைய மன்னுயி ரெல்லாந் தொழும்.
Thannuyir thaan arap petraanai enaiya Mannuyir ellaam thozhum.

Meaning

A person who has fully let go of thinking about “me” and “my life” is honored by every other living being in the world.

Elementary

Most of us think ‘me first’. A person who, after lots of practice, stops thinking ‘me, me, me’ becomes so good and gentle that everyone — people and even animals — looks up to them.

University

The first line is famously compressed: தன்னுயிர் தான் அறப் பெற்றான் can be read as ‘one who has become rid of [attachment to] his own life and of the ego “I”’ (Parimelazhagar, Varadarasan) or as ‘one who has fully gained possession of his own self’ (Pope’s ‘utter self-control’). Both readings converge on self-mastery culminating in egolessness. The consequence — ‘all other enduring lives worship him’ — echoes the Indian belief, also found in Jain and yogic texts, that the ahiṃsā-perfected sage commands the reverence even of animals. Placed after 267, the verse presents egolessness as the gold that emerges from the refining fire.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Patañjali teaches that when a yogi is firmly established in non-violence, hostility is abandoned by all beings in his presence.
— Yoga Sutras 2.35 [3]
The perfected ascetic radiates a peace that transforms how all creatures relate to him.
Close to ‘all living beings worship him’ — the universal response to the perfected self.
Christianity
“He humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross. Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow.”
— Philippians 2:8-10 (KJV) [4]
Christ’s total self-emptying (kenosis) is followed by universal homage.
Same arc: complete surrender of self leads to worship by all beings.
Islam
“Charity does not decrease wealth, no one forgives another except that Allah increases his honor, and no one humbles himself for the sake of Allah except that Allah raises his status.”
— Sahih Muslim 2588 [5]
Self-effacement before God leads to exaltation.
Egolessness brings honour, as in the Kural.
Judaism
“He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city.”
— Proverbs 16:32 (KJV) [4]
Self-mastery is ranked above military conquest.
Affirms the supreme worth of the self-conquered person that the Kural celebrates.
Buddhism
“Even the gods hold dear the wise one, whose senses are subdued like horses well trained by a charioteer, whose pride is destroyed and who is free from the cankers.”
— Dhammapada 94 [6]
The arahant whose pride is gone is cherished even by the gods.
Near-exact match: pride (ego) destroyed → revered by all beings, even divine ones.
Jainism
“Happy are we, happy live we who call nothing our own; when Mithilâ is on fire, nothing is burned that belongs to me.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 9.14 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [7]
Nami, having given up his kingdom, is free of every sense of 'mine'; at the end of the dialogue Indra himself reveals his true form and pays reverence to him again and again (9.59).
The Kural says one free of 'I' and 'mine' is worshipped by all beings; Nami shows that freedom from possession, and the king of the gods bows before him.
Sikhism
“Among all persons, the supreme person is the one who gives up his egotistical pride in the Company of the Holy.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 266, Raag Gauree, Sukhmani Sahib, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
In the Sukhmani Guru Arjan says the greatest of all people is the one whose ego (haumai) has been erased in holy company.
The Kural says the one who has lost the sense of 'I' and 'mine' is worshipped by all; Guru Arjan likewise ranks the ego-free person above everyone.
Greek Mythology
Heracles, after completing his grievous labours, is received among the immortals on Olympus, given Hebe as wife, and honoured as a god.
— Hesiod, Theogony 950-955 [9]
Endured toil ends in apotheosis and worship.
Endurance leading to universal honour; but Heracles’ path is heroic, not ego-renouncing.
Philosophy
“But verily, if I were not Alexander, I would be Diogenes.”
— Plutarch, Life of Alexander 14 (trans. B. Perrin) [10]
After the self-denying Cynic asks the world-conqueror merely to stand out of his sun, Alexander confesses his admiration.
The man who wants nothing commands the reverence even of the most powerful — the Kural’s point in miniature.

Real Life Example

A million mourners honour Brother André, the humble doorkeeper
January 1937 · Montreal, Quebec, Canada
Alfred Bessette, known as Brother André, was a lay brother of the Congregation of Holy Cross who served for decades as doorkeeper of the Collège Notre-Dame in Montreal, welcoming the sick and troubled who came to him. He was the driving force behind the building of Saint Joseph's Oratory on Mount Royal. When he died on 6 January 1937, more than a million people came to pay their respects, and he was canonized in 2010.
— Sources: [11] [12] [13]
A man with no rank, wealth or education beyond the basics drew one of the largest outpourings of public reverence in Canadian history.
The kural says the one who has let go of the sense of I and mine is worshipped by all, and a self-effacing porter was honoured by multitudes.
