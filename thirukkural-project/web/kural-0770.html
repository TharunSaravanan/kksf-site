Kural 770

The Excellence of an Army · Padaimaatchi · படைமாட்சி

நிலைமக்கள் சால உடைத்தெனினும் தானை தலைமக்கள் இல்வழி இல்.
Nilaimakkal saala udaiththeninum thaanai thalaimakkal ilvazhi il.

Meaning

Even if an army has many loyal soldiers, it cannot last without good leaders.

Elementary

A choir may have lots of good singers, but without a conductor they start and stop at different times and it sounds like a mess. Valluvar says an army is the same: many brave soldiers are not enough without a good leader.

University

Nilaimakkaḷ (நிலைமக்கள்) are ‘steadfast men’, read by Parimelazhagar as long-serving, permanent soldiers, and cāla (சால) means ‘in abundance’; talaimakkaḷ (தலைமக்கள்) are the ‘head-men’, i.e. commanders or generals. The final il vaḻi il (இல்வழி இல்) is a terse play: ‘where they are not, (the army) is not’, which Mu. Varadarasanar glosses as the army having no glory and Solomon Pappaiah and Kalaignar as its not being able to hold its ground. The couplet closes the chapter by turning from the troops to their leadership, preparing for the next chapter, Paṭaiccerukku (படைச்செருக்கு, the martial pride of warriors). Both sources agree (சந்தி: சால வுடைத்தெனினுந் ... தலைமக்க ளில்வழி யில்).

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Our strength is immeasurable, and we are perfectly protected by Grandfather Bhīṣma, whereas the strength of the Pāṇḍavas, carefully protected by Bhīma, is limited.”
— Bhagavad Gita 1.10 (Prabhupada, As It Is) [3]
Duryodhana measures the two armies chiefly by the generals who lead and protect them.
His reasoning assumes the Kural’s point: an army’s worth depends on its commander.
Christianity
“For if the trumpet give an uncertain sound, who shall prepare himself to the battle?”
— 1 Corinthians 14:8 (KJV) [4]
Paul uses the image of an unclear battle signal to argue for clear, intelligible speech in the church.
Without a clear lead, as the Kural says, even willing soldiers cannot act as an army.
Islam
“O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you.”
— Qur’an 4:59 (Saheeh International) [5]
The community is commanded to follow rightful authority so that it acts as one body.
It assumes, like the Kural, that a body of people needs recognised leaders to be effective.
Judaism
“Let the LORD, the God of the spirits of all flesh, set a man over the congregation, who may go out before them, and who may come in before them, and who may lead them out, and who may bring them in; that the congregation of the LORD be not as sheep which have no shepherd.”
— Numbers 27:16–17 (JPS 1917, Mechon-Mamre) [6]
Moses, nearing his death, asks God to appoint a leader for Israel, and Joshua is commissioned.
‘Go out before them’ is military language: without such a head, as the Kural says, the host is no host.
Buddhism
“If, dear sirs, when the devas have gone into battle, there should arise fear, terror, or horripilation, then on that occasion you should catch sight of the top of my standard. For when you have caught sight of the top of my standard, whatever fear, terror, or horripilation there is will be abandoned.”
— Samyutta Nikaya 11.3, Dhajagga Sutta (trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu) [7]
Sakka tells the gods’ army that the sight of their leader’s banner will steady them; the Buddha then offers the Triple Gem as a surer refuge.
An army steadied by sight of its leader is the Kural’s talaimakkaḷ principle.
Jainism
“There is now no Gina, but there is a highly esteemed guide to show the way; now being on the right path, Gautama, be careful all the while!”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 10.31 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
Even without a living Jina, the community of seekers depends on a respected guide to lead it along the path.
The Kural says that however many steadfast soldiers there are, an army cannot stand without worthy leaders, and the Jain verse likewise treats a trusted guide as essential to those on the path.
Sikhism
“If a hundred moons were to rise, and a thousand suns appeared, even with such light, there would still be pitch darkness without the Guru.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 463, Raag Aasaa (Asa di Vaar, salok), Guru Angad (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Angad's salok says that no amount of light is any use without the Guru to guide.
The Kural says an army with steadfast soldiers in plenty cannot stand without worthy leaders; Guru Angad says that a hundred moons and a thousand suns still leave darkness without the Guru.
Greek Mythology
“...we cannot all be kings; it is not well that there should be many masters; one man must be supreme—one king to whom the son of scheming Saturn has given the sceptre of sovereignty over you all.”
— Homer, Iliad, Book 2 (trans. Samuel Butler) [10]
Odysseus rallies the Achaeans, who are rushing for the ships, by insisting that an army needs one recognised leader.
The scene shows a host on the point of dissolving for want of command, as the Kural warns.
Philosophy
After the Greek generals are seized, Xenophon tells the remaining officers that without commanders nothing fine or useful can be done anywhere, least of all in war, where good order saves men and disorder has already destroyed many; they elect new generals at once.
— Xenophon, Anabasis 3.1.38 [11]
The leaderless Ten Thousand recover only by choosing new commanders.
It is a direct historical demonstration of the Kural: many good soldiers, but no army, until leaders are found.

Real Life Example

Darius III flees and his vast army collapses at Gaugamela
1 October 331 BCE · Gaugamela, northern Mesopotamia (present-day northern Iraq)
At Gaugamela the Persian king Darius III faced Alexander the Great with an army far larger than the Macedonian force, on a plain he had levelled for his chariots and cavalry. When Alexander drove toward Darius's position, Darius fled the field, as he had at Issus two years earlier. The Persian army broke and was routed, and Alexander went on to take Babylon, Susa and Persepolis.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A huge army of brave soldiers fell apart once its commander abandoned the field, deciding the fate of the Persian Empire.
The kural says that however many steadfast soldiers an army has, it cannot stand without worthy leaders, and Gaugamela shows a mighty host dissolving when its leader failed.
