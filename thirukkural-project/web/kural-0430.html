Kural 430

Knowledge Possession · Arivudaimai · அறிவுடைமை

அறிவுடையார் எல்லா முடையார் அறிவிலார் என்னுடைய ரேனும் இலர்.
Arivudaiyaar ellaa mudaiyaar arivilaar Ennudaiya renum ilar.

Meaning

People who have wisdom have everything. People without wisdom have nothing, whatever else they own.

Elementary

Imagine someone with a huge pile of money but no sense. They would probably lose it all or use it badly. Someone with good sense can build up whatever they need. Valluvar says good sense is the one thing that counts as having everything.

University

The chapter ends with a sweeping summary built on the verb உடைமை (possession) that gives the chapter its title. அறிவுடையார் எல்லாம் உடையார் means 'the possessors of wisdom are possessors of all'. என்னுடையரேனும் இலர் means 'whatever they possess, they have nothing'. The point is that wisdom is the meta-possession that makes other possessions useful and secure. Without it, wealth, power, and allies are squandered or turned against their holder. It is striking that this appears in Porutpal, the book on wealth and statecraft: Valluvar ranks arivu above porul (wealth) itself. The Talmud's 'if you have acquired knowledge, what do you lack?' and Socrates's argument in the Euthydemus that wisdom is the only unconditional good are close parallels. Solomon's story, in which he chooses wisdom and receives everything else too, dramatises the same claim.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“In this world, there is nothing so sublime and pure as transcendental knowledge. Such knowledge is the mature fruit of all mysticism.”
— Bhagavad Gita 4.38 [4]
Krishna declares that nothing in the world equals knowledge (jnana) in worth or purifying power.
Both texts rank wisdom above every other possession.
Christianity
“When Solomon asks God only for “an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad”, God grants it and adds: “I have also given thee that which thou hast not asked, both riches, and honour.”
— 1 Kings 3:9-13 (KJV) [5]
Solomon chooses discernment over wealth, long life, or victory, and receives the rest as well.
This dramatises the Kural: the one who has wisdom has everything.
Islam
“He gives wisdom to whom He wills, and whoever has been given wisdom has certainly been given much good. And none will remember except those of understanding.”
— Qur'an 2:269 (Sahih International) [6]
The Qur'an calls hikmah (wisdom) a gift containing 'much good' (khayran kathiran).
Both texts treat wisdom as the possession that brings abundant good.
Judaism
The Talmud cites a popular saying: if one has acquired knowledge (da'at), what does he lack? If one lacks knowledge, what has he acquired?
— Babylonian Talmud, Nedarim 41a (cf. Leviticus Rabbah 1:6) [7]
The rabbis hold that knowledge is the possession that makes every other possession worthwhile, and without it nothing else counts.
This is the Kural in both halves, with an almost identical structure.
Buddhism
“Better it is to live one day wise and meditative than to live a hundred years foolish and uncontrolled.”
— Dhammapada 111 [8]
The Dhammapada holds that a single day of wisdom outweighs a century of foolish life.
Both texts see wisdom as making a life full and its absence as making everything else worthless.
Jainism
“He who knows one thing, knows all things; and he who knows all things, knows one thing.”
— Acaranga Sutra I.3.4.1 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22) [9]
Jacobi's note explains that true knowledge of any one thing cannot be separated from true knowledge of everything. In Jain thought the soul's own nature is knowledge, which reaches its completion in omniscience (kevala-jñāna).
Valluvar says those who have wisdom have everything; the Acaranga makes a parallel claim about knowledge itself: whoever truly has it has, in principle, all of it.
Sikhism
“O Nanak, those who do not think of the Guru, and who think of themselves as clever, shall be left abandoned in the field, like the scattered sesame.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 463, Raag Aasaa (Asa di Vaar, salok), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
In Asa di Vaar Guru Nanak says the self-satisfied who ignore the Guru are like hollow sesame plants left behind in an empty field.
The Kural says those without wisdom have nothing, whatever else they own; Guru Nanak says those without the Guru's wisdom, however clever they think themselves, are left empty and abandoned.
Greek Mythology
Zeus swallows Metis, the goddess of wisdom and counsel, so that her wisdom becomes his own. Only then is his rule secure, and Athena is later born from his head.
— Hesiod, Theogony 886-900 [11]
In Hesiod, Zeus's supreme and lasting kingship depends on possessing wisdom within himself.
This mythically parallels the Kural: possessing wisdom secures possession of all.
Philosophy
Socrates argues that wealth, health, beauty, and power are neither good nor bad in themselves. Guided by wisdom they become great goods, and guided by ignorance great evils. He concludes that wisdom is the only good and ignorance the only evil.
— Plato, Euthydemus 278e-282a [12]
In this dialogue with young Clinias, Socrates shows that all other possessions depend on wisdom for their value.
This is the Kural's logic exactly: with wisdom one has everything, and without it one has nothing, whatever one owns.

Real Life Example

Japan's Charter Oath pledges to seek knowledge throughout the world
6 April 1868 · Kyoto, Japan
Shortly after the fall of the Tokugawa shogunate, the new government issued the Charter Oath in the name of the young Emperor Meiji, a five-article statement of the principles of the new regime. Its final article pledged that knowledge would be sought throughout the world to strengthen the foundations of imperial rule. The pledge became a guiding principle of the Meiji reforms, under which Japan studied and adopted foreign learning in many fields.
— Sources: [13] [14]
Japan's new leaders treated knowledge itself, rather than any single material resource, as the foundation on which national strength would be built.
The kural says those who possess wisdom possess everything, and the Charter Oath made the pursuit of knowledge the basis of a whole nation's future.
