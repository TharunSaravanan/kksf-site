Kural 786

Friendship · Natpu · நட்பு

முகநக நட்பது நட்பன்று நெஞ்சத்து அகநக நட்பது நட்பு.
Muganaga natpadhu natpandru nenjaththu aganaga natpadhu natpu.

Meaning

Friendship that only makes the face smile is not real friendship. Real friendship makes the heart smile inside.

Elementary

Some people smile at you only on the outside, like a mask, but inside they don’t really care. A true friend is happy all the way inside when they see you, even if they don’t make a big show of it. Real friendship is a smile in the heart, not just on the face.

University

The couplet turns on a pair of near-rhyming compounds: muga-naga (முகநக, ‘so that the face smiles’) and aga-naga (அகநக, ‘so that the inside smiles’), with nenjatthu (நெஞ்சத்து, ‘in the heart’) making the inner sense explicit. Naga (நக) means to laugh, smile or blossom, so both commentators and translators often render it as the face or heart ‘blooming’. Parimelazhagar takes the first kind of friendship to be outward courtesy only, and the second to be friendship rooted in love (anbu). The word natpu appears four times in seven words, which gives the verse its proverbial ring. The text is the same on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Chanakya Niti advises avoiding the person who speaks sweetly to your face but works to ruin you behind your back, comparing him to a pot of poison with a layer of milk on top.
— Chanakya Niti 2.5 [3]
The sweet surface hides a poisonous inside, just as a pleasant face can hide ill will.
This is the Kural’s face-only friend, whose smile does not reach the heart.
Christianity
“Let love be without dissimulation. Abhor that which is evil; cleave to that which is good.”
— Romans 12:9 (KJV) [4]
Paul asks that love be genuine, with no pretence behind it.
Paul’s love ‘without dissimulation’ is the Kural’s smile of the heart rather than of the face.
Islam
“The worst people in the Sight of Allah on the Day of Resurrection will be the double faced people who appear to some people with one face and to other people with another face.”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 6058 [5]
The Prophet condemns the two-faced person who shows a different face to different people.
Both texts warn against friendship worn on the face but absent from the heart.
Judaism
“Many a friend doth a sweet palate make; And gracious lips that greet (?) with peace. Be they that are at peace with thee many; But he that hath thy secret one of a thousand.”
— Ben Sira 6:5-6 (The Wisdom of Ben Sira, Cambridge, 1899) [6]
Ben Sira says pleasant speech wins many friends, but the one you can trust with your heart is one in a thousand.
Friends of the lips are many, friends of the heart are rare: the same contrast as the Kural’s face and heart.
Buddhism
“One is a bottle friend; one says, ‘friend, friend’ only to one’s face; one is a friend and an associate only when it is advantageous.”
— Sigalovada Sutta, DN 31 (trans. Narada Thera) [7]
The Buddha lists false friends, including the one whose friendship exists only in what he says to your face.
The ‘friend, friend’ only to one’s face is exactly the Kural’s face-smiling friendship that is no friendship.
Jainism
“It is better to be without the love of those who are without sincerity.”
— Naladiyar, ch. 22 'The choice of friends', stanza 9 (English tr. in The Indian Antiquary; wisdomlib.org) [8]
The Naladiyar holds that affection that is not sincere is worth less than no affection at all.
The Kural rejects friendship that only makes the face smile and accepts only the kind that gladdens the heart, and the Naladiyar likewise rejects love that lacks inner sincerity.
Sikhism
“They alone are true, whose love for God is deep and heart-felt. Those who have one thing in their heart, and something else in their mouth, are judged to be false.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 488, Raag Aasaa, Sheikh Farid (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Sheikh Farid, whose hymns are part of the Guru Granth Sahib, sets heart-felt love against love that lives only on the lips.
The Kural says friendship that only makes the face smile is not friendship, while friendship that makes the heart smile is; Farid likewise counts true only the love that is in the heart, not merely on the lips.
Greek Mythology
In Aesop’s fable of the two travellers and the bear, one man scrambles up a tree and leaves his companion to lie still on the ground; when the bear has gone, the one who was left says the bear whispered to him never to travel with a friend who deserts you at the first sign of danger.
— Aesop, Fables (trans. G. F. Townsend), ‘The Bear and the Two Travelers’ [10]
The fable exposes a friendship that was pleasant on the road but empty when it was tested.
The deserter’s friendship was only on the face, never in the heart.
Philosophy
“In everything false pretence is bad, for it suspends and vitiates our power of discerning the truth. But to nothing it is so hostile as to friendship; for it destroys that frankness without which friendship is an empty name.”
— Cicero, De Amicitia 25 (trans. E. S. Shuckburgh) [11]
Laelius argues that pretence destroys friendship because it takes away the openness that friendship depends on.
Cicero’s ‘empty name’ is the Kural’s face-only friendship that is not friendship at all.

Real Life Example

Montaigne's friendship with Etienne de La Boetie
c. 1559-1563 · Bordeaux, France
Around 1559 Michel de Montaigne, then a member of the Parlement of Bordeaux, met Etienne de La Boetie, who also worked for the Parlement, and the two formed a deep friendship that lasted until La Boetie's death at the age of thirty-two in August 1563. Montaigne later oversaw the publication of several of his friend's works. In his essay Of Friendship he set this bond apart from ordinary friendships, which he called mere acquaintances, describing a friendship in which two souls are blended so completely that the seam joining them disappears.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Montaigne's essay made this friendship the classic European example of a bond rooted in the heart rather than in convenience or outward courtesy.
The kural says friendship that only makes the face smile is not friendship, while friendship that makes the heart smile is, and Montaigne drew the same line between ordinary acquaintance and what he shared with La Boetie.
