Kural 956

Lineage · Kudimai · குடிமை

சலம்பற்றிச் சால்பில செய்யார்மா சற்ற குலம்பற்றி வாழ்தும்என் பார்.
Salampatrich chaalbila seyyaar maasatra kulampatri vaazhdhum enbaar.

Meaning

People who decide to live true to their spotless family name will never use tricks or do anything shameful.

Elementary

If your family has always been known for honesty, you think, 'I will not be the one who spoils our name.' So you do not cheat, even when it would be easy.

University

Salam (சலம்) means deceit or crookedness, which Mu. Varadarasanar and Kalaignar render as vanchanai (வஞ்சனை); chaalbila (சால்பில) are acts lacking saalbu (சால்பு, worthiness, nobility of character, the subject of chapter 99). Maasatra kulam (மாசற்ற குலம்) is 'a family free of stain', and vaazhdhum enbaar (வாழ்தும் என்பார்) are 'those who say, we shall live (by it)', so the couplet describes a conscious resolve to keep faith with an unblemished line. Solomon Pappaiah adds 'even when poverty comes', reading the couplet as continuing the thought of 955 that hardship tests the well-born. The Tamil text is identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net, both dividing the line as மா சற்ற / மாசற்ற across the metrical break.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
On the battlefield Arjuna fears that destroying families will destroy the ancient family traditions (kula-dharma), and that when these perish the whole family is overcome by lawlessness.
— Bhagavad Gita 1.39-41 [3]
Arjuna treats the inherited duties of a family line as something precious that wrong action can wipe out.
He shares the Kural's concern to keep a family's tradition unstained, though Krishna goes on to overrule his conclusion.
Christianity
“But have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully; but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God.”
— 2 Corinthians 4:2 (KJV) [4]
Paul says his company has given up every hidden dishonesty and crafty dealing.
Its refusal of 'craftiness' is the Kural's refusal of salam, deceit.
Islam
“And do not mix the truth with falsehood or conceal the truth while you know [it].”
— Qur'an 2:42 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur'an forbids deceit that blends truth and falsehood.
It forbids the salam, crookedness, that the Kural's well-born refuse.
Judaism
Proverbs says that a righteous man who walks in his integrity leaves his children blessed after him.
— Proverbs 20:7 [6]
The integrity of one generation becomes the inheritance of the next.
Like the Kural, it ties personal honesty to the good name of the family line.
Buddhism
“Whosoever is given to anger, is miserly, has base desires, and is selfish, deceitful, shameless and fearless (in doing evil) — know him as an outcast.”
— Vasala Sutta, Sn 1.7, v. 18 (tr. Piyadassi Thera) [7]
The Buddha says that deceit and shamelessness, not birth, are what make a person low.
It agrees with the Kural that deceit is unworthy of anyone claiming a good name, though it denies that birth gives that name.
Jainism
“I am the daughter of the Bhôga-king, and you are an Andhakavrishni; being born in a noble family let us not become like Gandhana-snakes; firmly practise self-control!”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 22.43 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
When the monk Rathanemi tries to seduce the nun Rajimati in a cave, she reminds him of their noble houses and calls him back to his vows, and he returns to the Law.
This is the Kural's resolve exactly: because we live by a spotless lineage, we will not do anything unworthy.
Sikhism
“One knows the Truth only when the Truth is in his heart. The filth of falsehood departs, and the body is washed clean.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 468, Raag Aasaa, Asa di Vaar, salok of Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Asa di Vaar, Guru Nanak says truth is real only when it lives in the heart, and then the stain of falsehood is washed away.
The Kural speaks of those who resolve to live true to a spotless line and never stoop to deceit; Guru Nanak locates that spotlessness in a heart cleansed of falsehood.
Greek Mythology
Glaucus tells Diomedes that his father Hippolochus sent him to Troy urging him always to be the best and not to bring shame on the race of his fathers, who were the noblest in Ephyra and Lycia.
— Homer, Iliad 6 (tr. Samuel Butler) [10]
The Lycian hero's whole code of conduct is framed as loyalty to an ancestral line.
Like the Kural's well-born, Glaucus acts as he does because he will not stain his family's name.
Philosophy
“Count it the greatest of all sins to prefer life to honour, and to lose, for the sake of living, all that makes life worth having.”
— Juvenal, Satire 8 (tr. G. G. Ramsay, 1918) [11]
Writing to a young noble, Juvenal says that no pressure, not even a tyrant's threat, should make him trade honour for survival.
The Kural's well-born refuse unworthy acts for the same reason: honour is what makes their life worth having.

Real Life Example

Helmuth James von Moltke and the Kreisau Circle
1940-1945 · Kreisau, Silesia, and Berlin, Germany
Helmuth James Graf von Moltke, a lawyer from the distinguished Prussian Moltke family, gathered opponents of the Nazi regime from different backgrounds, including on his family estate at Kreisau, to plan the principles of a just order for Germany after Nazism. He was arrested in January 1944 for warning a friend of an impending arrest. Tried before the People's Court in January 1945, he was condemned although his part in the July 1944 plot against Hitler could not be proved, and he was executed at Plotzensee prison in Berlin on 23 January 1945.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Moltke refused to accommodate a criminal regime even though his family name and position could have bought him safety.
The kural says those resolved to live true to a spotless family line will not stoop to unworthy acts, and Moltke held to his principles to the end rather than compromise them.
