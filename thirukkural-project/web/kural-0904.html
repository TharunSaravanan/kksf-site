Kural 904

Being Led by Women · Penvazhich Seral · பெண்வழிச் சேறல்

மனையாளை அஞ்சும் மறுமையி லாளன் வினையாண்மை வீறெய்த லின்று.
Manaiyaalai anjum marumaiyi laalan vinaiyaanmai veereydha lindru.

Meaning

A man who is afraid of his wife loses the good that leads to a happy next life. Even if he is skilled at his work, it will never win him praise.

Elementary

A painter who is too afraid of anyone's opinion to paint what he really sees will never make a great painting. Fear holds back good work.

University

Marumai-ilaalan (மறுமையிலாளன்) means 'one without (merit for) the next world'; Parimelazhagar explains that such a man cannot perform the duties of the householder (illaram) properly and so loses the reward of the hereafter. Vinai-aanmai (வினையாண்மை, the capacity to direct undertakings) may exist, but it will not attain veeru (வீறு, distinction, acclaim) among the good, because one who lacks his own nature cannot bring work to completion. Manakkudavar adds that this verse means such a man will not create wealth. Textual note: ytamizh, thirukkural.net and thirukkural.com divide the line as 'வீறெய்த லின்று' while kuraltheni prints 'வீறெய்தல் இன்று'; this is a difference of word-spacing (சீர் division vs. word division), not wording. Kalaignar gives a distinctive modern reading, taking the man as one who fears to undertake married life at all, a reinterpretation that avoids the gendered fear. Contemporary critics observe that the verse links domestic authority to public efficacy in a way typical of premodern political ethics.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
King Shantanu, smitten by Satyavati, accepts her father's condition that her sons inherit the throne, leading his heir Devavrata to renounce kingship and marriage forever as Bhishma.
— Mahabharata, Adi Parva (Sambhava Parva) [5]
A king's infatuation leads him to a concession with far-reaching consequences for the dynasty.
As the Kural warns, a ruler's judgment bent by attachment compromises his work and legacy.
Christianity
Pilate's wife sends him a message during Jesus' trial urging him to have nothing to do with that just man, but Pilate yields to the crowd instead.
— Matthew 27:19 [6]
Here the wife gives the righteous counsel and the husband's failure is in not heeding her.
A counterpoint to the Kural: in this story a man's worth would have been served by listening to his wife.
Islam
“Your wealth and your children are but a trial, and Allāh has with Him a great reward.”
— Qur'an 64:15 (Saheeh International) [7]
Family and possessions are described as tests whose handling determines one's reward with God.
The Kural similarly ties a man's handling of domestic life to his merit in the next world.
Judaism
“But there was none like unto Ahab, which did sell himself to work wickedness in the sight of the LORD, whom Jezebel his wife stirred up.”
— 1 Kings 21:25 (KJV) [6]
The book of Kings presents Ahab as a king whose deeds were driven by his wife's instigation.
This matches the Kural's view that a ruler led by his wife forfeits a good name and a good hereafter.
Buddhism
“For a person tormented by evil thoughts, who is passion-dominated and given to the pursuit of pleasure, his craving steadily grows. He makes the fetter strong, indeed.”
— Dhammapada 349 (Buddharakkhita) [8]
Domination by passion strengthens the bonds that hold one back.
The Kural's man, dominated within his household, similarly cannot achieve distinction.
Jainism
“His father and mother, his children and wife who claim him, will admonish him: 'See, you are our supporter; care not for the next world in order to support us.'”
— Sutrakritanga 1.2.1.19 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [9]
The Sutrakritanga warns that family, wife included, can pull a person away from the merit and discipline that shape his next existence.
Kural 904 says the man ruled by fear of his wife becomes marumai-ilaalan, 'one without the next world', and this verse shows that same pull from the Jain side.
Sikhism
“While laughing, playing, dressing and eating, he is liberated.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 522, Raag Gujri (Gujri ki Vaar), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
Guru Arjan teaches that liberation is reached within ordinary household life, by one who has met the True Guru, not by leaving it.
The Kural fears that a man ruled by his wife fails as a householder and loses the merit of the next world; Sikhism agrees the household is where one earns spiritual reward, though it does not make the wife the threat.
Greek Mythology
The seer Amphiaraus foresees his death in the war of the Seven against Thebes, but his wife Eriphyle, bribed with the necklace of Harmonia, compels him to go.
— Apollodorus, Library 3.6.2 [11]
Bound by an agreement to abide by his wife's decision, a wise man is forced into a doomed undertaking.
The Kural's man whose actions are governed by his wife cannot bring his work to a good end.
Philosophy
Epictetus begins his handbook by distinguishing what is in our power from what is not, warning that one who fears what others control becomes a slave.
— Epictetus, Enchiridion 1 [12]
Freedom, for the Stoic, lies in governing one's own judgments rather than being ruled by fear of others.
The Kural's fearful husband lacks the inner freedom that the Stoics thought necessary for good action.

Real Life Example

Mark Antony follows Cleopatra out of the Battle of Actium
2 September 31 BCE · Actium, off the west coast of Greece
At the naval Battle of Actium, Mark Antony and Cleopatra's fleet fought Octavian's forces under Agrippa. During the battle Cleopatra withdrew with her Egyptian ships, and Antony broke away with a small number of ships and followed her. The rest of his fleet surrendered to Octavian, and his land army surrendered about a week later, leaving Octavian master of the Roman world.
— Sources: [13] [14] [15]
One of Rome's most experienced generals abandoned his own fleet and army in mid-battle to follow his partner, and his military reputation never recovered.
The kural says a man ruled by his wife will not win acclaim for his work however capable he is, and Antony's long record as a commander was overshadowed by his departure from Actium.
