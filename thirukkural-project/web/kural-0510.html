Kural 510

Selection and Confidence · Therindhu Thelithal · தெரிந்து தெளிதல்

தேரான் தெளிவும் தெளிந்தான்கண் ஐயுறவும் தீரா இடும்பை தரும்.
Thaeraan thelivum thelindhaankan aiyuravum Theeraa idumbai tharum.

Meaning

Trusting someone you have not checked, and doubting someone you checked and then trusted, both bring endless trouble.

Elementary

There are two ways to mess up with friends. One is trusting someone you just met with a big secret. The other is having a friend who has always been loyal and still checking their bag every day. Both make life miserable. Check carefully once, then trust.

University

The closing couplet names two opposite errors: தேரான் தெளிவு, confidence without examination (the fault of 508), and தெளிந்தான்கண் ஐயுறவு, suspicion toward someone already tested and trusted. Both produce தீரா இடும்பை, ‘unending affliction’, repeating word for word the second line of 508, so the chapter ends by balancing its warnings: examination is required, but once done it must be honoured. Suspicion of a proven servant damages morale, invites real disloyalty, and wastes the effort of testing, points modern commentators such as Pappaiah and Kalaignar make explicit in the setting of appointments. The next chapter (52, தெரிந்து வினையாடல்) goes on to how the trusted person should be employed. Both sources agree on the text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
After Sita has been rescued and her purity publicly confirmed by the fire ordeal, Rama later sends her into exile in response to gossip in Ayodhya, bringing lasting grief to both of them and to their family.
— Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 118 and Uttara Kanda 43-45 [3]
Doubt revived after a trust has been proven leads to lasting sorrow.
This is the Kural’s second error: suspicion toward one already tested and trusted.
Christianity
“Then saith he to Thomas, reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands; and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side: and be not faithless, but believing.”
— John 20:27 (KJV) [4]
Jesus rebukes Thomas for holding on to doubt despite the reliable testimony of the other disciples.
Persisting in doubt when evidence already warrants trust is the error the Kural warns about.
Islam
“O you who have believed, avoid much [negative] assumption. Indeed, some assumption is sin.”
— Qur’an 49:12 (Sahih International) [5]
Believers are told not to harbour baseless suspicion of one another or spy on each other.
Unfounded suspicion toward those already proven trustworthy is the Kural’s ஐயுறவு.
Judaism
“Joshua ben Perahiah used to say: appoint for thyself a teacher, and acquire for thyself a companion and judge all men with the scale weighted in his favor.”
— Mishnah, Pirkei Avot 1:6 (trans. Joshua Kulp) [6]
The Mishnah pairs choosing a companion with the duty to give people the benefit of the doubt.
Choose carefully, then do not meet the chosen one with suspicion, which is the balance of the Kural.
Buddhism
The Buddha compares a mind troubled by doubt (vicikicchā) to muddy water stirred up and set in the dark, in which a person cannot see their own reflection; doubt is one of the five hindrances.
— Aṅguttara Nikāya 5.193, Saṅgārava Sutta [7]
Persistent doubt keeps the mind from seeing clearly and acting well.
The Kural likewise treats needless doubt as a source of lasting affliction.
Jainism
“The persons possessed of Right Faith are free from doubts and are therefore fearless.”
— Saman Suttam v. 232 (Essential requisites of Right Faith; English tr. T.K. Tukol and K.K. Dixit; jainworld.com) [8]
Freedom from doubt (nihshankita) is the first of the eight limbs of right faith: once the truth has been tested and accepted, wavering suspicion is a fault.
The kural condemns doubting one already examined and trusted; Jainism likewise counts doubt about what has been rightly accepted as a flaw that brings fear and instability.
Sikhism
“One who has no faith in the True Guru, and who does not love the Word of the Shabad, shall find no peace, even though he may come and go hundreds of times.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 591, Raag Vadhans, Vadhans Ki Vaar (salok), Guru Amar Das (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Amar Das says that someone who withholds trust from the Guru gains nothing however often he returns, and remains without peace.
The Kural says that doubting one who has been tested and trusted brings unending trouble; Gurbani likewise says that withholding faith from the proven Guru leaves one without peace.
Greek Mythology
Cephalus, doubting his faithful wife Procris, tests her in disguise; their marriage is later poisoned by mutual suspicion, and Procris, secretly following him out of jealousy, is killed by his own javelin.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 7.661-862 [10]
Suspicion toward a proven partner destroys what it was meant to protect.
Doubting the one already proven brings grief that cannot be undone, as the Kural warns.
Philosophy
Aristotle observes that only the friendship of good people is proof against slander, because it is not easy to believe anyone speaking against someone we have ourselves tested over a long time; among such friends there is trust and the thought ‘he would never wrong me’.
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 8.4 (1157a20-24) [11]
Long testing produces a trust that should not be shaken by talk.
The Kural too says that once someone is tested, doubting them is the error.

Real Life Example

Admiral Yi Sun-sin is removed from command
1597 · Joseon Korea
In 1597, during the Japanese invasions of Korea, Admiral Yi Sun-sin, who had already won a series of naval victories, was falsely accused, arrested and reduced to the rank of a common soldier. His replacement, Won Gyun, led the fleet into a defeat at Chilcheollyang in which almost all of its warships were lost. Yi was then restored to command and, with only about a dozen ships, won the Battle of Myeongnyang in October 1597.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
A government that doubted its proven commander lost nearly its entire navy before turning back to him.
The kural warns that doubting someone already tested and trusted brings endless trouble, and Korea's naval disaster of 1597 shows it.
