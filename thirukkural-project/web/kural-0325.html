Kural 325

Not Killing · Kollaamai · கொல்லாமை

நிலைஅஞ்சி நீத்தாருள் எல்லாம் கொலைஅஞ்சிக் கொல்லாமை சூழ்வான் தலை.
Nilaianji neeththaarul ellaam kolaianjik Kollaamai soozhvaan thalai.

Meaning

Many people give up the world because they fear the endless cycle of life and death. The greatest of them is the one who fears the wrong of killing and carefully keeps from it.

Elementary

Some people give up their toys, their fancy food and their comfy beds to become monks. That’s hard! But Valluvar says the very best of them is the one who is most careful never to hurt a living thing. Being gentle matters more than giving things up.

University

Valluvar compares two kinds of fear: nilai añci — fear of the “state” of worldly existence and rebirth that drives ascetics to renounce (nīttār) — and kolai añci, fear of the guilt of killing. He declares that among all renunciants, the one moved by the second fear and who deliberates on non-killing is talai, the head. This subordinates the whole apparatus of asceticism to a single ethical commitment, a characteristic Kural move that ranks inner moral conduct over outward religious status. The same move appears in the Mahabharata’s claim that ahimsa is itself the highest penance, and in the Dhammapada’s definition of the true monk as one who has laid down violence toward all beings.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Abstention from injury is the highest religion. It is, again, the highest penance.”
— Mahabharata, Anushasana Parva, Section CXV (K. M. Ganguli trans.) [4]
Bhishma declares that ahimsa is not only the highest dharma but also the highest form of tapas (austerity).
Directly supports the Kural: non-killing ranks above every other austerity practised by renunciants.
Christianity
“And though I bestow all my goods to feed the poor, and though I give my body to be burned, and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing.”
— 1 Corinthians 13:3 (KJV) [5]
Paul teaches that even the most extreme renunciation is worthless without love.
Like the Kural, it subordinates outward renunciation to an inner ethical disposition — here love, there non-killing.
Islam
“The cases which will be decided first (on the Day of Resurrection) will be the cases of blood-shedding.”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 6533 [6]
The Prophet teaches that bloodshed is the first matter between people that God will judge.
Reflects the Kural’s kolai añci — the fear of bloodguilt as the gravest concern of a God-fearing person.
Judaism
“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”
— Micah 6:8 (KJV) [5]
After asking whether God wants thousands of rams or even one’s firstborn, Micah answers that God wants justice and mercy instead.
Like the Kural, it ranks ethical mercy above elaborate religious self-sacrifice.
Buddhism
“Even though he be well-attired, yet if he is poised, calm, controlled and established in the holy life, having set aside violence towards all beings — he, truly, is a holy man, a renunciate, a monk.”
— Dhammapada 142 (Buddharakkhita trans.) [7]
The Buddha says the true renunciant is marked not by outward austerities but by having laid down violence toward all beings.
Almost exactly the Kural: among renunciants, the truest is the one who has renounced harming life.
Jainism
“He who thoroughly knows living beings, whether they move or not, and does not injure them in any of the three ways, him we call a Brâhmana.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 25.23 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895) [8]
The monk Jayaghosha tells a sacrificing priest that the true holy man is defined not by birth or ritual but by knowing all living beings and injuring none in thought, word or act.
Valluvar places at the head of all renunciants the one who dreads killing; the Uttarādhyayana likewise makes non-injury the mark of the true ascetic.
Sikhism
“You call yourself the most excellent sage; then who would you call a butcher?”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1103, Raag Maaroo, Bhagat Kabir (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Kabir mocks those who kill in religious sacrifice yet claim the rank of the highest sages: by their deeds they are no different from butchers.
The Kural ranks highest among renunciants the one who fears the sin of killing; Kabir denies the title of great sage to anyone who kills, whatever his religious standing.
Greek Mythology
When Apollo killed the Cyclopes, Zeus required even this god to atone by serving a mortal, Admetus, as a herdsman for a year; Heracles likewise served Omphale in expiation for killing Iphitus.
— Apollodorus, Library 3.10.4 and 2.6.2–3 [10]
In Greek myth even gods and the greatest heroes had to submit to humiliating servitude to cleanse bloodguilt.
Reflects kolai añci — the dread of bloodguilt is so great that it outranks divine or heroic status.
Philosophy
Empedocles, in his Purifications, portrays himself as an exiled daimon and calls on mortals to cease from the harsh-sounding slaughter by which, unknowingly, they devour one another.
— Empedocles, Katharmoi, fragments B136–137 (Diels-Kranz) [11]
The philosopher-purifier treated abstention from bloodshed as the core of the purified, god-like life.
Like the Kural, it makes refusal to kill the heart of the path of spiritual purification.

Real Life Example

The Swiss Brethren adopt the Schleitheim Confession
February 1527 · Schleitheim, Schaffhausen, Switzerland
Leaders of the Swiss Brethren, an early Anabaptist movement, met at Schleitheim and agreed seven articles of faith, with Michael Sattler as their main author. The articles called for separation from the world and rejected the use of weapons of violence, treating the sword as belonging outside the perfection of Christ. The Swiss Brethren refused military service and were harshly persecuted, and Sattler was executed later that year.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Schleitheim became a founding statement for the nonresistance later kept by Mennonite and Hutterite communities.
The kural says the foremost of all who renounce the world is the one who fears the guilt of killing, and these separatists made the refusal to wield the sword central to their renunciation.
