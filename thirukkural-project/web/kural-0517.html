Kural 517

Selection and Employment · Therindhu Vinaiyaadal · தெரிந்து வினையாடல்

இதனை இதனால் இவன்முடிக்கும் என்றாய்ந்து அதனை அவன்கண் விடல்.
Idhanai idhanaal ivanmudikkum endraaindhu Adhanai avankan vidal.

Meaning

Once you decide that “this person can do this job in this way,” hand the job over to them and leave it in their hands.

Elementary

When your teacher knows you’re good at drawing and have the markers you need, she gives you the class poster to make and then lets you do it, without hovering over every line.

University

The couplet is built on demonstratives: இதனை (this task), இதனால் (by these means), இவன் (this man), and then அதனை அவன்கண் விடல் (leave that with him). Delegation follows a specific three-way match of task, resources and person; Varadarasan glosses இதனால் as “by this instrument” and Pappaiah as the manpower and money available. விடல் (to release) implies real handing over, not a nominal assignment followed by micromanagement. Jethro’s advice in Exodus 18, and the master in the parable of the talents who gives each servant a share “according to his several ability” and then departs, express the same trust.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In the Ramayana, when the monkey search parties set out for Sita, Rama gives his signet ring to Hanuman alone, judging that he is the one most likely to find her; Hanuman goes on to leap the ocean to Lanka and succeed.
— Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 44 [3]
Rama works out who can accomplish the mission and with what, then entrusts it to him completely.
This man, this task, these means, then leave it to him: exactly the Kural’s formula.
Christianity
“And unto one he gave five talents, to another two, and to another one; to every man according to his several ability; and straightway took his journey.”
— Matthew 25:15 (KJV) [4]
The master gives each servant as much as that servant can handle, then leaves them to get on with it.
Allotting by ability and then really leaving is the heart of Kural 517.
Islam
“Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due and when you judge between people to judge with justice.”
— Qur’an 4:58 (Sahih International) [5]
Classical exegesis applies this verse to giving offices and responsibilities to those who are rightly suited to them.
Giving each trust to the person it properly belongs to is the Kural’s entrusting principle.
Judaism
“...every great matter they shall bring unto thee, but every small matter they shall judge themselves; so shall they make it easier for thee and bear the burden with thee.”
— Exodus 18:22 (JPS 1917) [6]
Jethro tells Moses to let the appointed judges settle ordinary cases themselves and bring him only the hard ones.
Real delegation to proven people, releasing the task to them, is what the Kural commands.
Buddhism
When Devadatta leads a group of new monks away to Gayasisa, the Buddha sends Sariputta and Moggallana, his two ablest disciples, to win them back; they succeed.
— Vinaya, Cullavagga VII [7]
The Buddha matches a delicate mission to the two disciples best suited to it and leaves it to them.
Choosing the right people for a specific task and trusting them to finish it reflects Kural 517.
Jainism
“'What shall I do now? I want to be employed, sir, in doing some work or in studying.' If he is ordered to do some work, he should do it without tiring; if he is ordered to study, he should do it without allowing himself to be affected by any pains.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 26.9-10 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
In the daily routine of the Jain monk, the superior assigns each disciple either work or study, and the disciple carries out the assigned task fully.
The kural says that once you have decided who will do a task, you should hand it over to him; the Jain routine shows a superior assigning work and the disciple taking it on wholly.
Sikhism
When Guru Arjan compiled the first definitive collection of Sikh scripture, he entrusted the writing of the text to Bhai Gurdas, a senior member of his congregation, who served as scribe of the Adi Granth. The work was finished in 1604 and installed in the Darbar Sahib at Amritsar.
— The Pluralism Project, Harvard University, 'The Sikh Scripture' (paraphrase) [9]
The Harvard Pluralism Project records that the Guru chose a learned and trusted disciple for the most important writing task of the early community.
The Kural says that once one has decided that this person will do this task by these means, the task should be handed over to him; Guru Arjan handed the writing of the scripture to Bhai Gurdas.
Greek Mythology
Asked to pick a partner for a dangerous night raid on the Trojan camp, Diomedes chooses Odysseus for his cleverness and courage, saying that with him they could return even through blazing fire.
— Homer, Iliad 10.242-247 [10]
Diomedes chooses the partner whose skills fit the mission.
Matching a particular person to a particular task by known ability echoes the Kural’s “this man will do this.”
Philosophy
Aristotle notes that masters who can afford it appoint a steward to run the household so that they themselves can devote their time to politics or philosophy.
— Aristotle, Politics I.7, 1255b [11]
Once a competent manager is in place, the master hands over management entirely.
Handing work to a capable agent and leaving it with him is the Kural’s advice.

Real Life Example

Lincoln leaves the 1864 campaign plans to Grant
30 April 1864 · Washington, D.C., United States
After appointing Ulysses S. Grant general-in-chief of the Union armies in March 1864, President Abraham Lincoln wrote to him on 30 April, the eve of Grant's spring campaign. In the letter Lincoln said he did not know and did not seek to know the details of Grant's plans, and that he was content to leave them to him. Grant crossed the Rapidan River with the Army of the Potomac days later, beginning the campaign that led to the end of the war.
— Sources: [12] [13] [14]
After years of interfering with generals who failed him, Lincoln found one he trusted and left the work in his hands.
The kural says that once you have decided this person will do this task by these means, you should hand the task to him and leave it with him, as Lincoln did with Grant.
