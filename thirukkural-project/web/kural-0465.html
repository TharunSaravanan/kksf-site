Kural 465

Acting after Due Consideration · Therindhu Seyalvakai · தெரிந்து செயல்வகை

வகையறச் சூழா தெழுதல் பகைவரைப் பாத்திப் படுப்பதோ ராறு.
Vakaiyarach choozhaa thezhudhal pakaivaraip, Paaththip patuppadho raaru.

Meaning

Rushing to fight enemies without planning every step is like preparing a field for them to grow strong in.

Elementary

If you rush to fight someone without planning properly, you actually help them — like planting a seed in good soil and watering it so it grows bigger.

University

The metaphor is agricultural. பாத்தி is a prepared garden bed, and பாத்திப் படுத்தல் means settling a plant in such a bed so it takes root. A poorly planned attack does not just fail. It strengthens the enemy, who gains confidence, allies, and legitimacy from beating back a rash assault. வகையற (exhaustively, covering every category) points to the sixfold considerations of statecraft (strength, place, time, means, allies, and so on) that commentators read into the phrase. Pope renders it: ‘With plans not well matured to rise against your foe, / Is way to plant him out where he is sure to grow!’ It is the chapter’s one explicitly military couplet.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Kautilya teaches that before marching, the conqueror must assess his own and the enemy’s relative strength, the suitability of place and time, the season for campaigning, and possible revolts and losses.
— Kautilya, Arthashastra, Book 9, Chapter 1 [5]
The Arthashastra says a king should work out many factors before going to war, not just whether he is angry or brave.
An itemised version of the Kural’s ‘வகையற’ — deliberating every category before rising against a foe.
Christianity
Or what king, going to make war against another king, sitteth not down first, and consulteth whether he be able with ten thousand to meet him that cometh against him with twenty thousand?
— Luke 14:31 (KJV) [6]
A wise king works out whether his army can win before he marches. If it can’t, he seeks peace instead.
Same scenario as the Kural: deliberate fully before rising against an enemy.
Islam
The Qur’an instructs the believers to prepare against their enemies whatever strength they can, including steeds of war, so as to deter them.
— Qur’an 8:60 (Sahih International) [7]
Muslims are told to get properly ready before facing an enemy, not to rush in unprepared.
Preparation before confrontation is the Kural’s point, though the Qur’an stresses deterrence.
Judaism
Every purpose is established by counsel: and with good advice make war.” And: “For by wise counsel thou shalt make thy war.
— Proverbs 20:18; 24:6 (Hebrew Bible; KJV rendering) [6]
The Bible says plans hold up when made with advice, and that war especially should be waged only with good counsel.
Directly applies deliberation before action to going to war.
Buddhism
Before attacking the Vajjians, King Ajatasattu sends his minister Vassakara to ask the Buddha. After hearing the seven conditions that keep the Vajjians strong, the minister concludes they cannot be defeated by force alone.
— Mahaparinibbana Sutta, Digha Nikaya 16 [8]
Even an ambitious king checked first whether his attack could work, and the answer showed him it couldn’t.
A narrative case of assessing an enemy’s strength before rising against it.
Jainism
“Making Faith his fortress, Penance and Self-control the bolt (of its gate), Patience its strong wall, so that guarded in three ways it is impregnable; making Zeal his bow, its string Carefulness in walking (iriyâ), and its top (where the string is fastened) Content, he should bend (this bow) with Truth, piercing with the arrow, Penance, (the foe's) mail, Karman--(in this way) a sage will be the victor in battle and get rid of the Saṃsâra.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 9.20–22 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [9]
When Indra urges King Nami to fortify his city, Nami answers that the real campaign is fought against karma, and he lists in detail the fortress, wall, bow, string and arrow it requires.
Like the Kural's warning against attacking a foe without thorough planning, Nami's war against karma is to be fought only once every defence and weapon has been prepared.
Sikhism
After Guru Arjan's execution, Guru Hargobind, the sixth Guru, bore two swords for temporal and spiritual authority, trained in arms, built up a Sikh army, fortified his towns and in 1609 built the Akal Takht at Amritsar, recognizing that a clash with Mughal power was coming. Britannica records that his Sikhs later defeated Shah Jahan's armies four times.
— Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Guru Hargobind" (paraphrase) [10]
Guru Hargobind did not rush into conflict after his father's martyrdom. He first prepared, militarily and institutionally, for a confrontation he saw coming.
The Kural warns that rising against foes without full preparation only strengthens them; Guru Hargobind's long preparation before facing Mughal forces shows the opposite course.
Greek Mythology
Adrastus led the Seven against Thebes although the seer Amphiaraus foresaw that the expedition would fail. Six of the seven champions died, and Thebes held.
— Apollodorus, Library 3.6; Aeschylus, Seven Against Thebes [11]
An army marched against a city despite warnings that the plan was doomed, and it was destroyed.
A rash, ill-considered attack that failed and left the enemy secure, as the Kural warns.
Philosophy
Sun Tzu observes that the general who wins makes many calculations in his temple before the battle is fought, while the general who loses makes but few calculations beforehand.
— Sun Tzu, The Art of War, ch. 1 (tr. Lionel Giles) [12]
Battles are mostly won or lost in the planning, before any fighting starts.
Near-identical doctrine: exhaustive calculation before engaging the enemy.

Real Life Example

The Jameson Raid into the Transvaal
29 December 1895 - 2 January 1896 · Transvaal (now South Africa)
Leander Starr Jameson led about 500 armed men from the borders of Bechuanaland into the Boer republic of the Transvaal, expecting a rising by foreign residents in Johannesburg that never happened. The column was surrounded and surrendered at Doornkop on 2 January 1896. Cecil Rhodes was forced to resign as prime minister of the Cape Colony, while President Paul Kruger gained international sympathy and won re-election in 1896.
— Sources: [13] [14]
The badly prepared raid did not weaken Kruger's government but made it stronger and deepened the divisions that led to the South African War of 1899-1902.
The kural says an attack launched without thorough planning settles the enemy in a prepared bed where he grows strong, and the Jameson Raid left Kruger more secure than before.
