Kural 442

Seeking the Aid of Great Men · Periyaaraith Thunaikkodal · பெரியாரைத் துணைக்கோடல்

உற்றநோய் நீக்கி உறாஅமை முற்காக்கும் பெற்றியார்ப் பேணிக் கொளல்.
Utranoi neekki uraaamai murkaakkum Petriyaarp penik kolal.

Meaning

Care for and keep close the people who can remove troubles that have already come and protect you from troubles still to come.

Elementary

Some wise people can help you fix a problem you already have, and can also warn you about problems before they happen. This kural says: treat people like that with great care and keep them close.

University

‘Noi’ literally means disease, but here it stands for any affliction — political, financial, or personal. The couplet distinguishes two functions of the wise counselor: the remedial (utra noi neekki, removing the affliction already suffered) and the preventive (uraamai murkaakkum, guarding beforehand against what has not yet arrived). The latter is the rarer gift, since it requires foresight. ‘Petriyaar’ denotes those who possess this capacity as a settled quality, and ‘penik kolal’ (cherishing and holding) implies that such people must be honored and maintained, not merely consulted when convenient.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In the Mahabharata (Adi Parva), Vidura foresees Duryodhana’s plot to burn the Pandavas in the house of lac at Varanavata; he warns Yudhishthira in a coded message and secretly sends a miner to dig an escape tunnel.
— Mahabharata, Adi Parva (Jatugriha episode), tr. K. M. Ganguli [4]
Vidura both anticipates a danger before it strikes and provides the means of escape when it arrives, so the Pandavas survive the fire.
Vidura is exactly the Kural’s ‘petriyaar’: one who guards against harm before it comes and removes it when it does.
Christianity
“Sirs, ye should have hearkened unto me, and not have loosed from Crete, and to have gained this harm and loss.”
— Acts 27:21 (KJV) [5]
Paul had warned the ship’s crew not to sail; ignored, they are caught in a storm. Paul then gives the counsel that saves everyone aboard.
Paul functions first as a preventive counselor (ignored) and then as a remedial one (heeded) — the two roles the Kural names.
Islam
Before the Battle of the Trench (5 AH), the Prophet accepted the counsel of Salman al-Farsi to dig a defensive trench around Medina, a Persian tactic unknown to the Arabs; the besieging confederates could not break through.
— Ibn Ishaq, Sirat Rasul Allah (tr. A. Guillaume) [6]
By heeding a knowledgeable companion, the Prophet protected the city from a danger before it arrived.
Illustrates the Kural’s ‘guarding in advance’ through valuing the counsel of a capable adviser.
Judaism
Joseph interprets Pharaoh’s dreams and counsels him to store grain during seven years of plenty; Pharaoh appoints him over Egypt, and the land survives seven years of famine.
— Genesis 41 [5]
Joseph’s foresight lets Egypt prepare for a disaster before it arrives, and his administration carries it through when it does.
Pharaoh ‘cherishes and holds’ the one who wards off disaster in advance — the exact wisdom of the Kural.
Buddhism
The Buddha tells Sigala that a good-hearted friend who gives good counsel restrains one from doing evil, encourages one to do good, informs one of what one does not know, and points out the path to heaven.
— Sigalovada Sutta, DN 31 (tr. Narada Thera) [7]
The true friend-counselor is defined by protecting you from wrong before it happens and guiding you toward good.
Parallels the Kural’s protective, forward-looking adviser who must be valued.
Jainism
“As to one who has lost his way in the wood, others who have not, (show it, thus some) teach the path which is salutary to men. Therefore (he should think): this is for my good that those who know put me right.”
— Sutrakritanga 1.14.10 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The Sutrakritanga compares the person who has strayed to a traveller lost in a forest, and the wise to those who know the road and lead him back to safety.
The kural praises counsellors who remove troubles that have already come and guard against those still to come. The Jain image shows the knowing guide rescuing a person already lost and setting him on a road that keeps him safe from then on.
Sikhism
“In the Company of the Holy, the five passions are brought to rest.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 271, Raag Gauree (Sukhmani), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the Sukhmani Guru Arjan lists the benefits of the Saadh Sangat, including bringing the five vices under control.
The Kural values those who remove troubles that have come and guard against troubles to come; Guru Arjan presents holy company as the force that subdues the inner afflictions behind them.
Greek Mythology
In Odyssey 10, Hermes meets Odysseus on the way to Circe’s house, warns him of her drugs, and gives him the herb moly; Odysseus is protected from transformation and forces Circe to restore his men who had been turned into swine.
— Homer, Odyssey 10 [10]
The divine helper both forestalls the danger to Odysseus and enables the rescue of the men already afflicted.
An exact dramatization of the Kural’s two functions: removing trouble that has come, preventing trouble yet to come.
Philosophy
Aristotle writes that friendship helps the young to keep from error, and aids older people by ministering to their needs and supplementing the activities that are failing from weakness.
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII.1 (tr. W. D. Ross) [11]
For Aristotle, friends both prevent mistakes and make up for losses already suffered.
Mirrors the Kural’s protective and remedial roles of the worthy companion.

Real Life Example

Koprulu Mehmed Pasha becomes Ottoman grand vizier
15 September 1656 · Constantinople (now Istanbul), Ottoman Empire (now Turkey)
In 1656, after the Venetians defeated the Ottoman fleet at the Dardanelles and occupied nearby islands, the court of the young sultan Mehmed IV appointed the elderly provincial governor Koprulu Mehmed Pasha as grand vizier, on his condition that he be allowed to govern without interference. He suppressed rebellious governors and rivals, reorganised the army and rebuilt the fleet, which recovered islands lost to Venice in 1657. Members of his family held the office for much of the following half century, and historians credit the first two Koprulu viziers with a temporary revival of Ottoman power.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Entrusting the state to an able minister both ended an immediate crisis and laid foundations that protected the empire for decades.
The kural urges a ruler to cherish those who can remove troubles that have come and guard against those still to come, and Koprulu did both for the Ottoman throne.
