Kural 468

Acting after Due Consideration · Therindhu Seyalvakai · தெரிந்து செயல்வகை

ஆற்றின் வருந்தா வருத்தம் பலர்நின்று போற்றினும் பொத்துப் படும்.
Aatrin varundhaa varuththam palarnindru, Potrinum poththup patum.

Meaning

Work that is not done the right way will fail, even if many people stand by to help and protect it.

Elementary

If you work hard but in the wrong way, the project will fall apart — even if lots of people are trying to help hold it together.

University

ஆறு here means ‘way, proper method’. வருந்தா வருத்தம் (‘toil not toiled [in the right way]’) is effort poured out without a method. The verb போற்று (to guard, cherish, maintain) and பலர்நின்று (many standing together) anticipate the objection that sheer numbers or goodwill can make up for bad method. Valluvar denies it. பொத்துப் படும் means ‘it will spring a leak / be breached’, an image of a vessel or dam giving way. The couplet adds to the chapter’s argument: deliberation must choose not only whether to act but how, and a wrong method cannot be rescued by resources applied afterwards.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Krishna says that one who discards the injunctions of scripture and acts on the impulse of desire attains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the supreme goal.
— Bhagavad Gita 16.23 [4]
If you ignore the proper way and just do what you feel like, your effort won’t bring success.
Effort outside the proper method (ஆறு) is fruitless, as the Kural says.
Christianity
Jesus compares the one who hears his words and does not do them to a foolish man who built his house on sand: the rain fell, the floods came, the winds beat on it, and it fell, and great was its fall.
— Matthew 7:26–27 (KJV) [5]
However much work goes into a house, it collapses if it isn’t built the right way on a solid foundation.
Labour without the right method ends in collapse, the Kural’s ‘breach’.
Islam
The Qur’an speaks of the greatest losers in their deeds: those whose effort is lost in worldly life while they think they are doing well in their work.
— Qur’an 18:103–104 (Sahih International) [6]
Some people work very hard and even think they’re doing great, but their effort is wasted because it isn’t on the right path.
Effort outside the right way is lost, however sincere, as the Kural says.
Judaism
If the iron be blunt, and he do not whet the edge, then must he put to more strength: but wisdom is profitable to direct.
— Ecclesiastes 10:10 (Hebrew Bible; KJV rendering) [5]
Chopping wood with a dull axe takes much more effort; a little wisdom about the right method saves a lot of wasted strength.
Wrong method multiplies toil; right method (wisdom) makes it succeed, as the Kural says.
Buddhism
The Buddha tells Bhumija that one who seeks oil by pressing sand will get none, whether he has hopes or not, because that is not the way to get oil; one who presses sesame seeds will get it.
— Bhumija Sutta, Majjhima Nikaya 126 [7]
No matter how hard or hopefully you squeeze sand, you won’t get oil. You need the right method.
An exact parallel: the outcome depends on the method, not on effort or wishes.
Jainism
“If an ignorant man should eat but a blade of Kuśa-grass every month, (the merit of his penance) will not equal the sixteenth part of his who possesses the Law as it has been taught.”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 9.44 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
Nami tells Indra that even the harshest austerity, done without right knowledge, is worth almost nothing next to practice guided by the true teaching.
The Kural says effort spent without the proper method will fail however much it is supported; Jainism likewise holds that great toil without the right way yields little.
Sikhism
“Hundreds of thousands of clever tricks, but not even one of them will go along with you in the end. … O Nanak, it is written that you shall obey the Hukam of His Command, and walk in the Way of His Will.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1, Japji Sahib (pauri 1), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the first pauri of Japji Sahib, Guru Nanak says that endless cleverness achieves nothing; only walking in the way of God's will (hukam) succeeds.
The Kural says effort spent in the wrong way fails whatever help it has; Guru Nanak says countless clever efforts fail unless they follow the right way.
Greek Mythology
In the underworld the Danaids are condemned to carry water forever in leaking vessels, so the jar they labour to fill never fills.
— Plato, Gorgias 493b; later mythographic tradition [10]
They work without end, but because their jars have holes, all their effort is wasted.
Endless labour through a ‘breached’ vessel echoes the Kural’s பொத்துப் படும் (springs a leak).
Philosophy
Francis Bacon observes that a lame man who keeps to the right road outstrips a runner who takes the wrong one.
— Francis Bacon, Novum Organum (1620), Book I, Aphorism 61 [11]
Going the right way matters more than how fast or strong you are.
Method beats force, the Kural’s core claim.

Real Life Example

The French attempt to build the Panama Canal fails
1881-1889 · Isthmus of Panama (then part of Colombia)
Ferdinand de Lesseps, celebrated for building the Suez Canal, led a French company that began digging a sea-level canal across Panama in 1881, financed by public share subscriptions in France. Landslides, flooding and tropical diseases defeated the method, and an estimated 22,000 workers died; a change to a lock canal came only in 1887. In 1889 the shareholders dissolved the company and all work stopped, with about 40 percent of the excavation done.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Great prestige, huge sums of money and the backing of many investors could not save a plan built on a method that did not suit the ground.
The kural says effort not spent in the proper way will fail even if many stand by to support it, and the French canal collapsed despite vast public backing because its method was wrong.
