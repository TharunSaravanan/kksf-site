Kural 505

Selection and Confidence · Therindhu Thelithal · தெரிந்து தெளிதல்

பெருமைக்கும் ஏனைச் சிறுமைக்கும் தத்தம் கருமமே கட்டளைக் கல்.
Perumaikkum aenaich chirumaikkum thaththam Karumame kattalaik kal.

Meaning

Whether a person is great or mean and small, their own actions are the test that shows it.

Elementary

A jeweller checks whether a ring is real gold by rubbing it on a special black stone and looking at the streak. You can check whether someone is really kind the same way, by looking at what they actually do, not what they say or what they wear.

University

கட்டளைக் கல் is the goldsmith’s touchstone (உரைகல்): gold rubbed on it leaves a streak whose colour shows its purity. The couplet says that deeds (கருமம், from Sanskrit karma) are such a stone for both பெருமை (greatness) and சிறுமை (meanness), and the emphatic ஏ in கருமமே excludes other tests such as birth, wealth or eloquence. It completes the lesson of 502: family is a starting presumption, but conduct is the decisive proof. Sivayogi Sivakumar reads கட்டளைக் கல் as an inscribed ‘edict stone’ that guides conduct, but the touchstone reading of Varadarasan, Pappaiah, Kalaignar and Pope (‘touchstone true’) is standard. Both sources agree on the text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“…theoretical and practical knowledge, foresight, retentive memory, and affability shall be tested from successful application in works…”
— Kautilya, Arthaśāstra 1.9 (trans. R. Shamasastry) [3]
Kautilya lists how each quality of a candidate minister is to be verified, and ability is judged by work actually done.
Work as proof of worth is the Kural’s touchstone.
Christianity
“Wherefore by their fruits ye shall know them.”
— Matthew 7:20 (KJV) [4]
Jesus says people are known by what their lives produce, as trees are known by their fruit.
‘Fruits’ play the role of the Kural’s touchstone of deeds.
Islam
“Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you.”
— Qur’an 49:13 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur’an says that tribes and peoples exist so we may know one another, but true nobility is measured by righteousness.
Greatness is measured by conduct, not lineage, as the Kural says.
Judaism
“Study is not the most important thing, but actions; whoever indulges in too many words brings about sin.”
— Mishnah, Pirkei Avot 1:17 (trans. Joshua Kulp) [6]
Shimon ben Gamaliel teaches that deeds, not learning or talk, are what finally count.
The Kural too treats action as the decisive test.
Buddhism
“Not by birth is one an outcast; not by birth is one a brahman. By deed one becomes an outcast, by deed one becomes a brahman.”
— Sutta Nipāta 1.7, Vasala Sutta, v. 21 (trans. Piyadassi Thera) [7]
Insulted as an ‘outcast’, the Buddha replies that high and low status come from conduct, not birth.
This is the Kural’s claim that deeds are the touchstone of greatness and meanness.
Jainism
“By one's actions one becomes a Brâhmana, or a Kshattriya, or a Vaisya, or a Sûdra.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 25.33 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
Jayaghosha teaches that a person's standing is made by deeds, not by birth or outward marks.
The kural makes each person's own deeds the touchstone of greatness or meanness; the Jain verse likewise makes actions alone the measure of what a person is.
Sikhism
“Apply the Touchstone of Truth to your mind, and see if it comes up to its full weight.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 22, Siree Raag, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Nanak borrows the goldsmith's touchstone and scale: a person's inner life is to be tested against truth as gold is tested on the stone.
The Kural calls a person's own deeds the touchstone of greatness and meanness; Guru Nanak uses the same touchstone image for testing a person's worth.
Greek Mythology
Instead of taking the safe sea route, the young Theseus walks the bandit-infested road to Athens, defeating Periphetes, Sinis, Sciron and Procrustes, so that his deeds announce him before his father Aegeus recognises him.
— Plutarch, Life of Theseus 6-12 [10]
Theseus proves he is worthy of his royal birth by what he does on the road.
His deeds, not his birth tokens alone, are the touchstone of his greatness.
Philosophy
Aristotle argues that we become just by doing just acts and brave by doing brave acts, so that character is formed and shown in activity.
— Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics 2.1 [11]
For Aristotle, virtue is a settled disposition revealed by repeated action.
If character shows itself in action, then deeds are the fair touchstone the Kural names.

Real Life Example

Bonaparte creates the Legion of Honour
19 May 1802 · Paris, France
As First Consul, Napoleon Bonaparte created the Legion of Honour by a law of 19 May 1802. It was an order of merit for both military and civil service, open to recipients without regard to birth or religion. It remains France's highest order of merit.
— Sources: [12] [13]
In a society that had long rewarded noble birth, the state chose to honour people for what they had actually done.
The kural calls deeds the touchstone of greatness and meanness, and the Legion of Honour made deeds, not birth, the test for honour.
