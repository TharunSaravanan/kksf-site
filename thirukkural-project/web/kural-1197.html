Kural 1197

The Solitary Anguish · Thanippadar Mikudhi · தனிப்படர் மிகுதி

பருவரலும் பைதலும் காணான்கொல் காமன் ஒருவர்கண் நின்றொழுகு வான்.
Paruvaralum paidhalum kaanaankol kaaman oruvar-kan nindrozhuku vaan.

Meaning

Kaman, the god of love, works on only one side. Does he not see the pain and sadness he leaves in me?

Elementary

It’s like a game where only one team is allowed to play and the other just has to watch. It doesn’t seem fair. Here the girl feels that love has been fair to her husband but very unfair to her.

University

Kāmaṉ is the god of love (Sanskrit Kāma, Manmatha), whose flower-arrows awaken desire. The heroine complains that he oruvarkaṇ niṉṟu oḻukuvāṉ, ‘stays and works upon one person only’, i.e. on her, not on her absent husband, and asks whether he does not see her paruvaral (distress) and paital (sorrow). Parimelazhagar’s reading, followed by Varadarasanar and Puliyur Kesikan, takes the complaint as against Kāma’s partiality; Pappaiah and Kalaignar connect paital with the pallor (pacalai) of the previous chapter. Pope: ‘While Kaman rushes straight at me alone, / Is all my pain and wasting grief unknown?’ Text identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Sent by the gods, Kama aims his flower-arrow at the meditating Shiva so that he will love Parvati; Shiva, disturbed, opens his third eye and burns Kama to ashes, leaving Parvati’s love unanswered until she wins him through austerity.
— Kalidasa, Kumarasambhava, Canto 3 (paraphrase) [3]
In Kalidasa’s telling, Kama’s arrow stirs longing in one lover while the other remains untouched.
Parvati, yearning while Shiva is unmoved, is the Kural’s heroine on whom Kama works from one side alone.
Christianity
“Stay me with flagons, comfort me with apples: for I am sick of love.”
— Song of Solomon 2:5 (KJV) [4]
The bride confesses herself weakened and ill with love.
The Kural’s heroine is likewise laid low by a love that overpowers her body and spirit.
Islam
The women of the city gossip that the governor’s wife has been seeking to seduce her young servant Joseph, saying he has struck her heart with love, while Joseph himself remains unmoved.
— Qur’an 12:30 (paraphrase; cf. Saheeh International) [5]
Surah Yusuf portrays a passion that seizes one person entirely while the other stays untouched.
As the Kural’s Kama acts on one side only, here love has overpowered one heart and left the other free.
Judaism
“I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles, and by the hinds of the field, that ye awaken not, nor stir up love, until it please.”
— Song of Songs 2:7 (JPS 1917, via Mechon-Mamre) [6]
The Song warns that love is a power that should not be roused before its time, because once awakened it cannot be controlled.
The Kural’s heroine has felt love roused in her alone, the danger the Song warns against.
Buddhism
In the Padhana Sutta, Mara approaches the striving Bodhisatta and is answered that Mara’s first army is sensual desire, followed by discontent, hunger and craving; the Bodhisatta resolves not to be overcome by them.
— Sutta Nipata 3.2, Padhana Sutta (paraphrase) [7]
Buddhism personifies desire as Mara, also called Kama, whose forces attack the mind.
The Kural’s Kama who torments one person is, in Buddhist terms, Mara’s first army, to be resisted rather than appealed to.
Jainism
“Pleasant things (by themselves) do not cause indifference nor emotions (as anger, &c.); but by either hating or loving them, a man undergoes such a change through delusion.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 32.101 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
The Uttaradhyayana teaches that suffering comes not from objects or outside powers but from one’s own attachment.
Where the Kural’s heroine blames Kama for her pain, the Jain text locates its source in her own love itself.
Sikhism
“O sexual desire, you lead the mortals to hell; you make them wander in reincarnation through countless species.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1358, Saloks Sehskritee, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the Saloks Sehskritee, Guru Arjan addresses kaam (lust), one of the five thieves, as a force that drags people into suffering and rebirth.
The Kural's wife complains that Kama, the god of love, works only on her and ignores her pain; Gurbani also speaks to desire directly, but treats it as a thief to be overcome, not a god to be pleaded with.
Greek Mythology
Stung by Apollo’s mockery, Cupid fires two arrows: a golden one that sets Apollo burning with love for the nymph Daphne, and a leaden one that makes Daphne flee from love; Apollo pursues while she escapes, until she is turned into a laurel.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 1.452-567 (paraphrase) [10]
Ovid’s Cupid deliberately makes love one-sided, wounding one heart with desire and the other with aversion.
This is the Kural’s complaint exactly: the love-god has struck one side only and left the lover to suffer.
Philosophy
Lucretius describes the lover as wounded by the darts of Venus, so that the wound festers and the craving grows, and he advises against feeding a passion that brings such pain.
— Lucretius, On the Nature of Things 4.1048-1072 (paraphrase) [11]
Lucretius treats love as a wound inflicted by Venus that torments the one who receives it.
His wounded lover is the Kural’s heroine struck by Kama, though Lucretius answers with Epicurean detachment.

Real Life Example

W. B. Yeats and his unreturned love for Maud Gonne
1889–1903 · London, England, and Dublin, Ireland
The Irish poet W. B. Yeats met Maud Gonne in 1889 and later wrote that the troubling of his life began then. About two years later he proposed and she refused; he asked again in 1899 and she declined, admiring him but not returning his love, since her passion was for Ireland's cause. In 1903 she married Major John MacBride, and Yeats's unreturned love fed many of his greatest poems over some forty years.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Yeats's case is a classic of love that took hold of one person completely while leaving the other untouched in the same way.
The kural complains that the god of love works on one side only, and in Yeats's story love struck the poet again and again while Maud Gonne stayed free of it.
