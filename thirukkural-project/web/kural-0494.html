Kural 494

Knowledge of Location · Idanarithal · இடனறிதல்

எண்ணியார் எண்ணம் இழப்பர் இடனறிந்து துன்னியார் துன்னிச் செயின்.
Enniyaar ennam izhappar idanarinthu Thunniyaar thunnich cheyin.

Meaning

Enemies who planned to beat you will lose their plans if you find the right place, stay close to it, and act from there.

Elementary

A turtle pulled all the way into its shell makes the curious dog give up and walk away. When you are in the right place and stay there, the other side stops trying.

University

The verse repeats words in pairs: ‘enniyaar ennam’ (the plan of those who planned) and ‘thunniyaar thunni’ (those who reached the place, keeping close to it). ‘Thunnuthal’ means to approach, cling to or stay close; commentators read the second ‘thunni’ as keeping firmly to the chosen ground instead of drifting away from it. Parimelazhagar takes ‘enniyaar’ as enemies who expected to win, and ‘ennam izhappar’ as more than tactical defeat: they give up the intention itself. Kalaignar has the foe not even daring to think of victory, and Pulavar Kesikan takes ‘thunni’ as pressing the battle closely. Where 493 was about winning, this kural is about deterrence: the right position ends the contest before it begins.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
In the Sabha Parva, Krishna tells Yudhishthira that the Yadavas, worn down by Jarasandha’s repeated invasions of Mathura, moved to Kushasthali (Dvaraka), a fortress on the sea. There they felt safe from his attacks.
— Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva, Section 14 (tr. K. M. Ganguli) [3]
Instead of fighting on exposed ground, the Yadavas took a position their powerful enemy could not easily reach.
As the Kural says, taking and holding the right place frustrates an enemy who planned to win.
Christianity
“Behold, I send you forth as sheep in the midst of wolves: be ye therefore wise as serpents, and harmless as doves.”
— Matthew 10:16 (KJV) [4]
Jesus sends his disciples into hostile territory and tells them to be shrewd about how they move there.
The Kural’s idea of knowing where you stand so that enemies’ designs fail is close to the ‘wise as serpents’ half of this saying.
Islam
“And Allāh repelled those who disbelieved, in their rage, not having obtained any good.”
— Quran 33:25 (Sahih International) [5]
This verse closes the Quran’s account of the Trench, when the confederate army besieging Medina gave up and left empty-handed.
Enemies who ‘planned’ to take Medina ‘lost their plan’ (ennam izhappar) once the defenders held their prepared ground.
Judaism
“And the children of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites that inhabited Jerusalem; but the Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem unto this day.”
— Judges 1:21 (KJV) [4]
The Jebusites held their hill-city so firmly that the tribe of Benjamin had to give up trying to expel them.
A people fixed in a strong place outlasted their attackers’ plans, which is Kural 494’s scenario.
Buddhism
“Realizing that this body is as fragile as a clay pot, and fortifying this mind like a well-fortified city, fight out Mara with the sword of wisdom. Then, guarding the conquest, remain unattached.”
— Dhammapada 40 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [6]
The verse tells the practitioner to make the mind a fortified city from which Mara, the tempter, can be fought off.
Holding a fortified position so that the attacker’s design fails is the kural’s point, moved inward.
Jainism
“The Buddhas that were, and the Buddhas that will be, they (as it were) have Peace as their foundation, even as all things have the earth for their foundation. And if any accidents whatever befall him who has gained that (foundation), he will not be overpowered by them as a mountain by the storm.”
— Sutrakritanga I.11.36-37 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [7]
Here 'Buddhas' means the Tirthankaras; the sage who stands firmly on the ground of peace cannot be overpowered, as a mountain is not moved by a storm.
The kural says that those who hold fast to their chosen ground defeat the plans of their enemies; the Jain sage who holds to the foundation of peace is similarly beyond the reach of every assault.
Sikhism
“My enemies and opponents have grown weary of attacking me, and now, I have become happy, O my friends and Siblings of Destiny.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 826, Raag Bilaaval, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
In a hymn of thanksgiving, Guru Arjan says that those who set out to harm him have worn themselves out and given up, because he stands under the Lord's protection.
The Kural says enemies who planned to win drop their plans when the defender holds firm to the right ground; Guru Arjan describes opponents who grew weary and gave up.
Greek Mythology
Behind the walls of Troy, the Trojans held off the Achaean army for ten years. The besiegers never took the city by assault, and in the end had to resort to the trick of the wooden horse.
— Homer, Iliad (siege narrative), and Odyssey 8.492–520 on the horse [9]
A strong position frustrated a superior coalition for a decade, until the defenders were drawn out of their own security.
As long as the Trojans stayed with their ground (thunni), the attackers’ plans kept failing, which is the Kural’s point.
Philosophy
“Hence the skillful fighter puts himself into a position which makes defeat impossible, and does not miss the moment for defeating the enemy.”
— Sun Tzu, The Art of War IV.14 (tr. Lionel Giles) [10]
Sun Tzu says a well-chosen position removes the enemy’s chance of winning.
This is the Kural’s claim that holding the right place makes the enemy’s plans come to nothing.

Real Life Example

Masséna halts before the Lines of Torres Vedras
October 1810 - March 1811 · North of Lisbon, Portugal
During the Peninsular War, Wellington had a great network of forts, redoubts and barriers built across the hills north of Lisbon, using the natural terrain and blocking the roads, and laid waste the country in front of them. In October 1810 his Anglo-Portuguese army withdrew behind these Lines, and Marshal Masséna's French army, arriving before them, did not attempt a full assault. After a winter of great hardship and heavy losses from hunger, the French began to retreat from Portugal in March 1811.
— Sources: [11] [12]
Without a major battle, a well-chosen and fortified position defeated an invasion simply by making the enemy's plan impossible.
The kural says that if those who have found the right place hold fast to it, enemies who planned to beat them will have to give up their plans, which is exactly what Masséna did.
