Kural 203

Dread of Evil Deeds · Theevinaiyaccham · தீவினையச்சம்

அறிவினுள் எல்லாந் தலையென்ப தீய செறுவார்க்கும் செய்யா விடல்.
Arivinul ellaan thalaiyenba theeya, Seruvaarkkum seyyaa vidal.

Meaning

Wise people say the highest wisdom of all is not doing bad things even to those who hurt us.

Elementary

If someone knocks down your sandcastle, you might want to knock down theirs. But the smartest thing — smarter than anything in any book — is to not do something bad back. Being kind even to someone who was mean to you is the cleverest thing of all.

University

Here Valluvar relocates the virtue of non-retaliation from the realm of emotion (forgiveness) into the realm of intellect: it is the ‘head’ (தலை) of all knowledge. செறுவார் means those who actively hurt or oppress one — genuine enemies, not mere irritants. The claim is that true intelligence sees what the vengeful cannot: that returning evil only multiplies it (the logic of 202) and binds the retaliator to the same consequences. The idea stands in striking agreement with Socrates in the Crito, who argues on rational grounds that one must never return wrong for wrong.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“One who is not envious but is a kind friend to all living entities, who does not think himself a proprietor and is free from false ego, who is equal in both happiness and distress, who is tolerant, always satisfied, self-controlled… such a devotee of Mine is very dear to Me.”
— Bhagavad Gita 12.13–14 (Prabhupada, As It Is) [4]
Krishna describes the ideal devotee as without hatred toward any being (adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānām) and tolerant (kṣamī) — a person who does not return hostility.
Shares the ideal of non-hostility toward all, including those who harm; less explicit about enemies than the Kural.
Christianity
“Recompense to no man evil for evil… Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”
— Romans 12:17, 21 (KJV) [5]
Paul instructs believers never to repay wrong with wrong, but to defeat evil by doing good.
A direct parallel: do no evil even to those who have done evil to you.
Islam
“And not equal are the good deed and the bad. Repel [evil] by that [deed] which is better; and thereupon the one whom between you and him is enmity [will become] as though he was a devoted friend.”
— Qur’an 41:34 (Sahih International) [6]
The Qur’an teaches responding to hostility with something better than the offense, promising that enmity can turn into friendship.
Same refusal to answer harm with harm, plus a practical wisdom about its effects.
Judaism
“Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD.”
— Leviticus 19:18 (KJV) [5]
The Torah forbids taking revenge or even nursing a grudge, grounding the command in love of neighbour.
Forbids retaliation, the core of the Kural; Torah’s scope is framed around one’s own people.
Buddhism
“Hatred is never appeased by hatred in this world. By non-hatred alone is hatred appeased. This is a law eternal.”
— Dhammapada 5 (trans. Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Buddha presents non-retaliation as a law of nature: returning hatred only perpetuates it; only non-hatred ends it.
Like Valluvar, frames non-retaliation as insight into how things work — the highest wisdom.
Jainism
“A monk should not be angry if beaten, nor should he therefore entertain sinful thoughts; knowing patience to be the highest good, a monk should meditate on the Law. If somebody strikes a restrained, resigned Śramaṇa somewhere, he should think: ‘I have not lost my life.’”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 2.26–27 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895) [8]
Mahāvīra's teaching on the 'troubles' (parīṣaha) a monk must bear includes being beaten. He must not answer with anger, or even with sinful thoughts, toward the one who strikes him.
Valluvar calls refusing to harm even those who harm us the highest wisdom; the Jain canon likewise calls patience under attack 'the highest good' and forbids returning evil even in thought.
Sikhism
“Fareed, answer evil with goodness; do not fill your mind with anger.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1381, Salok Sheikh Farid (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In a salok preserved in the Guru Granth Sahib, Sheikh Farid tells the seeker to repay those who do evil with good and to keep anger out of the heart.
The Kural calls refraining from evil even toward those who harm us the highest of all wisdom; Farid gives the same counsel, and goes further by asking for active good in return.
Greek Mythology
At the close of the Oresteia, Athena persuades the Furies to abandon the endless blood-for-blood vendetta of the house of Atreus and accept a court of justice, transforming them into the ‘Kindly Ones’.
— Aeschylus, Eumenides (paraphrase) [10]
Greek myth mostly celebrates vengeance; the Eumenides is its famous turning point, where wisdom (Athena) breaks the cycle of retaliation.
Next-best match: wisdom ending retaliation, though via public justice rather than personal non-retaliation.
Philosophy
“Then we ought not to retaliate or render evil for evil to any one, whatever evil we may have suffered from him.”
— Plato, Crito 49c (Jowett) [11]
In prison, Socrates reasons with Crito that since doing wrong is never right, returning wrong for wrong can never be right either.
Exact parallel, reached — as in the Kural — through reason rather than sentiment.

Real Life Example

Herbert Hoover's American Relief Administration feeds famine-stricken Soviet Russia
1921-1923 · Soviet Russia (Volga region and beyond)
In 1921, after the writer Maxim Gorky appealed abroad for help with a severe famine in Soviet Russia, Herbert Hoover, head of the American Relief Administration, negotiated an agreement to organise relief inside the country, even though the United States and the Soviet government were deeply hostile to each other. Funded by voluntary contributions and a 20 million dollar appropriation from the US Congress, the ARA's kitchens were feeding nearly 11 million people a day by the summer of 1922, and it also supplied medicine to thousands of hospitals. Historians still debate the mission's politics, but its scale is well documented.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A country that regarded the Soviet regime as an enemy nevertheless fed millions of its people in their time of need.
The kural calls it the highest wisdom to refrain from doing harm even to those who are hostile to us, and the ARA went further, doing large-scale good in a country whose government the United States opposed.
