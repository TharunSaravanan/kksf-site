Kural 1090

Mental Disturbance Caused by Her Beauty · Thagaiyanankuruthal · தகையணங்குறுத்தல்

உண்டார்கண் அல்லது அடுநறாக் காமம்போல் கண்டார் மகிழ்செய்தல் இன்று.
Undaarkan allathu adunaraak kaamampol kandaar makizhseydhal indru.

Meaning

Strong drink brings joy only to those who drink it. Love is different, because it brings joy even to those who only look.

Elementary

A treat only makes you happy once you eat it. But seeing someone you love, like a parent coming home, makes you happy just by looking. The young man says love is special in this way: just seeing her fills him with joy.

University

Undaar (உண்டார்) is 'those who have drunk' and kandaar (கண்டார்) 'those who have seen'; the pairing undaar / kandaar carries the whole comparison. Adu-nara (அடுநறா) is literally 'cooked (boiled, distilled) toddy', i.e. strong drink, and makizh-seythal (மகிழ்செய்தல்) is 'causing delight or intoxication'. Mu. Varadarasanar: toddy intoxicates only those who drink it, not, like kaamam, those who merely see it. The verse closes the chapter by turning the hero's distress into joy, and its praise of love over drink sits easily with the Kural's earlier chapter against intoxicants (Kallunnaamai, ch. 93). Pope: 'The palm-tree's fragrant wine, / To those who taste yields joys divine; / But love hath rare felicity / For those that only see!' Identical on both sources.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“While contemplating the objects of the senses, a person develops attachment for them, and from such attachment lust develops, and from lust anger arises.”
— Bhagavad Gita 2.62 (tr. A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, 1972) [3]
The Gita traces how merely dwelling on what the senses present gives rise to attachment and desire.
The Kural celebrates the fact that mere seeing intoxicates; the Gita analyses the same process as a danger.
Christianity
“How fair is thy love, my sister, my spouse! how much better is thy love than wine! and the smell of thine ointments than all spices!”
— Song of Solomon 4:10 (KJV) [4]
The Song of Songs declares love far more delightful than wine.
The Kural makes the same comparison: love outdoes strong drink.
Islam
“And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquility in them; and He placed between you affection and mercy.”
— Qur'an 30:21 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur'an presents love between spouses as a divine sign that brings peace and joy.
The Kural praises the joy of love; the Qur'an grounds that joy in God's design.
Judaism
Genesis says that Jacob served seven years for Rachel, and they seemed to him only a few days because of his love for her.
— Genesis 29:20 (paraphrase; cf. JPS Tanakh on Sefaria) [6]
Love lets Jacob find delight simply in being near Rachel, even before marriage.
The Kural, too, says love gives joy just by seeing, before anything is 'tasted'.
Buddhism
“...a Sakyan girl — the envy of the countryside — glanced up at me, with her hair half-combed, and said, 'Hurry back, master.' Recollecting that, I don't enjoy leading the holy life.”
— Udana 3.2, Nanda Sutta (tr. Thanissaro Bhikkhu; excerpt) [7]
For Nanda, the memory of one glance is enough to unsettle his whole life as a monk.
The Kural says love intoxicates by sight alone; Nanda is intoxicated by the mere memory of a sight.
Jainism
“...thus she was seen by Rathanêmi, whose (peace of) mind became (thereby) disturbed...”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 22.34 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45; excerpt) [8]
In the Uttaradhyayana, the monk Rathanemi happens to see Rajimati sheltering from the rain in a cave, and the sight alone throws his mind into turmoil.
The Kural says love acts through sight alone; this Jain story shows the same, as a warning.
Sikhism
“O Baba, the mind is intoxicated with the Naam, drinking in its Nectar. It remains absorbed in the Lord's Love.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 360, Raag Asa, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Speaking to yogis who used intoxicants, Guru Nanak says the true intoxication is the mind drinking in the Naam (divine Name) and staying absorbed in God's love.
The Kural says strong drink delights only those who drink it, while love delights even those who merely look; Guru Nanak also sets love above wine, calling love of the Name the truer intoxication.
Greek Mythology
Narcissus falls in love simply by looking at a beautiful face in a pool, and cannot tear himself away from the sight.
— Ovid, Metamorphoses 3.339-510 (paraphrase) [10]
Ovid's myth shows love born and fed entirely through the eyes.
The Kural, too, says love can intoxicate by sight alone.
Philosophy
Kant argued that a judgment of beauty is a 'disinterested' delight: we take pleasure in the beautiful simply in contemplating it, without needing to possess or consume it.
— Immanuel Kant, Critique of Judgment (1790), §§2-5 (paraphrase) [11]
For Kant, beauty pleases in the act of looking, unlike the merely agreeable, which pleases through consumption.
This is the Kural's contrast exactly: drink must be consumed, but beauty delights the one who only sees.

Real Life Example

Shah Jahan builds the Taj Mahal for Mumtaz Mahal
1631–1648 · Agra, India
After his wife Mumtaz Mahal died in 1631, the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan ordered a great white marble mausoleum built in her memory at Agra. Construction ran from 1631 to 1648, and both of them are buried there. UNESCO, which listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1983, calls it one of the universally admired masterpieces of the world's heritage.
— Sources: [12] [13]
The Taj Mahal is the world's best-known monument to love, admired by people who never knew the couple.
The kural says love, unlike strong drink, delights even those who only look; the love behind the Taj Mahal still moves the countless visitors who simply look at it.
