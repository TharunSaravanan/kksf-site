Kural 587

Detectives · Otraadal · ஒற்றாடல்

மறைந்தவை கேட்கவற் றாகி அறிந்தவை ஐயப்பாடு இல்லதே ஒற்று.
Maraindhavai kaetkavat raagi arindhavai Aiyappaadu illadhae otru.

Meaning

A spy is someone who can uncover hidden secrets and has no doubt at all about what he has found.

Elementary

A good detective in a mystery story does two things: she finds the hidden clues, and she checks them until she is sure before she tells anyone. Guessing isn’t enough. This kural says a real investigator uncovers secrets and makes sure they are true.

University

மறைந்தவை கேட்க வற்று ஆகி means ‘being able (வற்று, ‘capable’) to hear what is concealed’. அறிந்தவை ஐயப்பாடு இல்லது means ‘having no doubt about what has been learned’. Mu. Varadarasanar glosses the second half as being able to decide firmly without wavering. Pappaiah adds that the spy draws secrets ‘from the very mouth of those who did the deed’, and Kalaignar that he learns through those who are present at secret meetings. So the couplet sets two standards, access to secrets and certainty about them, and the next two couplets (588-589) add a check on that certainty from the king’s side. The text is identical across the three sources, apart from the sandhi யறிந்தவை / யையப்பா டில்லதே in thirukkural-thamizh.org.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“A skilful person capable of guessing the mind of others is a fraudulent disciple. Having encouraged such a spy with honour and money rewards, the minister shall tell him, ‘Sworn to the king and myself, thou shalt inform us of whatever wickedness thou findest in others.’”
— Kautilya, Arthashastra 1.11 (trans. R. Shamasastry) [4]
Kautilya’s first class of spy is chosen for his skill at reading hidden intentions and is bound by oath to report them.
Reading what others hide is the Kural’s மறைந்தவை கேட்க வற்று.
Christianity
“For there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed; neither hid, that shall not be known. Therefore whatsoever ye have spoken in darkness shall be heard in the light; and that which ye have spoken in the ear in closets shall be proclaimed upon the housetops.”
— Luke 12:2-3 (KJV) [5]
Jesus teaches that whispered secrets will eventually be heard and brought into the open.
Secrets told ‘in the ear in closets’ are exactly the மறைந்தவை the Kural’s spy is able to hear.
Islam
“And a man came from the farthest end of the city, running. He said, ‘O Moses, indeed the eminent ones are conferring over you [intending] to kill you, so leave [the city]; indeed, I am to you of the sincere advisors.’”
— Quran 28:20 (Sahih International) [6]
A well-wisher learns of the chiefs’ secret plot and rushes to warn Moses with a clear, certain report.
He learns a closed council’s hidden plan and delivers it without hesitation, as the Kural’s ideal informant would.
Judaism
“And one of his servants said, None, my lord, O king: but Elisha, the prophet that is in Israel, telleth the king of Israel the words that thou speakest in thy bedchamber.”
— 2 Kings 6:12 (KJV) [5]
The king of Aram suspects a traitor because Israel always knows his plans, and learns that the prophet Elisha reveals even words spoken in his bedchamber.
Knowing ‘the words spoken in the bedchamber’ is the furthest reach of the Kural’s hearing of hidden things.
Buddhism
In the Mahā-Ummagga Jātaka, the sage Mahosadha’s trained parrot, sent to the court of King Cūḷani-Brahmadatta, overhears the king and his chaplain Kevaṭṭa secretly plotting to conquer all of India and brings the plan back to his master.
— Mahā-Ummagga Jātaka (Jātaka 546), as summarised in the Dictionary of Pali Proper Names (paraphrase) [7]
The Jātaka shows a hidden plot being discovered and reported with certainty in time for the wise minister to act.
The parrot overhears what was meant to stay hidden, which is the Kural’s ideal of hearing the concealed.
Jainism
“(The excellence of faith depends on the following) eight points: 1. that one has no doubts (about the truth of the tenets); 2. that one has no preference (for heterodox tenets); 3. that one does not doubt its saving qualities …”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 28.31 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The first limb of right faith in Jainism (niḥśaṅkita) is freedom from doubt about what has been rightly understood.
The Kural's ideal spy is left with no doubt about what he has found; Jainism likewise prizes conviction free of doubt as the first mark of sound understanding.
Sikhism
“O Nanak, the Creator knows everything; nothing can be hidden from Him.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 302, Raag Gauree (Gauree ki Vaar, salok), Guru Ram Das (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In a salok of Gauree ki Vaar, Guru Ram Das reminds the listener that no concealed act or intention escapes the Creator's knowledge.
The Kural's spy can draw out what is hidden and is left with no doubt about it; Gurbani gives such complete and certain knowledge of hidden things to God alone.
Greek Mythology
When Demeter searches everywhere for her stolen daughter, it is Helios, the Sun who watches over gods and men, who tells her the hidden truth: Zeus had given Persephone to Hades, who carried her off to the underworld.
— Homeric Hymn to Demeter 62-87 (paraphrase) [10]
Only the all-seeing Sun knows the concealed deed, and he reports it plainly and with certainty.
Helios has what the Kural requires: access to what is hidden and an account free of doubt.
Philosophy
Descartes’ first rule of method is never to accept anything as true that he does not clearly know to be so, and to include nothing in his judgments beyond what is presented to his mind so clearly and distinctly that he has no occasion to doubt it.
— René Descartes, Discourse on Method, Part 2 (paraphrase) [11]
Descartes makes freedom from doubt the test for accepting any proposition.
The Kural’s ஐயப்பாடு இல்லது (‘free of doubt’) applies the same standard to a spy’s findings.

Real Life Example

Marian Rejewski breaks the German Enigma machine
1932-1939 · Warsaw, Poland
At the end of 1932 Marian Rejewski, a young mathematician in the Polish Cipher Bureau, used mathematical methods to reconstruct the wiring of the German military Enigma machine, and with Jerzy Rozycki and Henryk Zygalski he went on to read German Enigma messages through the 1930s. In 1938 the team built a device called the bomba to help find daily settings. In July 1939, at Pyry near Warsaw, Polish intelligence handed over its methods and reconstructed Enigma machines to British and French representatives.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Rejewski drew out secrets that Germany believed to be beyond reach, and did so with mathematical rigour that left no doubt about the results.
The kural describes the spy who can draw out what is hidden and has no doubt about what he has found, and Rejewski's reconstruction of Enigma met both standards.
