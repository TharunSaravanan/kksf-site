Kural 919

Wanton Women · Varaivin Makalir · வரைவின் மகளிர்

வரைவிலா மாணிழையார் மென்தோள் புரையிலாப் பூரியர்கள் ஆழும் அளறு.
Varaivilaa maanizhaiyaar mendhol puraiyilaap pooriyargal aazhum alaru.

Meaning

The soft arms of jewel-wearing women who keep no limits are like a deep, hellish swamp that low and foolish men sink into.

Elementary

Picture a pretty field that is really a deep mud bog. It looks soft and inviting, but anyone who walks in without thinking gets stuck and sinks. Valluvar says that ‘love’ that is only bought and sold is like that bog.

University

This couplet supplies the chapter’s title word: varaivilaa (வரைவிலா), ‘without varaivu’. Mu. Varadarasanar glosses varaivu as ‘a moral limit’ (ozhukka varaiyarai), and Puliyur Kesigan and Solomon Pappaiah as ‘not distinguishing between high and low’ among those who pay; in akam poetry varaivu also means ‘marriage’, which yields the reading ‘women outside wedlock’. Maanizhaiyaar (மாணிழையார்) is ‘those with fine jewels’. Alaru (அளறு) means both mire or mud and naraka (hell), and the commentators exploit both: Kalaignar speaks of ‘the mire called hell’. Puraiyilaa pooriyargal (புரையிலாப் பூரியர்கள்) are ‘base men without worth’; Sivayogi Sivakumar takes purai as ‘fault’, so ‘those who do not know what is wrong’. Both sources agree on the text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“There are three gates leading to the hell of self-destruction for the soul—lust, anger, and greed. Therefore, one should abandon all three.”
— Bhagavad Gita 16.21 (tr. Swami Mukundananda) [3]
Krishna names lust and greed among the gates to hell.
Kural 919 pictures the place where lust on one side and greed on the other meet as alaru, a hell-mire.
Christianity
“Her feet go down to death; her steps take hold on hell.”
— Proverbs 5:5 (KJV) [4]
Proverbs describes the path of the ‘strange woman’ as leading down to death and hell.
Valluvar’s alaru is the same downward destination.
Islam
The Qur’an tells believers not even to come near zina (unlawful intimacy), calling it a shameful deed and an evil way.
— Qur’an 17:32 [5]
The command is to keep clear of the path altogether, not merely to avoid the final act.
Kural 919 likewise treats the path itself as a mire that swallows those who enter.
Judaism
“A prostitute is a deep pit; A forbidden woman is a narrow well.”
— Proverbs 23:27 (Revised JPS 2023, Sefaria) [6]
Proverbs compares such company to a deep pit that is hard to climb out of.
Almost the same image as Valluvar’s alaru, the mire in which base men sink.
Buddhism
“Whoever is overcome by this wretched and sticky craving, his sorrows grow like grass after the rains.”
— Dhammapada 335 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Dhammapada calls craving ‘sticky’, something that clings and spreads sorrow.
Valluvar’s mire is also sticky: those who step in sink further.
Jainism
“As an elephant, sinking down in a quagmire, sees the raised ground but does not get to the shore, so do we who long for sensual pleasures, not follow the path of monks.”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 13.30, cf. 13.34 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [8]
King Brahmadatta admits to the sage Citra that he is stuck in sensual pleasure like an elephant in mud, and the lecture ends with him sinking into the deepest hell.
It closely matches Kural 919's picture of the soft arms of women without limits as a hell-like mire into which undiscerning men sink.
Sikhism
“Those fools, who drink in the wine of evil-mindedness, become the husbands of prostitutes.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 399, Raag Aasaa, Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Guru Arjan says that people who drink the wine of bad counsel become fools in the company of prostitutes. Elsewhere Gurbani holds out the hope that even the prostitute Ganika was saved by remembering the Name, so no one is beyond grace.
The Kural calls such company a hell-like mire for base, undiscerning men, and Guru Arjan likewise names the evil-minded fool as the one who sinks into it.
Greek Mythology
Odysseus’ scouts in the land of the Lotus-Eaters taste the honey-sweet lotus and lose all wish to return home; he has to drag them back to the ships by force.
— Homer, Odyssey 9 [10]
The episode shows pleasure that makes people forget their purpose and stay stuck.
Kural 919’s base men likewise sink into a sweet-seeming mire they cannot leave.
Philosophy
“...he who passes unsanctified and uninitiated into the world below will live in a slough, but that he who arrives there after initiation and purification will dwell with the gods.”
— Plato, Phaedo 69c (tr. Benjamin Jowett) [11]
Socrates cites the mystery teaching that the impure soul ends in a slough, a muddy swamp, in the afterlife.
Plato’s slough and Valluvar’s alaru are the same image: an unpurified life sinks into mire.

Real Life Example

Jahandar Shah's brief reign and devotion to Lal Kunwar
1712-1713 · Delhi, Mughal Empire, India
Jahandar Shah became Mughal emperor in 1712 after a war of succession. Britannica describes him as a weak and degenerate ruler who raised the courtesan Lal Kunwar, a former dancing girl, to the position of empress-consort and lavished riches on her while failing to pay his soldiers. Within a year his nephew Farrukh-Siyar, backed by the Sayyid brothers, defeated him, and he was put to death in 1713.
— Sources: [12] [13]
An emperor's indulgence and neglect of his army brought a swift end to his reign and his life.
The kural describes a hell-like mire into which undiscerning men sink through women who keep no limits, and contemporary historians saw Jahandar Shah's reign as just such a descent.
