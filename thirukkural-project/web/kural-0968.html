Kural 968

Honour · Maanam · மானம்

மருந்தோமற்று ஊன்ஓம்பும் வாழ்க்கை பெருந்தகைமை பீடழிய வந்த இடத்து.
Marundhomatru oonombum vaazhkkai perundhagaimai Peedazhiya vandha idaththu.

Meaning

When your honor and greatness are being destroyed, why hold on to life just to feed your body? Is that a medicine that will let you live forever?

Elementary

Everyone grows old one day, no matter what. This kural asks: if you will not live forever anyway, why trade away your good name just to stay comfortable a little longer?

University

The rhetorical question marundho (மருந்தோ, 'is it a medicine?') implies a medicine against death (saavaamai, deathlessness), as Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah both supply. Oon ombum vaazhkkai (ஊன் ஓம்பும் வாழ்க்கை) is a life that merely 'nourishes the flesh', set against perundhagaimai (பெருந்தகைமை, nobility) and peedu (பீடு, dignity, greatness), whose destruction is the occasion. The argument is that since death cannot be escaped, saving the body at the cost of honour gains nothing lasting. Kalaignar makes this explicit: there is no medicine for not dying, so do not lower your worth for a better life. Text identical on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net (some editions divide it மருந்தோமற் றூனோம்பும்).

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“One who has taken his birth is sure to die, and after death one is sure to take birth again. Therefore, in the unavoidable discharge of your duty, you should not lament.”
— Bhagavad Gita 2.27 (tr. A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada; vedabase.io) [3]
Krishna reminds Arjuna that death is certain for all who are born, so fear of it should not turn him from duty.
The Kural uses the same certainty of death to argue that clinging to bodily life at the cost of honour gains nothing.
Christianity
“For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: and whosoever will lose his life for my sake shall find it. For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?”
— Matthew 16:25-26 (KJV) [4]
Jesus teaches that holding on to life at any cost loses what really matters.
The Kural similarly asks what is gained by preserving the body while losing one's worth.
Islam
“Say, ‘Indeed, the death from which you flee - indeed, it will meet you. Then you will be returned to the Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, and He will inform you about what you used to do.’”
— Quran 62:8 (Sahih International) [5]
The Quran reminds those who run from death that it will meet them anyway.
Like the Kural, it says no amount of self-preservation is a medicine against death.
Judaism
“There is no man that hath power over the wind to retain the wind; neither hath he power over the day of death; and there is no discharge in war; neither shall wickedness deliver him that is given to it.”
— Ecclesiastes 8:8 (JPS 1917, via Sefaria) [6]
Kohelet says no one controls the day of death, and wickedness cannot save the wicked.
This supports the Kural's question: dishonourable survival is no defence against death.
Buddhism
“Neither in the sky nor in mid-ocean, nor by entering into mountain clefts, nowhere in the world is there a place where one will not be overcome by death.”
— Dhammapada 128 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [7]
The Buddha says there is nowhere to hide from death.
The Kural concludes from the same fact that honour should not be traded for a little more life.
Jainism
“As a lion takes hold of an antelope, so Death leads off a man in his last hour; neither mother, nor father, nor brother will, at that time, save a particle (of his life).”
— Uttaradhyayana Sutra 13.22 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The monk Citra warns King Brahmadatta that death takes everyone and no one can save even a fraction of a life.
The Kural uses the same truth to show that clinging to the body at the cost of dignity is pointless.
Sikhism
“Death would not be called bad, O people, if one knew how to truly die.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 579, Raag Vadhans, Alahaniyan, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the Alahaniyan, songs of mourning in Raag Vadhans, Guru Nanak says death is not to be feared by one who knows how to die rightly.
The Kural asks whether a life that only keeps the body going can be a medicine against death; Guru Nanak also refuses to treat mere survival as the highest good, since what matters is dying rightly.
Greek Mythology
In the Iliad, Sarpedon tells Glaucus that if they could escape old age and death by avoiding battle he would not fight; but since countless fates of death stand over men and none can escape them, they should go forward and win glory.
— Homer, Iliad 12.322-328 [10]
Because death cannot be avoided, Sarpedon argues, it is better to live and die with honour.
The same reasoning as the Kural: since there is no medicine against death, do not trade honour for survival.
Philosophy
“The difficulty, my friends, is not in avoiding death, but in avoiding unrighteousness; for that runs faster than death.”
— Plato, Apology 39a-b (tr. Benjamin Jowett; classics.mit.edu) [11]
Socrates says the real danger is not death, which comes anyway, but doing wrong.
The Kural likewise says saving one's body is no remedy, while losing one's worth is the real loss.

Real Life Example

Dietrich Bonhoeffer returns to Germany from New York
July 1939 · New York, United States, and Berlin, Germany
In June 1939 the German pastor and theologian Dietrich Bonhoeffer, an opponent of the Nazi regime, accepted an offer from friends to come to Union Theological Seminary in New York, which would have kept him safe abroad. Within weeks he decided that his place was in Germany and sailed home in July 1939, shortly before the outbreak of war. He joined the resistance to Hitler, was arrested in 1943, and was executed at Flossenburg concentration camp on 9 April 1945.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Bonhoeffer judged that a safe life abroad would cost him his integrity and his right to help rebuild Germany after the war.
The kural asks whether a life that merely keeps the body going is any medicine against death when one's dignity is at stake, and Bonhoeffer gave up safety rather than live with that loss.
