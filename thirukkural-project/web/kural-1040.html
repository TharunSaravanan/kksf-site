Kural 1040

Agriculture · Uzhavu · உழவு

இலமென்று அசைஇ இருப்பாரைக் காணின் நிலமென்னும் நல்லாள் நகும்.
Ilamendru asaii iruppaaraik kaaNin nilamennum nallaaL nakum.

Meaning

When the good lady called Earth sees people sitting lazily and saying, 'We are poor,' she laughs at them.

Elementary

Imagine someone sitting beside a big empty field and complaining, 'I have nothing to eat!' Valluvar says the Earth would giggle, because the field is right there, ready to grow food for anyone willing to work it.

University

ilam endru (இலம் என்று, 'saying "we have nothing"') and asaii iruppaar (அசைஇ இருப்பார், 'those who sit idle', with asaii lengthened by alapedai) are the people the Earth laughs at. nilam ennum nallaaL (நிலம் என்னும் நல்லாள்) means 'the good woman called Earth', and nakum (நகும்) means 'will laugh'. Puliyur Kesikan, following Parimelazhagar, says she laughs to herself, because the remedy for their poverty lies in her and needs only effort; Solomon Pappaiah reads the laughter as mockery, and Kalaignar names her Bhoomiththaai (Mother Earth). The chapter ends where it began, with land as the answer to want, and leads directly into Nalkuravu (ch. 105, Poverty). Pope's rendering is 'The earth, that kindly dame, will laugh to see, / Men seated idle pleading poverty.' The Tamil text matches on ytamizh.com and thirukkural.net.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working. One cannot even maintain one’s physical body without work.”
— Bhagavad Gita 3.8 (Bhagavad-gītā As It Is, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda, vedabase.io) [3]
Krishna tells Arjuna that action is better than inaction and that even keeping the body alive requires work.
Sitting idle and pleading want contradicts the plain fact the Gita states and the Kural's Earth laughs at.
Christianity
“The sluggard will not plow by reason of the cold; therefore shall he beg in harvest, and have nothing.”
— Proverbs 20:4 (KJV) [4]
The lazy man who will not plough in season ends up begging at harvest time.
The sluggard's self-made poverty is exactly what the Kural's Earth laughs at.
Islam
“It is He who made the earth tame for you - so walk among its slopes and eat of His provision - and to Him is the resurrection.”
— Qur'an 67:15 (Saheeh International) [5]
The Qur'an presents the earth as made ready for human use and tells people to go out into it and seek their provision.
The earth is ready and waiting, so idling and pleading poverty is what the Kural's Earth laughs at.
Judaism
“Rava said to the Sages who would attend his study hall: I implore you; during the months of Nisan and Tishrei, the crucial agricultural periods, do not appear before me. Engage in your agricultural work then so that you will not be preoccupied with your sustenance all year.”
— Babylonian Talmud, Berakhot 35b (William Davidson Edition, Koren-Steinsaltz English, via Sefaria) [6]
Even the great teacher Rava sends his students to their fields in the farming seasons rather than have them neglect their livelihood.
Rava expects even scholars to farm in season rather than plead want, in keeping with the Kural.
Buddhism
“The idler who does not exert himself when he should, who though young and strong is full of sloth, with a mind full of vain thoughts — such an indolent man does not find the path to wisdom.”
— Dhammapada 280 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita, accesstoinsight.org) [7]
The Dhammapada condemns the able-bodied person who sits in sloth when he should be making an effort.
The strong idler is the person the Kural's Earth laughs at, though here the loss is spiritual rather than economic.
Jainism
“As the fallow leaf of the tree falls to the ground, when its days are gone, even so the life of men (will come to its close); Gautama, be careful all the while!”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 10.1 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, sacred-texts.com) [8]
Mahavira repeatedly urges his disciple never to be careless (pramada) for even a moment, because life is short.
The Jain refrain against negligence matches the Kural's scorn for idle people who let what is in reach go unused.
Sikhism
“Fareed, musk is released at night. Those who are sleeping do not receive their share.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1382, Saloks of Sheikh Farid (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
Sheikh Farid says the fragrance of grace is handed out in the night hours, and those who lie asleep miss their portion.
The Kural says Earth laughs at those who sit idle and complain that they have nothing; Farid likewise says those who sleep through the time of giving go without their share.
Greek Mythology
“Work is no disgrace: it is idleness which is a disgrace. But if you work, the idle will soon envy you as you grow rich”
— Hesiod, Works and Days 311-313 (tr. H.G. Evelyn-White, 1914, sacred-texts.com) [10]
Hesiod tells Perses that idleness, not labour, is shameful, and that the worker soon prospers.
Hesiod's scorn for the idle matches the Earth's laughter at those who sit and plead poverty.
Philosophy
“Of course there are—sluggards must be included; for you can't do your own business when you are asleep, nor make others do theirs.”
— Xenophon, Oeconomicus 12.12 (tr. E.C. Marchant, Loeb 1923, Perseus Digital Library) [11]
Ischomachus names sluggards, alongside drunkards, as people who can never become careful managers of a farm.
The sleeping sluggard who gets nothing done is the idle person the Kural's Earth laughs at.

Real Life Example

Yacouba Sawadogo turns barren land into forest with zai pits
c. 1980 onwards · Yatenga region, Burkina Faso
During a severe drought around 1980, farmer Yacouba Sawadogo began improving the traditional zai planting pits, making them wider and deeper and preparing them before the rains, on land that had been barren and abandoned. He created a forest of nearly 40 hectares with more than 60 species of trees and shrubs, and from 1984 held market days where farmers from many villages learned the technique.
— Sources: [12] [13]
His work helped restore the productivity of tens of thousands of hectares in the region and earned him the Right Livelihood Award and the UN's Champions of the Earth award.
The kural says the Earth laughs at those who sit idle crying 'we have nothing', and Sawadogo showed that even land given up for dead would yield to effort.
