Kural 446

Seeking the Aid of Great Men · Periyaaraith Thunaikkodal · பெரியாரைத் துணைக்கோடல்

தக்கா ரினத்தனாய்த் தானொழுக வல்லானைச் செற்றார் செயக்கிடந்த தில்.
Thakkaa rinaththanaaith thaanozhuka vallaanaich Chetraar seyakkidandha thil.

Meaning

Enemies can do nothing against someone who is able to live as one of a group of good and worthy people.

Elementary

If you stay close to good and wise people and act like one of them, your enemies will find there is nothing they can do to hurt you.

University

‘Thakkaar inaththan’ — one who belongs to the fellowship of the worthy — moves from the friendship of individuals to membership in a circle. ‘Thaan ozhuka vallaan’ (one able himself to conduct himself so) implies that belonging requires one’s own discipline; the worthy will not keep company with the unworthy. The consequence is framed in the negative with total scope: ‘setraar seyak kidandhadhu il’ — there remains nothing for enemies to do. The protection is both practical (good advice closes vulnerabilities) and reputational (a ruler surrounded by the virtuous is hard to isolate).

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
Throughout the Mahabharata, Duryodhana’s schemes against the Pandavas — poisoning Bhima, the house of lac, the dice game and the attempt to disrobe Draupadi — repeatedly fail because the Pandavas are sheltered by Vidura’s counsel and Krishna’s friendship.
— Mahabharata, Adi and Sabha Parvas (tr. K. M. Ganguli) [4]
Those who kept company with the wise and the divine proved unassailable, despite their enemies’ power.
Illustrates the Kural: enemies can find no effective harm to do to one who walks among the worthy.
Christianity
“And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.”
— Ecclesiastes 4:12 (KJV) [5]
Qoheleth observes that a person alone can be overpowered, but companions together withstand attack.
Companionship as protection against enemies, as in the Kural.
Islam
“… when he said to his companion, ‘Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us.’”
— Quran 9:40 (Sahih International) [6]
During the Hijra, the Prophet and Abu Bakr hid in the cave of Thawr with pursuers at the entrance; the Quran recalls the Prophet reassuring his companion, and the pursuers did not find them.
The righteous companionship of the cave, under divine protection, left enemies unable to harm them.
Judaism
Jonathan, son of King Saul, binds himself in covenant to David, warns him of Saul’s intent to kill him, and helps him escape.
— 1 Samuel 20 [5]
David’s friendship with the righteous Jonathan shields him from a powerful enemy.
Like the Kural, a worthy ally renders the enemy’s designs ineffective.
Buddhism
The Buddha teaches that monks who feel fear in lonely places should recollect the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Sangha — the community of the noble ones — and their fear, trembling and dread will pass.
— Dhajagga Sutta, SN 11.3 [7]
Taking refuge in the noble community dispels fear, like soldiers who take heart looking at their commander’s banner.
Belonging to the worthy (the Sangha) as protection against threats — a softer, internal version of the Kural’s claim.
Jainism
“The grass near the stump of the tree will not shake with the plough of the ploughman. Feeble though they be, the anger of enemies will not come on those who have joined the society of the good.”
— Naladiyar, ch. 18 'Good Society', stanza 8 (English tr. in The Indian Antiquary; wisdomlib.org) [8]
The Naladiyar compares a person who stands among the good to grass sheltered by a tree stump, which the plough passes by without uprooting.
This is almost the same thought as the kural, which says enemies can do nothing to one who lives in the fellowship of the worthy. Both promise that even a weak person is safe from enemies inside that circle.
Sikhism
“In the Company of the Holy, all one's enemies become friends.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 271, Raag Gauree (Sukhmani), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In the Sukhmani Guru Arjan teaches that within the Saadh Sangat enmity dissolves and even former enemies become friends.
The Kural says enemies can do nothing to one who lives in the fellowship of the worthy; Guru Arjan says that in such fellowship enmity itself disappears.
Greek Mythology
In Odyssey 22, when Odysseus fights the suitors, Athena appears at his side in the likeness of Mentor and turns aside the spears the suitors throw, so that none can harm him or his companions.
— Homer, Odyssey 22 [10]
The suitors, though many, can do nothing against the hero who stands with his wise ally.
Enemies find nothing they can do to one standing with the worthy.
Philosophy
Epicurus taught that it is not so much our friends’ help that helps us as the confidence of their help.
— Epicurus, Vatican Sayings 34 [11]
The assurance of trustworthy friends is itself a form of security against fortune and enemies.
Security through reliable, worthy friends, as in the Kural.

Real Life Example

Frederick the Wise shelters Martin Luther at the Wartburg
1521-1522 · Wartburg Castle, Eisenach, Germany
After the Diet of Worms in 1521, Emperor Charles V declared Martin Luther an outlaw. Luther's protector, Frederick III, Elector of Saxony, known as Frederick the Wise, arranged for him to be taken on the road and hidden at the Wartburg castle, where he lived in disguise for nearly a year. There Luther translated the New Testament into German, and it was published in 1522.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Under the protection of a respected prince and his circle, a man condemned by the most powerful ruler in Europe was able to continue his work unharmed.
The kural says enemies can do nothing to one who lives within the company of the worthy, and Luther's protectors made the emperor's sentence powerless against him.
