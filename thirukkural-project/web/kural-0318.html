Kural 318

Not Doing Evil · Innaa Seyyaamai · இன்னா செய்யாமை

தன்னுயிர்க்கு இன்னாமை தானறிவான் என்கொலோ மன்னுயிர்க்கு இன்னா செயல்.
Thannuyirkku innaamai thaan arivaan en kolo Mannuyirkku innaa seyal.

Meaning

A person knows well how pain feels in their own life. So why do they cause that same pain to other living beings?

Elementary

You know how much a bee sting hurts, because it happened to you. So why would anyone go around stinging others? This kural asks: if you know how pain feels, how can you give it to someone else?

University

Kural 318 turns the rule of 316 into a question, and the rhetorical form (en kolo, ‘why, then?’) exposes a contradiction in the person who harms. The contrast between thannuyir (one’s own life or self) and mannuyir (the lives that dwell on earth, all living beings) widens the circle beyond humans, in keeping with the Kural’s broad concern for all creatures, developed further in the next chapter on not killing. The argument is essentially one of consistency: whoever knows pain from the inside cannot claim not to know what they are doing when they cause it. Similar appeals to self-knowledge appear in the Gita’s ‘comparison to one’s own self,’ the Dhammapada’s instruction to put oneself in another’s place, and the Torah’s reminder that Israel knows the heart of the stranger because it was once a stranger.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“He is a perfect yogī who, by comparison to his own self, sees the true equality of all beings, in both their happiness and their distress, O Arjuna!”
— Bhagavad Gita 6.32 (As It Is) [3]
Krishna says the highest yogi uses his own experience of joy and sorrow to understand everyone else’s.
Both texts ground concern for others in self-knowledge: your own distress tells you what others feel.
Christianity
“And as ye would that men should do to you, do ye also to them likewise.”
— Luke 6:31 (KJV) [4]
Jesus tells his followers to treat others exactly as they would want to be treated.
Both make one’s own experience the measure of right treatment of others.
Islam
“Whoever wishes to be delivered from the fire and enter the garden should die with faith in Allah and the Last Day and should treat the people as he wishes to be treated by them.”
— Sahih Muslim 1844a (sunnah.com) [5]
The Prophet makes treating people as you wish to be treated a condition for salvation.
Both appeal to what one knows one wants, or does not want, for oneself as the guide for treating others.
Judaism
“Also thou shalt not oppress a stranger: for ye know the heart of a stranger, seeing ye were strangers in the land of Egypt.”
— Exodus 23:9 (KJV) [4]
The Torah forbids oppressing strangers because Israel knows from its own history exactly how a stranger feels.
This is the Kural’s argument: you know this pain yourself, so how can you inflict it on another?
Buddhism
“All tremble at violence; life is dear to all. Putting oneself in the place of another, one should not kill nor cause another to kill.”
— Dhammapada 130 (trans. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [6]
The Buddha reminds us that every being fears violence and loves life, so we should put ourselves in their place and never harm them.
Both reason from one’s own fear of harm to the duty not to harm any living being.
Jainism
“All beings are fond of life, like pleasure, hate pain, shun destruction, like life, long to live. To all life is dear.”
— Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.2.3.4 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 22, 1884; wisdomlib.org) [7]
The Ācārāṅga states the universal fact on which ahiṃsā rests: every living being values its own life and recoils from pain.
Valluvar asks why someone who knows his own pain inflicts it on other lives (mannuyir). The Jain sutra answers from the other side: every one of those lives feels what he feels.
Sikhism
“You say that the One Lord is in all, so why do you kill chickens?”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1350, Raag Prabhaatee, Bhagat Kabir (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
Kabir exposes the contradiction in someone who professes that God dwells in every creature yet takes a creature's life.
The Kural asks why one who knows pain in one's own life inflicts it on other lives; Kabir's question likewise exposes the self-contradiction in harming beings one admits are like oneself.
Greek Mythology
In the last book of the Iliad, old King Priam comes to Achilles to beg for the body of his son Hector. He asks Achilles to think of his own aged father. Achilles, remembering his father and his own grief, weeps with Priam and gives back the body.
— Homer, Iliad 24.477–551 [9]
Achilles stops abusing Hector’s body once he recognises Priam’s grief as the same grief his own father will feel.
Achilles moves from inflicting pain to showing mercy exactly when he realises he knows that pain from the inside, the Kural’s point.
Philosophy
“When asked how men might live most virtuously and most justly, he said, ‘If we never do ourselves what we blame in others.’”
— Diogenes Laertius, Lives of Eminent Philosophers 1.36 (Thales) (trans. C. D. Yonge) [10]
Thales, one of the Seven Sages, sums up the just life as never doing what we condemn when others do it.
Both expose the inconsistency of the one who harms: we know what we object to, so we must not do it ourselves.

Real Life Example

Anna Sewell writes Black Beauty to win kindness for horses
1877 · Old Catton, Norfolk, England
Anna Sewell, who was disabled from a young age and had difficulty walking, spent the last years of her life confined to her house as an invalid while writing Black Beauty, a fictional autobiography of a horse. She stated that one of her aims was to induce kindness, sympathy and an understanding treatment of horses. The book, first published in 1877 by Jarrold of Norwich, is said to have been instrumental in ending the cruel use of the checkrein, or bearing rein.
— Sources: [11] [12]
Sewell used her final years to make readers feel the suffering of animals from the inside, by telling the story in the horse's own voice.
The kural asks why anyone who knows pain in his own life would inflict it on other living beings, and Sewell's book made readers recognise in a horse's suffering the same pain they knew themselves.
