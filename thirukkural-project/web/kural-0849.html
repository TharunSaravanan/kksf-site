Kural 849

Ignorance of Fools · Pullarivaanmai · புல்லறிவாண்மை

காணாதான் காட்டுவான் தான்காணான் காணாதான் கண்டானாம் தான்கண்ட வாறு.
Kaanaadhaan kaattuvaan thaankaanaan kaanaadhaan kandaanaam thaankanda vaaru.

Meaning

If you try to make a fool see the truth, you end up looking like the fool, because the fool is sure they already see everything their own way.

Elementary

Try explaining to a little brother who is sure the moon follows his car that it only looks that way. He just laughs at you and says you don’t understand. The poet says that when you try to teach someone who is sure he’s right, you end up looking silly.

University

The kural is a wordplay on kaan (காண், to see): kaanaadhaan (one who does not see) is set against kaattuvaan (one who shows) and kandaan (one who has seen), and the line turns on ‘thaankanda vaaru’ (in the way he himself saw). Mu. Varadarasanar reads it as: one who tries to enlighten a fool becomes a fool himself, while the fool appears wise by his own lights; Pappaiah adds that the fool regards his teacher as the fool. Kalaignar sharpens the second half: the fool shows himself off as wise on the strength of what he alone knows. Puliyur Kesigan gives a different construal: the fool who shows others only what he saw remains one who never saw the truth. The density of the kaan-roots makes the kural famous for its sound-play. Both sources give identical text.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“And that knowledge by which one is attached to one kind of work as the all in all, without knowledge of the truth, and which is very meager, is said to be in the mode of darkness.”
— Bhagavad Gita 18.22 (Prabhupada, As It Is) [3]
The Gita defines the lowest knowledge as a narrow view mistaken for the whole.
The fool ‘sees as he has seen’ and takes his small view for everything.
Christianity
“Speak not in the ears of a fool: for he will despise the wisdom of thy words.”
— Proverbs 23:9 (KJV) [4]
Proverbs advises against trying to teach a fool, who will only scorn the teaching.
The kural’s would-be teacher is scorned and made to look the fool.
Islam
“Take what is given freely, enjoin what is good, and turn away from the ignorant.”
— Qur’an 7:199 (Sahih International) [5]
The Prophet is told to urge what is right but not to get entangled with the obstinately ignorant.
Arguing with the fool only makes the teacher look foolish, so wisdom turns away.
Judaism
Proverbs warns that whoever corrects a scoffer brings shame on himself, and advises not to rebuke a scoffer lest he hate you, but to rebuke a wise man who will love you for it.
— Proverbs 9:7–8 (Tanakh) [6]
Instruction helps only those willing to receive it; aimed at the scoffer, it rebounds on the teacher.
This is the kural’s first half: the teacher of the fool is the one who ends up disgraced.
Buddhism
In the Udana, a king has blind men touch different parts of an elephant; each declares the elephant is like a pot, a fan, a pillar or a brush according to the part he felt, and they come to blows over it.
— Udana 6.4, Tittha Sutta [7]
The Buddha uses the parable for teachers who cling to partial views and quarrel over them.
Each blind man ‘sees as he has seen’ and cannot be taught otherwise, exactly kandaanaam thaankanda vaaru.
Jainism
“Now this driver (viz. Garga), who had to deal with bad bullocks, thought: 'What have I to do with bad pupils? I am disheartened. 'As are bad pupils, so are bad bullocks; I shall leave these lazy donkeys, and shall practise severe austerities.'”
— Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 27.15–16 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45, 1895; sacred-texts.com) [8]
The learned elder Garga, worn out by pupils who argue and will not be convinced by reasons, finally gives up teaching them and turns to his own austerities.
The Kural warns that whoever tries to show the truth to a fool ends up looking foolish himself, and Garga's story shows the same trap: the teacher tires himself out and has to walk away.
Sikhism
“I do not understand myself, and yet I try to teach others. Such is the guide I am! O Nanak! The one who is blind shows others the way, and misleads all his companions.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 140, Raag Majh, Majh Ki Vaar (salok 2 before pauri 6), Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In Majh Ki Vaar Guru Nanak, speaking humbly in his own voice, describes the guide who lacks understanding yet teaches others and leads them astray.
The Kural says the fool shows others only what he himself saw and remains one who never saw the truth; Guru Nanak's blind guide does the same.
Greek Mythology
Cassandra, given true prophecy but cursed by Apollo never to be believed, foresees the fall of Troy and Agamemnon’s murder, yet those around her dismiss her as raving.
— Aeschylus, Agamemnon [10]
The one who truly sees is taken for mad by those who cannot see.
Like the kural’s teacher, the seer is made to look the fool by the unseeing.
Philosophy
In the allegory of the cave, the prisoner who has seen the sun returns to free the others; with eyes unused to the dark he seems ridiculous to them, and they would rather kill him than be led up.
— Plato, Republic VII (516e–517a) [11]
Plato shows the cave-dwellers mocking the one who tries to show them reality.
The teacher looks the fool, while the prisoners trust what they themselves have seen.

Real Life Example

Geologists dismiss Alfred Wegener's continental drift
January 1912 onward · Frankfurt and Marburg, Germany
On 6 January 1912 the German scientist Alfred Wegener presented his theory that the continents had once been joined and had drifted apart to a geological meeting in Frankfurt, and he expanded it in later books. Most geologists of his day reacted with hostility, attacked Wegener personally as an outsider, and dismissed the idea as a fairy tale. Only decades later, in the 1960s, did new evidence lead to the theory of plate tectonics and the acceptance of his central idea.
— Sources: [12] [13]
Wegener's case shows how a person who presents an unwelcome truth can be treated as the foolish one by those who are sure of their own view.
The kural says whoever tries to show the truth to those who will not see ends up looking like the fool, because they are certain they already see everything their own way, and Wegener was ridiculed by geologists who were the ones in error.
