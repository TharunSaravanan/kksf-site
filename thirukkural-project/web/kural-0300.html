Kural 300

Veracity · Vaaymai · வாய்மை

யாமெய்யாக் கண்டவற்றுள் இல்லை எனைத்தொன்றும் வாய்மையின் நல்ல பிற.
Yaam meyyaak kandavatrul illai enaiththondrum Vaaymaiyin nalla pira.

Meaning

Of all the things we have found to be truly real, there is nothing at all that is better than truthfulness.

Elementary

Imagine you searched the whole world and all the best books for the most precious treasure. Valluvar says he did that kind of search — and the greatest treasure he found was simply telling the truth.

University

The chapter closes with a personal verdict — one of the rare places Valluvar speaks in the first person (yaam, ‘we’). ‘Meyyaak kandavai’ means ‘things seen/found as real,’ and there is a deliberate play between mey (reality, truth) and vaaymai (truthful speech): among all that is mey, nothing surpasses vaaymai. Pappaiah reads kandavai as ‘the books I have examined,’ making this a survey of all moral literature; Kalaignar takes it as the conclusion of research into what is true. Either way, the couplet sets veracity at the summit of all virtues, bringing the chapter full circle from its opening definition.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“There is no virtue equal to Truth: there is nothing superior to Truth. O king, Truth is God himself; Truth is the highest vow.”
— Mahabharata, Adi Parva, Section 74 (tr. K. M. Ganguli) [3]
Shakuntala tells King Dushmanta that nothing is higher than truth — it is God himself.
The same verdict as the Kural: nothing in existence is better than truth.
Christianity
“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”
— John 14:6 (KJV) [4]
Jesus identifies himself with truth itself, the one path to God.
Truth placed at the very summit of reality, as in the Kural.
Islam
“Allāh will say, ‘This is the Day when the truthful will benefit from their truthfulness.’ For them are gardens [in Paradise] beneath which rivers flow… That is the great attainment.”
— Qur’an 5:119 (Sahih International) [5]
On the Day of Judgment, truthfulness is named as the quality that brings the ‘great attainment.’
Truthfulness as the final, decisive good — the Kural’s verdict.
Judaism
“Rabbi Ḥanina said: The seal of the Holy One, Blessed be He, is truth [emet].”
— Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 55a (William Davidson / Koren-Steinsaltz tr.) [6]
The Talmud says God’s own signature — the mark he sets on all things — is truth (emet, spelled with the first, middle and last letters of the Hebrew alphabet).
If God’s own seal is truth, nothing can be higher — the Kural’s conclusion.
Buddhism
“Truth is the highest of savors.”
— Alavaka Sutta, Sutta Nipata 1.10 (tr. Thanissaro Bhikkhu) [7]
Asked by the demon Alavaka what is the highest of all tastes, the Buddha answers ‘truth,’ and adds: go and ask others if anything better than truth, self-control, endurance and relinquishment can be found.
After surveying all good things, truth stands at the top — the Kural’s verdict.
Jainism
“Truth alone is the essence in the world.”
— Praśnavyākaraṇa Sūtra 2.2, English rendering as given on Jainworld, 'Jina Sutra – Quotations' [8]
The Praśnavyākaraṇa, a Śvetāmbara Aṅga that sets out the five vows, praises truth as the very essence of the world.
Valluvar ends the chapter by saying that among all the real things he has found, none is better than truthfulness. The Jain Aṅga makes the same supreme claim: truth alone is the essence of the world.
Sikhism
“Truth is higher than everything; but higher still is truthful living.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 62, Siree Raag, Guru Nanak (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In a hymn in Siree Raag, Guru Nanak gives his verdict that Truth surpasses all else and that living truthfully (sach aachaar) ranks higher still. This is one of the best-known lines in Sikh ethics.
The Kural closes with Valluvar's own verdict that among all things found to be real, nothing is better than truthfulness. Guru Nanak gives the same judgement: nothing ranks above truth lived out.
Greek Mythology
“I have escaped. There is strength in my truth.”
— Sophocles, Oedipus Tyrannus 356 (tr. R. C. Jebb, rev. Alex Sens) [10]
The blind prophet Teiresias, threatened by King Oedipus, answers that the truth he holds is his whole protection — and the truth indeed prevails over the king.
Truth as the strongest possession, stronger even than a king’s power.
Philosophy
“Truth is the beginning of every good thing, both to Gods and men.”
— Plato, Laws 5.730c (tr. Benjamin Jowett) [11]
In the Laws, Plato’s Athenian says truth leads all goods, and that anyone who would be blessed should share in truth from the start of life.
Truth at the head of all goods — Valluvar’s closing verdict.

Real Life Example

Truth Prevails becomes the motto on the Czechoslovak and Czech presidential flag
1918-1938 (retained in law in 1993) · Prague, Czechoslovakia (now the Czech Republic)
After Czechoslovakia became independent in 1918, its first president, Tomas Garrigue Masaryk, drew on the heritage of the reformer Jan Hus and placed a version of the Hussite motto Truth Prevails on the presidential flag. When the Czech Republic was established, its law on state symbols retained the words Pravda vitezi (Truth Prevails) on the flag of the president, where they remain.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A modern state chose truth, above power or glory, as the single watchword to fly over its head of state.
The kural concludes that among all things found to be real, nothing surpasses truthfulness, a verdict a whole nation echoed by making Truth Prevails the motto of its highest office.
