Kural 299

Veracity · Vaaymai · வாய்மை

எல்லா விளக்கும் விளக்கல்ல சான்றோர்க்குப் பொய்யா விளக்கே விளக்கு.
Ellaa vilakkum vilakkalla saandrorkkup Poyyaa vilakke vilakku.

Meaning

Not every lamp is a real lamp. For people of good character, the lamp of truthfulness is the only real lamp.

Elementary

A flashlight helps you see in a dark room. But what helps you see the right thing to do when you are confused inside? This kural says truthfulness is that special inner flashlight — and for wise people, it’s the only lamp that really matters.

University

The couplet repeats vilakku (lamp) four times, playing on its meanings: ordinary lamps dispel outer darkness, but ‘the lamp that does not lie’ (poyyaa vilakku) dispels inner darkness. Varadarasan’s gloss adds the parenthetical contrast explicitly: lamps that remove darkness outside versus the lamp of non-falsehood that removes darkness within. Saandror — the noble, the wise — is a key Kural term (cf. ch. 99, Saandraanmai), suggesting truthfulness is the defining light of the fully mature person. The truth-as-light metaphor is among the most widely shared images across religious traditions.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
“From evil lead me to good, From darkness lead me to light, From death lead me to immortality.”
— Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.3.28 (tr. Swami Madhavananda) [3]
This ancient prayer (‘asato mā sad gamaya, tamaso mā jyotir gamaya’) pairs moving from untruth (asat) to truth (sat) with moving from darkness to light.
Truth and light are one movement — the Kural’s lamp of truth.
Christianity
“If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth.”
— 1 John 1:6 (KJV) [4]
John says that walking in darkness while claiming to be close to God is itself a lie; ‘God is light’ (1:5), and walking in the light means living in truth.
Truth and light belong together, falsehood and darkness together — the Kural’s image.
Islam
“Allāh is the Light of the heavens and the earth. The example of His light is like a niche within which is a lamp… Light upon light.”
— Qur’an 24:35 (Sahih International) [5]
The famous Verse of Light describes God’s guidance as a perfect lamp lighting the heart of the believer.
A lamp of divine truth that outshines every ordinary lamp — close to the Kural’s image.
Judaism
“O send out thy light and thy truth: let them lead me; let them bring me unto thy holy hill, and to thy tabernacles.”
— Psalm 43:3 (KJV) [4]
The Psalmist asks God’s light and truth to guide him like lamps on the way.
Truth as a guiding light — the Kural’s ‘lamp that does not lie.’
Buddhism
“When this world is ever ablaze, why this laughter, why this jubilation? Shrouded in darkness, will you not see the light?”
— Dhammapada 146 (tr. Acharya Buddharakkhita) [6]
The Dhammapada urges people wrapped in the darkness of ignorance to look for the true light of the Dhamma.
An inner light beyond ordinary lamps, sought by the wise — the Kural’s lamp.
Jainism
“Enlightened by the light of Truth, the wise transcends death.”
— Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.3.3 (Jainworld cites 3/3/66), English rendering as given on Jainworld, 'Jina Sutra – Quotations'; cf. Ācārāṅga II.16.6 (tr. Jacobi, SBE 22) [7]
This Jainworld rendering of an Ācārāṅga passage pictures truth as a light that lets the wise go beyond death. The same text calls the great vows, which include truthfulness, a 'light, illumining the three worlds'.
Valluvar says the lamp of truthfulness is the only real lamp for the wise. The Jain text likewise speaks of 'the light of Truth' as what truly illuminates the wise.
Sikhism
“If a hundred moons were to rise, and a thousand suns appeared, even with such light, there would still be pitch darkness without the Guru.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 463, Raag Aasaa (Asa di Vaar, salok), Guru Angad (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [8]
In a salok in Asa di Vaar, Guru Angad says that no amount of outward light can lift inner darkness. Only the Guru's light can do that.
The Kural says not every lamp is a true lamp, and for the wise only the lamp of truthfulness is real. Guru Angad likewise sets countless outer lights against the one inner light that truly dispels darkness.
Greek Mythology
“He does not grasp falsehood, and he is deceived by neither god nor man, neither in deeds nor in thoughts.”
— Pindar, Pythian 3.28-30 (tr. Diane Arnson Svarlien) [9]
Pindar describes Apollo (Loxias), the shining god of light and prophecy, as one who never touches falsehood and cannot be deceived.
In Greek myth the god of light is also the god of truth — light and truthfulness are one, as in the Kural.
Philosophy
“He lit a lamp in broad daylight and said, as he went about, ‘I am looking for a man.’”
— Diogenes Laertius, Lives of Eminent Philosophers 6.41 (tr. R. D. Hicks) [10]
Diogenes the Cynic carried a lamp in daylight to show that ordinary light could not find a genuine human being — later retold as searching for an honest man.
An ordinary lamp cannot reveal the true person; only honesty does — close to the Kural’s ‘not every lamp is a lamp.’

Real Life Example

Nehru's broadcast after Gandhi's death: the light that shone was no ordinary light
30 January 1948 · New Delhi, India (All India Radio)
On the evening of 30 January 1948, after Mahatma Gandhi was assassinated, Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru spoke to the nation over All India Radio without a prepared text. He began by saying that the light had gone out of their lives, then corrected himself, saying that the light that had shone in India was no ordinary light and would still be seen a thousand years later, because it represented the living, eternal truths. He urged his listeners not to respond to the killing with violence.
— Sources: [11] [12]
Nehru described Gandhi's lifelong devotion to truth as a light that death could not put out.
The kural says ordinary lamps are not true lamps and that for the wise only the lamp of truthfulness is the real lamp, which is the very image Nehru reached for when he spoke of Gandhi.
