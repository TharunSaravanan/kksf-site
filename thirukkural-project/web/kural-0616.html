Kural 616

Manly Effort · Aalvinaiyudaimai · ஆள்வினையுடைமை

முயற்சி திருவினை ஆக்கும் முயற்றின்மை இன்மை புகுத்தி விடும்.
Muyarchi thiruvinai aakkum muyatrinmai Inmai puguththi vidum.

Meaning

Effort brings riches and success. Not making an effort brings poverty.

Elementary

If you water a plant every day, it grows and gives you flowers. If you forget, it dries up. Effort is the water that makes good things grow, and not trying lets things wilt.

University

This is perhaps the most quoted couplet of the chapter, built on the paired opposites முயற்சி / முயற்றின்மை and ஆக்கும் / புகுத்திவிடும். Parimelazhagar reads திருவினை as திரு (wealth, prosperity) plus the empty increment இன் and the accusative ஐ, so ‘effort creates wealth’; Mu. Varadarasanar and Solomon Pappaiah follow this, whereas Sivayogi Sivakumar splits it as திரு + வினை, ‘auspicious deeds’, and Kalaignar as ‘excellent achievements’. இன்மை, ‘non-being’, is the standard Kural word for poverty (compare ch. 105, நல்குரவு), and புகுத்திவிடும் pictures idleness as actively ushering poverty into the house. The text is identical in both editions, thirukkural-thamizh.org printing the sandhi forms திருவினை யாக்கும் and யின்மை.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
A well-known verse of the Hitopadesha says that tasks are accomplished by effort, not by wishing, just as deer do not walk into the mouth of a sleeping lion.
— Hitopadesha, Introduction (the udyamena verse) [3]
Even the lion, king of beasts, must hunt; desire alone never produces results (udyamena hi sidhyanti kāryāṇi na manorathaiḥ).
Both the fable-book and the Kural teach that effort alone brings gain, while idleness brings nothing.
Christianity
“For even when we were with you, this we commanded you, that if any would not work, neither should he eat.”
— 2 Thessalonians 3:10 (KJV) [4]
Paul rebukes idlers in Thessalonica, telling them to work quietly and earn their own bread.
Paul and Valluvar both tie livelihood directly to labour and want to idleness.
Islam
“And that there is not for man except that [good] for which he strives”
— Qur’an 53:39 (Sahih International) [5]
The Qur’an lays down that a person’s portion is tied to his own striving (sa‘y).
This is the Kural’s claim that what effort earns is what one has.
Judaism
“In all labour there is profit; But the talk of the lips tendeth only to penury.”
— Proverbs 14:23 (JPS 1917) [6]
The proverb contrasts toil that yields gain with idle talk that leads to want.
The same two-part structure as the Kural: labour brings profit, its absence brings poverty.
Buddhism
“Living in this way, he leaves many duties undone, new wealth he does not get, and wealth he has acquired dwindles away.”
— Digha Nikaya 31, Sigalovada Sutta (trans. Narada Thera) [7]
The Buddha lists the excuses of the idle man, too cold, too hot, too early, too late, and states the economic result.
Idleness admitting poverty is the Kural’s second line in the Buddha’s words.
Jainism
“Saddalaputta, is your ware made by dint of exertion and manly strength, or on the other hand, is it made without exertion and manly strength?”
— Uvasagadasao (Upāsakadaśāḥ), ch. 7, Saddalaputta (tr. A.F.R. Hoernle), as quoted in the jainworld.com chapter 'Rival Sects' [8]
Mahavira asks the Ajivika potter Saddalaputta this question to refute Gosala's fatalism (niyati), which denied any role to exertion. The potter's own reactions show that he really does believe effort and conduct make things happen, and he becomes a Jain lay follower.
The Kural says effort creates prosperity; Mahavira makes the same point by showing that even the potter's prosperous trade depends on 'exertion and manly strength'.
Sikhism
“Make the effort, and you shall live; practicing it, you shall enjoy peace. Meditating, you shall meet God, O Nanak, and your anxiety shall vanish.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 522, Raag Gujri (Gujri ki Vaar, salok), Guru Arjan (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [9]
In a salok of Gujri ki Vaar, Guru Arjan uses the word udam (effort, exertion) and promises that the one who makes the effort and keeps practising it will gain life and peace.
The Kural says effort brings prosperity and idleness brings want; Guru Arjan likewise says effort brings life and peace and puts anxiety to flight.
Greek Mythology
“Through work men grow rich in flocks and substance, and working they are much better loved by the immortals.”
— Hesiod, Works and Days 293-319 (trans. H. G. Evelyn-White) [10]
Hesiod urges his brother Perses to work rather than seek wealth through lawsuits and idleness.
Hesiod and Valluvar give the same economic law: work creates wealth.
Philosophy
John Locke argues that it is labour that puts the difference of value on everything, reckoning that of the products useful to human life the far greater part is owed to labour rather than to nature.
— John Locke, Second Treatise of Government, ch. 5, §40 [11]
In his chapter on property, Locke makes labour the main source of wealth and value.
Locke gives a political-economic version of the Kural’s ‘effort produces wealth’.

Real Life Example

Konosuke Matsushita starts a workshop in his home in Osaka
7 March 1918 · Osaka, Japan
On 7 March 1918, twenty-three-year-old Konosuke Matsushita founded Matsushita Electric Housewares Manufacturing Works in Osaka with his wife Mumeno and her younger brother Toshio Iue. Their first product was an improved attachment plug, made partly with metal parts recycled from used light bulbs, and sales were weak until Matsushita began dealing directly with wholesalers. The business was incorporated in 1935 as Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. and became one of the world's largest electronics makers, renamed Panasonic in 2008.
— Sources: [12] [13]
A three-person family workshop grew, through persistent effort, into a global company and a source of prosperity for many thousands of employees.
The kural says effort builds up prosperity while lack of effort ushers in poverty, and the Matsushita workshop is a classic case of wealth built from almost nothing by work.
