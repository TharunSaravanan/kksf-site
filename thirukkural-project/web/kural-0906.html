Kural 906

Being Led by Women · Penvazhich Seral · பெண்வழிச் சேறல்

இமையாரின் வாழினும் பாடிலரே இல்லாள் அமையார்தோள் அஞ்சு பவர்.
Imaiyaarin vaazhinum paatilare illaal amaiyaardhol anju pavar.

Meaning

Even if they live as grandly as the gods, men who fear their wife's slender, bamboo-like arms have no real honour.

Elementary

A superhero with amazing powers is not much of a hero if he is too scared to act. What makes someone admirable is courage, not just strength or riches.

University

Imaiyaar (இமையார், 'the unblinking ones') is a classical Tamil epithet for the gods; paatu (பாடு) here means dignity or worth, and paatilar those lacking it. Amai-aar-thol (அமையார்தோள்) compares the wife's shoulders or arms to amai (bamboo), a conventional image of feminine grace; Parimelazhagar notes that the slender image heightens the irony: a warrior who conquered heroes' arms yet fears these gentle ones has no aanmai (manliness). He reads 'living like the gods' as the life of heroes who reached heaven through valour, and says this verse closes a group of four (903-906) on the fault of fearing the wife. Manakkudavar takes it to mean that even the wealthy are not respected by others. Kalaignar again reinterprets, aiming the criticism at men who submit to a wife merely for beauty, not for wisdom or character. The text is uniform across sources. Modern critics note the verse's equation of dignity with masculine dominance as a historically situated value.

Through the Lens of

Hinduism
King Pururavas, after the apsara Urvashi leaves him, wanders distraught and pleads with her to return, while she counsels him to recover himself.
— Rigveda 10.95 (Pururavas-Urvashi dialogue) [5]
A great king is reduced to helplessness by his attachment to a celestial woman.
Like the Kural's man who lives like the gods yet lacks dignity, Pururavas's royal splendour cannot hide his dependence.
Christianity
Samson, the strongest man in Israel, yields his secret to Delilah's persistent pleading and loses his strength and freedom.
— Judges 16 [6]
Extraordinary power proves useless once a man cannot stand firm against the woman he loves.
This mirrors the Kural's point that even godlike strength is worthless when a man is governed by a woman's will.
Islam
When the governor's wife falsely accuses Joseph, her husband, though he sees the truth, merely tells Joseph to overlook the matter and asks his wife to seek forgiveness.
— Qur'an 12:25-29 [7]
A powerful official acknowledges wrongdoing at home but lacks the resolve to act on it.
The Kural's dignified-seeming man who will not stand up within his own household finds a parallel in the governor's weak response.
Judaism
God tells Abraham to listen to Sarah in all that she says regarding Hagar and Ishmael.
— Genesis 21:12 [6]
Here divine instruction explicitly directs a patriarch to follow his wife's counsel.
A counterpoint to the Kural: in Genesis, heeding one's wife can be the path God endorses.
Buddhism
The Buddha's half-brother Nanda, newly ordained, pines for the beauty he left at home, until the Buddha shows him a higher aim and he becomes an arahant.
— Udana 3.2 (Nanda Sutta) [8]
A prince-turned-monk is held back by longing for a woman until he redirects his aspiration.
As with the Kural's godlike man without dignity, Nanda's status means little until he masters his attachment.
Jainism
“This has been done by many men who for the sake of pleasures have stooped so low; they become the equals of slaves, animals, servants, beasts of burden--mere nobodies.”
— Sutrakritanga 1.4.2.18 (tr. Hermann Jacobi, SBE 45) [9]
The Sutrakritanga says men who give themselves over to a woman for pleasure lose every bit of standing and become 'mere nobodies'.
This is Kural 906's point that men who fear their wife's arms have no paatu, no real dignity, however splendidly they live.
Sikhism
“They are not said to be husband and wife, who merely sit together. They alone are called husband and wife, who have one light in two bodies.”
— Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 788, Raag Soohee (Vaar), Guru Amar Das (tr. Sant Singh Khalsa) [10]
Guru Amar Das defines true marriage as a spiritual partnership of two people sharing one divine light, not a relationship of domination or fear.
The Kural mocks men who live in fear of their wives; Gurbani replaces that picture of rule and fear with partnership between equals.
Greek Mythology
Ares, the god of war, is caught in Hephaestus's net with Aphrodite and becomes a laughing stock before the assembled gods.
— Homer, Odyssey 8 (Song of Demodocus) [11]
The mightiest warrior-god is rendered ridiculous by his passion.
Like the Kural's man living like the gods yet lacking dignity, Ares's divinity cannot save him from humiliation.
Philosophy
Plutarch advises that a husband should govern his household not as a master over property but as the soul over the body, through sympathy and goodwill.
— Plutarch, Conjugal Precepts (Moralia 142E) [12]
Plutarch's ideal marriage combines partnership and affection with the husband's responsible leadership.
This reflects the same ancient household ideal behind the Kural, while emphasising mutual goodwill rather than fear in either direction.

Real Life Example

Madame de Pompadour's influence at the court of Louis XV
1745-1764 · Versailles, France
Louis XV declared he would rule without a chief minister, but Britannica records that his indolence and lack of self-confidence produced a court of rival ministers and mistresses, among whom Madame de Pompadour exerted considerable influence. She helped bring her protege the Duke de Choiseul into the ministry, and in 1756 the king, prompted by her, set aside his secret foreign policy. His reign saw reverses in foreign and military affairs, and he became unpopular with his subjects.
— Sources: [13] [14]
A king who presided over the most splendid court in Europe came to be seen as yielding his authority to a favourite, and lost the respect of his people.
The kural says that even those who live as splendidly as the gods have no real dignity if they fear the arms of a woman, and Louis XV's grandeur at Versailles did not protect his reputation.
